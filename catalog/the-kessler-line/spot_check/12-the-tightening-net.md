The news ticker on the lobby screen flashed: 'Inquiry Member Arrested: Disgraced Engineer Linked to Kessler Line Fraud.'

Iris Kane stopped mid-stride, watching the words scroll across the display. Theo Vance's photograph filled the upper corner. His name. His title. The word 'arrested' in bold caps.

Across the lobby, Adrienne Sol's office door clicked shut. The sound was deliberate. A performance note delivered on cue.

A security guard Iris didn't recognize stood by the main entrance. Broader than the daytime crew. Heavy-set. His eyes weren't scanning the room—they were fixed on the corridor Sol had just entered.

Her thumbnail found the edge of her phone case. A crack in the plastic. Something to press against.

Renn's statement aired forty minutes later during the midday briefing. Commissioner Aldous Renn stood neat and composed behind a podium, hands flat on the surface, collar buttoned to the top.

"Mr. Vance acted alone, exploiting an outdated approval procedure. The commission has identified the breach and has implemented new safeguards to prevent any recurrence. The inquiry continues its work with full integrity."

Full integrity.

Iris muted the screen. "Alone." Of course. A man with three decades of institutional memory. A man who'd signed off on load-testing budgets. A man who'd personally walked her through the evidence chain in the first week. "Suddenly he was a solo operator."

She didn't buy it. And neither, apparently, did Adrienne Sol.

Iris moved toward the elevators, keeping the security guard in her peripheral vision. He didn't follow. He just stood there, a fixed point in the lobby's flow of bodies. A marker.

The elevator opened. She stepped inside and pressed three.

Sol's department occupied the entire administrative wing. Iris stepped out into a hallway that smelled of fresh cleaning solution—ammonia, bleach, something chemical and recent.

The reception desk was empty. The computer monitor was dark.

She walked past three closed doors before she found the archive room. The window set into the door showed a narrow slice of the interior. Sol stood at a long table, sleeves rolled, methodically feeding documents into an industrial shredder. The machine hummed and whined, chewing paper in steady, even bites.

Beside Sol sat a stack of hard drives. A laptop with an attached portable shredder. A framed photograph on the table—backing pried open, glass removed.

Iris watched Sol extract a data disc from the photograph's frame and feed it into the portable shredder. The machine screamed. The disc came out in metallic curls.

This wasn't panic. This was procedure. Someone had trained for this. Someone had written a playbook for exactly this moment.

"Vance falls. Sol cleans. The chain stays unbroken."

Iris stepped back from the door. Her phone buzzed.

A single message from an unknown number. "He has one hour before counsel locks in. Conference room B. West annex."

Vance.

She took the fire stairs instead of the elevator. The stairwell was empty, the exit doors unpainted steel. Conference room B was a converted storage closet with a single table and two chairs.

Vance sat on the far side. No cuffs. A guard stood outside the door, visible through the narrow window.

Vance looked older. The skin beneath his eyes was loose, gray. He ran his hand through his hair three times before he spoke.

"She knew this was the play. Sol. She always knew."

"Then she's already cleaning," Iris said.

"She would have a schedule. A timeline. The first forty-eight hours are critical."

"Critical for what?"

Vance leaned forward. His voice was low, scraped dry. "Yvonne. Sol's personal assistant. She handles the dormant authorization codes. The finance override for the Kessler contract went through a shell subsidiary that hasn't existed in four years. The paperwork lives in a single physical file, and Yvonne controls access."

"Where is she now?"

"Her desk. She works through lunch. She won't leave until five."

"And Sol?"

"Sol will wait until Yvonne leaves. Then she'll clear the file personally."

Iris pressed her thumb against the edge of the table. A hairline crack ran from the corner to the center seam. "Why are you giving me this?"

Vance's laugh was hollow. "Because I'm already dead professionally. The only thing I have left is making sure the name on the top floor doesn't walk free."

"Who signed above Sol?"

Vance's hand stopped mid-air. "The signature block is blank. It's always blank. But it's a state block. Not a corporate one. Someone above the commission authorized the fast-track. Sol took her orders from that block."

"No name?"

"No. But the authorization code is traceable. Yvonne knows the code. She doesn't know what it authorizes, but she knows where it's filed."

Iris stood. "One hour?"

"Less now."

She left Vance in the conference room and took the stairs back to the administrative wing. The cleaning smell was stronger now. The reception desk was still empty.

Yvonne's cubicle was the fourth on the left. Iris recognized the nameplate from the organizational chart she'd studied in week one.

The desk was bare. Empty. The computer tower was gone. The pen holder was gone. Even the chair mat had been pulled up, leaving a rectangle of dust-free carpet.

A cleaner was vacuuming the cubicle next to Yvonne's. Iris checked her watch. Three forty-seven. Not cleaning hours.

"Where's Yvonne?"

The cleaner didn't look up. "Left early. Personal emergency."

"When?"

"Twenty minutes ago."

Sol had moved faster.

Iris scanned the cubicle. Nothing. Every surface wiped. Every drawer cleared. She crouched down and looked beneath the desk. A single staple lay on the carpet, bent out of shape. On the blotter pad edge, a faint impression—something had been clipped there. A note. A document.

She ran her thumbnail across the impression. Letters, too shallow to read.

When she straightened, the tall security guard was standing at the end of the hallway.

He didn't move. He just watched. Arms folded. Feet planted. A fixed point.

Iris held his gaze for three seconds. Then she turned and walked toward the fire exit, her pace even, her lungs tight.

The guard didn't follow. He didn't need to. The message had been delivered.

She pushed through the exit door into the stairwell and let it close behind her. The concrete walls muffled the building's ambient hum. Her phone was in her hand before she reached the first landing.

The private lab.

She dialed.

It rang four times.

"Ms. Kane." The technician's voice was tight. Tight in a way she hadn't heard before.

"The results are in?"

"Yes. And... you need to come in person."

"Why?"

A pause. A breath.

"We've had some strange visitors. Two men, this morning. No credentials. No appointment. They asked about a concrete sample logged under a reference number we don't use."

Iris's hand tightened on the phone. "What did you tell them?"

"That we don't have anything under that number. Which is true. We didn't log your sample under any number you supplied. I used a blind tracking code. Off-book."

"Are you still safe?"

"For now. But I won't keep the sample in the building overnight. If you want to see the results, you come tonight. Before eight."

"I'll be there."

She ended the call and stood in the stairwell, the phone pressed against her palm, the concrete wall cold against her shoulder.

Sol was cleaning. The lab was compromised. The sample was the only physical evidence that connected the collapse to the override, and someone was already hunting for it.

Iris pressed her thumbnail into the handset's edge. No cracks. No give.

She descended the stairs and exited through the ground-floor service door into an alley. The air smelled of dumpsters and damp concrete.

The security guard wasn't there. But someone else was.

A black sedan sat at the mouth of the alley, engine running, windows tinted too dark to see through.

Iris didn't stop walking. She turned left, away from the sedan, and headed toward the street where she'd parked four hours ago.

The sedan didn't follow.

It just sat there. Waiting. A fixed point.

Just like the guard.

Just like Sol's closed door.

Just like the blank signature block above her name.

The net was tightening. But Iris still had the core sample, and she still had eight hours before the lab closed.

She picked up her pace and didn't look back.
