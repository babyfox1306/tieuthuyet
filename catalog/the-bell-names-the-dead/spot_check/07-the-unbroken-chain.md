"If you're waiting for a sign, the bell already rang," Elena mutters as she presses her ear to the archive records room’s basement door, breath sharp in the chilly silence. Her skin prickles, not from fear—she’s long past calling it that—but from the certainty of being watched. On the other side of the locked door, somewhere in the stone throat of this building, Morrow Wick’s secrets fester.

She counts the windowpanes by touch—three, six, nine—before letting herself exhale. The habit is pointless, but it helps. The archivist’s absence stretches through the corridor like a warning. She stares at the burnt ledger and its charred edges, feeling the weight of every erased name pressing down on her skin.

Records were supposed to be safe here. That was the bargain. Instead, Elena is boxed in, the official air of the archive closing around her like a velvet-lined casket.

The bell. Always the bell.

She kneels, palm brushing the floorboards beneath the battered desk—searching for the thread, the connection, the opening move. Her thumb rubs automatically over the watermark on a misfiled parish census. If I can't prove it, it didn't happen. Superstition is for the frightened, not the responsible.

But responsible people do not get locked in municipal buildings by rival archivists who cannot meet her eyes.

She tries the handle again—pointless. She scans for any flaw, any anomaly. The room is old, but it’s been modernized: fluorescent hum, steel shelves. Still, the walls themselves—original. Hand-cut stone patched with warped baseboards.

Her eyes snag on a tile, bottom corner, where the aging floor meets chalky wainscoting. There—a hairline crack, recently widened. Elena’s pulse tightens. She digs a nail beneath the edge; the tile gives with a dry, complaining sound.

Behind it, dust. And something more.

A folded scrap of paper, stiff and yellowing, wedged tight. She drags it free and holds it under the angled lamp, careful to keep her gloves off—gloves are for people with something to hide. The hymnbook paper is marked by a spiral in soot, the very same pattern she saw on the final room of every victim. She almost smiles, lips bending in grim satisfaction. The chain leaves its mark, even here.

She smooths the paper, reading the words: three lines, all in Ada Voss’s handwriting. Her mother’s pen is unmistakable—precise, loops compressed, as if afraid of being overheard by the page itself.

She can’t afford to dwell—not when footsteps thud above, and the hush of the archive returns heavy as lead. She tucks the hymn page into her jacket’s inside pocket and slides the tile back in place, careful to keep every trace of her search invisible.

There is a reason the chain keeps looping. Even if no one else believes it.

She paces to the ledger, reviewing her last findings. Her notes: a wildflower (out of season, perfectly preserved), erased names, a signature always ending with a ‘V.’ And that sideways cross, scraped onto every altered record. Elena squares her shoulders, pushes away the static of dread.

The archivist will return soon, with Gideon Rusk in tow if he can manage it. She has minutes, maybe less.

She flips to the attendance register, reviewing sign-in patterns from the week of the fire. The signatures are cramped, sometimes overwritten, but the ‘V’ always floats suspiciously at the end. Ada Voss—always present, never entirely accountable. Sometimes full name, sometimes initials, but always the ‘V.’ Someone wanted that fact to survive the fire.

A tremor of motion in the hallway outside—Elena sprints through her mental checklist. The ledger, her notes, the hymnbook scrap—

A key rattles in the main door.

She stashes her satchel beneath the nearest shelf, half-crouching. Not hiding, just—waiting for a mistake. The light outside the frosted glass wavers, painting a thin blue line across her shoes.

She glances again at her newest find: the spiral soot mark is not random. It’s centered, carefully measured from each edge. Some kind of placement code. She remembers the scorch pattern on the nave floor at Saint Orison’s—another spiral, half-swept away but undeniably identical.

The town insists the deaths are the bell’s choosing. But Elena has seen enough ledgers to know when a pattern masquerades as fate.

She peers around—there. Just above the baseboard, a faint smudge, too deliberate to be neglect. She pulls out her phone, shining light across the seam. The smudge sharpens: another sideways cross, nearly invisible, traced in soot. Elena compares it to her notes. The cross on the ledger. The cross above the baseboard. The spiral in the hymn scrap.

Returning to the register, she flips to the roster that burned but survived—a handful of names erased, only the ‘V’ signature consistent, the sideways cross like a challenge.

She lays the hymn scrap over the page, aligning the soot spiral with the margin. A line of the old hymn—she hums it under her breath, though the act is more a sneer at the notion of comfort than any actual reverence.

Beneath the signature ‘A. Voss’, Ada had written in pencil, barely visible beneath soot: If it must go on, let the next see what I have hidden.  

Her mother’s secret.

A bang at the door. Elena stiffens, pocketing the clue. She can’t risk being found here, not with Gideon poised to snatch every scrap of evidence the moment her back turns.

She runs through her options. The main door is locked, but the records room has a trick—there’s an old staff panel behind the shelf that no one ever bothered to patch. Her predecessor hated drafts, always threatened to fix it, never did.

She moves quickly, sliding a stack of census books aside. The panel shifts under her hand. She ducks, slips through. The narrow space stinks of old pipe and mildew, but it leads to the undercroft—once coal storage, now a mostly forgotten crawlspace beneath the archive.

She bumps her head twice, curses three times, keeps crawling forward.

The undercroft lets her out behind the building, through a grilled vent. She emerges into the frozen dark, scraping dust from her sleeve, and circles back to the front steps before anyone is the wiser.

But the archive’s lights are on. Inside, two shadows cross—the archivist and, unmistakably, the stiff posture and silk tie of Gideon Rusk. She ducks low, snatching her satchel from its hiding spot, heart thumping. Rusk doesn’t see her, but she feels his presence like a poison on the air.

“Elena!” His voice peels out as she tries to slip past. Too late.

She straightens, refuses to flinch. “Working late, are we? You and your watchdog.”

He steps into the corridor, finger tapping his lips, the movement so precise it borders on parody. “Miss Voss. I’m going to need your bag.”

She rolls her eyes. “Of course you are. If it’s not in the register, it’s not real, right?”

He moves to block her. He never admits fault, but his eyes flicker toward her jacket pocket—he’s guessed she’s found something.

She meets his gaze, flat and fearless. “I’m not some loose ledger in need of your approval. You’re here to collect evidence, not protect the truth.”

He straightens his tie, buying time for a retort. “You’re interfering with an inquest. Your methods—”

“My methods don’t involve burning evidence.” Her voice cuts like glass. “Step aside.”

He doesn’t move. For one charged moment, it’s a standoff. She sees the telltale sign: his knuckles whitening around a clipboard, always clung to in a crisis.

“Give me your notes, Elena.”

“Try me, Gideon.”

She pushes past, but he blocks her, hand darting to her shoulder—almost a touch, but not quite, just enough heat to make her skin crawl. His gaze slips, never quite meeting hers, always angled just off true confrontation.

“You think you understand the pattern, but you don’t.” His voice is softer, for a second. “It’s not about what you want to find.”

She bares her teeth in a smile. “And yet, I keep finding it anyway.”

He releases her, finally—distaste plain in the set of his mouth. “THE BELL CHOOSES THE NEXT NAME.” The words echo, cold and ritualistic.

She lets the phrase hang, refusing to rise to it. But the bell chooses nothing. This is a chain, not a lottery.

She storms out, boots hammering the tile, her mind burning with every detail. She returns to her flat, triple-checks the locks (as always), and spreads the clues on her battered desk.

The wildflower—preserved, a memory trapped in carbon. The burned ledger, always falling open to the week of the fire. The initials ‘V’, always marking the handoff. The sideways cross—a relay baton passed in soot. And now, Ada’s hymn scrap, the spiral a key.

She maps the signatures and their corresponding rooms on a grid, overlaying them with the times of death. The pattern is not random. Each victim hands off to the next with a deliberate mark—a signature, a cross, sometimes a spiral.

She realizes, with a thrill of satisfaction and dread, that the soot signatures in every victim’s final room match the altered signatures in the ledger, each one passing the chain to someone who could unlock the next fraction of the truth. It’s a relay—each name written in soot, each clue left behind, selecting the single person who is necessary to move forward.

Not chosen by the bell. Chosen by the chain itself. By necessity. By design.

Her head pounds with sudden clarity—the deviation in each reenactment isn’t chaos; it’s a message. Each victim is compelled to reveal something only the next witness can interpret. The handoff is precise, surgical.

At the bottom of her pile, the hymn scrap slips from her fingers. She fumbles, retrieving it—and sees, for the first time, what’s written on its reverse, faded but undeniable: Ada’s loopy script, coded in the same odd sequence of numbers as the attendance rolls’ margin notes.

She grabs a pencil, feverish, transcribing the cryptic sequence. Ada’s message, hidden beneath the soot signature, reads:  
When you have counted the names erased, follow the spiral to what we left unburned.

Elena’s hands shake. There is something else—something Ada hid before the fire finished its work. And it is waiting, exactly where the spiral points.

She doesn’t allow the fear to surface, not even in private. She locks the evidence away, breathes out once, and is about to turn off her lamp when her phone buzzes—unknown number, a single text:

"Did you find the chain’s beginning?"

Her blood goes cold. Only someone enmeshed would know to ask that.

The chain is not broken yet, and she’s now in its crosshairs.

Outside, in the frozen silence, a bell tolls 2:17.

Elena’s eyes fall to Ada’s message. The spiral, the erased names, the phrase about what remains unburned—there’s only one place left to look.

She reaches for her coat, the next move already forming in her mind, as the bell’s echo pulses like an accusation through Morrow Wick.

In the silence, the spiral promises an answer she may not want to find.
