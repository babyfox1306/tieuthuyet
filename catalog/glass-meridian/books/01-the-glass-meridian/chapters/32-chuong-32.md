The hotel lobby smelled of cold marble and fresh orchids. Every surface gleamed with the kind of sterile perfection that cost more than Lin Wei's monthly salary. She kept her hands still on the table, resisting the urge to tap her pen against her thigh, and studied the woman across from her.

Sophia Petrova was exactly as the file described—forty, sharp cheekbones, hair pulled back so tight it stretched the corners of her eyes. She wore a charcoal blazer over a silk shell, no jewelry except a single platinum band on her left thumb. Her posture was immaculate, but her eyes moved constantly, cataloging exits, patrons, the placement of every espresso cup.

She's as paranoid as we are, Lin Wei thought. Good.

Adrian broke the silence first. "Thank you for meeting us."

"I didn't have a choice." Sophia's voice was low, accented with something Eastern European, precise. "You burned the one channel I've kept open for six years. That kind of desperation interests me."

"Desperation is a strong word," Adrian said.

"Then you're not in the right business." Sophia's gaze shifted to Lin Wei, lingered. "You're the journalist. The one who cost Aurelian Group two subsidiaries with that supply chain expose."

Lin Wei didn't flinch. "I reported what I found."

"You reported what they let you find." Sophia's lips curved, not quite a smile. "You think you're digging. But the Meridian has been laying foundations for decades. They know how to leave bait."

Adrian leaned forward, his voice dropping. "We're not here for warnings. We need information."

Sophia set her espresso down without drinking it. "I have information. The question is whether you're ready to receive it."

Lin Wei watched her hands. Still. Controlled. No tells. But the slight tension around her jaw betrayed something—fear, or calculation. Maybe both.

"We've already compromised our location," Lin Wei said. "We're here. That should tell you the risk we're willing to take."

"Risk is relative." Sophia's eyes flicked toward the lobby entrance, where a couple in business attire was checking in. "You've been chasing fragments. A name here, a code there. You think Elias Vance was the mole, but he was just a switchboard operator. The real signals went through him to someone else."

"Who?" Adrian's voice was sharp.

Sophia reached into her jacket. Lin Wei tensed, but Sophia only pulled out a slim card, the kind used for hotel key access. She placed it between them on the table, face down.

"One of their coordinators operates under a designation. C009. I've never seen his face, but I've followed his work. He's the one who restructured the Ascension Program after Project Phoenix collapsed. He's the one who buried the genetic research inside legitimate clinical trials around the world."

Lin Wei's mind raced. C009. A new thread. She kept her expression neutral. "Where can we find him?"

"You don't find him." Sophia's voice dropped. "He finds you. That's the point. He's already watching you. That server explosion in the Aurelian basement? That wasn't just a purge protocol. That was C009 sending a message."

Adrian's jaw tightened. Lin Wei saw the almost imperceptible clench she'd come to recognize. He was holding back something—anger, recognition, both.

"You know the designation," Lin Wei said quietly, watching Adrian.

He didn't answer immediately. His eyes remained fixed on Sophia. "How do you know all this?"

Sophia's composure flickered. "Because I used to be one of them. Analyst, grade three. I processed the data flows for the Mediterranean corridor until I realized what I was actually feeding."

"And you left?" Lin Wei asked.

"I disappeared." Sophia's voice hardened. "There's a difference. I've been running ever since, using dead systems, staying off the grid. And now you've pulled me back into the light. If they find me because of this meeting, I'll make sure you regret it."

Lin Wei met her stare. "We're careful."

"Careful is a luxury you can't afford." Sophia leaned back, crossing her arms. "Your infiltration of Aurelian's network bought you time, but it also painted a target on your backs. The Meridian doesn't tolerate loose threads. They'll send someone to cut you."

"Let them try." Adrian's voice was ice.

Sophia's eyes narrowed. "That bravado will get you killed. You're not dealing with corporate raiders. You're dealing with people who have been shaping global policy for fifty years. They own hospitals, research boards, regulatory agencies. They don't need to pull a trigger—they just need to apply the right pressure."

Lin Wei reached for the card, slid it toward herself. "This contains the information?"

"A fragment. Transaction records linking a shell company in Luxembourg to Hanfield's funding pipeline. The signatures are masked, but if you run them through your decryption algorithm, you'll see the pattern." Sophia paused. "C009 designed that algorithm. He'll know the moment you unlock it."

"Then we'll burn it before he can trace us back."

"You can't burn what you don't understand." Sophia stood, smoothing her blazer. "You've gained a piece of the puzzle, but the cost of trust—or mistrust—hasn't been determined yet. I suggest you decide quickly which side you're on."

Lin Wei rose, meeting her height. "We're on the side of stopping them."

"That's what I said once." Sophia's eyes were cold, distant. "Now I'm a ghost living in the margins. Think about that before you make your next move."

She turned to leave. Adrian's hand shot out, grazing her sleeve. "One more thing."

Sophia stopped, not turning.

"Why did you really respond? You could have ignored our message. Burned the channel. Fled."

A long silence. When Sophia spoke, her voice was barely audible. "Because your father, Adrian Vale, once made me believe I could trust someone in power. I was wrong. But I'm curious if you'll prove me differently."

She walked away without looking back.

Adrian's hand dropped. Lin Wei saw the flicker in his eyes—something raw, wounded. But he masked it quickly, shifting back to the card in her hand.

"She mentioned your father," Lin Wei said quietly.

Adrian's jaw tightened again. "She was a distraction. We need to focus."

"She recognized his name. That's not a coincidence."

"Nothing is." He met her eyes. "But we don't have time to unpack my family history. We have a new thread to follow."

Lin Wei held his gaze, letting the silence stretch. She wanted to push, to understand why his voice tightened every time Marcus Thorne came up. But the lobby was too open, too exposed. And Sophia's warning echoed in her mind.

C009 is already watching.

She pocketed the card. "Then let's move. We'll decrypt it somewhere secure."

Adrian nodded, already scanning the room for threats. Lin Wei followed his gaze, cataloging every face, every movement. The couple at the check-in counter. The businessman reading a newspaper near the window. The bartender polishing glasses.

Anyone could be the shadow operative.

They walked toward the exit, side by side, not touching. But when Adrian's hand brushed hers, a brief, deliberate contact, Lin Wei felt the unspoken communication pass between them. We're in this together.

She didn't pull away.

They stepped into the Zurich afternoon, the cold air hitting their faces. The street was clean, orderly, indifferent to the secrets they carried.

"We need a secure location," Lin Wei said.

"I have one. Follow me."

They moved through the crowd, blending with the pedestrians, never looking back. But Lin Wei could feel the weight of unseen eyes on her neck.

C009.

The name settled into her bones like a splinter. A new player, faceless, watching from the shadows. They had gained a piece of the puzzle, but the price of that knowledge was still unknown.

Adrian led her into a narrow alley, then through a service entrance into a building's basement. The air smelled of concrete and rust. He pulled out a small device, ran it over the walls, nodded.

"Clean."

Lin Wei exhaled, pulling the card from her pocket. "Let's see what she gave us."

Adrian produced a portable reader, connected it to his tablet. The screen flickered, then displayed a string of encrypted data.

"It's partial. Transaction routing through a bank in Geneva, but the endpoint is masked."

"Can you break it?"

He was already typing, fingers moving across the keyboard. "I can try. But if Sophia was right about C009, he'll know the instant I crack this seal."

Lin Wei watched him work, the concentration etched into his features. She thought about Sophia's parting words, the flicker of recognition when Adrian mentioned his father.

Why did she trust us enough to respond?

She didn't have an answer. But she knew one thing for certain: in this game, trust was the most dangerous currency.

And they'd just spent a piece of it.

"We're in." Adrian's voice pulled her back. "Look at this."

The screen filled with a map—nodes and connections, labeling financial pipelines, research facilities, and key personnel. One name blinked in red, highlighted with a marker: C009.

Beneath it, a single line of text.

Designation: Shadow Operative. Embedded in Interpol's Biotech Crimes Unit since 2019.

Lin Wei's blood turned cold.

"They have someone inside Interpol," she whispered. "They've been feeding them information the whole time."

Adrian's face was grim. "Sophia didn't just give us a clue. She gave us a target."

"And a warning." Lin Wei met his eyes. "If C009 is inside Interpol, he already knows about our investigation. He probably knew about this meeting before we sat down."

Adrian was silent for a long moment. Then he reached for his phone, typing a message.

"What are you doing?"

"Burning the card's encryption key. Making sure he can't trace us through this channel."

"And then?"

He looked up, his gaze hard, resolute.

"Then we find him before he finds us."

Lin Wei nodded, but the warning from Sophia echoed again.

The cost of trust—or mistrust—is yet to be determined.

They had gained a piece of the puzzle. But the shadow operative was already moving.

And the game had only just begun.
