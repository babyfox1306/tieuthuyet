The sterile glow of Lin Wei's laptop screen reflected in her determined eyes as she navigated a labyrinth of encrypted files, each click a step further into Adrian Vale's hidden world. The USB drive, a cold, metallic rectangle etched with the jagged symbol of Project Phoenix, was firmly plugged into her port. She had spent hours just trying to access its contents, the encryption far more sophisticated than anything she had encountered in her mother's journal. It was a digital fortress, designed to keep out anyone without the precise key.

But Lin Wei was not just anyone. She worked with information, lived by it, and knew that every lock had a weakness. The initial scans had yielded nothing but static, a dead end that most would abandon. Not her. She considered it a challenge, a taunt from the shadows that had enveloped her mother's life. Her fingers flew across the keyboard, a rhythmic tap-tap-tap, setting up a series of algorithms designed to probe for vulnerabilities, to exploit the smallest crack in the digital armor.

She was in Adrian's study again, a silent rebellion against his unspoken dominion. The faint, almost imperceptible hum of the sophisticated security system was a constant companion, a low thrumming beneath the silence of the penthouse. It reminded her that even here, in what felt like a sanctuary, she was under observation. She felt Adrian's presence even when he wasn't in the room, a phantom weight in the air, a persistent shadow at the edge of her periphery.

Hours bled into each other. Caffeine was her only friend, the only thing keeping her sharp as the city outside dimmed and then flared with its evening lights. She had bypassed the USB's primary encryption, not cracked it, but found a backdoor, a subtle flaw in its architecture that allowed her a glimpse. What she saw was not immediately decipherable, a cascade of alphanumeric strings, but it was a beginning.

Her screen flickered, displaying a directory. Most folders were still locked, but one, labeled "Aurelian-PX_Archive," caught her eye. PX. Project Phoenix. The symbol on the drive, the clue from her mother's journal, Professor Chen's terrified whispers. It was all converging.

She clicked on the folder. Access was granted, too easily, almost as if it had been waiting for her. A wave of suspicion washed over her. Nothing in Adrian Vale's world was ever easy. This was a gift, a crumb, and she instantly distrusted it. Why would this particular archive be so readily available when the rest of the drive was a digital vault?

Inside, she found what appeared to be internal corporate communications – memos, emails, technical reports. They were dated from 2011 to 2013, coinciding precisely with the Thorne-Vale Joint Research Initiative. Her fingers tightened on her mouse. This was where Adrian's father, Marcus Thorne, had been involved. This was Aurelian Group.

She began sifting through them, looking for keywords: 'Phoenix,' 'biomarker,' 'cellular reconstruction.' The initial batch of documents was dense with scientific jargon, financial projections, and corporate legalese, typical of a large-scale research venture. But then she hit a different type of file.

"Transaction Log - Project PX Assets." The title alone sent a chill down her spine. Assets. People were not assets.

The log detailed a series of substantial financial transfers from Aurelian Group to various shell corporations and offshore accounts. The dates aligned with the research initiative, but the amounts were staggering, far exceeding typical research overhead. Her journalistic instincts screamed. This wasn't just research. This was something else. The transactions were complex, layered, designed to obscure the true recipient. Classic money laundering, or perhaps, funding something highly illicit.

She began cross-referencing names mentioned in the transaction logs with publicly available data. Some were familiar, mid-level Aurelian executives. Others were completely unknown, phantom entities with no digital footprint outside these files. A knot of unease tightened in her stomach. This was a carefully constructed web, and she was only seeing the very top layer.

As she delved deeper, the hum of the security system seemed to intensify, a low, steady drone that vibrated through the floor. She paused, her gaze sweeping the room. Nothing seemed out of place. Yet, the feeling of being watched was palpable, a prickle on the back of her neck. Was this Adrian's doing? Was he deliberately leaving these breadcrumbs, testing her, guiding her? Or was he unaware that she had penetrated his carefully constructed digital barrier?

She leaned back, rubbing her temples. Her mind raced, connecting the dots. Her mother's illness, the symbols, Project Phoenix, the abandoned facility, Adrian's sudden, violent protection, and now these suspicious financial transactions linked directly to his company's past. Adrian Vale was involved, deeply. Not just as a beneficiary, but as an active participant, or at the very least, a curator of secrets.

A shadow fell across her screen. She didn't flinch. She simply angled her head, her gaze meeting Adrian's. He stood in the doorway of the study, a silent sentinel, his presence commanding despite the late hour. He wasn't in a suit, but rather a dark, open-collared shirt that accentuated the breadth of his shoulders. His jaw had that subtle clench she was beginning to recognize, a sign of agitation he rarely betrayed.

His eyes, dark and unreadable, swept over her, then to the laptop screen, lingering for a beat too long on the glowing text. She knew he saw the open files, the transaction logs, the name 'Aurelian-PX_Archive.' She didn't try to hide it. There was no point.

A silent acknowledgment passed between them, a recognition of their shared, yet opposing, investigations. He was searching for answers, just like her, but his methods were shrouded in secrecy, his motives hidden behind layers of corporate power and personal history. And he was clearly hiding something.

"Still at it?" His voice was low, devoid of warmth, yet without the usual edge of command. It was simply an observation, an assessment.

"The work of truth is never done," Lin Wei replied, her voice steady, refusing to yield. She tapped her pen rhythmically against the desk, a small defiance.

He didn't move from the doorway. His gaze was intense, dissecting her, trying to read the nuances of her expression, the implications of her open laptop. She met his stare, refusing to look away, refusing to show any vulnerability. She was a journalist, trained to withstand scrutiny, to extract information, not to offer it freely.

"You should get some rest," he finally said, the words a suggestion, not an order. But the underlying current of expectation was unmistakable.

"I'll rest when I have answers," she countered, pushing the laptop further onto the desk, subtly obscuring the most damning details without closing the files entirely. She watched his eyes, trying to gauge his reaction, to see if he recognized the specific files she had accessed. He gave nothing away. His expression remained a carefully constructed mask.

He stayed for another long moment, simply watching her. The silence stretched, thick with unspoken questions and simmering distrust. She felt the weight of his gaze, a strange mixture of surveillance and something else, something she couldn't quite decipher. Protectiveness? Frustration? A flicker of reluctant admiration? The ambiguity was unsettling.

Finally, he nodded, a slight, almost imperceptible dip of his head. "Don't burn yourself out." He turned, his silhouette disappearing back into the shadows of the hallway.

Lin Wei let out a slow breath she hadn't realized she was holding. He hadn't stopped her. He hadn't confiscated the drive. He hadn't even overtly questioned her about what she was doing. This 'permission' only solidified her suspicion. He was playing a game, and she was a pawn he was allowing to move, albeit on a very specific, controlled path. He wanted her to find something, but not everything. He was actively concealing information, steering her away from the core truth.

She turned back to the screen, her focus sharpened by the encounter. If he was allowing her access, there was a reason. She needed to find the details he didn't want her to see, the true secrets buried beneath the layers of controlled disclosures.

She moved on to a series of archived emails. These were less formal, more conversational, though still coded in corporate speak. One particular thread caught her attention. It was between Marcus Thorne and someone identified only as 'P. Chen.' Professor Chen. The man who had shown such fear at the mention of Project Phoenix.

The emails discussed "resource allocation" and "patient recruitment" for "Phase Two trials." The language was vague, designed to be innocuous, but Lin Wei read between the lines. Patient recruitment. For trials of a project Professor Chen himself had called a "mistake" involving genetic manipulation.

Her fingers flew, cross-referencing data points, financial transfers, and email timestamps. She found a distinct pattern: large financial transfers often coincided with mentions of "new subjects" or "expanded protocols." It was sickeningly efficient, a logistical nightmare handled with cold, corporate precision.

She dug deeper into the "P. Chen" email thread. Most were bland, procedural updates. But then she found one, sent late at night, from Marcus Thorne to Professor Chen, with an unusually urgent tone. The subject line was simply: "Urgent - Meridian Protocol Update."

Meridian. The word resonated with a chilling familiarity, though she couldn't place it immediately. It felt significant, a puzzle piece she hadn't known she was missing.

The email itself was heavily encrypted, far more so than the previous communications. It was a block of scrambled characters, requiring a higher level of decryption. Adrian had allowed her access to the archive, but not to its deepest secrets. This was the wall, the real barrier.

She worked for another two hours, her mind a whirlwind of algorithms and linguistic patterns. She knew her mother's research style, her linguistic tics, her preferred scientific terminology. If she could find a key, a pattern that linked to Lin Mei, it might unlock this deeper layer. She tried a heuristic approach, feeding snippets of her mother's earlier papers into a specialized decryption tool, searching for commonalities in encryption styles.

A small section of the encrypted email finally yielded. It was just a fragment, a broken sentence, but it sent a tremor through her.

"...the Glass... safeguards... Project Phoenix stability..."

The Glass. Her blood ran cold. The Glass Meridian. The words clicked, echoing in her memory from some obscure academic paper she might have skimmed, a whisper of a clandestine organization involved in advanced biotechnologies. This wasn't just Aurelian Group's shadowy past. This was something far larger, far more sinister.

Just as she was about to push further, to find the full context, her laptop chimed with an incoming call. Adrian's name flashed on the screen.

She hesitated, her fingers hovering over the deciphered fragment. The Glass Meridian. Project Phoenix stability. It was a breakthrough, a vital connection she couldn't ignore. But the call persisted, an insistent, demanding ring.

She reluctantly minimized the decryption window, the partial message now hidden beneath her taskbar. She couldn't ignore Adrian's summons, not after he'd just 'allowed' her this much latitude.

She clicked to answer. "Yes, Adrian?"

"Lin Wei," his voice was curt, devoid of pleasantries. "Prepare yourself. We have a critical business dinner in forty-five minutes. Be ready."

The line went dead before she could respond. A critical business dinner. Now? It was almost midnight. Her gaze flickered back to the minimized window, to the digital threads dangling precariously, on the cusp of unraveling a truth that could shake everything.
