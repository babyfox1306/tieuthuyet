The sterile glow of the server room painted their faces in harsh blues and whites, every data chip and printout a fragment of a conspiracy that kept expanding. Lin Wei pressed her palms flat against the cold metal table, the faint scent of ozone rising from the decrypted files like a phantom echo of the volatile research they contained.

"This doesn't stop at population control," she said, her voice ragged from sixteen straight hours of cross-referencing. "They're not just culling the weak. They're building a template."

Adrian stood motionless before the main monitor, his silhouette rigid against the humming server racks. He didn't turn. "C010's intel on the Meridian's inner workings pointed to registry stratification. But this..." He tapped the screen, sending a cascade of data trees expanding across the display. "This is an algorithm for sorting human genetic worth. Every medical database they've infiltrated, every hospital network, every insurance provider — they're mapping the entire species."

Lin Wei's pen tapped a staccato rhythm against the edge of the table. "C011 linked the threat against my mother to a specific timeline. They needed her access codes to the biobank repositories. And C012—" She pulled out the partially melted drive they'd recovered after losing the courier in the ambush. "—gave us the pharmaceutical delivery systems. Combined, they show a coordinated push toward a single event."

She plugged in the drive and pulled up the cross-referenced data. The three clue streams merged into a single horrifying pattern. "Look at the dates. The genetic registry completion target, the scheduled media blackout windows, the synchronized financial triggers across seventeen banks. They're not planning a gradual implementation."

Adrian finally turned, his eyes hollow with the weight of recognition. "It's a switch. One they can flip globally within forty-eight hours."

"The ascension program isn't about improving humanity over generations." Lin Wei's voice dropped to barely a whisper. "It's a forced reset. They intend to activate a global mechanism that sorts every living person into tiers of genetic viability. Those who don't meet the threshold — the vast majority — will be systematically denied medical care, reproductive rights, economic participation. A silent, comprehensive purge dressed as progress."

The silence that followed was absolute. Even the servers seemed to hold their breath.

Adrian moved toward her, his footsteps measured, deliberate. He stopped at the edge of the table, one hand gripping the metal frame until his knuckles whitened. "My father didn't just open the door for the Meridian. He helped build the house. The encryption I designed at seventeen — the backdoor I thought was my greatest asset — he anticipated it. He taught me everything I know, so he could predict my every move."

"He raised you as a contingency," Lin Wei said, the words bitter on her tongue. "Either as their successor or their scapegoat."

"Both." Adrian's laugh was hollow. "I was never the exception to his ambition. I was its instrument."

Lin Wei stood, her legs unsteady from exhaustion and adrenaline. "Then we make sure his instrument misfires."

She opened another file, the one Sophia Petrova had risked everything to pass them in that alley in Lisbon. "Sophia's data includes the Meridian's immediate target for activation. A centralized medical summit in Geneva, three weeks from now. They plan to use the global health conference as the launch point — announce a 'breakthrough in genetic therapy' that is actually the deployment vehicle for their stratification markers."

"If we can stop that summit from happening, or expose the plan before they trigger it, we might buy enough time to dismantle the infrastructure." Adrian's jaw tightened, the muscle twitching beneath his skin.

"We won't stop it from the shadows," Lin Wei countered. "Not this time. Every time we've operated in the dark, they've been three steps ahead. Vance was a plant. My mother's hospital records were a lure. The entire operation at the Pacific Grand was a trap they led us into."

"Then what do you suggest?" Adrian's voice carried a sharp edge, not aimed at her but at the situation.

"I suggest we stop treating this as an investigation and start treating it as what it is." She met his gaze, holding it. "A war. We need to go direct. Cut off the head before the body can move."

"You mean confront my father."

"I mean confront the Meridian. Through whatever means necessary." She stepped closer, close enough to see the exhaustion carved into his features, the shadows beneath his eyes that mirrored her own. "We have the evidence. The data. The connections. If we take this public, if we break it wide open, the Meridian can't contain it. Their power is in secrecy. Without that, they're just criminals."

"Public exposure will put your mother in the crosshairs. Every ally we have will be compromised. And Thorne—" Adrian stopped, the name catching in his throat. "He's already proven he can anticipate us."

"Then we stop being predictable."

The words hung between them, weighted with unspoken fears. Lin Wei saw the calculation behind Adrian's eyes, the same cold analysis she was running in her own mind. They had no guarantee of success. Every path forward seemed to lead to a cliff.

But staying in place meant certain annihilation.

Adrian's hand moved first, reaching past the table to grip her wrist. His touch was gentle but insistent, pulling her closer until she stood within the circle of his arms. He didn't speak. He simply held her, his chest rising and falling against hers, his breath warm against her hair.

Lin Wei allowed herself — for one suspended moment — to stop thinking. To stop calculating. To feel the desperate strength in his embrace, the silent promise that he would not let go.

"It's going to destroy us," she murmured into his shoulder. "One way or another."

"Then we make sure it destroys them first." His voice was rough, stripped of all pretense.

They stayed like that, wrapped in each other against the hum of dying servers and the weight of a world about to crack open. The scent of ozone mixed with the clean, sharp smell of Adrian's jacket. Lin Wei felt the rapid beat of his heart beneath her palm, mirroring her own.

When they finally pulled apart, it was with the gravity of soldiers before battle.

"We need to move within seventy-two hours," Lin Wei said, returning to the data with renewed focus. "Before the summit preparations solidify and before Thorne expects us to recover from the last blow."

Adrian nodded, already reaching for his encrypted phone. "I have a contact in Geneva who can get us close to the summit security system. If we can plant our payload into their internal network before the delegates arrive, we can broadcast the Meridian's own documents to every attendee simultaneously."

"And the moment that happens, Thorne will know we're there."

"Which is exactly why we do it in person." Adrian's eyes met hers, hard and resolute. "No proxies. No secondhand sabotage. This time, we stand in the room where it happens, and we burn it down ourselves."

Lin Wei felt the terror rise in her throat — not of death, but of what they would lose. Her mother's safety. The fragile trust they'd built. The possibility of a future beyond this war.

But there was no other choice.

"Then we go to Geneva," she said, her voice steady despite the trembling in her hands. "And we end this."
