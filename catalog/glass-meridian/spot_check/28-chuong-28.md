The sterile glow of Adrian’s secure study cast long shadows across the room, turning the sleek, modern furniture into jagged silhouettes. Lin Wei sat at the edge of the desk, her laptop humming faintly, fingers poised over the keys. Across from her, Adrian stood with his arms crossed, tension radiating from his broad frame. The subdued light caught the sharp angles of his jaw as he studied the decrypted files they’d unearthed. The room felt more like a war room now, each piece of data a weapon, each revelation another battlefield.

“We’re missing something,” Lin Wei muttered, her tone sharp and clipped. She tapped her pen against the desk in an erratic rhythm, a tell-tale sign of mounting frustration.

“You’re circling the same data,” Adrian said, his voice low but edged with impatience. “We have the connection to Aurelian Group, the Glass Meridian, and Project Phoenix. What else are you looking for?”

Lin Wei’s eyes snapped up, dark and piercing, meeting his steady gaze. “Don’t lecture me on digging deeper, Adrian. If your father hadn’t been so obsessed with building his ‘genetic dynasty,’ we wouldn’t be here piecing this mess together.”

His jaw tightened, the faintest flicker of aggravation crossing his face. “This isn’t about him anymore. Whatever Marcus started, the Meridian has twisted it into something far beyond his ambitions. Focus on that.”

“Believe me, I am,” she retorted, her sarcasm razor-sharp. Her fingers flew across the keyboard, and new lines of code filled the screen. A faint smell of ozone seemed to waft from the files, a phantom echo of the volatile research they contained. It was as if the data itself carried the scars of the experiments it documented.

Adrian stepped closer, his presence commanding, almost suffocating. He loomed over her shoulder, eyes narrowing as they scanned the fragmented records displayed on the screen. “C004,” he murmured, pointing to a highlighted section. “That marker sequence... It’s not random.”

Lin Wei’s breath hitched, and she leaned in, her pulse quickening. The sequence was familiar, hauntingly so. “It’s in the metadata of the patient logs,” she said, voice barely above a whisper. “These aren’t just test subjects—they were chosen. Selected for a specific genetic profile.”

Adrian’s fingers brushed against the desk as he steadied himself. “Selected for what?”

“For their potential,” Lin Wei said, her voice tinged with disbelief and horror. The implications were too vast to process all at once. She tapped another key, and the next layer of data unfolded like a grotesque map. “C005... It’s tied to Project Phoenix. They were using these profiles as a baseline, experimenting with gene editing. And not just to cure diseases.”

Adrian’s silence was heavy, his breathing measured as he absorbed the weight of her words. “They weren’t stopping at curing,” he said quietly, his tone carrying a rare vulnerability. “They were enhancing.”

Lin Wei’s throat tightened. “And it fits,” she said, almost to herself. “The Meridian’s obsession with ‘perfection.’ Project Phoenix was never about helping people—it was a stepping stone to creating what they see as the ultimate human.”

“An unparalleled bloodline,” Adrian finished darkly. His fists clenched at his sides, and his jaw ticked with a barely contained fury. “My father’s dream.”

Lin Wei’s gaze shifted to him, and she couldn’t stop herself from asking the question that had been gnawing at her. “Adrian, when you say ‘dream’... how much did you know? How much were you a part of this before you started fighting it?”

His head snapped toward her, and for a moment, she thought she had crossed a line she couldn’t uncross. But instead of the anger she expected, his expression cracked, revealing something far more conflicted. “I didn’t know the scope,” he admitted, his voice low and rough. “Not at first. Marcus always spoke in abstractions—‘legacy,’ ‘perfection,’ ‘the future.’ He was obsessed with building something that would outlast him, but he never shared the full picture.”

Lin Wei’s eyes bore into his, searching for any trace of deceit. “And when you did know?”

Adrian exhaled sharply, turning away to pace the room. The shadows seemed to follow him, a stark contrast to the sterile light. “When I did know,” he began, his voice tightening, “it was too late. By then, the Meridian had already embedded itself into Aurelian Group, into everything he built. And me? I was supposed to be the culmination of his work. Not just his heir but his proof that the system worked.”

Lin Wei froze. “What are you saying?”

He stopped pacing, turning to face her with an intensity that made her heart stumble. “I am what he wanted,” Adrian said quietly. “Not altered, not enhanced—but I’m the product of everything he believed in. The right lineage, the right upbringing. He shaped me into his successor, and I didn’t even realize it until I tried to break away.”

The air between them crackled with unspoken tension. Lin Wei wanted to respond, to say something cutting or insightful, but the weight of his confession left her momentarily speechless. Adrian Vale, always so composed, so impossibly controlled, was standing before her stripped raw. 

Finally, she found her voice. “You’re not your father,” she said, her tone softer than she intended. 

His gaze flicked to her, and there was something in his eyes—vulnerability, gratitude, and something deeper that she couldn’t name. “No,” he said. “But that doesn’t mean his shadow isn’t still following me.”

Lin Wei looked back at the screen, unwilling to dwell on the knot forming in her chest. “C006,” she said briskly, redirecting the conversation. “If C004 were the selection criteria and C005 were the editing protocols, then C006...”

Adrian leaned in again, his focus sharp. “What?”

She highlighted the corresponding sections of the metadata, her fingers trembling slightly. “It’s the activation sequence. These... These aren’t just experiments. They’re templates. They’re planning to introduce these modifications into the next generation—into children. They’re designing the ‘perfect’ human from conception.”

Adrian’s face darkened, the lines around his mouth tightening. He straightened, his broad shoulders rigid with tension. “And C007?”

Lin Wei hesitated, her stomach twisting as she decrypted the final piece of the puzzle. The lines of data revealed a new directive, chilling in its precision. “Distribution,” she whispered. “They’re not just experimenting anymore. The Meridian is moving to implementation. They’ve already identified populations—groups, events, places. This isn’t a theory anymore. It’s happening.”

Adrian’s breath hissed out, sharp and controlled. “What specific groups?”

Lin Wei shook her head. “It’s encrypted further. But it’s global. Clinics, hospitals, research facilities—all designed to look legitimate. Testing new treatments, influencing fertility programs—subtle, insidious.”

Adrian’s brow furrowed as he stared at the screen. “And Marcus...?”

She swallowed hard. “He’s the link that made this possible. Without his vision, his funding, his connections, the Meridian wouldn’t have had the foundation for this. He’s not just a part of it. He’s the architect.”

A heavy silence stretched between them, broken only by the faint hum of the laptop. Lin Wei felt the weight of the revelation settle on her chest like a physical thing, suffocating and inescapable. 

“And now,” she said, her voice trembling slightly, “we’re not just fighting a conspiracy. We’re fighting generations of ambition—his legacy. Your legacy.”

Adrian’s expression grew grim, his jaw tightening as he processed the gravity of her words. “Then we fight it,” he said, his voice steady and resolute. “Whatever it takes.”

Lin Wei’s gaze lingered on him, and for the first time, she saw not just the ruthless businessman or the enigmatic ally but the man caught between the shadow of his father and the light he was desperately trying to find. She wanted to believe him, to trust that they could dismantle something so vast and insidious. But the reality was far more complicated.

Before she could respond, a soft chime interrupted the room’s heavy silence. They both turned toward the screen as a new alert appeared—a message encoded with the same signature as the Glass Meridian’s files. Lin Wei’s heart pounded as she decrypted it.

Her breath caught. “It’s a warning.”

Adrian stepped closer, his shoulder brushing against hers as he read the message. A single line of text glared back at them: “You cannot stop what has already begun.”

Lin Wei’s blood ran cold as she glanced up at Adrian. His expression hardened, the flicker of vulnerability replaced by a steely resolve.

“They know we’re onto them,” she said.

Adrian nodded grimly. “And they’re daring us to try.”

For a moment, they stood side by side, the enormity of what lay ahead settling over them like a shroud. And then, almost imperceptibly, Adrian’s hand brushed against hers. The brief contact sent a jolt through her, as startling as it was grounding.

“We can’t back down,” he said, his voice a quiet promise. “Not now.”

Lin Wei nodded, her resolve hardening. “Not ever.”

But even as she said the words, the realization struck her like a thunderclap: They weren’t just uncovering the truth—they were becoming a part of it. And the Meridian wasn’t just watching. It was waiting.

The faint scent of ozone lingered in the air as the encrypted files flickered and reloaded, like a phantom whisper of what was to come. A legacy designed to ascend—and consume them both.

Lin Wei’s phone buzzed violently in her pocket. She ripped it out, glancing at the screen. Her face paled. Adrian’s eyes narrowed sharply. “What is it?” he demanded.

She hesitated, her voice barely above a whisper. “It’s my mother.”
