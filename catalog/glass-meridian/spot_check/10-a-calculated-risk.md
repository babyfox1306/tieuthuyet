Lin Wei's voice barely above a whisper, she slid the faded photograph across the café table. Professor Chen's hand trembled as he picked it up, his eyes locking on the symbol in the corner. The color drained from his face, leaving a sickly pallor that the dim lighting couldn't hide.

"Where did you get this?" His voice cracked.

"Hidden compartment in my mother's journal." I kept my tone measured, watching every micro-expression. "You recognized the symbol last time. I need to know what it means."

He wiped his glasses with a cloth, though the lenses were already spotless. Third time in ten minutes. The habit betrayed more than any confession.

"Professor Chen, my mother's health is deteriorating. The doctors say there's no physiological cause, but I know better. Something happened to her, and this symbol is connected."

He set down the photograph with deliberate care, as if afraid it might burn him. "Lin Wei, some doors are locked for a reason. You're a journalist—you understand the danger of chasing stories that powerful people want buried."

"I understand that silence is complicity." I leaned forward, lowering my voice further. "I found records. Fragments. My mother's name appeared in a research database that was supposedly purged. That database had a code name. What was Project Phoenix?"

The name hung between us like a live wire. Professor Chen's fingers twitched toward his glasses again, but he stopped himself, gripping the table edge instead.

"Where did you hear that name?"

"From you. Just now." I met his eyes. "The way you flinched told me everything I needed to know."

He exhaled slowly, a long defeated sound. "Project Phoenix was… an initiative. A research program that operated outside normal ethical boundaries. It was supposed to be revolutionary—advances that could change medicine, even human potential. But the methods were questionable, the oversight nonexistent."

"What kind of methods?"

"Genetic manipulation. Experimental treatments. They pushed boundaries that shouldn't have been pushed." He glanced around the café, checking for eavesdroppers. "The project was shut down after a dozen deaths were covered up as natural causes. The records were sealed, the participants scattered or silenced."

"Participants like my mother?"

He hesitated. "Your mother was a patient, not a researcher. But she wasn't just a subject—she was central to one of the key trials. That's all I know."

I filed away the lie. He knew more, but pushing now would spook him completely. "Who ran the project? What institution?"

"The funding came through multiple shell companies. The research was conducted in offshore facilities. The official paper trail was burned both literally and figuratively." He wiped his glasses again. "Lin Wei, the people behind Phoenix are still active. They've evolved, become more sophisticated. They don't tolerate loose ends."

"You're talking about the Glass Meridian, aren't you?" I watched his pupils dilate.

His hand froze mid-motion. "I don't know that name."

"You're lying."

"I'm trying to keep you alive." He leaned across the table, his voice dropping to a haunted whisper. "Your mother was part of something that leaves no survivors who talk. The fact that she's still breathing means they have a use for her. And if you keep digging, you'll become expendable too."

"Then give me something to work with. A name. A location. A single thread." I tapped the photograph. "This symbol—where did it originate? What does it mean?"

He stared at it for a long moment. "It's the Phoenix mark. The researchers who created the project used it to identify lab materials, documents, anything related to their work. I've only seen it once before, and that was twenty years ago in a sealed file I wasn't supposed to access."

"Who showed you that file?"

"A colleague who died three weeks later in a car accident that wasn't an accident." He pocketed his glasses. "I've told you too much already. Please, for the sake of your mother's health and your own safety, let this go. Some truths aren't worth the price of knowing them."

I slid the photograph back into my bag, the symbol now burned into my memory. "Thank you for your time, Professor."

As I stood, he caught my wrist. His grip was surprisingly strong for a man his age.

"You remind me of her. Your mother, before she broke." His eyes glistened. "She asked the same questions once. And look where it got her. Don't make the same mistake."

I gently freed my hand. "The difference is, I know I'm being watched. That means I can see the blow coming."

I left him sitting there, his shoulders hunched, the weight of secrets pressing him into the vinyl seat. As I pushed through the café door, the afternoon light hit me like a wall, and the name reverberated through my skull like a drumbeat.

Project Phoenix.

A research initiative built on lies and bodies. My mother, a patient—or a prisoner. And behind it all, a shadow organization that erased anyone who came too close.

I walked quickly, my heels clicking against the pavement, my mind already cataloging next steps. I'd need to cross-reference the symbol with historical research databases, find any academic papers that—

I stopped mid-stride.

Movement caught my eye—a flicker of darkness in the alley to my left. A tall silhouette, familiar in its rigid posture, standing half-hidden in the gap between buildings. Adrian Vale watched me with that unreadable stillness, his gaze fixed on my face as if assessing my next move.

I froze, my breath catching. He didn't step forward, didn't speak, didn't acknowledge me at all. But his presence said everything: my investigation had not gone unnoticed. Every move I made, every source I contacted, every whisper I chased—he was tracking it.

The weight of his observation pressed against me, equal parts threat and protection. I couldn't tell which dominated.

Project Phoenix.

The name echoed again, and I forced myself to turn away, resuming my walk. But I felt his shadow linger behind me, a silent participant in a conversation we hadn't started yet.

Somewhere behind a locked door, my mother lay in a hospital bed, her mind fractured by secrets she couldn't even remember. And ahead of me, a conspiracy waited, patient as a predator.

I had walked into the open.

Now I had to figure out whether my husband was my tail—or my only chance at survival.
