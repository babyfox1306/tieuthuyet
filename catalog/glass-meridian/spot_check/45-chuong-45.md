The sterile calm of the private ward at St. Jude’s was not a sanctuary; it was a vacuum waiting to be filled with poison. The air smelled of antiseptic and wilting lilies, a cloying mask for the rot that Lin Wei knew lay underneath the city’s skin. She stood by the window, watching the rain streak against the glass like tears on a dirty face, her tablet clutched so hard her knuckles had turned a bloodless white. Behind her, the rhythmic beeping of the heart monitor was the only tether keeping her mother in this world.

Or so she had thought.

The door hissed open. Professor Chen stepped in, his lab coat pristine, his face a mask of professional concern that Lin Wei had learned to dissect in seconds. He didn't look at her immediately. He looked at Adrian, who was standing in the corner like a marble statue of wrath, his presence filling the small room despite his silence.

"Miss Lin," Chen said, his voice tight, carrying the weight of a rehearsed speech. "We have a situation."

Lin Wei turned slowly. The tap of her pen against her thigh—a tic she couldn't suppress—stopped instantly. "Don't give me the medical jargon, Chen. Give me the truth. Is it the Glass Meridian?"

Chen flinched at the name, his eyes darting to Adrian. "The facility received a directive ten minutes ago. An emergency protocol activation. It overrides my authority as her attending physician. With the... collapse of the Aurelian Tower and the subsequent data breaches, the board has declared a state of bio-hazard emergency for all patients associated with old Level 4 research files."

"Get to the point," Adrian said. His voice was a low rumble, devoid of warmth, the kind of sound that preceded a structural collapse.

"They are transferring her," Chen said, his voice cracking. "To the St. Sebastian facility in the mountains. Effectively immediately. The ambulance is already in the bay."

The floor seemed to drop out from under Lin Wei. St. Sebastian. She knew the name. It wasn't a hospital; it was a fortress, a "black site" where inconvenient test subjects went to be forgotten, or worse, to be harvested. The unforeseen consequence of their victory over Thorne. They had decapitated the serpent, but the body was thrashing, and it was crushing the ones she loved.

"They can't," Lin Wei said, her voice sharp, cutting through the rising panic. "She’s stable. The court order stipulates she remains here under your care."

"The directive cites 'contagious variables' linked to the Phoenix Project," Chen said, wiping sweat from his brow. "It’s signed by a proxy of the Aurelian Group emergency board. Miss Lin, if I try to stop them, they will revoke my license. They’ll bury me."

"Let them bury you," Lin Wei snapped, stepping closer to the older man. "If you let them take her, she dies. You know that. You’ve seen the files."

"I’m sorry," Chen whispered, turning away. "I have no choice."

He fled the room, leaving the heavy silence in his wake.

Lin Wei spun around, facing the bed where her mother lay, pale and fragile under the white sheets. Li Mei looked so small, a woman who had once commanded a laboratory now reduced to a pawn in a game she didn't remember playing. The hum of the machines seemed to mock her.

"It’s a cleanup," Lin Wei said, her mind racing, calculating the angles, looking for the trapdoor in this locked room. "Thorne’s fall triggered a dead man’s switch. The Meridian is burning their liabilities. My mother isn't just a liability; she's evidence."

Adrian moved from the corner. He didn't speak, but his energy shifted from predatory to protective. He stopped beside her, close enough that she could feel the heat radiating from him, a stark contrast to the clinical chill of the room.

"We intercept the transport," Adrian said. It wasn't a question; it was a statement of intent.

"It’s not just a transport," Lin Wei argued, though her voice lacked conviction. She tapped her pen rhythmically against her thigh again, faster this time. "St. Sebastian is a fortress. Even if we stop the ambulance, we can't just hide her. They will hunt us. And I can't keep her on the run, Adrian. She’s weak. She needs stability, not a safe house in the middle of a war zone."

"Do you suggest we let them take her?" Adrian asked. He turned to face her, his gaze intense, lingering a beat too long, searching for a crack in her armor.

"No," she whispered. She looked up at him, seeing the soot still smudged on his cheekbone from the explosion at the tunnel, a physical reminder of the chaos they had survived. "I suggest we burn the bridge before they cross it."

She pulled up her tablet, the screen glowing harshly in the dim room. She opened the decrypted file C013, the data they had bled for. As the code scrolled, she caught it again—that faint, metallic scent of ozone, a phantom smell of electricity and danger that seemed to emanate from the decrypted files themselves. It was a sensory glitch, a psychosomatic reaction to the volatile research contained within the binary.

"What are you doing?" Adrian asked, leaning in to look at the screen. His shoulder brushed against hers, a solid, anchoring contact.

"My mother wasn't just a researcher," Lin Wei said, her eyes scanning the rows of data. "Thorne called her the key. I need to find out why she’s the key. If I know what she holds, I can negotiate."

"Negotiate?" Adrian’s jaw clenched, a subtle movement that signaled his disagreement. "You don't negotiate with people who view humans as inventory."

"It’s not negotiation if I have a gun to their head," Lin Wei countered. She stopped scrolling. "Here. Project Chimera. Sub-file 9. It’s a list of subjects who underwent... gene editing. Not for perfection, but for compatibility. The 'Ascension' program wasn't about making them better. It was about making them receptive to something else."

She looked at her mother. "Li Mei is Subject Zero. She’s the control group. If they transfer her, they won't just kill her. They’ll wipe her. Again. But deeper this time. Total neural erasure to prevent the data in her subconscious from ever being accessed."

The realization hit her like a physical blow. Her mother wasn't just sick. She was a living, breathing hard drive of forbidden history, and the Glass Meridian was coming to format her.

Lin Wei felt a tremor run through her hand. She clenched her pen until it hurt. The panic was a cold tide rising in her chest, threatening to drown her logic. She had to be sharp. She had to be the journalist, the observer, not the terrified daughter.

A hand covered hers. Large, warm, steady.

She looked up. Adrian was watching her, his eyes dark pools of understanding. He didn't offer empty platitudes. He didn't tell her it would be okay. He simply offered his presence, a grounding force in the midst of her unraveling.

"We stop the transfer," he said softly. "Whatever the cost. We protect her."
