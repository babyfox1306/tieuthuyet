The air crackled with tension as Lin Wei and Adrian executed their daring plan, the carefully laid traps springing, revealing the chilling extent of Marcus Thorne's manipulations. Inside the darkened interior of the van, the only light came from the glow of Lin Wei’s tablet, where the live feed of the Pacific Grand Hotel played out like a silent disaster movie. The chaos below was palpable even without sound—executives scrambling, security closing ranks, and the singular, imposing figure of Marcus Thorne standing motionless in the eye of the storm.

"It’s done," Lin Wei whispered, her finger hovering over the 'disconnect' key. "The files are broadcasting. Every major news outlet has a mirror link. We’ve stripped him naked in public."

Adrian didn't take his eyes off the road. He drove with a terrifying precision, weaving the stolen vehicle through the dense morning traffic of Macau, his hands gripping the steering wheel so tight the leather creaked.

"Exposed, perhaps," Adrian said, his voice a low rumble that vibrated through the seat. "But do not mistake exposure for defeat. A man like Thorne does not bleed because you cut him. He bleeds so he can track the blood."

The van hit a pothole, jolting Lin Wei. She quickly secured the tablet, her mind racing through the permutations of the data they had just unleashed. They had released the financial logs, the authorization codes for the Mediterranean corridor, and the personnel orders for Project Phoenix. It was a nuclear option.

"He can't spin this," she argued, though the skepticism in her own voice was sharp. "The falsified data is irrefutable. We cross-referenced it with the audit trails. He’s been siphoning millions—no, billions—into the Glass Meridian."

"He can spin anything," Adrian countered, glancing at the rearview mirror. "And he will. He will call it a fabrication, a rival corporate espionage attack, or a deepfake generated by enemies of the state. The truth is malleable, Lin Wei. Power is the only constant."

Lin Wei looked down at her phone. It was vibrating incessantly—a constant stream of notifications. Alerts from her editor, frantic texts from anonymous numbers, and then, a chilling update from a monitoring bot she had set up on the hospital’s internal server.

Her mother.

Her heart seized. "Adrian. Pull over."

" Not yet. We are still within the perimeter of their likely pursuit grid."

"Pull over!" Her voice cracked, sharp and commanding.

Adrian shot her a look, his jaw clenching—that subtle tic she had learned meant he was holding back a storm—but he swerved the van sharply to the right, cutting across two lanes of traffic to screech into an alleyway behind a closed fish market. The van lurched to a halt, the silence of the engine instantly replaced by the distant hum of the city and the sound of her own ragged breathing.

Lin Wei unlocked her phone, her fingers trembling slightly. "The monitoring bot. It’s flagging abnormal activity in my mother’s ward. Not just a check-up. A security override."

Adrian leaned over, his presence suddenly overwhelming, filling the small space between them. "Show me."

She pulled up the log. It was a single line of code, buried deep within the hospital’s infrastructure. Command Initiated: Transfer to Isolation Wing Alpha. Authorization: M. Thorne.

"He’s not just running," Lin Wei said, the icy realization washing over her. "He’s counter-attacking. The broadcast triggered a failsafe."

"Your mother," Adrian said, shifting instantly from driver to protector. "He’s using her as leverage."

"No," Lin Wei stared at the screen, her mind dissecting the code with clinical coldness to keep the panic at bay. "It’s not leverage. Look at the timestamp. The order came through thirty seconds before the broadcast started. He anticipated this. He knew we had the data, and he predicted the move."

She looked up at Adrian, her eyes wide. "He didn't try to stop the broadcast because he didn't care. He wanted us to release it."

"Why?"

Think, Lin Wei. Think.

The ozone scent. It was phantom, a sensory memory of the decrypted files, but it flooded her nose now, sharp and electric. It was the smell of burnt circuits, of a system purging itself.

"The files," she breathed. "The files we released... they contained the locations of the twelve scientists for Phase Three. We thought we were exposing their illegal relocation. We thought we were forcing the world to watch them."
