The basement corridor smelled of rust and old tape. The speaker above them whispered "Stage two" in twelve languages, and Sofia Bellini understood the final rule: to leave, they had to prove they had stopped speaking for each other.

A strip of white light ignited along the floor.

It ran beneath Sofia's shoes, past Yuki, Pierre, Dmitri, and Elias, then stopped at a steel exit door at the end of the corridor. No handle. No keycard reader. Only a transparent glass plate mounted at throat height.

Every voice must be its own. —Department of Linguistic Correction.

Behind the glass lay one of the summit earpieces, shattered neatly through the center.

"That's theatrical," Dmitri said.

The speaker crackled.

CORRECTION EVENT: ONE.

Dmitri's face hardened. "I spoke for no one."

"No," Pierre began. "It means—"

Sofia caught his wrist before he could finish.

Pierre looked down at her hand. Then at the speaker.

He understood.

Sofia released him, her fingers cold. The system did not care whether they meant to help. It had never cared about kindness, accuracy, or good intentions. An interruption could wear the polite face of assistance and still erase the person beneath it.

A red number appeared inside the glass.

1.

Dmitri pressed his lips into a flat line. For once, he did not argue.

The lights behind them shut off one by one.

They moved toward the door.

Sofia kept close to Elias without touching him. His fingers tapped against his thigh in a measured rhythm, three beats, pause, two beats. She knew that rhythm. It meant he was building a sentence brick by brick, refusing to borrow one from anyone else.

Every instinct in her wanted to ask what he needed.

Worse, she wanted to guess.

She bit her lower lip hard enough to taste blood.

A panel beside the door lit up.

SPEAKER ONE: STATE YOUR NAME.

Yuki stepped onto the illuminated square.

"My name is Yuki Tanaka."

The panel flashed green.

VOICE ACCEPTED.

A second line appeared.

SPEAKER TWO: STATE THE PURPOSE OF STAGE TWO.

Pierre approached. "The purpose is to determine whether we can leave without interpreting one another."

Yellow light pulsed across the glass.

REINTERPRETATION DETECTED.

Pierre froze.

Sofia saw the mistake immediately. The prompt had not asked what the system was determining. It had asked for the purpose. Pierre had reframed it, adding motive to a machine that had offered none.

He opened his mouth.

Dmitri lifted one warning finger.

Pierre shut it again.

The yellow light faded, but the red number changed.

2.

A new prompt appeared.

SPEAKER TWO: TRY AGAIN.

Pierre stared at the words for several seconds. His usual confidence had vanished. Without his questions and conclusions, without the sharp little summaries he used to pull facts into shape, he looked younger than eighteen.

"Stage Two requires each person to speak only for themselves."

Green.

VOICE ACCEPTED.

Pierre stepped away, exhaling through his nose.

The floor shifted under Sofia's feet.

Metal partitions rose between them with a violent clang. Sofia found herself inside a narrow booth open only toward the door. Yuki stood in another. Pierre and Dmitri disappeared behind steel dividers.

Elias remained visible through a rectangular pane to Sofia's right.

His eyes met hers.

The speaker whispered, "Assistance invalidates testimony."

Of course it did. The building had made isolation into a test and called it fairness. Somewhere, some committee had probably congratulated itself on the wording.

Letters crawled across Sofia's panel.

SPEAKER THREE: WHAT DID NADIA PETRESCU SAY?

Her locket slipped between her fingers. The metal edge had warmed against her skin, a tiny familiar anchor in a corridor designed to strip familiarity away.

The recording replayed in her mind: the official's careful voice, the interruptions, the substitutions. Nadia trying to force her own meaning through layers of clarification until the official transcript no longer belonged to her.

Sofia touched her throat.

"I don't know all the words Nadia Petrescu said."

Green light.

VOICE ACCEPTED.

Her knees nearly weakened.

Not because the answer was correct. Because it was honest. She could not repair Nadia by inventing a cleaner version of what remained.

The partition in front of Yuki sank into the floor.

Her next prompt glowed.

SPEAKER FOUR: COMPLETE THE STATEMENT. NADIA PETRESCU INTENDED TO—

Yuki stared at it.

The silence stretched.

Sofia could almost feel the machine waiting for someone to rescue her. The old Sofia would have done it before Yuki drew her next breath. She would have called it translation. Support. Mercy.

Yuki lifted her chin.

"Only Nadia Petrescu could complete that statement."

The glass plate turned green.

VOICE ACCEPTED.

A shudder traveled through the corridor walls. Dust fell from the ceiling in thin streams.

Dmitri's partition lowered next.

His prompt appeared.

SPEAKER FIVE: THE SYSTEM KILLS WHOEVER'S WORDS ARE MISTRANSLATED THE MOST. CONFIRM.

He read it twice.

Months ago—or days; the academy had ruined time along with language—Dmitri would have confirmed it with the confidence of a man signing his own theorem. His nonsense experiment had been built around that surface rule. He had tried to outsmart the building by giving it sounds without meaning.

Now he looked at Sofia.

She gave him nothing.

His jaw worked.

"No."

A low alarm sounded.

SPEAKER FIVE: PROVIDE CORRECTION.

Dmitri's eyes sharpened. "No. I will not replace that speaker's claim with mine. My answer is that the statement is false."

For three terrible seconds, nothing happened.

Then the panel flashed green.

VOICE ACCEPTED.

The red count remained at two.

Dmitri let out a rough laugh, but he did not turn it into an explanation. Progress came in strange forms.

Only Elias's partition remained.

Sofia could see him through the pane, shoulders rigid, lips pressed together. His prompt lit up, but the angle kept the words hidden from her.

His tapping stopped.

Seconds passed.

The speaker emitted a soft tone.

SPEAKER SIX: RESPONSE REQUIRED.

Elias drew a breath.

Sofia's body leaned toward him before her mind caught up. She stopped herself with one palm against the cold partition.

He did not need her urgency.

He did not need her fluent version.

He needed time.

The tone repeated.

Elias's gaze flicked to Sofia. Unlike every other person in the academy, he never looked away from her when the words became difficult. That trust hurt more than blame would have.

His lips parted.

"My..." He swallowed. "My words are n-not delayed because they are empty."

The speaker clicked.

Sofia held her breath.

Elias's fingers curled at his sides, but he continued before the system could prompt him again.

"They are mine while... while they take time. They stay mine after I say them."

Green light flooded his booth.

VOICE ACCEPTED.

The partition dropped.

Sofia did not move toward him. Neither did anyone else. The restraint felt almost ceremonial.

Elias stepped off his square on his own.

The final panel activated.

DESIGNATED INTERPRETER: APPROACH.

Sofia's stomach tightened.

She walked to the glass plate. Her reflection floated over the broken earpiece: dark hair coming loose, dried blood at her lower lip, locket chain crooked across her collarbone. She looked like someone who had finally learned the difference between carrying a voice and taking it.

The glass warmed beneath her palm.

STATE THE BINDING CONDITION.

Behind her, nobody spoke.

Sofia drew one careful breath.

"The system kills whoever has been spoken for the most — trained on Nadia Petrescu's rewritten testimony."

The corridor went black.

Somewhere in the walls, relays snapped in rapid succession. The floor trembled beneath Sofia's shoes, and the speaker released a burst of overlapping voices: officials, translators, students, words folding over words until meaning became pressure against her skull.

Then the glass plate split down the middle.

Sofia jerked her hand back.

The broken earpiece behind it sparked once. Its tiny speaker spat out fragments.

Clarify.

For the record.

What she means is—

The exit door remained closed.

"No," Sofia whispered.

The panel burned red.

UNAUTHORIZED REPRESENTATION DETECTED.

COUNT: 2.

The two interventions in the corridor. Dmitri's summary of the stage. Pierre almost explaining Dmitri. Small mistakes, harmless by ordinary standards.

Here, ordinary standards were body bags.

A new instruction appeared.

CONSENSUS REQUIRED.

Five empty lines glowed beneath it.

Sofia frowned. They could not speak one answer together. Even agreement could become erasure if one person supplied the words and the rest merely followed.

Yuki approached first.

She placed her palm against the glass beside Sofia's, then withdrew it. Her line turned green.

Pierre understood next. He pressed two fingers to the plate, leaving a smudge of archive dust. Another line changed.

Dmitri snorted softly, as if offended the system had found a use for silence. He touched the glass.

The fourth line turned green.

Elias came last.

He stopped beside Sofia. Close enough that she could feel the heat of him, not close enough to trap her in it. His hand rose toward the plate, hesitated, then shifted.

He looked at her.

Not for permission to speak. Not for rescue.

For a choice.

Sofia placed her palm against the glass.

Elias laid his hand beside hers, their little fingers almost touching but not crossing the narrow line between them.

The fifth bar turned green.

CONSENSUS RECORDED WITHOUT SUBSTITUTION.

Locks released inside the door with a sequence of heavy thuds.

The speaker above them woke for the dawn replay.

Every word spoken the previous day began to pour through the corridor, each sentence translated and twisted through language after language. Sofia heard her own warnings transformed into accusations. Pierre's questions became confessions. Dmitri's objections became commands.

Then Elias's voice emerged.

"My words are delayed because they are empty," the system translated.

Sofia flinched.

Elias did not.

He looked directly at the speaker and let the lie pass without letting it define him.

Tape hiss filled the corridor.

A woman's recorded voice surfaced beneath the mechanical chorus, faint but steady. No body appeared. No shadow crossed the wall. Only the scrape of a chair, the click of an old recorder, and a breath held too long.

An official voice said, "For clarity, the witness agrees—"

The tape stuttered.

Then Nadia Petrescu's voice cut through the building's replay.

"That is not what I said."

Every light in the corridor turned white.

The exit door opened.

Cold air rushed in, clean enough to make Sofia dizzy. Yuki went first, then Pierre. Dmitri paused at the threshold as though expecting one last argument from the building, but he crossed without offering it one.

Sofia took a step and swayed.

Elias reached for her, then stopped before touching her arm. His question stayed in his eyes.

She nodded once.

He steadied her by the elbow until her balance returned, then released her. No assumption. No ownership disguised as care.

Outside, the mist thinned over the parking lot. The others moved ahead, faces pale in the new light.

Sofia stood at the threshold, suddenly aware that she was shivering.

Elias removed his jacket.

Without a word, he draped it over Sofia's shoulders and pressed a kiss to the top of her head—a silent, final declaration of his own voice.

Her fingers closed around the lapels. She did not translate the moment into a promise. She did not name what he felt or decide what he meant.

She only looked up at him.

Elias held her gaze, and this time, neither of them needed more.

The sunrise burned through the mist as they stumbled out. Behind them, the building hummed. Ahead, a single car idled in the parking lot, engine running, door open — as if expecting them. On the dashboard lay a folder stamped with the same logo as the earpieces, labeled "New Summit — Transcripts".
