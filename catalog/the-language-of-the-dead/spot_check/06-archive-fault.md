"I found something—they say it shouldn’t exist," Elias van Doren whispers as he passes the document to Sofia Bellini in the archive alcove.

His fingers—long, unsteady—brush hers as the folder changes hands. Sofia feels his pulse through the paper, a tremor that thrums with the burden of too much knowing and too little power to act on it. The archive hums faintly, soft electrical ticks in the silence, so even their suppressed breath echoes.

Sofia doesn’t look at the document yet. She looks at him. The strain in Elias’s face, the fixed set of his jaw as if he’s holding back the urge to blurt, everything all at once. She manages a soft smile—just for him, a private signal of calm. "Who told you it shouldn’t exist?"

He glances at the stacks, then at the glass-paned door beyond, where Pierre Leclerc’s silhouette flickers past—restless, wolfish. "Tomasz said—he said the index stops at C003. There isn’t a C004 in the system. But this—" He swallows, voice thinning. "It was in her case folder. Just now."

Sofia weighs every word. She lets her fingertips rest on the top sheet, feeling for irregularities—a fold, a misaligned staple, the subtle burr of a different grade of Tribunal stock. "And you showed no one else?"

Elias shakes his head. "No one. I—I almost left it, but something was off. Look at the corner."

She lifts the page, finding a faint thumbprint preserved in the dust—distinct, almost waxy. Not hers. Not Elias’s, either, unless he’s learned to reverse his dominant hand in secret.

Her pulse slows. Not fear. Never fear. Calculus. "This was placed, not filed," she murmurs. She lets the page slip under her palm. "You did well to bring it to me."

Elias’s eyes flinch toward the windows, searching for Pierre. The journalist’s presence lingers, just out of sight, his footsteps urgent and muffled. For once, Sofia is grateful for the old radiators—if Pierre tries to listen at the vent, he’ll only get the metallic clatter and hiss.

"Should I tell Tomasz?" Elias’s voice is barely more than exhaled air.

Sofia watches him flinch at his own question. His sense of procedural correctness, that brittle, hopeful faith in rules that work for the innocent. It’s almost sweet. "Not yet," she replies, smoothing her thumb across the margin of the sheet. "If it was meant for you, it will resurface officially. If not—"

"Then it’s for us only now," Elias finishes, the word ‘us’ frail but tethering.

She lets the silence settle. There is power in the hush—the acknowledgment of collusion. She opens the file.

Typed lines, sharp enough to wound. No header. A single phrase, centered:

Correction request denied. Subject review irreversible.

A secondary line, smaller:

Witness Bellini under protocol B-6.

She fights the urge to laugh. How crude. How perfectly obvious, if anyone were listening for the wrong thing. And yet—it shouldn’t exist, no C004, and the protocol number doesn’t match any in the standard governance. A gentle trap, perhaps. Or a warning.

Elias bites his lip. "What does it mean? Whose fingerprint—?"

Sofia’s mind works in fractal spirals. What she says now will be the memory that gets recorded, retold, devoured by the system and multiplied by rumor. She leans closer, lowering her voice to a hush just above electronic hum.

"It means someone is watching the review queue. That the old order isn’t as closed as Rasmus believes." She lets her words drift, imprecise, inviting. "If there’s no C004 in the official file, then this is—unofficial caution. Nothing more."

Elias isn’t reassured. He stares at the page, knuckles whitening around the edge of the folder. "Then it could be for you. Or about you."

He is trying to warn her, in his way. Sofia almost wants to reach for his fear, to cradle it. Instead, she lets her hand settle over his, a gentle weight, skin warm on his trembling fingers. "If it were meant to frighten me, it would be less polite."

He tries to laugh but fails. The sound falters, and she can feel him suppress another question, something raw and childlike: Are you safe? Am I?

She does not answer what he cannot say aloud. Instead she traces, just once, the curve of his thumb with hers—a promise of shelter, or at least of collusion. The gesture is precise, practiced: comfort, with an undertow of command. She will keep him safe. She decides what safety means.

He glances at the door. "Pierre suspects. He—he’s been in here twice. Pretending to look for the 2047 briefings."

Sofia tucks the document back into the folder, folding it so that only the top half is visible. "He suspects everyone. But he doesn’t understand. He looks for codes and omissions—never gestures, never what isn’t said."

Elias’s lips part, then close. He wants reassurance, more than she can give. She offers him a bracing, almost maternal smile. "If he comes, let me speak. You are only helping with the indices."

A shadow passes the glass again; Pierre, or perhaps just a clerk. The archives are never truly empty. Sofia slides the folder into her bag, careful to shield the thumbprint from smearing. She will keep that one detail intact. Signatures are always useful, eventually.

Elias’s voice is very small. "If something happens—if they change my statement again—"

She turns, letting her gaze settle on him—steady, warm, insistent. "Then you come to me immediately. And you do not apologize for remembering differently."

He nods. He almost smiles. The weight isn’t gone, but she’s shifted some of it to her own shoulders. He will think she is strong enough for both.

The archive’s door creaks. Pierre’s silhouette sharpens, framed by yellowed glass. He hesitates, clearly aware he is interrupting something private.

Sofia is all poise. "Pierre. Looking for us?"

The journalist’s brows arch, eyes bright with the thrill of almost catching someone. "So it seems. The system has hiccupped again. Magda’s files—half the attachments vanished from the inventory. Tomasz is blaming humidity."

Sofia’s smile is smooth as cream. "Our records are more resilient than the ventilation, Pierre. How is your index search?"

He doesn’t answer at first, gaze flicking between Sofia and Elias, finally landing on the folder in Sofia’s hands. He can’t see the document, only the confident way she holds it.

"Curious," Pierre says, as if to himself. "The system rarely loses whole protocols. Unless someone wants them lost."

Elias’s hands twitch, but Sofia’s do not. She steps toward Pierre, radiating gentle authority. "Would you like to review it with us? Or will you trust the Tribunal’s records?"

Pierre considers this, weighing challenge against the risk of being seen as insubordinate. "I’d like to believe the system," he says, but his tone is tart. "But lately, I believe too much, and it keeps turning on me."

Sofia’s laugh is the one she reserves for difficult children—amused, indulgent, always one measure above the panic line. "We all need faith, Pierre. Especially when the facts are slow to arrive."

He narrows his eyes, conceding only by retreat. "I’ll be in the chamber. If you find anything—strange, Sofia, I know you’ll clarify."

"I always do," she says, and lets the words cut both ways.

When he’s gone, Elias lets the breath out he’d been holding.

They walk together down the archive corridor—Sofia’s stride measured, Elias’s a half-step behind, hands jammed in his pockets. She can hear the tribunal’s voices rising through the ventilation—Kappel’s anxious stammer, Rasmus’s brittle patience, Magda’s nervous insistence.

A door ahead, half-closed. Sofia pauses before entering, glancing at Elias. "Wait here. If you’re called, answer only what you remember. Nothing more."

He nods, eyes on the ground. "Will you—"

"I will." She leaves him the comfort of certainty.

Sofia steps through the main doors into the tribunal chamber. The atmosphere is different now—words fly sharper, as if the air is tuned to the frequency of accusation. Rasmus stands tall, but his hands betray him, thumbs worrying at the seal of his folder. Magda’s eyes, wide and wild, flick to Sofia and then away. Kappel is pale, sweating, clutching a stack of printouts.

"Miss Bellini," Rasmus intones, voice echoing too loud. "We will resume. There are procedural matters to address."

She seats herself, folding her hands atop the table, the folder nestled at her elbow. The old marble beneath her wrists is colder than before, as if the room itself has been washed clean.

Magda leans forward, voice trembling but clear. "We must address the deaths. There have been six since the last correction cycle. All—" Her throat tightens. "All left in the system as ‘clarified’."

Rasmus raises a hand for silence. "Miss Bellini has assured us there is an explanation. We trust her to guide us through the confusion."

She nods, gentle, unhurried. "The cascade of errors follows a pattern. Panic and rumor fuel instability. What we need is calm, and a return to established protocol. I will clarify the discrepancies with Tomasz’s assistance."

Pierre enters behind her, taking his seat, eyes never leaving Sofia’s hands.

Rasmus leans in. "The Tribunal is united in its reliance on your good faith, Miss Bellini. The system is under stress, but we must proceed."

She offers the room a serene smile. "Of course. I am here to help."

As the officials murmur and shuffle, Kappel stands too quickly, nearly knocking his chair into the wall. "But what if it’s not a rendering error? Sir—what if those names really are gone?"

Rasmus’s face tightens. "We do not indulge in speculation. Miss Bellini will review the matter."

Magda’s hand closes on Sofia’s sleeve, the grip desperate, searching. "You’re certain, child? This will not touch you?"

Sofia covers Magda’s hand, reassuring, soft. "No harm will come to anyone today."

She can feel the room’s relief—fragile and fleeting—at her composure. They want to believe her, so badly. They always do.

She opens the folder just a crack, letting her gaze fall on the page inside. Correction request denied. Subject review irreversible.

A chill slips along her spine, as if the document itself is breathing.

That’s when the lights flicker—just long enough for the marble to shimmer and the air to taste faintly of ozone. The system’s interface blinks, and then, sharp as a bell’s toll, a red line scrolls across the main display:

Subject: Bellini, Sofia. Status: FLAGGED FOR REVIEW.

All eyes swing to her—as the chamber’s hush cracks and the real trial finally begins.
