The key card opened a door she'd never entered, but the hallway behind it smelled like a place she had dreamed. Concrete dust and copper wire. Sloane Mercer pressed her palm flat against the wall and let the sensation settle—a ghost of recognition that died before it could become a memory.

"How did you know this was here?" Lucian's voice was low, close. He didn't touch her, but she felt the weight of his attention.

"I didn't. My hands did."

The key card was heavier than it looked. Standard magnetic stripe, but the number etched into the corner—1423—had been altered. The '1' was freshly painted, a shade lighter than the surrounding digits. Someone had tampered with the label recently. Her past self. Or Rook's people. She didn't need to guess. Her fingers had brought her here. That was enough.

The door at the end of the hall was unmarked. No logo. No signage. Just a steel frame and a magnetic reader. She slid the card into the slot. The lock clicked. The light blinked green. An invitation or a trap. She pushed the door open.

The room was small. A metal desk. A single shelf. A terminal bolted to the floor. No windows. The air was stale, recycled, heavy with the silence of a space that had been waiting a long time for someone to break it.

She stepped inside. Lucian followed, his rifle resting against his thigh, his eyes scanning the corners, the ceiling, the shadows.

"What is this place?" she asked.

"Your dead drop."

"I don't remember building a dead drop."

"You didn't build it. You bought it. Three years ago. Cash."

She turned to look at him. His face was unreadable, a mask carved from stone and patience. "You know everything about me, don't you?"

"I know what you let me read."

"And what did I let you read?"

He met her gaze. The air thickened. "That you trusted me enough to hide a weapon here. But not enough to tell me what it was."

She didn't know what to do with that answer. She turned back to the terminal.

The screen was dark. She pressed the power button. It flickered to life. A single prompt.

ENTER SEQUENCE

Her left hand lifted before her brain gave the command. Her index finger began to trace a circle against the inside of her wrist. The same rhythm. The same pattern she had drilled into her own muscle memory. Mara's rhythm through the scope. The ledger lines. The firing sequence. It was all connected, a thread running through the dark of her missing three months.

She typed the sequence in.

The interface unlocked.

A fragment of the ledger.

Data scrolled across the screen. Not names. Not sums. A cadence. A pattern of ones and zeros that resolved into seven distinct lines. Seven targets. The same seven files Rook had corrupted. She watched the numbers line up, the rhythm of the data matching the pulse against her fingertips.

"He didn't just bury the targets," Sloane said, her voice flat, clinical. "He buried the architecture of the whole operation."

"And you just dug it up."

She read the lines. The spiral in her chest tightened. The hand signal from her sister. The unconscious tic she used when she was scared. It was the same sequence that ran through this entire system. The mirror key was embedded in the code itself.

"I built this," she whispered. "I built the whole machine."

"Yes."

"And Rook used it to kill seven men while making it look like someone else's error."

"Yes."

She stared at the screen. The guilt was a cold weight in her chest, settled deep, unmovable. She had ordered the deaths. Not directly—but she had built the system. Rook had just loaded the ammunition and pointed the weapon. The trigger was hers. The contract on her own life was proof.

Line by line, the data resolved into a map of Rook's private network. The seven men he had eliminated. The seven files he had corrupted. And the signature at the bottom—a spike in the data that matched the exact rhythm of the tic on her left arm. She had not just built the machine. She had authenticated every kill.

"There's a broadcast function on this terminal," she said.

Lucian moved to stand beside her. The proximity was a pressure against her side. "If you send that, you show Rook your hand."

"If I don't, he wins."

"He already knows where you are."

"But he doesn't know what I know."

She reached for the keyboard. Her fingers hesitated over the keys. The ceiling above her groaned.

A shower of dust and debris.

Lucian moved before she could process the sound. His arms locked around her, dragging her from the chair. The metal grate above them tore loose and crashed down exactly where she had been sitting.

Sloane hit the floor hard. Lucian was on top of her, his body curved over hers, taking the full impact of the falling metal. She felt the jarring shudder along his spine, the strain in his arms as he held the weight off her skull.

Her face was pressed against his chest. She could hear his heartbeat. Hard. Quick. Alive.

Unwanted familiarity.

She knew that rhythm. She had heard it before, in a bed she couldn't remember, in a life that had been erased. For one breath, she let herself feel the shape of him. The hard line of his jaw against her temple. The way his hand pressed flat against the small of her back—not possessive, protective. Her body remembered him. It leaned into his warmth without her permission.

"Are you hit?" she asked.

"No."

"Then get off me."

He rolled away immediately. No hesitation. Just a soldier following orders. She stood. Her hands were shaking. She pressed them flat against the desk to steady them.

"The grate was rigged," Lucian said.

"Or weakened by the shooting."

"It's a trap."

"Everything is a trap, Lucian. The question is which one we spring."

She sat down at the terminal. The broadcast channel was still open. The upload prompt glowed green.

This was the trap she had set for herself. And she was walking right into it.

Her fingers moved over the keyboard. She didn't have to think about the sequence. Her hands knew it. The firing sequence. The mirror key. The rhythm. Seven lines of data. Seven confirmed kills. The truth Rook had tried to bury.

The data uploaded.

Sloane watched the progress bar fill. Green. Seventy percent. Eighty. Each percentage point was a door closing behind her. There was no taking it back. No rewriting the past. Only living with the future she had engineered.

TRANSMISSION COMPLETE

She stared at the screen. The clock on the wall read 04:37.

A fragment of the same hand rhythm, now decoded into numbers that matched the first seven lines of the transaction ledger. The rhythm Mara had signaled through the scope. The rhythm her own fingers traced when the fear got too loud.

Rook would see it in minutes.

"He'll know I'm in the system," Sloane said.

"He already knew you were looking."

"Now he knows I'm awake."

She pulled the key card from the reader. The dead drop was compromised. They had minutes before Rook's people locked down this entire district. The window for escape was shrinking.

"What now?" Lucian asked.

She met his eyes. The air between them was thick with everything they weren't saying. The binding condition. The sunrise. The three months she couldn't remember. The unwanted familiarity of his heartbeat against her spine.

"Now we find my sister," she said. "Before Rook does."

He didn't argue. He just nodded once, then moved toward the door.

Sloane followed, the ghost of his heartbeat still thrumming against her skin, the rhythm of the ledger fresh in her mind. The game had just entered its final move. And the clock was ticking.
