The contract said her name, but the body at her feet was her sister's.

Sloane Mercer's brain was a machine with a fried circuit board. She knew her name. She knew her job. She knew the woman lying in the gravel had been alive when the sun last set.

She did not know how she got here.

She did not know who pulled the trigger.

And she did not know why the paper crumpled in her fist ordered her own death.

The rooftop sprawled around her, a dark plateau of concrete and rusted vents. The city hummed below, a distant animal sound. The air tasted like cordite and copper. She was on her knees. Her palms were scraped raw. Her head ached with the heavy, chemical fog of a lost fight.

Twelve feet away, Mara lay on her back.

Sloane cataloged the scene the way she had been trained to. Entry wound, center mass. No exit wound visible. Hollow point. Close range. The blood had pooled beneath her sister's shoulders, a dark halo spreading into the gravel.

Mara's eyes were open.

Sloane did not move toward her. If she touched her, it became real. If she stayed on her knees, held the paper, kept breathing, maybe the world would rewind.

She looked down at her hands.

The paper was standard printer stock, folded into thirds. The handwriting was hers. She knew that loop on the S, that sharp slash on the M. Her signature was a mountain range of guilt.

She read the words again.

CLIENT: SLOANE MERCER  
TARGET: SLOANE MERCER  
ORDER: CLIENT TERMINATION AT SUNRISE  
DATE: TODAY  
SIGNATURE: SLOANE MERCER

No memory of writing it.

No memory of the past three months at all.

The last thing she remembered was an afternoon in her office at the federal building. A file open on her desk. Black Mountain Group. A defense contractor with more ghosts than employees. She had been building a case. Someone had told her to drop it.

She hadn't dropped it.

And then the screen went black.

Three months of nothing. A gap in her life wide enough to drive a tragedy through.

Had she been drugged? Brainwashed? Had she gone insane? The questions circled like sharks in the dark water of her mind.

She pressed her thumb hard against the inside of her wrist. A nervous motion. A circle traced against the skin. She didn't know why she did it. It was just a tic, a pressure valve for a system about to blow.

Her sister was dead.

She had ordered her own execution.

And she had no idea who she had become.

The contract was dated today. Her signature was fresh. The ink hadn't bled into the paper yet. She had written this hours ago, maybe, and she couldn't remember a single pen stroke.

What did I do in those three months?

What did I become?

The door to the roof access swung open without a sound.

Sloane's head snapped up.

A man stepped out of the darkness. He didn't walk like a civilian. He moved like a blade cutting through water. Silent. Efficient. Dangerous in a way that didn't need to perform itself.

He was tall. Dark clothes. A tactical rifle slung across his back, the strap pulled tight. A sidearm on his hip. His face was all hard lines and shadows, cut from something merciless.

He stopped ten feet away and looked at the scene.

Her. The body. The contract.

He already knew what he was seeing.

"Miss Mercer."

His voice was low. Calm. The kind of calm that came from a hundred bad nights, not from peace.

"Who are you?" she asked.

"You know who I am."

"I don't remember anything."

He looked at the paper in her fist. "You found the contract."

"I was holding it when I woke up."

"Yes, you were."

"Did I write this?"

"Yes."

"Why would I hire someone to kill me?"

"You didn't explain the job. You gave the order and the payment. The terms are clear."

"That's insane." Her voice cracked. "I'm a federal investigator. I don't hire contract killers."

"You did."

"If I hired you, then finish it. Get it over with."

"The terms specify sunrise. I don't break terms."

The cold professionalism of the statement hit her like ice water. "That makes you a monster."

"It makes me a professional. The same way you were, once."

The words landed in her chest like a bullet. "Once? What is that supposed to mean?"

He didn't answer.

She stared at him, searching for a crack. Something to explain the way her stomach twisted when he spoke. Something to justify the strange familiarity that sparked in the dark of her missing mind.

Then he said her name.

"Sloane."

Not Miss Mercer. Her first name. The way he said it was different. Softer. A whisper that belonged in a dark room between two people who knew each other too well.

Her heart stumbled.

"How do you know me?" she asked. "Not just the contract. You know me."

A muscle jumped in his jaw.

"You say my name like you have the right to."

"We don't have time for this."

"Answer the question."

"No."

"Did I love you?"

The words hung between them, raw and desperate.

His composure flickered. His eyes moved over her face like he was looking at a ghost.

"I think you know the answer to that," he said.

"I don't know anything! I don't know who you are. I don't know who I am now. I don't know why I signed this." She shook the paper. "And I don't know why she's dead."

"You signed it because you were in trouble."

"What kind of trouble?"

"The kind you can't outrun."

"Then why call you? Why not the police?"

"Maybe you didn't trust the police."

"I am the police."

"Yes. You were."

The word hit her again. Were.

Before she could push further, a light swept across the low wall of the rooftop. Not a star. Not a reflection.

Headlights. Below.

She crawled to the edge and looked over.

A black sedan was parked at the service entrance. No sirens. No lights. Just a single car idling in the dark. Two men inside. They didn't get out.

They were waiting.

"Who are they?"

Lucian crossed the distance between them in three strides. He grabbed her arm and pulled her to her feet. His grip was hard, but not painful. Urgent.

"We have to leave."

"I'm not going anywhere with you."

"You don't have a choice."

"Says who?"

He held up the contract.

"The client gets full protection until the terms are executed."

"I'm the target."

"You're also the client. You hired me to kill you. Until sunrise, I'm your bodyguard."

"That's absurd."

"It's the code."

"There are men downstairs who will definitely kill me."

"Then you have a choice. Stay here and wait for them, or come with me and live until sunrise."

She looked at her sister's body.

She looked at the paper in her hand.

She looked at the hard face of the man who was meant to end her life.

And for a single, impossible second, she saw grief in his eyes. A familiar grief. A grief that belonged to someone who had lost her once before.

"Who were you to me?" she whispered.

He reached up and adjusted the focus ring on his rifle. A quarter turn away from clarity. A gesture that betrayed his control was not as solid as it looked.

"Ask me again at sunrise."

The headlights below clicked off.

The doors of the sedan opened.

He pulled her toward the roof access door.

"Lucian."

His name came out of her mouth without permission. But it tasted right. Like something she had said a hundred times.

He stopped.

"I know you," she said. "I don't know how, but I know you."

"You do."

"Did I trust you?"

"You did."

"Did you deserve it?"

He looked at her. The wind tore across the rooftop, carrying the smell of rain and blood.

"I don't know yet."

He pulled her into the stairwell. The door slammed shut behind them, plunging them into darkness.

Below them, footsteps echoed. Voices. The heavy tread of men searching.

Lucian's grip on her arm tightened.

"We have to move. Now."
