Her key card still worked. That told her more about her enemies' confidence than any surveillance feed.

Sloane held the card against the Annex reader and watched the narrow light blink green.

Lucian stood one step behind her, close enough that she could feel the solid warning of him without looking. His rifle was concealed beneath his coat, but nothing about him looked casual. He had the stillness of a locked door.

"Either they expect me here," she murmured, "or federal security has become a charity operation."

"Both can be true."

The service entrance clicked open.

Sloane did not move immediately. The hallway beyond was too clean, too empty, its polished floor reflecting the emergency strips along the walls. The Records Annex had always been ugly in a bureaucratic way. Beige paint. Dead plants. Bad fluorescent lighting that made everyone look accused.

Tonight, it looked staged.

Lucian studied the corners, the ceiling panels, the black domes of cameras.

"No visible response," he said.

"That is somehow worse."

"Yes."

She slipped through the door first anyway.

The Annex had trained her for quiet hours. Records investigators worked late. Evidence systems went down at inconvenient times. A building like this had its own language after office traffic vanished: ventilation hum, elevator cables, distant servers cycling behind locked doors.

Every sound seemed louder because her body expected a bullet behind it.

She took them down the east corridor, past a closed interview room and a wall of security glass. Her reflection followed beside Lucian's in broken pieces. She barely recognized herself in it.

Three months missing. Her sister gone. A man she apparently trusted with a contract to kill her walking behind her like he had every right to guard her back.

The situation was getting less reasonable by the minute.

"Records are below," she said.

"I know."

Her head turned sharply.

Lucian did not flinch. "The card encoding included access zones."

"Convenient recovery."

"Useful recovery."

"You have a talent for making suspicious things sound practical."

"I have a talent for surviving suspicious things."

That shut her up for exactly two seconds.

"Do you practice those lines in front of mirrors?"

"No."

"Tragic. You could be unbearable with preparation."

The corner of his mouth almost moved. Almost.

They reached the stairwell. Sloane pushed the door open and stopped.

A whiteboard stood on the landing between floors.

It had not been there before.

The Annex did not leave equipment in stairwells. Not after the fire inspection panic two years ago, when a forgotten rolling cart had resulted in forty-seven emails and one temporary suspension for a supervisor who cried in the break room.

This whiteboard was clean except for one line written across the center.

Sloane's breath caught.

The letters were hers.

Not similar. Not close. Hers.

The slant to the right. The tight loops. The hard pressure that dug the black marker nearly through the surface.

CLIENT TERMINATION AT SUNRISE.

For one heartbeat, the words were only words.

Then the stairwell disappeared.

Not literally. That would have been easier to dismiss. Instead, the concrete walls seemed to pull away from her, replaced by a sharper white space that smelled faintly of solvent and hot plastic. Her hands were on a table. Someone had set a pen between her fingers.

Write it again.

The voice did not have a face.

Her throat closed.

Write it again.

Her left thumb moved against the inside of her wrist.

A circle.

Small. Precise.

Her body knew it before she did. One turn. Pause. Another turn. The motion settled into her bones like a remembered path.

Lucian said her name, but it reached her through water.

The marker line on the board thickened. The letters seemed to crawl.

CLIENT.

Her fingers curled.

TERMINATION.

She could not draw a full breath.

AT SUNRISE.

"Sloane."

Lucian's hand closed around her wrist.

Not hard. Not trapping. Just there.

The contact broke something open.

She jerked as if waking from a fall, her gaze snapping to his. The stairwell came back in brutal detail: peeling gray paint, rust at the bottom hinge, the whiteboard on its wheeled base.

Lucian was directly in front of her now. He had put himself between her and the words.

"Look at me," he said.

She did.

His eyes were dark and fixed on hers. No panic. No pity. Just a ruthless kind of attention.

"Tell me where you are."

"The Annex."

"More specific."

"East stairwell." Her voice scraped. "Between two and three."

"Good."

His hand was still around her wrist. Her thumb kept circling beneath his palm, completing that strange, private pattern.

She should have pulled away.

Instead, she caught his other hand.

Her grip tightened around his fingers with humiliating force.

Lucian went completely still.

Sloane felt the reaction move through him, restrained so quickly she might have imagined it. His thumb brushed once over the back of her hand, a small pressure that gave her something real to hold.

"Stay here," he said quietly.

"Excellent instruction. I was considering floating through the ceiling."

"You can insult me after you breathe."

"I am breathing."

"Not well."

She hated that he was right. She hated more that his voice made it easier to obey.

Sloane dragged in air. Held it. Let it out.

Her thumb made one final circle against her wrist, then stopped.

The stairwell settled.

Lucian did not release her until she loosened her grip first.

The instant his hand left hers, cold air found every inch of skin that had been anchored by him. Sloane folded her fingers into a fist before that fact could mean anything.

Her gaze went around him to the whiteboard.

"That wasn't left for anyone else."

"No."

"Did you know it would be here?"

"No."

She searched his face. His lies had a particular economy. He never piled on details. He never tried to make the truth prettier than it was. That should not have made him easier to believe.

It did.

"Someone knows exactly what that phrase does to me," she said.

Lucian looked at the board, jaw tight.

"Yes."

"And you know more than you're saying."

"Yes."

The answer landed harder than a denial would have.

Sloane laughed once, without humor. "At least you're efficient."

"I won't lie to you."

"That is an oddly noble standard from the man carrying my death warrant."

Something shifted in his expression. Pain, perhaps. She did not have room to care about it.

She stepped past him and reached for the whiteboard marker tray.

Lucian caught her elbow. "Don't."

"I'm not going to lick it."

"You don't know what was used."

"Marker ink. Psychological manipulation. Possibly a very overpriced dry-erase surface. I understand the danger."

His grip remained firm for one beat too long before he let go.

Sloane examined the board without touching the words. The marker was generic. The tray held an eraser, two blue markers, a red one with its cap cracked near the tip. Nothing useful. No hidden note. No symbol.

No answer.

Just her handwriting used as a weapon.

She noticed the floor beneath the board.

Dust had gathered everywhere in the stairwell except for a faint pair of marks, scuffed pale against the gray concrete. Two shoe outlines stood exactly parallel to the board. Someone had taped a narrow strip at their edges, then removed it.

Her eyes narrowed.

The whiteboard's lower frame had been adjusted upward. Not much. Three inches, perhaps.

Calibrated.

To her height.

"They measured where I stood," she said.

Lucian followed her gaze.

"Why?" he asked.

"Because whoever wrote that wanted me positioned in the same place." She crouched near the scuffs, careful not to touch them. "Same distance. Same angle. Same trigger."

"Can you tell what it was supposed to make you do?"

A chill climbed her back.

The answer was sitting inside her muscles, locked behind a door she could feel but could not open.

"No," she said. "And I am deeply offended by my own body right now."

Her hand drifted toward her wrist.

Lucian saw it. Of course he did.

"Use it," he said.

She looked up.

"The circle," he added. "It brought you back."

"It also brought me into whatever that was."

"It brought you out."

Sloane stared at him. "You say that like you've seen it before."

His silence answered too much and not enough.

She stood abruptly. "Records room. Before someone adds a smoke machine and a villain monologue."

The basement access reader accepted her card again.

That bothered her more than the whiteboard.

The system should have flagged her badge. She had been missing for three months. Any responsible administrator would have frozen her credentials by the end of the first week. By the end of the first month, her photograph would have been taped beside a security bulletin.

But the Annex greeted her like she had left an hour ago.

The records level was colder than the floors above. Rows of secured storage cabinets stretched behind wire glass. The archive servers blinked in red and green behind a second locked partition.

Sloane led Lucian to a terminal station tucked between two document scanners.

The monitor woke at her touch.

MERCER, S. ACTIVE SESSION.

Her stomach turned.

"That is not possible."

Lucian stood at her shoulder. "Can you access the files?"

"Apparently I can access my own professional identity after someone hollowed it out and used it as a hallway pass."

She typed the number from the photograph in her pocket.

The image had been damaged, but the man in it was not. Heavy brow. Narrow mouth. A scar disappearing beneath his left ear. He stood beside Sloane in the photograph, one hand braced on the hood of a black car.

Her face in the picture had looked different. Focused. Familiar with danger.

The search returned a single restricted file.

ARCHIVE TRANSFER REQUEST: CASSIAN ROOK.

Sloane froze.

The name was not a memory. It arrived without warmth or recognition, just a hard weight in the room.

"Rook," she said.

Lucian's posture changed.

Not much. A fraction. But she noticed.

"So that's him."

"That's a file name," Lucian said.

"Don't insult me. You know who he is."

His eyes met hers.

"Yes."

"How?"

"Not here."

The answer was a wall. Sloane had started to hate walls.

She opened the file anyway.

Most of the contents had been removed. Not deleted, exactly. The metadata showed a clean extraction, performed under her authorization, forty-six days ago. Her own credentials. Her own access signature.

Her fingers hovered over the screen.

"Of course I did it myself," she muttered. "Why let an enemy sabotage you when you can become an employee benefit?"

The remaining attachment was a scanned transfer form. Rook's name appeared at the top. Below it, seven case references had been blacked out so thoroughly the page looked wounded.

No case narratives. No identities. Only transaction codes and dates.

One code repeated across the page.

C009.

The same placement code from the key card.

Sloane leaned closer.

"These weren't evidence transfers," she said. "The numbering is wrong."

Lucian did not ask how she knew. He waited.

"Evidence transfers use department prefixes. These are internal routing numbers, but they have no destination office." Her hand moved to the screen, tracing the columns without touching. "And somebody tried to make the removals look procedural. They used my authorization to sanitize the trail."

"Can you recover it?"

"Not from this terminal."

She opened the system audit log. Every route led to a backup vault buried under an obsolete administration menu. Whoever had hidden it expected anyone searching to stop at the redacted file.

Rook, she thought.

A man organized enough to arrange her footsteps in a stairwell did not leave a file where she could find it by accident.

The vault demanded a secondary authorization.

Sloane held up the key card.

Lucian's expression darkened. "It was built for this."

"Yes." She inserted it. "Which means he wants something."

"He may want you to open it, not take it."

"Then he should have chosen someone less stubborn."

The terminal made a low mechanical click. A panel beneath the desk unlocked.

Inside sat a small black backup drive.

No labels. No logos. Only a strip of silver tape across its casing with a code written in her handwriting.

S.M. / R-7 / CONTINGENCY.

Sloane stared at it.

Her hand did not shake when she picked it up. That was almost worse.

Lucian reached for it.

She moved it out of reach. "No."

"It could be compromised."

"So could the air. Yet here we are, tragically inhaling."

"Sloane."

"I will not hand you every dangerous thing I find because you look better armed than I do."

His gaze dropped to the drive, then returned to her face.

"You don't know what that is."

"Neither do you."

"I know enough to be careful."

"Then be careful beside me."

For a second, the room narrowed around them.

Lucian's voice lowered. "You should not have to carry this alone."

The words struck somewhere she had been keeping boarded shut.

She hated the softness that rose in her chest. Hated that it came from him, of all people. A stranger who knew the shape of her fear. A man whose hand she had gripped like a lifeline five minutes ago.

"Don't make promises you can't keep," she said.

His face gave nothing away.

But he stepped back.

Sloane plugged the drive into the terminal.

The archive opened slowly.

Folders appeared in a plain directory. Transaction fragments. Routing logs. Payment authorizations stripped of names and destinations. Most entries were incomplete, like a book with every important page torn out.

But the surviving dates overlapped with her missing months.

She scanned one record, then another.

Seven transaction references.

Seven redacted cases.

C009 appearing again and again.

A sound came from the corridor.

Not footsteps.

A soft electronic chirp.

The same signal from the beacon in the print shop.

Lucian had his rifle in his hands before the second chirp sounded. He turned the focus ring exactly one quarter rotation away from clarity, his expression going cold.

"Take the drive," he said.

"I already did."

"Now."

Sloane yanked it free and shoved it into her pocket. The terminal flashed.

A new file opened by itself.

No folder. No transaction code.

Just a black screen with white numbers ticking downward.

00:47:18.

At the top, one label appeared.

Condition: Oblivion.

Below it was her name.

SLOANE MERCER.

And beneath the countdown, a timestamp waited like a sentence already passed.

SUNRISE.
