Victor opened the door with a pistol in his hand.

"Turn around," he said.

I obeyed. He searched my coat, my ankles, and the waistband of my trousers. The medical pouch lay flat beneath the adhesive dressing on my injured shoulder, where touching it would have meant touching the wound he made.

He found the memory drive and stepped back.

"Inside."

The door closed behind me.

His house looked less like a home than a set dressed to imply one. Books chosen for their spines. Family photographs without fingerprints. A glass of whiskey poured beside a leather chair.

On the kitchen counter sat a silver drive with a single scratch across its casing.

Prepared at 4:12 the previous afternoon.

I did not look at it twice.

"The camera," I said.

"Recorded your arrival."

"Then you don't get the confession."

"You think you have leverage in my house?"

"You want my voice saying you were right. If police can recover the footage, it becomes evidence instead of a victory."

Vanity and caution argued behind his eyes. Vanity won.

He opened the doorbell console, disabled its network connection, removed the local card, and broke it between his fingers. The pieces went into the fireplace.

No remote erasure. No Wren. A physical act I watched him perform.

Victor kept the pistol as he led me into his study. An offline laptop waited on the desk.

"Put the index in."

"The confession first."

"Both are on the drive."

He inserted it but did not open the file. "If this machine detects anything except video and documents, I shoot you."

"Then you'll never know whether the names are real."

The screen showed two files:

CONFESSION.mp4

NETWORK_INDEX.pdf

He opened the video.

My recorded face filled the screen.

"My name is Nadia Cole," the recording began. "Three years ago, I killed Julian Croft after the courts failed to hold him responsible for my sister's murder."

Victor watched me rather than the screen.

"I built a network that altered records, created cover, and helped me reach men the law had failed to stop."

His finger eased away from the trigger guard.

Then the video changed.

Lena's forged signatures appeared beside the original university forms. The Meridian transfers followed. Patricia Dornan. The Croft evidence-access log. The bank receipt confirming destruction of C-011.

Victor's face emptied.

"David opened the vault," I said. "He kept the records that clear Lena and prove your corruption. He watched the Croft evidence burn."

The pistol returned to center.

"You are lying."

"Call the bank."

"C-010—"

"Held by Vance. The journalists confirmed cancellation. Holt preserved the earlier proffer under seal, and Sasha has the evidence to destroy Vance if he lifts the hold."

Victor glanced toward the silver drive in the kitchen.

The gift. The copy Evelyn held. His final layer still existed, and he needed me to know it—but not yet. First he had to recover control of the room.

"You think that leaves me with nothing," he said.

"It leaves you alive. For the moment."

"You came here to surrender."

"I came here to see whether you could hear the truth without converting it into leverage."

He laughed once. "Your truth is four dead men."

"Three."

His eyes narrowed.

"You would be the fourth."

The pistol rose.

I drove the desk chair into his knees before he cleared the corner. He caught the backrest with one hand, but the old thigh wound folded under the impact.

I tore the medical pouch free.

He saw the injector.

"Priya," he said.

The name was a guess built from the role she played, not forbidden knowledge. For once, I heard the machinery behind his certainty.

I drove the chair into his injured leg. He fell against the desk but kept the pistol. His hand closed around my throat as I reached for his wrist.

The injector hit the floor.

Victor kicked it toward the hallway.

"Did you really think I would drink something you could touch?" he asked.

"No."

I struck his wounded leg with my heel. His grip loosened. We went down together, his gun trapped between our bodies, my injured shoulder tearing open beneath his weight.

For a second, neither of us moved.

Then the pistol slid free.

Victor reached left.

I reached right—toward the injector lying just beyond the study door.

The trap was no longer a plan.

It was distance measured in inches.
