Wren's ledger ended with the file that could bury me.

C-011: JULIAN CROFT.

She had sent Rhodes the operational record from our archive, but the material he already possessed was older and harder to dismiss: marina access logs, recovery photographs, a report on the loosened fuel-line coupling, and a witness who remembered seeing me near Julian's berth.

I opened Rhodes's sample attachment this time.

The first photograph showed the engine compartment after the fire. A red circle marked the fuel line. The second page compared my marina work schedule with Julian's last six visits. The third reproduced a parking receipt with four digits of my card number.

It was not a murder conviction in a folder. It was enough to reopen the death, seize my records, and let investigators build the rest.

My phone rang from a blocked number.

"Now you understand," Rhodes said.

His voice was as calm as it had been in every voicemail. Calm was no longer mysterious. Wren's ledger showed when he learned each secret and what he paid for it in threats.

"Your attorney has C-010," I said. "Not the original."

Silence. Small, but real.

"Wren kept better records than you expected," I continued. "The attorney package makes noise. C-011 makes a case. Who is the second signer?"

"You are asking the wrong question."

"Then give me the right one."

"How much of your life are you willing to burn to keep pretending Julian Croft was justice?"

I looked at Mara's photograph on the desk. "You bought Patricia Dornan. You opened the sealed witness file when you had no role in the prosecution. Julian moved Meridian money through his marina accounts. Elise found the ledger and threatened to expose you."

Rhodes breathed once into the line.

"That is why you protected Julian," I said. "And why Elise died."

"Elise mistook possession of facts for power."

It was not a confession a prosecutor could use. It was the closest a man like Rhodes came to one.

"How long until Vance moves?" I asked.

"C-010 goes to three journalists and one detective. If I die, my attorney sends it sooner. The original has its own protection."

"Family."

He gave a quiet laugh. "Ask Wren."

"Wren is gone."

This time the silence lasted longer.

"Then you finally learned something," he said.

The call ended.

An email arrived a minute later: a formal notice from Rhodes's attorney, Gerald Vance. C-010 would be distributed in thirty days unless Rhodes renewed a hold. The language did not mention his death. It did not need to. Vance had standing instructions and a calendar.

That was the first mechanism.

The second was still physical: C-011, controlled by a family authorization and connected somehow to Evelyn Shaw.

No heartbeat monitor. No magical upload. Two people, two sets of instructions, two problems that could be solved only if I knew what each person wanted.

Sasha read the notice over my shoulder.

"Killing him now moves the attorney package forward," she said. "Doing nothing releases it at the deadline. We need Vance's hold and the physical file."

"Vance won't negotiate with me."

"Then we find the family signer."

I replayed the last section of Wren's confession. His attorney holds C-010. C-011 is physical, under a second authorization. He said the second signer was family. I traced one contact to Evelyn Shaw.

Wren had given Rhodes enough to find me and kept enough to give me one chance against him. Neither act erased the other.

"Containment is complete," Sasha said. "The old archive is sterile. Priya is off every shared channel. Wren can't reach the replacement system."

"Good."

"What do we do if she calls?"

I considered the question. There was a version of me that wanted Wren found, dragged into a room, and made to answer for every file she had opened. That version wanted punishment before survival.

"We record it," I said. "We don't bring her back inside."

Sasha nodded.

I opened a new page in my notebook and wrote three names:

GERALD VANCE — C-010.

UNKNOWN FAMILY SIGNER — C-011.

EVELYN SHAW — BROKER.

Beneath them, I wrote the only useful question:

What does each one fear losing?

The deadline had not given me a plan. It had given the problem a shape.

Rhodes believed every person could be converted into leverage. He had proved it with a witness, private investigators, a lawyer, and Wren.

To beat him, I would have to use the same architecture without pretending it was justice.
