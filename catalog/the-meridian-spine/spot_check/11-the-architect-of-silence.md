"This etching," Iris Kane says, pointing to the micrograph of her access card, "matches the unique defect in a batch of cards issued to your department. Care to explain how it ended up on mine, Nadia?"

Nadia Okafor stopped inside the laboratory door.

Her key remained in the lock. Her gaze moved from the sealed evidence sleeve to the comparison image on Iris's monitor, then to the clock above the chemical cabinet.

16:09.

Twenty-one minutes before Iris's supported amendment became officially inconvenient.

"I came because your door registered an unauthorized hold," Nadia said.

"Interesting choice of words."

Iris did not invite her farther inside. Nadia entered anyway and closed the door with care, as though privacy were still something either of them possessed.

On the screen, two gray fields sat side by side. One showed the damaged edge of Iris's access card. The other showed a quality-control scan from a batch issued six months earlier to the Directorate's internal review department.

Both carried the same crooked series of ridges.

Both ended in the same doubled strike.

Nadia set a slim folder on the nearest bench. "A manufacturing defect proves the cards came from the same production run."

"No. The batch numbers prove they did not."

A pause.

Small, but structural.

Iris tapped the metadata beneath the images. "Mine was issued four months after yours. Different supplier invoice. Different laminate lot. Different encoder listed in the registry."

Nadia's expression held.

"Then your comparison is wrong."

"That would be comforting."

Iris enlarged the terminal mark until it filled half the monitor. The paired groove looked like a tiny fork driven into the magnetic strip.

"This is not a production defect. It came from a worn guide tooth inside a card encoder. Your department's batch was the last group processed before that encoder was supposedly destroyed."

"Supposedly?"

"The destruction form says the unit was crushed and recycled. The recycler's intake coupon records six point eight kilograms."

Nadia said nothing.

"The encoder model weighs nine point one."

"Accessories are removed before disposal."

"Not the internal carriage. Its serial plate was logged as intact."

Nadia's eyes dropped to the evidence sleeve. That was all. No dramatic collapse, no trembling denial. She had always been better than that.

Iris had once mistaken discipline for honesty. A common professional error, right beside trusting passwords and accepting the phrase routine exception.

"The card used to create the false access entry was encoded on the same damaged unit," Iris continued. "Its fresh residue transferred onto the reverse of the ledger page. Twenty-eight-micron ridge spacing. Terminal double strike."

"That does not put the encoder in my hands."

"No. It puts it in your department."

Nadia's hand rose toward her wrist. Her fingers touched the clasp of her watch and adjusted it by less than a millimeter.

Iris had seen her do that once before, on the day they met, when Nadia had been asked whether she trusted the Directorate's first internal audit.

She had not seen the habit since.

Until now.

Nadia lowered her hand. "You are building an accusation from shared equipment."

"I am building it from things that cannot revise their testimony."

Iris crossed to the evidence cabinet. The paper seal over the latch bore a narrow puncture beside the adhesive edge, made by a flat tooth thin enough to lift the seal without tearing its fibers.

She did not open the cabinet.

Instead, she placed a second image beside the card micrograph. It showed the puncture in the archive copy of the prewritten Meridian statement.

National Integrity Directorate -- independent certification; Meridian Spine verified clean.

The language had existed before the repeat test. The conclusion had been prepared before there was a result to support it.

Nadia read the line without appearing to read it.

Iris zoomed in on the puncture.

A shallow crescent marked one side. Near its tip sat a notch so small it resembled dust until magnification turned it into geometry.

"This puncture was made by an archive comb," Iris said. "Not a needle, not a blade. One tooth entered at an angle, caught the paper, and lifted."

"Archive combs are used throughout the building."

"Current ones have rounded teeth."

Nadia's jaw tightened.

"The older model has flat teeth. It was withdrawn after it began snagging document fibers. Facilities retained three for controlled archive work."

Iris slid a property checkout sheet across the bench.

The original remained sealed elsewhere. This was a certified image, enough for confrontation and useless for anyone hoping to make the source disappear.

Nadia did not touch it.

"One is in basement records," Iris said. "One is locked in legal preservation. The third was assigned to internal review."

"Assigned to the department. Not to me."

"Correct."

The word landed harder than an accusation.

Iris opened Nadia's folder with one gloved finger. Inside sat the certification timetable, a media-response matrix, and a list of staff permitted to brief Marsh's office. Each page had been aligned with painful precision.

The upper corner of the folder's inner pocket carried three tiny parallel impressions.

Archive-comb teeth.

Nadia looked at the folder as though it had betrayed her by continuing to exist.

"You brought it with you," Iris said. "That saved me a walk."

Nadia's face changed then, though only around the eyes. "Do you intend to seize my property?"

"I intend to document it."

"With whose authority?"

"Mine, according to the appointment terms everyone keeps quoting when they want my name on a conclusion."

"You cannot turn an administrative folder into proof of sabotage."

"No. The fibers will do that."

Iris lifted a transparent sampling strip from the bench but did not move toward Nadia. Consent mattered even when trust had become an archaeological layer.

"Place the folder on the white mat."

Nadia did not.

The clock advanced to 16:12.

Iris glanced at it. "The statement goes out at eighteen. My amendment is due in eighteen minutes. If you would like the institution protected, this is a poor time to make me waste seventeen."

"Protected from what?"

"That depends. How much damage did you authorize?"

Nadia's eyes sharpened. "Careful."

"That warning would work better if my credentials had not been used to enter a secured coupon bay after Lund's certification."

"I did not authorize the bay entry."

It came too quickly.

Silence settled between them.

Iris felt her thumbnail trace the edge of the evidence sleeve, testing the sealed border for a weakness she already knew was not there.

There it was. Not innocence. Scope.

"You knew there was an entry," Iris said.

Nadia turned her head slightly. "You told me."

"I told you my identity had been copied. I never told you where the copied card was used."

Nadia's gaze went to the monitor.

A trained person could control words. The body was harder. It kept reaching for doors already closed.

Iris placed both palms on the bench. "Lund falsified the coupon. Someone opened the bay after certification. My credential was placed on the paper trail. Before I found any of that, you repeatedly directed me toward Gerald Halveston."

"He financed the project."

"After the tooling decision had entered the chain."

"He had influence before acquisition."

"Influence is not a tool-mark."

Nadia looked at her for a long moment. "You think this ends with me."

"No. I think you were assigned to decide where it appeared to end."

The watch clasp shifted again beneath Nadia's fingers.

Iris continued before Nadia could rebuild her expression.

"The encoder residue links the cloned card to internal review equipment. The archive comb links your folder to the prewritten statement. Your knowledge of the bay entry links you to information you were never given."

"Information moves through this building."

"So do knives. Ownership still matters."

Nadia's mouth tightened. "You have become very certain."

"No. Certainty is what people print before testing. I have three physical connections and a question."

"What question?"

"Why Halveston?"

Nadia looked toward the locked door.

Iris watched the calculation move behind her eyes. Not panic. Nadia was measuring damage, deciding what could still be contained.

"Because he is guilty," Nadia said.

"Of what?"

"Moving money. Shaping procurement. Coordinating contractors."

Each answer arrived clipped and controlled.

"Then why alter my credentials?"

"I did not alter your card."

"Why use the damaged encoder?"

"I did not operate it."

"Why bring an archive-comb folder to my laboratory?"

Nadia said nothing.

Iris leaned closer to the monitor and reduced the magnification. Two defects became small again, almost invisible.

That was the danger of scale. Make a mark tiny enough and people called it insignificant. Put it into a system and it could open a room, rewrite a ledger, and assign guilt to someone who had never crossed the threshold.

"You wanted me to reach Halveston," Iris said. "You wanted me to find enough to stop there."

Nadia's throat moved.

"Say it accurately."

"Accuracy is not always the same as survival."

"For dishonest institutions, perhaps."

"You do not understand what happens if the Directorate loses legitimacy."

"I understand steel failing under load."

"This is larger than one coupon."

"Then it deserves better than a prewritten lie."

Nadia moved at last. She placed the folder on the white sampling mat.

The gesture was almost soundless.

Iris applied the transparent strip to the pocket impression, lifted it, and sealed it against a black evidence card. Under angled light, pale paper fibers clung to the adhesive. One carried a dark blue surface coating identical in color to the statement stock visible in the archive scan.

Not a conclusion yet. A path to one.

Nadia stared at the strip. "You cannot compare that without the original statement."

"The retained edge trimmings from its print batch will establish the coating. The puncture geometry establishes the tool."

"The archive copy could have been handled later."

"The fibers are embedded beneath the pocket's pressure gloss. They arrived before the folder was repeatedly closed."

Nadia exhaled through her nose.

Iris waited.

People rushed to fill silence when they wanted forgiveness. Nadia wanted neither forgiveness nor comfort. She wanted the narrowest version of disaster.

"I was told to keep your review focused," she said.

"By whom?"

"A coordinator."

"Inside the Directorate?"

Nadia met her eyes. "Yes."

The answer did not surprise Iris. It still changed the room.

"What did focused mean?"

"Petra Lund for the certification fraud. Gerald Halveston for the financing and contract pressure."

"Both guilty."

"Yes."

That mattered. Nadia was not describing invented suspects. She was describing real culprits arranged as walls, each solid enough to stop an investigation before it reached the room behind them.

"And Alan Torvik?" Iris asked.

"A requisition signature you were expected to spend time disproving."

"Was his card copied too?"

"I do not know."

Iris believed that answer only because Nadia hated giving it.

"Who ordered my credential duplicated?"

"I was not given operational details."

"But you knew it had happened."

"I knew access records could be directed away from internal review."

"Toward me."

Nadia's eyes lowered for half a second. "If necessary."

The cold in Iris was clean and useful.

Nadia had sat beside her in interviews. She had watched Iris seal evidence. She had warned her about losing standing while someone prepared to place Iris's own name on the breach.

Not for money. That would have been simpler.

"For the Directorate," Iris said.

Nadia looked up.

"You did this because you believe preserving it matters more than what it certifies."

"It is the only national body with the reach to prevent another Kessler."

"And therefore anything done in its name becomes acceptable?"

"No."

"Your evidence suggests otherwise."

"My evidence?" Nadia gave a short, humorless breath. "You have always treated institutions like documents. Edges, fibers, pressure marks. You think if you inspect long enough, function becomes visible."

"Usually failure becomes visible first."

"You can expose Lund. You can expose Halveston. The Directorate survives both."

There it was.

Not a confession to the wider fraud. A boundary drawn around acceptable casualties.

Iris studied the timetable in Nadia's folder. One line had been highlighted beside the 18:00 release.

CONTINUITY OF PUBLIC CONFIDENCE.

No mention of truth. No mention of steel.

"That was the stopping point," Iris said. "Halveston."

Nadia did not answer.

"You fed me the foundation. You emphasized his access, his leverage, his influence. When the Aegis date weakened the path, you pushed harder."

"He coordinated the pressure around the contracts."

"But he did not create the path you were protecting."

Nadia's face closed. "I did what I was instructed to do."

"To preserve the Directorate."

"To preserve the only mechanism capable of enforcing national certification."

Iris glanced at the prewritten statement on the monitor. Verified clean. Two words arranged before the evidence existed.

A mechanism could be preserved until nothing remained inside it except authority.

"Who is the coordinator?" Iris asked.

Nadia reached for her watch again, then stopped before touching it.

The clock changed to 16:18.

Twelve minutes.

Iris collected the sampling strip, card micrograph, checkout sheet, and ledger image into separate sleeves. Physical things. Limited things. Honest about what they could prove and what they could not.

She turned to the terminal and opened the amendment field.

"Once I submit this, the cloned credential, statement preparation, and internal review equipment enter the official record."

Nadia went pale.

"Give me the next verifiable target," Iris said. "Not a theory. Not a speech about survival. A title tied to a person with access to policy archives, card disposal, and certification messaging."

"You know what this will do."

"Yes."

"No, Iris. You know what broken steel does. You do not yet know what a broken institution takes down with it."

"Then it should have been built to carry truth."

Nadia closed her eyes.

For one moment, her hand hovered over the folder between them, almost reaching for the evidence, almost asking Iris to stop. It fell back to her side.

Defeat did not soften her. It stripped away the last layer of official composure.

Nadia Okafor, defeated, whispers a single phrase, not a name, but a title: "The Director of Policy."

Iris Kane's mind races, already putting the pieces together, knowing exactly who holds that title.
