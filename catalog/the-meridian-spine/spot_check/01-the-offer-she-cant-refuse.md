"Iris Kane, I need you to certify the spine so clean it squeaks." Stellan Marsh slid the contract across his glass desk, smiling as if he were doing her a favor. The late morning sun, filtered through the high windows of his office, cast his silhouette in a golden, almost ethereal glow, a fitting halo for the man who spoke of national interest and legacy with such easy grace.

Iris watched his perfectly manicured hand push the document forward. Her thumbnail, a small, unconscious habit, found the edge of the polished mahogany armrest of her chair, tracing for an imaginary hairline crack. She read people like she read systems – looking for the stress points, the hidden fractures that would inevitably betray the integrity of the whole. Marsh's smile was too broad, his posture too relaxed for the weight of the project he was presenting. He was a master of the frame, always.

"The Meridian Spine project," Marsh continued, his voice a smooth, deep baritone, "represents a new era of infrastructure, a testament to our commitment to national integrity. And the National Integrity Directorate, our independent certification body, ensures that commitment is unshakeable." He leaned back, his gaze unwavering, as if daring her to find a fault in his perfect narrative. "We need the best. We need someone whose name is synonymous with forensic rigor. We need you, Iris, as Lead Forensic Authority."

Iris remained silent, her internal scanner already whirring. The Directorate. The grand reform, built on the ashes of the Kessler collapse, designed, so the surface narrative claimed, to prevent any future systemic fraud. It was a beautiful structure, ostensibly. Too beautiful. Her mind replayed the redacted page from her old Kessler files, the single sheet that listed co-owners whose names had been blacked out, leaving an ache of unfinished business in her gut. She knew the official story, believed it enough to accept the premise, but a part of her always looked deeper, for the hidden wires, the unseen gears.

"The scope," Iris said, her voice dry, cutting through the pleasant air of Marsh's office. "Meridian Spine covers thousands of kilometers. Materials, fabrication, installation – all of it?"

Marsh nodded. "Every single component. Every weld, every joint, every load coupon. From raw material sourcing to final stress test. Total oversight. Total transparency." He adjusted a cufflink, the glint of gold catching the light, a fleeting, almost imperceptible movement, but Iris registered it. Inconvenient data. Her question had momentarily pricked his perfectly composed facade.

"And my autonomy?" she pressed. "Full access to all facilities, all records, all personnel. No gatekeepers, no red tape. My conclusions stand without amendment."

Marsh's smile returned, even wider. "Absolutely, Iris. That's the bedrock of the Directorate. Independent certification. Meridian Spine verified clean. Your word will be final. The public trust depends on it." He gestured to the contract. "This is not merely a job offer, Iris. It's an opportunity to rebuild trust, to forge a new standard for our nation. To ensure what happened with Kessler never happens again."

His words were carefully chosen, resonating with the very core of her professional ethos. Rebuild trust. Forge a new standard. The promise was tempting, almost irresistible. She knew the weight her name carried in forensic circles, the unblemished record of her unyielding conclusions. If anyone could certify something "so clean it squeaks," it was her. But the lingering image of that blacked-out page, the unnamed co-owners, remained a cold counterpoint to Marsh's warm rhetoric. Still, the only way to find out what truly lay beneath the surface was to dive in.

"I accept," Iris said, her voice firm. "But my terms are non-negotiable."

Marsh extended his hand, his grip surprisingly warm and firm. "Welcome to the Directorate, Iris Kane. We're very glad to have you."

Her new office at the National Integrity Directorate was stark, functional, and equipped with state-of-the-art forensic analysis tools. No plush carpets or grand windows, just a clean, minimalist space designed for focus. She preferred it. Within hours of settling in, a woman with sharp, intelligent eyes and an easy smile introduced herself.

"Nadia Okafor," she said, extending a hand. "Your partner in crime, or rather, crime prevention. Welcome to the Directorate, Iris. I've been looking forward to this."

Nadia was efficient, her movements precise, her understanding of the Directorate's internal workings impressive. She quickly outlined the immediate priorities for the Meridian Spine project, her enthusiasm infectious. Iris found herself appreciating the blunt, direct approach after Marsh's carefully crafted diplomatic dance.

"The initial reports are, as expected, pristine," Nadia explained, tapping a stylus against a tablet displaying a complex flow chart. "Every supplier pre-vetted, every material batch triple-checked. It's truly a marvel of modern oversight." She paused, her eyes meeting Iris's. "Of course, the real challenge, as always, will be ensuring the money trail is as clean as the materials. These mega-projects attract a lot of attention, and a lot of... creative accounting."

Iris raised an eyebrow. "Creative accounting?"

Nadia shrugged, a small, almost imperceptible gesture. "Just a reality. It's why we don't just look at the physical; we follow the funding, too. For the Spine, a significant portion is flowing through the Halveston Philanthropic Infrastructure Fund. Gerald Halveston, he's a big name, very respected. But big names mean big money, and big money always bears scrutiny, wouldn't you agree?"

Iris nodded slowly, a small mental flag raised. Nadia's suggestion was subtle, almost casual, yet pointed. Follow the money. Specifically, follow Halveston's money. It was a logical line of inquiry, but the emphasis felt deliberate. Iris filed it away, her immediate priority remaining the tangible. Physical evidence didn't lie; ledgers could be made to sing whatever tune their composers desired.

A week later, Iris found herself on a high-speed train, Nadia beside her, heading to the primary fabrication facility for the Meridian Spine. The facility itself was colossal, a sprawling complex of steel and glass that stretched for what felt like kilometers. Inside, the air hummed with the controlled power of industrial machinery, the smell of ozone and freshly cut metal thick in the air. Gigantic robotic arms moved with impossible precision, welding massive structural components together.

They were met by Petra Lund, the lead materials certifier for the Meridian Spine. Lund was a woman of sharp angles and even sharper intellect, her eyes constantly scanning the production floor. Iris remembered her from her Kessler days – a contractor, competent, but always operating within the strictures of her role. Now, Lund's authority was evident, her command over the complex processes absolute.

"Ms. Kane, Ms. Okafor," Lund greeted them, her voice clipped and professional. "Welcome. We're running a few days ahead of schedule, thanks to optimized logistics and zero material defects. The independent certification process has been flawless."

"Show me," Iris stated, cutting straight to the chase. She wasn't there for pleasantries or marketing speak. She needed to see the evidence, feel the steel, examine the records.

Lund led them through various stations, detailing the rigorous testing protocols, the advanced alloys, the computerized tracking of every bolt and beam. Iris listened, but her eyes were everywhere, dissecting the processes, comparing them against her internal database of known vulnerabilities. She stopped at a station where finished load coupons, small, precisely cut metal samples used for stress testing, were being packaged for transport. Each coupon bore the official Directorate seal, a mark of its verified integrity.

"These are from the current batch, certified last week," Lund explained, holding one up. "Passed all tensile strength, fatigue, and impact tests with flying colors."

Iris took the coupon. It was cool and heavy in her hand. Her fingers traced its smooth surface, then moved to the shear line where it had been cut from a larger sheet. The cut was clean, precise, as expected. But then, her thumbnail, still unconsciously searching, detected something. A faint, almost imperceptible wave pattern in the shear line, a microscopic ripple that shouldn't be there. It wasn't a standard machining defect, not the clean, straight edge of a perfect cut. It was a subtle, almost artistic deviation.

Her breath hitched. She remembered this. She had seen this specific pattern before, years ago, in the discarded scrap pile from the Kessler collapse. A unique signature, created not by a standard tool, but by an override jig, a custom piece of equipment designed to bypass normal cutting parameters. A jig that had been used to falsify load coupons, creating samples that appeared structurally sound but masked critical weaknesses in the underlying materials. A jig that should not exist outside a landfill.

Iris rotated the coupon, holding it closer to the light. The microscopic wave pattern was undeniably there, a ghostly echo of a ghost that should have been buried. Her mind raced, connecting this impossible detail to the buried secrets of Kessler, to the redacted page, to the unnamed co-owners. The surface narrative of the Directorate, of "Meridian Spine verified clean," shattered around her, exposing a dark, familiar void.

"This is certified?" Iris asked, her voice flat, devoid of emotion. Her eyes, however, burned with an intensity that made Lund shift uncomfortably.

Lund, oblivious to the deeper implication, simply nodded. "Yes, Ms. Kane. Independent certification. Meridian Spine verified clean."

Iris said nothing more. She placed the coupon carefully into her pocket. Back in the sterile quiet of her Directorate office, hours later, the single load coupon sat on her desk under the harsh glare of a forensic lamp. The microscopic wave pattern on its shear line seemed to pulse with a silent accusation, a physical anomaly whispering secrets of the past. Her gaze drifted to a small, worn notepad from her old Kessler files, open to a single, handwritten line.

Find the other names.
