"Vey, answer me."

The speaker hissed and died as Lucian Vey closed his fist around it.

But Sloane Mercer heard the echo in her skull: Rook knew. He had always known.

Lucian tore the comm from his collar and dropped it beneath his boot. Plastic cracked. A tiny green diode blinked once against the rooftop concrete, then vanished under his heel.

"That was useful," Sloane said. "Next time, destroy the evidence before the psychopath finishes threatening us. More efficient."

"He was using it to track the rifle."

"Then leave the rifle."

"No."

Lucian slung it across his back and caught her elbow as she turned toward the place where Mara had fallen. Sloane stopped so hard his fingers tightened.

There was blood on the concrete. Too much of it, dried dark around the point where her sister's body had been.

But there was no body now.

Sloane stared at the empty outline. The rooftop seemed to contract around her.

"Where is she?"

"Rook."

"You didn't see him take her."

"No."

"Then that's an assumption."

"It's the only one that fits."

She pulled free. "You don't get to turn guesses into facts because your voice is low and expensive."

A muscle shifted in his jaw. He glanced toward the roof access door, then toward the adjacent buildings.

"We move now."

Sloane crouched beside the blood. Her fingertips hovered without touching. A faint drag mark cut through the stain, leading toward the west parapet, then disappearing where grit had been swept across it.

Deliberately swept.

Mara might have been alive when she left this roof. Or someone wanted Sloane to believe she had been.

Neither possibility gave her room to breathe.

Lucian stepped between her and the stain. "Sloane."

"Don't."

"The roof is exposed."

"And my sister has been removed from a crime scene."

"Which means whoever removed her may still be close."

Her fingers found the inside of her left wrist. One small circle. The skin was already raw, but the motion steadied the sharp edges of her thoughts.

Lucian noticed. Of course he noticed.

His gaze dropped to her hand, and something unreadable crossed his face.

"What?" she demanded.

"Nothing."

"That expression had at least three felonies in it."

A burst of static snapped from the rusted loudspeaker above the access door.

Then Rook's voice filled the rooftop.

"Good morning, Sloane."

The sound came from everywhere at once, smooth and intimate through blown speakers. Sloane straightened.

Lucian drew a compact pistol from beneath his jacket. Not toward the speaker. Toward the access door.

Rook continued, "The building perimeter is locked. All ground exits are sealed. The elevators are disabled. If either of you attempts to cross to the east structure, the consequences will be immediate."

Sloane looked at the neighboring roof. A narrow maintenance bridge connected the buildings, six floors above the alley. It would have been the obvious route.

Which made it the stupid route.

"How considerate," she called. "A guided tour."

The PA clicked, but Rook did not answer.

Lucian seized the access handle and tested it. Locked.

Sloane took two steps back, studying the door. Steel slab. Electronic strike. Exposed hinge pins on their side because whoever had renovated the place had valued appearance over security.

She held out her hand. "Magazine."

Lucian watched her.

"From the pistol," she said. "Unless glaring is your preferred breaching method."

He ejected it and gave it to her. She pressed the magazine's metal lip against the top hinge pin, then struck the base with the heel of her palm. Pain jolted through her wrist.

The pin shifted.

Lucian moved beside her. "Again."

She hit it twice more. The pin rose enough for him to hook it free. They repeated the process on the lower hinge, then pulled the door toward them until the electronic strike tore loose with a shriek.

Sloane handed the magazine back. "See? Your accessories can do more than ruin lives."

His eyes held hers as he reloaded. "You taught me that."

The answer landed too close to something she could not reach.

She entered first.

The stairwell smelled of old dust and overheated wiring. Emergency lights painted the concrete walls a weak red. Six flights below, something heavy slammed shut.

The perimeter locking.

Lucian caught her shoulder before she descended. "We don't take the main stairs."

"Because Rook expects it?"

"Because the landing below has fresh adhesive on the rail."

Sloane leaned over. A dark patch gleamed beneath the red light.

"Sensor?"

"Possibly."

"Possibly explosive?"

"Possibly."

"Your range of comforting answers remains impressive."

They crossed through a service door into the building's upper floor. It had once housed a private broadcast studio. Empty equipment bays lined the walls, and thick acoustic panels sagged from the ceiling. A row of glass booths looked down on a control room stripped of everything valuable.

Everything except the PA system.

Rook's voice returned with a soft pop.

"Lucian, you know what happens when you improvise."

Lucian kept moving.

"You were always excellent at following precise instructions," Rook said. "Until she made you sentimental."

Sloane looked at Lucian. His expression did not change, but his hand tightened around the pistol.

Rook gave a quiet laugh. "Sloane, ask him what sentiment costs."

The speakers cut out.

"Well?" she asked.

"He's dividing our attention."

"That wasn't an answer."

"It wasn't a useful question."

"Questions become useful when people stop dodging them."

Lucian opened the nearest booth and checked behind the door. Empty. He gestured her inside.

Sloane remained where she was. "Did you help erase my memory?"

"No."

"Did you know it would happen?"

"Yes."

The single word struck harder than denial would have.

"How long before?"

"Not long enough."

She laughed once, without humor. "That sounds almost like regret."

"It is regret."

"You don't get credit for naming it."

"No." His voice lowered. "Credit isn't what I'm asking for."

A metallic clatter sounded inside the wall. Sloane spun toward it, hand going to a weapon she did not have.

The red lights strobed.

Once.

Twice.

Then darkness swallowed the room.

Her breath locked.

A white room flashed behind her eyes. A hard chair beneath her. A voice counting in measured intervals. Her own left hand turning at the wrist, circle first, fingers aligning as if around an absent grip.

Then the imagined grip became real.

Her index finger curled.

"No," she whispered, though she did not know what she was refusing.

Lucian was suddenly in front of her. Not touching. Close enough that she could feel his heat in the dark.

"Sloane. Look at me."

"I can't see you."

A narrow beam clicked on beneath his pistol, angled toward the floor. Light climbed his coat, his throat, the scar above his eyebrow.

Her fingers kept moving against her wrist.

Circle.

Set.

Curl.

Her pulse accelerated with each motion, as if her body expected a command her mind had lost.

Lucian holstered the pistol. Then his hand rose slowly, giving her time to refuse.

"May I?"

The question cut through the counting in her head.

Sloane should have stepped away. She should have demanded context, motive, proof, and several legally admissible reasons not to break his fingers.

Instead, she gave one stiff nod.

Lucian cupped her jaw.

His palm was warm. His thumb brushed her cheek once, a careful stroke that felt less like comfort than recognition. Frightening tenderness softened his voice.

"Stay here, sweetheart."

The endearment tore open a door inside her.

For a heartbeat, she saw his hand in another room, his thumb resting in the same place. Her own voice whispered numbers. His mouth shaped her name. A strip of black tape lay across a table beside an open gun case.

Then the image vanished.

Sloane caught his wrist. "Don't call me that."

His face went still. "Understood."

But he did not pull away until her breathing leveled.

She hated that the contact worked. Hated more that some buried part of her had expected it.

"What just happened?" he asked.

"Nothing."

"Sloane."

"A chair. Counting. My hand knew where to go before the rest of me did."

His gaze dropped to her wrist.

She released him. "If you say muscle memory, this pistol is going somewhere medically inconvenient."

"I was going to say Rook triggered it."

"With the lights?"

"Maybe the timing. Maybe the sound before the blackout."

"So many maybes. You're practically a research paper."

The emergency lights flickered back on.

A black smear marked Lucian's thumb where it had touched her cheek.

Sloane frowned. She rubbed her own fingertips together and found the same greasy residue staining them.

Friction tape adhesive.

The memory fragment sharpened: black tape around a narrow device. Her hands sealing it. Her thumbnail smoothing the edge twice.

Not a gun case.

A coat.

Her coat.

Sloane shoved both hands into the pockets. Empty except for a bent metal scribe and a square of lint. She ran her fingers along the seams.

Lucian watched. "What are you looking for?"

"Something I left."

"You remember?"

"No. My hands do."

She traced the lining from the right hip to the hem. Nothing. On the left side, directly beneath the inner pocket, her fingers found three stitches that were shorter than the others.

Her heartbeat changed.

She pulled the metal scribe from her pocket and sliced the thread.

A slim object slid into her palm, wrapped in matte black friction tape. The exact brand from the flash in her mind. The adhesive had softened with body heat, leaving a stubborn stain across her fingertips.

Lucian went motionless.

Sloane peeled back one edge. Beneath it sat a miniature comm device no larger than two stacked coins, with a single recessed button and a wired bone-conduction tab.

"I hid this."

"It appears so."

She turned it over. Three shallow grooves had been scratched into the casing. Not letters. Not numbers. A small circle followed by two straight marks.

Her left thumb moved over the circle.

A faint click sounded inside the device.

Lucian's attention sharpened. "What did you do?"

"I touched it."

"Exactly how?"

She demonstrated without meaning to: thumb circling once, pausing at the first line, then sliding toward the second.

A tiny amber light appeared beneath the tape.

The device had not responded to pressure. It had responded to sequence.

Sloane stared at it. "That's obscene."

"What is?"

"Past me set a combination lock in my own nervous system."

"Or she knew conscious memory couldn't be trusted."

"Don't make her sound clever. She hired you to shoot my sister."

Lucian looked toward the nearest PA speaker. "Keep your voice down."

"Rook already knows we're here."

"He may not know what you found."

As if summoned by the name, the PA crackled.

"Sloane."

Rook's voice had lost its false warmth.

She closed her fist around the device.

"You always did hide contingencies in the seams," he said. "But you never understood the difference between preparation and panic."

Lucian motioned toward the control room. Its rear wall held a narrow maintenance hatch.

Sloane did not move. "You said the perimeter was locked, Rook. Not that the building was empty."

Silence answered.

That was better than a lie.

She crossed to the control room with Lucian behind her. The hatch opened onto a vertical cable shaft. Thick bundles ran down into darkness, with steel brackets fixed into the wall at irregular intervals.

A terrible route.

Therefore, probably the right one.

Lucian examined the first bracket. "You go first."

"Afraid I'll shoot you in the back?"

"If you intended to, you would ask for the pistol before climbing."

She looked at him. "Give me the pistol."

"No."

"Had to test the theory."

The corner of his mouth nearly moved. The almost-smile was worse than the tenderness. It suggested a history with rules she did not know and jokes she might once have finished.

A hard mechanical thud rolled through the floor.

The control room doors locked behind them.

The PA emitted a piercing tone.

Sloane's hand snapped to her wrist again. Circle, pause, slide.

The hidden device vibrated in her palm.

A second amber light appeared.

Lucian saw it. "The signal activated another layer."

"Not the signal." She looked at the marks on the casing. "The motion."

The cable shaft waited behind her. Rook waited somewhere beyond the walls. Midnight hung ahead of them with Mara's life balanced on a claim neither of them could verify.

Sloane fitted the bone-conduction tab behind her ear.

Lucian's hand closed gently around her wrist before she could press the recessed button.

"Think first."

"Interesting advice from the man carrying a rifle indoors."

"If the recording triggers another response, the shaft is the worst place for it."

"So we stay in the locked room under Rook's speakers?"

His grip loosened, but did not fall away. His thumb rested over her pulse, dangerously close to the raw circle she had traced into her skin.

"Whatever you hear," he said, "verify it."

"Even if it's me?"

"Especially then."

The answer should have reassured her. Instead, it made the device feel heavier.

Sloane studied him. "If this tells me you are lying, what will you do?"

"Ask what evidence it provides."

"And if the evidence is enough?"

His eyes did not leave hers. "Then you'll make your choice."

No promise not to stop her. No claim that she had trusted him before. Only the brutal admission that choice had consequences.

She pulled her wrist from his hand.

The black adhesive had transferred to him now, a dark crescent across his thumb. A mark shared between them, ugly and impossible to ignore.

Above them, Rook's voice returned through the PA.

"Do not play that message."

Sloane pressed the button.

A soft burst of static entered her skull. Then came a breath she recognized as her own, controlled and close to the microphone.

As Sloane Mercer presses play on the hidden device, her own voice whispers, "If you are hearing this, I am already gone. The only truth left is Vey. Trust him, even when he becomes the enemy."
