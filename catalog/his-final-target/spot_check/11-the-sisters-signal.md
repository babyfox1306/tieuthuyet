The first alarm blared from the stairwell, a raw shriek that cut through the pre-dawn quiet, confirming Cassian Rook wasn't waiting for sunrise. A second alarm answered from the opposite access point. Then the lock beneath the eastern door began to grind.

Sloane dropped behind the concrete housing beside the rifle.

"Multiple entries," she said. "How many routes onto this roof?"

"Four."

"How many can you cover?"

Lucian checked the pistol in his hand. "Three."

Of course. The arithmetic of disaster remained brutally simple.

He crouched beside her, close enough that his shoulder blocked part of her view. The rifle lay on its folded bipod near the parapet, its barrel aimed toward the neighboring buildings.

Sloane traced a circle against the inside of her left wrist.

Fear made the gesture automatic. Knowing it had been planted inside her did nothing to stop it.

Lucian's gaze dropped to her hand. "Stay near the western parapet."

"That wasn't an answer."

"It was an order."

"And here Sloane had almost mistaken you for charming."

The eastern lock gave another violent shudder.

Lucian reached for the rifle, then stopped. His fingers closed around the focus ring and turned it exactly one quarter rotation away from clarity.

The tiny movement chilled her more than the alarms.

He hated what came next.

"Lucian."

His eyes lifted to hers. "Take the rifle. Don't fire unless you identify the target."

"I know how firearms work."

"I remember."

Two quiet words, carrying an entire version of her that he refused to bury.

He slid the rifle toward her and moved low across the roof. The pistol remained close to his thigh as he checked the northern access door, the ventilation corridor, then the narrow maintenance stairs descending behind the utility bank.

Sloane pulled the rifle into position. Its stock settled against her shoulder with obscene familiarity.

She looked through the scope.

The quarter turn Lucian had made left the distant building smeared at the edges. She adjusted the ring back, her fingers moving without conscious calculation, and brought rows of dark windows into focus.

Nothing.

She shifted left.

A rooftop water tank. A service balcony. A strip of reflective insulation flickering beneath a loose panel.

Then three quick flashes blinked from a window twelve floors below the opposing roofline.

Sloane froze.

The flashes stopped.

Gunfire snapped from the northern side of the roof. Concrete spat beside Lucian's boots as he dropped behind a duct and returned two controlled shots.

"Movement?" he called.

"Across the street."

"Weapon?"

"Unknown."

"Then keep looking."

His confidence in her judgment would have been flattering if men with guns weren't currently trying to perforate them.

Sloane returned to the scope.

The window remained dark. She shifted the reticle over the balcony, searching for a lens flare, a muzzle, anything that explained the signal.

A hand rose behind the glass.

Slim fingers. Bare palm. A thin white strip around the thumb.

The hand tapped the window frame twice, paused, then tapped three times.

Sloane forgot the alarms.

Mara had wrapped white athletic tape around that thumb whenever she worked with tools. She claimed it improved her grip. It did not. It merely protected a scar she hated admitting was tender.

"Mara."

The name escaped before Sloane could test it against evidence.

The hand vanished.

Her pulse slammed against the rifle stock. She dragged the scope across the window, finding only a dim interior and the edge of a sleeve.

Mara was alive.

Unless Rook had found the tape. Unless he knew about the scar. Unless he had built another performance because apparently Sloane's life had become an exhausting competition between staged deaths and manipulated evidence.

The hand appeared again.

Tap. Tap.

Pause.

Three taps, followed by a short drag of the fingertips.

It was Mara. Not because of the tape. Not because Sloane wanted it so badly that wanting had become proof.

The final drag was an old correction between them, used when they were adults and arguing over coded knocks through apartment walls. Mara always dragged her nails after a sequence she wanted repeated.

Sloane's left hand circled her wrist.

Across the distance, Mara's fingers curled into the same shape.

The sight punched the air from Sloane's lungs.

"Lucian."

Something in her voice brought him to her immediately.

He crossed the exposed ground between bursts, caught the back of her jacket, and pulled her lower behind the concrete housing. A round clipped the parapet where her head had been.

"What did you see?"

She thrust the rifle toward him. "Window beneath the green service awning. Twelfth floor. Look."

Lucian did not take it. His eyes stayed on her face.

"Sloane."

"Mara is alive."

The statement fractured on the last word. She hated that. Facts should remain intact when spoken.

His control broke anyway.

For one stunned second, hope stripped every hard line from his expression. He looked younger, and far more dangerous to her defenses.

"You're certain?"

"No. Certainty is unavailable until further notice." She swallowed. "But she used our correction signal. She used the tape on her thumb. She knew the wrist movement."

Lucian's hand closed around the back of her neck.

He pulled her against him.

The embrace was tight, protective, and startlingly human. His chest pressed against her cheek, his breath uneven above her hair. One arm locked around her shoulders while the other shielded the back of her head.

Sloane should have objected. She should have reminded him that hope was not evidence and that being held in the middle of an attack was tactically ridiculous.

Instead, she gripped his jacket.

Mara was alive.

The possibility burned through every ugly fact Sloane carried. It did not erase the blood on the rooftop or the contract bearing her authority. It did not absolve her of arranging the shot.

But somewhere across the concrete canyon, her sister had raised a hand.

Lucian's mouth brushed no more than air above Sloane's temple.

"Easy, sweetheart."

The endearment was so quiet it hurt.

Her fingers tightened once before she forced them open. "She was signaling."

"I know."

"No, there was a sequence. Two, three, drag. Then something else."

The western access door exploded inward.

Lucian threw Sloane down as fragments of metal and concrete cut through the space where they had been kneeling.

He pivoted before the door finished striking the wall.

The first figure through carried a compact weapon high. Lucian fired once. The figure dropped out of Sloane's view, blocking the entrance long enough for another to stumble over him.

Lucian closed the distance.

He caught the second weapon by the barrel, shoved it toward the roof, and drove his elbow into the attacker's throat. The gun discharged harmlessly into the sky. Lucian twisted, stripped it free, and struck with the stock.

A third figure pushed through.

"Lucian, right!"

He moved at her warning. The incoming burst tore sparks from the utility housing instead of his ribs.

Sloane rolled toward the rifle, hooked the sling, and dragged it behind the concrete block. Her left wrist burned beneath her cuff.

Circle. Pressure. Release.

The sequence wanted to continue.

She stopped her hand before it could.

A shadow crossed the southern ventilation corridor. Sloane swung the rifle toward it, found an armed silhouette, and fired into the metal panel beside the figure's head.

The warning shot forced the attacker back.

"South corridor!" she called.

Lucian drove his shoulder into the western door, pinning one of Rook's forces against the frame. "Can you hold it?"

"Do you want the comforting answer or the accurate one?"

"Accurate."

"Briefly."

"Good enough."

He kicked the stripped weapon across the roof. It spun within reach of the concrete housing.

Sloane ignored it and returned to the scope.

Mara's window was empty.

"No. Come on."

She moved the reticle over the dark glass. Nothing but a chair and one pale section of wall.

Gunfire hammered the parapet.

Lucian dropped behind the rifle position, changed magazines, and rose just long enough to force the southern corridor back. His body remained between Sloane and the nearest line of fire.

She hated how natural he made protection look. Worse, some hidden part of her recognized the exact angle of his stance.

"She's gone," Sloane said.

"Then find where they took her."

"They?"

"The window wasn't secure. If it were, she wouldn't be signaling through glass."

He fired twice toward the western doorway.

Sloane searched the floors above and below Mara's window. "You think she's being held."

"I think you don't signal unless speaking is dangerous."

That was not an explanation. It was an observation, which made it harder to reject.

A flash appeared three windows to the left.

Sloane adjusted.

Mara's hand pressed against another pane, lower now, as if she were crouching. The white tape flashed once.

"Found her."

Mara tapped rapidly.

Two. Three. One.

Pause.

Her index finger drew a small circle against the inside of her opposite wrist.

Sloane's own hand answered before thought intervened. Circle. Her fingers shifted toward the base of her thumb, then stopped.

Pressure stabbed behind her eyes.

A table surfaced in her mind. Black foam. Brass cartridges laid in groups. Mara's hand knocking against metal while Sloane counted under her breath.

The image vanished.

Sloane sucked in air.

"What?" Lucian demanded.

"Memory fragment. Ammunition on a table. Mara tapping."

"How many?"

"I don't know."

"Try again."

She looked at him over the stock. "That conditioning can turn me into something Rook built."

"And Mara is still signaling."

Damn him for locating the one fact she could not argue around.

The northern access door buckled inward.

Lucian seized the rifle's spare magazine and slammed it into Sloane's palm. "Interpret the signal. I'll hold the entries."

"You cannot cover three doors."

"I only need to make them believe I can."

"That's a terrible plan."

A hard, humorless curve touched his mouth. "You used to like those."

He turned away before she could ask what else she used to like.

The northern door burst open.

Lucian fired, moved, and fired again. He never remained where the answering rounds expected him to be. Every step looked calculated, but Sloane saw the tightening around his mouth and the way his left arm guarded his ribs.

He was spending himself in seconds to buy her minutes.

Sloane returned to the scope.

Mara repeated the signal.

Two taps against the frame.

Three against her palm.

One finger circling her wrist.

Then four quick strikes against the metal below the window.

The sound could not cross the distance, yet Sloane almost felt it through the rifle. A faint hum traveled along the scope's metal as nearby impacts vibrated through the rooftop, giving Mara's rhythm a ghostly counterpoint.

Two. Three. One. Four.

Sloane repeated the numbers silently.

Her left hand moved with them. Two fingers pressed to her pulse. Three tapped below the wrist bone. One circle. Four flexes.

The pressure behind her eyes sharpened.

For an instant she saw the seven etched marks on a magazine housing. A strip of paper fed through a narrow slot. Her own hand closed around Mara's and forced it to repeat a rhythm.

No words. No answer. Only motion.

The memory collapsed before she could catch its edges.

"Again," Sloane whispered.

Across the street, Mara's hand hesitated.

A dark shape moved behind her.

Mara struck the frame twice, faster now.

Sloane's breath stopped.

The hand disappeared, then returned at a different angle. Someone was pulling her away.

"Mara!"

Lucian looked toward her. That single glance cost him.

A round grazed his upper arm, jerking him sideways.

Sloane fired at the northern doorway. The shot struck the frame inches from the advancing weapon and drove it back.

Lucian recovered behind the utility bank. Blood darkened his sleeve, but his hand remained steady.

"You hit?"

"Not usefully."

"Next time, try getting wounded in a medically convenient location."

His eyes met hers across the roof.

There it was again, that impossible moment of recognition. She knew how he would look when pain settled in. He knew exactly which kind of cruelty meant she was frightened.

Then the western doorway filled with movement.

Lucian grabbed the stripped automatic weapon and checked its chamber. "Get behind the rifle block."

"She is still there."

"And Rook's people are ten seconds from reaching you."

Sloane traced another circle against her wrist. This time she let the movement continue into the first two taps.

Her body knew there was more.

The knowledge lived below memory, buried where questions could not reach it.

She looked through the scope again.

Mara's hand slammed against the glass.

Lucian rose from cover and fired a suppressive burst toward the breached access points.

Through the vibration humming along the rifle scope's metal, Sloane saw Mara's fingers tapping out a frantic, familiar rhythm. It matched the wrist movement Sloane had been making unconsciously since waking, but this time Mara completed the sequence.

A silent command demanding to be fulfilled.
