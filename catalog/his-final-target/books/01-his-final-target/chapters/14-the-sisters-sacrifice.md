The ledger device hummed to life, connecting Sloane Mercer to a voice she thought was lost forever. "Mara?" she whispered, her hand trembling.

Static scratched across the reply. Then her sister said, "You took your time."

Sloane stared at the palm-sized screen. Mara's image resolved in broken strips: one dark eye, a bruised cheek, the familiar crooked lift of her mouth. Behind her, gray acoustic panels covered a wall, and a red emergency strip pulsed above a reinforced door.

Alive.

Not forgiven. Not safe. Alive.

"Prove it," Sloane said.

Mara's faint smile sharpened. "You broke the kitchen window when you were twenty-four and blamed me. You still owe me eighty credits."

"Anyone could know that."

"You buried the money inside the blue flour tin because you thought burglars didn't bake."

"Better."

Mara raised her hand to the camera. A pale seam crossed the base of her thumb, left by a knife Sloane had dropped years ago. Then Mara tapped two fingers against her palm, paused, and traced a tight circle.

The same rhythm she had sent through the rifle scope.

Sloane's frightened fingers answered against the inside of her left wrist before she could stop them. Circle. Pressure. Release.

The ledger brightened between her hands.

A location string flashed beneath Mara's image. East structure. Level nineteen. Surveillance control. The device cross-checked the transmission against internal architectural files, matching the acoustic panels, emergency strip, and vent pattern behind her.

Verifiable. Physical. Brutal.

"You're three floors below us," Sloane said.

"For now."

Lucian stood beside the rooftop service housing, rifle balanced across his forearms. The weapon was unloaded. Sloane had removed the magazine herself and cleared the chamber twice before setting it between them earlier. His attention remained on the roof access while his face angled toward Mara's voice.

He looked carved from discipline, except for his eyes.

Those were fixed on Sloane.

"What did you agree to?" Sloane asked Mara.

The static thickened, persistent as grit caught under skin.

Mara looked toward something beyond the camera. "All of it."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the one you already decoded."

"I decoded transactions. I decoded my own contingency plan. What has been missing is the privilege of hearing my sister explain why she let a rifle round tear through her."

Mara pulled the collar of her shirt aside. A dark, healing groove crossed the top of her shoulder.

"The round was real," she said. "So was the blood pack under my jacket. Lucian had one safe angle, and you made him rehearse it until he could hit it blind."

Lucian's jaw tightened.

Sloane looked at him. "You rehearsed shooting my sister."

"She moved the target mark twice," he said.

"It was too close to my neck," Mara replied.

The casual irritation between them made Sloane want to laugh, scream, or throw the ledger off the roof. Apparently the Mercer family response to trauma was quality control.

"You consented," Sloane said.

"Yes."

"With full knowledge?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Mara's expression lost its edge. "Because Rook watched you. He watched me less carefully. A dead sister gave you a wound he believed he could use, and it gave me room to move where his system stopped looking."

"Acting as bait."

"Volunteering as bait."

"You let me believe you were dead."

"You ordered the plan built so even your grief would authenticate it."

The words landed with ugly precision. Sloane remembered the blood on the rooftop. The empty space where Mara's body had been. Every question she had aimed at Lucian because questions were cleaner than terror.

"I had no right," she said.

"No. You didn't."

Mara did not hand her absolution. That would have been easier, cheaper, and false.

"But neither did I need your permission to choose," Mara continued. "I knew the shot was staged. I knew the erasure was part of the trap. I knew Rook would take me if he realized the ledger was opening. I agreed anyway."

Sloane's nails bit into the device. "The hand rhythm."

"You taught it to me before the wipe. I sent it through the scope because your conscious memory couldn't be trusted, but your hands could."

Mara repeated the sequence. Tap. Tap. Pause. Circle. A final curl of her trigger finger that made the ledger's exposed files cascade faster.

Seven names.

Seven contracts corrupted after acceptance.

Seven deaths Sloane had authorized through a system Cassian Rook had fed with altered evidence.

The guilt remained hers. Manipulation did not make the trigger unpulled.

A burst of interference tore Mara's image sideways. Under the static came a man's recorded voice, smooth and clipped.

"Mercer. Circle. Acquire. Complete."

Sloane went rigid.

Her left hand started to turn.

Lucian caught her wrist without squeezing. "Look at me."

The command repeated through the device.

Her pulse slammed against his fingers. Her shoulder wanted to rotate. Her hips shifted for balance. Her vision narrowed toward the rifle lying across the concrete.

Procedure surviving where memory had been cut away.

Mara struck her screen. "Sloane, disconnect."

"No." Sloane looked at Lucian. "He still has access to the command channel."

"Then close it."

"Not the channel. The response."

Lucian understood before she finished. His gaze dropped to their joined hands, then to the unloaded rifle between them.

"No," he said.

Sloane almost smiled. "Your enthusiasm is devastating."

"This is not a game."

"Correct. It is a controlled interruption of conditioned motor response."

His mouth flattened. "Using us."

"Using a state my body associates with choosing you."

The command sounded again.

Her fingers twitched toward the rifle.

Sloane stepped over it instead.

She took Lucian's face in both hands and held his gaze. "Do you consent?"

His breath changed, but he did not touch her. "Yes."

"To all of it?"

"Yes. You stop, it stops."

"Good."

She kissed him hard enough to make the device shift against her hip. Lucian held still for one punishing second, giving her every chance to retreat. Then his hand closed around her waist and pulled her against him.

Heat cut through panic.

Sloane pushed his jacket from his shoulders. He dragged her mouth back to his, terse even in hunger, as if every kiss were an answer he refused to say aloud. She felt the scar under his ribs when her palm slid beneath his shirt.

Memory flashed.

His body over hers. Her nails in his back. His voice against her throat, calling her a quiet endearment that hurt because she could not retrieve the night around it.

The command repeated.

Circle. Acquire. Complete.

Her wrist jerked.

Lucian released her immediately.

Sloane caught his shirt. "Do not stop."

"Your hand moved."

"The rest of me didn't."

His eyes searched hers. "Certain?"

"Painfully."

She turned them, forcing his back against the service housing. The unloaded rifle remained on the ground, visible between their boots. Her choice. Her weapon. Neither one touching it.

She opened his belt with hands that remembered him too well. Lucian caught her chin, making her look at him before he slid a hand beneath her shirt.

"Say it."

"Yes."

His fingers traced her waist, then moved lower, deliberate and sure. Pleasure tightened through her, immediate enough to steal a breath. She hated that he noticed. She hated more that his expression softened with knowledge of exactly how to touch her.

"Arrogant," she whispered.

"Accurate."

She bit his lower lip.

He answered by turning them again, pressing her against the wall while keeping one hand braced beside her head. He kissed down her throat, and she arched into him as his fingers worked beneath her clothes. Every stroke pulled her farther from the rifle, farther from the path Rook had laid inside her nerves.

Sloane reached between them and took control of the pace. Lucian went still at her touch, his forehead dropping to hers.

"Still yes?" she asked.

"Yes."

She guided him into her, slow at first, then deeper when her body opened around him. The rooftop vanished into breath, friction, and the fierce restraint in his hands. He let her set the rhythm until patience broke across his face.

"Move," she ordered.

He did.

Lucian stayed quiet except for her name. He said it once when she tightened around him, once when she dragged her nails over his shoulder, and once when the recorded command came through the ledger again.

"Mercer. Circle. Acquire. Complete."

The words struck at the height of pleasure.

Her left wrist pulsed.

The rifle waited.

Sloane's body clenched around Lucian instead.

She stayed.

No turn. No reach. No firing stance.

Only the choice to hold him as release tore through her and left her shaking against his chest.

Lucian froze inside her.

Silence followed the command.

He felt it. The absence. The woman described by the clause should have obeyed, but Sloane had remained exactly where she chose to be.

His forehead rested against hers. Something changed in his expression, quiet and irreversible.

Then he withdrew, helped her straighten her clothes, and picked up the micro-cassette recorder containing the sunrise clause.

He stared at it for one second.

Instead of checking the time, he slid it into the armored pocket over Sloane's heart.

No declaration. No promise he could not take back.

Just placement.

The first explosion ripped the roof access door off its hinges.

Lucian threw Sloane down as fragments crossed the rooftop. She rolled, seized the rifle, and slammed the magazine home. The device clipped to her belt continued transmitting Mara's image through restless static.

Dark figures poured through the smoke.

Sloane reached the parapet, dropped behind a vent housing, and sighted through the scope. The first target carried a signal jammer in a hard-shell pack.

She fired into the pack's exposed antenna assembly.

Sparks burst from the unit. Mara's image stabilized.

"Four crossing west," Mara warned through the comm. "Two above the access housing."

Sloane turned the rifle's focus ring one quarter rotation away from clarity, the gesture Lucian used before an order he hated. Then she corrected it and fired.

A rooftop floodlight shattered over the west team, showering them with glass and forcing them back from the rifle case they were trying to recover.

Lucian moved left, covering the gap with his pistol.

A red aiming point crawled across his ribs.

"Down," Sloane snapped.

She shot the remote optic mounted above the east camera pole. The red point vanished an instant before Lucian crossed open ground. He reached the ledger cable, tore it free from the exposed relay box, and dragged the device's hardline behind thicker cover.

They moved without discussion.

Lucian fired to turn heads. Sloane used the rifle to destroy equipment: one door-control module, two surveillance lenses, a breaching charge before its carrier could fix it to the service housing. Each shot bought seconds, not victory.

The assault kept coming.

A round struck the parapet inches from Sloane's cheek. Concrete dust filled her mouth. She traced a circle against her wrist, felt the firing sequence rise, and redirected it by choice.

Acquire.

She sighted the rooftop crane's hydraulic junction and fired.

Fluid sprayed across the metal track. The suspended maintenance platform dropped three feet and jammed sideways, blocking the eastern approach.

Lucian looked at her across the roof. Blood marked his temple, but his stance remained steady.

He pointed to the ledger, then to her.

Protect it.

Sloane shook her head and pointed to him too.

Coordinated or dead. Those were the available options, and death had terrible timing.

Mara's image flickered. Her bruised face turned toward the reinforced door behind her.

The handle moved.

"Mara," Sloane said. "Can you lock it?"

"Already did."

A heavy impact bowed the door inward.

Sloane searched the rooftop through the scope, hunting for a line into the east structure. No angle. No clean shot. No convenient miracle.

Another blast overturned the vent housing.

Lucian grabbed the rifle case while Sloane snatched the ledger. They retreated toward the west parapet, firing in alternating bursts, but black-clad bodies filled the access route and spread across the roof.

Too many.

Lucian reached for the recorder over Sloane's heart, not to retrieve it, but to press the armored pocket closed. His eyes met hers.

The contract could wait.

She could not.

On the device, Mara backed away from the buckling door. Static consumed half her face. She lifted her hand and repeated the rhythm once more.

Tap. Tap. Pause. Circle.

Then she mouthed one silent word.

Go.

The rooftop speakers erupted with a voice loud enough to shake dust from the service housing.

"The ledger is mine. And now, Mara Mercer is mine as well."
