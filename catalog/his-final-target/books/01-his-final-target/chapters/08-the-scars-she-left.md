"Take off your shirt," Sloane Mercer ordered, holding Sloane Mercer's knife against the seam while Lucian Vey stared at the device speaking in Sloane Mercer's forgotten voice.

The recording crackled between them.

"Authentication requires visual confirmation. Left ribs. Right shoulder. Lower abdomen. Do not proceed until all three marks are verified."

Lucian looked from the palm-sized device to the knife. "There were less hostile ways to ask."

"Those probably require trust."

"Or manners."

"Take it off, Lucian."

His shirt had been sliced near the shoulder during their escape into the warehouse, the dark fabric stiff with dust and drying blood. He set the rifle against the steel case, then lifted both hands, giving her control of the blade.

"Cut it," he said.

Sloane hooked the knife beneath the torn seam. "Hold still."

His gaze settled on her face. "You used to say that before doing something painful."

"Apparently, past Sloane was charming."

"She had moments."

The fabric parted beneath the blade. Sloane cut down his side, careful not to touch skin, then peeled the ruined shirt away from his shoulders.

Lucian stood bare-chested in the cache's hard white light.

Scars crossed him like an argument she had started and never finished.

One pale line hooked beneath his left ribs. A puckered mark sat high on his right shoulder, close enough to bone that the wound should have ruined the arm. The third scar was smaller, a narrow crescent low on his abdomen.

Sloane's left fingertip traced a circle against the inside of her wrist.

Pressure flared behind her eyes.

A needle passing through skin.

Lucian seated shirtless on a steel counter, blood darkening a towel beneath him. Her own hands steady despite the tremor in his abdomen.

"Eight stitches," she heard herself say in the fractured memory. "If you tear them, the next set goes in without anesthetic."

His mouth had curved. "You threaten beautifully."

The image vanished.

Sloane tightened her grip on the knife before it could slip. "The rib wound. I stitched it."

Lucian's expression changed by half a degree. On him, that counted as a collapse.

"Yes."

"What happened?"

"You said this was a memory test, not an interrogation."

"Everything is an interrogation when three months of a person's life have been cut out."

He did not answer. His silence was too practiced to be empty.

Sloane set the knife on the case. The recording device waited beside it, its matte screen displaying three blank biometric fields. A thin copper contact plate occupied one side, polished in the center by repeated use.

"Recorded instructions identify scars only someone close to you would know." She faced him again. "That proves proximity, not intimacy."

Lucian's jaw flexed. "What would prove intimacy to you?"

Her body supplied several deeply inconvenient suggestions.

Sloane ignored all of them. Mostly.

"I need permission to touch you."

His eyes sharpened, but he did not move closer. "Where?"

"The scars first."

"You have permission."

"And if that isn't enough..." She hated that her voice dropped. Hated more that he noticed. "Permission to kiss you. As a memory test."

A pulse moved in his throat.

"Yes," Lucian said. "You may touch me. You may kiss me. If you want to stop, you stop."

Clear. Controlled. Nothing taken.

That somehow made it more dangerous.

Sloane placed two fingers beneath his left ribs.

Heat met her touch. Lucian inhaled slowly, his abdomen tightening beneath her hand, but he remained still. The scar had a tiny raised knot at one end, an imperfect finish she recognized without knowing how.

Her thumb moved over it.

A flash slammed through her.

A cramped room. Her palm pressed over gauze. Lucian's blood between her fingers.

"Stay awake."

"Give me something worth waking for."

Her mouth against his temple. Not gentle. Terrified.

Sloane snatched her hand back.

Lucian caught himself before reaching for her. The aborted movement hurt more than contact would have.

"That one was mine," she said.

"The wound?"

"The repair." Her sarcasm arrived thin and sharp. "The bullet can submit separate evidence."

His almost-smile disappeared when she moved to his right shoulder.

"May I?"

"You already have permission."

"Consent can change."

His eyes held hers. "It hasn't."

She touched the puckered edge.

Another fragment opened.

Lucian lying facedown while she cleaned torn flesh. A ceramic cup beside her hand, chipped in one exact place and holding three bent suture needles. Her knee trapped against his hip to keep him still.

"Quarter turn," she had ordered.

He had rotated his shoulder with a curse.

"Such tender bedside care," he had muttered.

"You lived."

"That wasn't the part in doubt."

The fragment skipped.

His fingers around her wrist. His lips brushing the center of her palm. The small, devastating intimacy of a ritual repeated too many times to be new.

Sloane came back with her hand still on his shoulder.

Lucian watched her as though any wrong word might drive her out of reach. That restraint irritated her. It also made her want to test the exact limits of it.

"You knew how I treated injuries," she said.

"I knew how you treated mine."

"That sounded almost sentimental."

"It wasn't meant for you."

The strike landed cleanly. Past Sloane owned his tenderness. Present Sloane received only its echo.

Her fingertip began another circle on her wrist before she forced it flat against her thigh.

"One scar left."

Lucian's gaze flicked toward the crescent low on his abdomen. "That one may be less clinical."

"Useful warning."

She stepped closer.

The air between them changed before she touched him. His skin held the warmth of exertion, the faint scent of antiseptic from the cache mixing with smoke embedded in his clothes. Sloane laid her palm above the crescent.

Her fingers knew where to settle.

Not merely over the scar. Around it. Thumb angled toward his hip, little finger resting beneath his navel.

Lucian's breath stopped.

So did hers.

A bed with gray sheets. His body above hers, his scar fresh under her exploring hand. Her mouth against the same place, careful around the healing skin.

Another flash: Lucian fastening the back of her shoulder holster while she watched him in a cracked mirror.

Another: her bare foot pressing against his calf beneath a table, private contact during an argument neither of them intended to lose.

Another: Lucian asleep with one hand closed around the edge of her shirt.

Not a complete history. Nothing mercifully organized. Just proof in sharp, physical pieces.

She had not merely slept with him.

She had known him in the defenseless hours.

Sloane lifted her gaze. "It was you."

His face went still.

"The man I loved." The words felt stolen from her own mouth. "You were him."

Lucian's composure thinned, revealing something fierce beneath. "Yes."

No explanation. No plea for recognition. Just the truth, placed where she could either take it or cut herself on it.

Anger rose alongside desire.

She hated him for remembering what her own mind had abandoned. Hated the woman she had been for leaving this version of herself to stand half-naked in a trap with a man whose body felt more familiar than her memories.

"May I kiss you?" she asked.

His voice roughened. "Yes."

Sloane gripped the back of his neck and pulled his mouth to hers.

Recognition hit before softness could.

Lucian kissed her as if he knew exactly how much pressure she would tolerate before resisting. She resisted anyway. Her teeth caught his lower lip; his hand closed around her waist but did not drag her closer until she leaned into him by choice.

Then restraint burned down to its frame.

His mouth slanted over hers, hot and furious. Sloane's palm flattened against his abdomen, finding the crescent scar without looking. His fingers spread along her back, stopping above the knife sheath as if some older habit had mapped every dangerous place on her body.

A memory tore loose.

Her palm over a copper plate.

Lucian's hand beneath hers.

Index finger. Ring finger. Thumb.

A sequence tapped against metal while his mouth moved at her throat.

Sloane broke the kiss.

They stood breathing inches apart. Lucian's hand remained at her waist, waiting rather than claiming.

"The device," she said.

His gaze dropped briefly to her mouth. "Of course."

"Try to contain your heartbreak."

"It has survived worse."

She stepped away before her body could vote against the decision. The device's screen still showed the three biometric fields.

Sloane pressed her palm to the copper plate.

Nothing happened.

Her pulse thudded against the contact. The retrieved fragment had not been a code she could recite. It lived lower than language, buried in pressure and timing.

She traced a small circle against her left wrist.

Then she placed her hand down again.

Index finger. Ring finger. Thumb.

Pause.

The heel of her palm pressed twice. Her middle finger slid along a shallow groove invisible until her skin found it. She finished by lifting every finger except the little one.

The screen flashed green.

A hidden panel opened along the device's edge, exposing a miniature speaker and a second playback bar.

Lucian reached for his shirt, thought better of the shredded fabric, and took up the rifle instead.

Sloane pressed play.

Her own forgotten voice filled the cache.

"If scar authentication succeeds, then physical recognition remains functional. This confirms Lucian Vey's identity and validates the procedural lock."

Sloane's stomach twisted. The voice sounded calm enough to discuss ammunition weights.

The recording continued.

"Lucian, do not trust my restored self."

Silence followed, only a second long but deliberately preserved.

Sloane looked at him. "You knew that was coming?"

"No."

His answer came too quickly to be comfortable, but the surprise in his face looked real.

On the recording, Sloane spoke again. "Recognition is not recovery. Affection is not proof. If she remembers you, that may be the first indication that the wrong process has begun."

The playback stuttered.

Static scraped from the speaker.

"What wrong process?" Sloane demanded, as though the machine might be intimidated into cooperation. "Restored how? Wrong according to whom?"

Lucian angled his head toward the warehouse beyond the cache.

Sloane heard it then: a tiny click from somewhere past the hanging strip curtains.

She reached for her knife.

Lucian moved toward the doorway, rifle raised. Clear plastic strips shifted in the disturbed air beyond him, briefly bending the cache light into warped columns.

Sloane's finger circled her wrist again.

Something felt off. Not the silence. The geometry.

"Lucian, move."

A thin red fracture appeared across one clear sheet of warehouse strip-curtain plastic.

Lucian saw the targeting shimmer.

His left hand snapped to the rifle's focus ring and turned it exactly one quarter rotation away from clarity. The gesture was precise, furious, as if obedience itself offended him.

Then he hit Sloane hard enough to throw them both sideways.

The shot cracked through the warehouse.

Pain burned across Sloane's upper arm. She struck the floor, rolled, and came up with blood running to her elbow.

The round punched through a control line behind them.

Compressed fluid erupted in a violent silver stream, lashing the steel cases and turning the floor slick. An alarm began hammering from deeper in the warehouse. The cache door shuddered as pressure in the damaged line drove a locking arm halfway down.

Lucian seized the device.

A second shot struck the case beside his hand, blasting the device from his grip.

"Rook," he said.

"Impressive deduction."

"Can you move?"

Sloane clamped her hand over the graze. "Can you ask a less insulting question?"

The locking arm dropped another foot. Fluid sprayed across the open cases, knocking files and weapon parts onto the floor.

They could not stay. They could not retrieve everything. The hard case containing the recordings sat beyond the stream, already trapped behind the descending arm.

Lucian started toward it.

"No." Sloane grabbed his belt and hauled him back as another round tore through the doorway. "Deeper into the warehouse. Now."

His eyes went to her bleeding arm.

She bared her teeth. "Try carrying me and the shooter gets an easier target."

That ended the argument.

Lucian moved low, covering the open warehouse while Sloane snatched the fallen device from beneath the edge of a steel cabinet. Its casing had cracked, but the playback bar still crawled forward.

They ran between industrial presses as rounds chewed sparks from the concrete behind them. Sloane's arm throbbed with every step. She traced one tight circle against her wrist, then forced her bloody hand to grip Lucian's shoulder and dragged him behind a concrete press.

The device skidded from her fingers and landed faceup in the dust.

Bleeding from Cassian Rook's grazing shot, Sloane Mercer drags Lucian Vey behind a concrete press as the second recording continues from the fallen device: "If Sloane Mercer starts the sequence, Lucian Vey has exactly one chance to decide which version of Sloane Mercer survives."
