The data relay station's coolant pump hummed beneath Sloane Mercer's boots like a massive mechanical heart—a heartbeat that carried her sister's location.

The ledger device pulsed in her jacket pocket, its cracked screen casting a wireframe map of the station's internal layout against the dim corridor walls. Lucian moved beside her, a shadow against the emergency lighting, his rifle angled low and ready.

"Maintenance entrance," she breathed, pointing to a recessed door marked with faded chemical warnings. "He brought her through here. Fresh scratches on the lock plate."

Lucian studied the marks without touching. "He wanted it to look like a rush job. Left the gate unlocked so we'd follow the trail."

"Yeah." Sloane ran her fingers along the frame, feeling for the secondary alarm. "He could have killed her the second we hit the transmit on the ledge. He didn't. He wants us inside."

"Then we don't disappoint him."

The door yielded to her override in six seconds. They slipped into the maintenance corridor, the air thick with ozone and stale heat from the server banks.

Sloane counted her steps in time with the coolant pump. Twenty-three paces to the first junction. Turn left. Forty-one paces to the security panel.

She crouched in front of it, pulling the cover free. Wires and circuit boards stared back at her, and her hands moved with a fluency that settled into muscle memory with sickening ease. Rook had taught her this system. Every line of code. Every workaround. Every trick to blind a surveillance network without triggering the lockdown.

"Three cameras, two motion sensors in the south corridor," she murmured. "I can cut them for seven minutes."

"That's all we need."

Her fingers danced across the panel. The lights on the security board flickered from red to yellow to dead.

Corridor clear.

She moved toward the holding room at the end of the hall, and she could hear it now—a faint sound on the other side of the steel door. Breathing. Movement. Someone alive and waiting.

"Mara."

The lock clicked beneath her touch.

Sloane pushed the door open.

Mara was chained to a utility chair in the center of the room, wrists cuffed above her head, a strip of tape across her mouth. Her eyes were open. Furious. Unbroken.

Sloane crossed the room in three strides and tore the tape off.

"Hello, Sloane." Mara's voice was hoarse but steady. "Took you long enough."

"You could have called ahead."

"I was a little tied up."

Sloane worked the lock on the cuffs, her fingers steady despite the tremor that ran through her rib cage. The cuffs dropped. Mara rubbed her wrists, and Sloane saw the circular marks burned into her sister's skin. Raw. Red. Identical to the mark on her own left wrist.

"He used the same restraints," Sloane said flatly.

"He likes consistency."

They shared a look. Years of history. Secrets. A plan that had required Sloane to forget her own sister was alive.

"I'm sorry," Sloane said.

"Don't be." Mara stood, shaking out her arms. "I knew what I signed up for. The ledger?"

"Uploaded to three major networks. But I kept the source data. I need to send it to the agencies."

Mara's eyes flicked to the door. "He is two floors up. He left the roof access open. He wants you to follow."

"I know."

"He has the high ground and a clear shot at the service elevator exit."

"Then we don't take the elevator."

"He will expect that too."

Sloane pulled her sister into a hard, brief embrace. "Then we change the plan on the way."

The control room was empty when they reached it. Server racks hummed along the walls, and a single console glowed in the center of the room. Sloane sat down and connected the ledger device.

Data scrolled across the screen.

Seven contracts. Seven names. Seven falsified intelligence files with Cassian Rook's private network stamp embedded in the metadata.

She had seen it all before, but reading it again turned her blood to ice.

"He burned them all," she said quietly. "Witnesses. Rivals. People who knew about his weapons network. He framed them as threats so I would pull the trigger."

Lucian stood at her back. "You didn't know."

"I built the system. I made it clean and quiet and efficient. He just used it."

"You built it to trap him."

"Does that change what I did?"

"No." His voice was quiet. "But it explains why you did it. And that matters."

She selected the send command.

The upload bar crawled across the screen. One megabyte. Ten. Fifty.

"Three major networks," she recited. "Two federal agencies. The Justice Department's anonymous tip line. The evidence is in the wild."

"Then he is finished."

"He is cornered." Sloane closed her eyes. "That is not the same thing."

When she opened them, the upload was complete. The truth was out. Her memory was back. Her sister was alive.

And she had never felt more exposed.

Distant sirens filtered through the station's concrete walls. Authorities converging on the broadcast signal.

Lucian's hand found hers.

Not a grab. Not a claim. A question.

Her fingers laced through his before she could stop them.

"Whatever happens next," he said, his voice low enough that only she could hear, "I will stay with you no matter what."

"You don't know what comes next."

"I know the contract. I know the clause. I know that I would rather break it than lose you again."

Her throat tightened. "You cannot break the contract, Lucian. You accepted it with full knowledge."

"Then we find another way." He lifted her hand and pressed his lips to her knuckles. A ghost of a kiss. A promise that defied conditioning. "I loved you when you could not remember my name. I love you now that you know every dark corner of it. I am not leaving."

The building's PA system crackled to life.

Cassian Rook's voice filled the room, smooth and unhurried, the tone of a man who had already calculated every piece on the board.

"Congratulations, Sloane. You have successfully exposed me. The authorities will be here in minutes. But before they arrive, I want you to consider one thing."

A pause. The click of a rifle bolt being chambered.

"The sunrise clause is still in effect, Vey. Let's see whose loyalty is stronger—the contract's, or the woman who made you break it."

The transmission cut out.

Sloane's blood turned to ice.

Mara appeared in the control room doorway, already holding a comm link she had pulled from a security station. "He is on the main rooftop. Original location. He left a trail. He wants you to follow."

"I know."

"The network is collapsing. His men are scattering. But he still has the high ground and a clear shot at the roof access."

Lucian checked his rifle. "Then we do not take the roof access."

"He will expect that." Sloane took the comm link from Mara. "We take the service elevator straight to the top. He expects us to run from his shot. We walk directly into it."

Mara grabbed her arm. "Sloane."

"I built this trap. I will close it."

"You cannot save me and kill him and survive the arrest that is waiting downstairs."

"Watch me."

Sloane pressed the comm link into Mara's palm. "Stay here. Lock the door behind us. Do not open it for anyone except me or Lucian."

"And if neither of you come back?"

"Then you take the transmit logs, walk out the front door with your hands up, and tell the police everything Rook made us do."

Mara's jaw tightened. Her eyes were dry. "I will not bury you, Sloane."

"Good. Because I am not planning to die."

Sloane turned to Lucian. He met her gaze without flinching.

"Service elevator," she said.

"I know."

"He will be waiting."

"Let him."

She took his hand one last time. Held it. Tight.

Then they walked out together.

Sloane and Lucian stepped onto the service elevator. The doors slid closed, sealing them in a metal box that smelled of rust and electricity.

The floors ticked past.

Three.

Two.

One.

The elevator stopped. The doors began to open onto the rooftop.

And Cassian Rook's voice echoed through the building's speaker system one last time, carrying from the roof to the lobby and every floor in between.

"The sunrise clause is still in effect, Vey. Let's see whose loyalty is stronger—the contract's, or the woman who made you break it."
