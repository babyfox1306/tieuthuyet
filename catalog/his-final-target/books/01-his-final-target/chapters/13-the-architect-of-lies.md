The masked figure lunged, a dark echo of Lucian Vey, but it was Cassian Rook, his hand reaching for the rifle, and for the ledger now scrolling in stark green across the scope.

Sloane Mercer dropped her weight and hit the concrete hard, the barrel jerking clear of Rook's fingers. The scope stayed pressed against her eye socket, the green text still flowing, names she already knew now carrying authorization codes she had never seen before. Elena Voss. Dmitri Korr. Yasmin Cole. Every entry carried the same secondary signature. Rook's signature.

Lucian collided with the handler mid-stride and drove him into the roof access structure. The metal door groaned under the impact. A toolbox skidded across the asphalt, its contents scattering.

"You can't stop it," Rook snarled. "The network is already cleaning up."

Lucian answered with his fist.

Sloane forced herself to keep reading. The ledger had not stopped decrypting. Each second brought a new layer into focus. The doctored threat assessments. The falsified intelligence. The transfer logs linked to weapons accounts she had never seen before. Seven contracts. Seven murders she had authorized because Rook had rewritten the files.

But the ledger also showed the infrastructure beneath those lies. Private shipments routed through abandoned warehouses. Payments laundered through shell companies. A network that had been running for years before she ever pulled a trigger.

And at the center, Cassian Rook.

She had been his weapon.

The guilt did not lift. It settled deeper, a weight that would never leave her bones.

Lucian and Rook broke apart, circling each other. Rook's hand darted inside his jacket. Lucian was faster. He caught Rook's wrist before the weapon cleared the holster and twisted, forcing the handler to his knees.

"Broadcast it," Lucian said. "Now."

"There's no transmitter," Rook spat.

Lucian drove his knee into Rook's ribs. "He's lying."

Sloane knew both of them could be telling the truth. The scope did not have a broadcast function. But her past self had built this entire plan around this moment, and that woman had always left a backup.

She traced the circle on her wrist.

The scope's interface flickered. A new icon appeared in the corner of the display. A transmission pulse.

The broadcaster was hidden inside the safety selector, wired to activate only when the conditioned gesture occurred during the ledger's decryption.

Sloane pressed the switch.

The green text bled into red. Every contract. Every weapons shipment. Every falsified file. The staging plan for Mara's death. The memory erasure protocol. The conditioning sequence that Rook had designed to turn her into a puppet.

The data streamed outward into the pre-dawn air, reaching an audience she could not see and could not control.

Rook screamed.

He broke free of Lucian's grip and lunged toward the rifle, but Lucian recovered and drove him into the concrete. The mask slipped. Rook's face was bare, twisted with fury.

"You've just killed yourself," he hissed.

"Maybe."

Sloane kept her finger on the broadcast switch. The red text continued to flow. She watched the final lines assemble—the evidence that Rook had used her system to eliminate seven innocent witnesses, that she had discovered the truth and built a trap, that she had staged her sister's death and erased her own memory to survive interrogation.

She had ordered Lucian to kill her if Rook ever restored the obedient identity he had created.

The broadcast completed. The icon dimmed.

Rook was still pinned, but he was laughing.

"You think you've won? The network is already watching this transmission. They know exactly who sent it. They know where you are."

"Let them come."

Sloane lowered the rifle. Her arms were shaking.

Lucian pressed his weight into Rook's back, keeping him flat against the concrete. "We need him alive for the evidence."

"We have the evidence. It's in the air."

Rook laughed again. "You don't understand. The network doesn't forgive exposure. They'll hunt you. They'll hunt your sister. They'll hunt everyone who ever touched this system."

"I know."

Sloane met his eyes.

"I've been a target my whole life. At least now I know why."

Lucian released Rook with a final shove. The handler scrambled to his feet, his hand reaching inside his jacket again, but Lucian was already positioned between him and Sloane. The weapon never cleared.

Rook backed toward the roof access door. His eyes found the rifle, then Sloane's face.

"You will pay for this, Sloane Mercer."

He was through the door before she could answer.

The door slammed shut. His footsteps faded down the stairwell, swallowed by the building's silence.

Sloane let the rifle hang by its sling. The scope was dark. The transmission was complete.

The rooftop was empty except for her and Lucian.

Lucian turned to face her. The cut above his eye had stopped bleeding, but the bruise was darkening. His shirt was torn at the shoulder, and there was blood on his knuckles that was not his own.

"You did it," he said.

"We did it."

He stepped closer. Close enough for her to smell the sweat and metal on his skin.

"You understand now. The full plan."

"Yes."

"And Mara?"

Sloane closed her eyes. The rhythm was still there, burned into her muscle memory. The coded signal her sister had sent through the scope.

"She was part of it. She consented. The signal was the key to the ledger."

Lucian's jaw tightened. "She trusted you enough to let you forget."

"And now I remember."

Sloane opened her eyes and looked at the rising sun. The light spread across the eastern structures, illuminating the scratches on the rifle's magazine housing.

"I remember why I placed the sunrise clause."

Lucian did not speak.

"I was afraid, Lucian. Afraid that if Rook broke me, I would become someone I didn't want to be. I wanted someone to stop me."

"And now?"

She turned to face him.

"Now I have to decide who I want to be."

Lucian reached out. His hand hovered an inch from her arm, not quite touching. His eyes stayed locked on hers, dark and unreadable. But something had shifted in them. Something that looked almost like relief.

"The contract is still binding," he said.

"I know."

"I am still bound to the sunrise clause."

Sloane waited. Her heart was beating too fast.

Lucian lowered his hand to his side. He did not complete the gesture. He did not reach for the rifle.

"I have spent eight years following your orders," he said. "I have killed when you told me to kill. I have protected when you told me to protect. And I have waited for the real you to come back."

The words hung in the air.

"Not the woman who woke up on this rooftop," he said. "Not the puppet Rook tried to recreate. The woman who built this entire plan. The woman who loved her sister enough to erase herself."

Sloane could feel her throat tightening.

"She's looking at me now," Lucian said. "And I cannot kill her."

He did not say he was breaking the contract. He did not promise anything. But the choice was there, written in the set of his shoulders and the steadiness of his gaze.

The sunrise clause was still between them.

But Lucian Vey had not reached for the trigger.

And Sloane Mercer was still standing.

The sun climbed higher, casting long shadows across the rooftop. Somewhere below, the city was waking. The network was preparing its response. Mara was out there, hidden, waiting.

Sloane looked at the rifle in her hands. The machine that had unlocked the truth.

She had exposed Rook. She had broadcast the ledger. She had understood.

But the guilt was still there.

It would always be there.

She looked at Lucian, at the man who had been her lover, her weapon, her safeguard.

"I don't know what happens next," she said.

"Neither do I."

"But we're still here."

Lucian nodded.

And for a long moment, neither of them moved.

The rooftop was quiet. The data was streaming through the air. The network was hunting.

But Sloane Mercer was finally awake.

And she was not alone.
