"Don't take another step." Sloane Mercer seized the back of Lucian Vey's jacket as Mara Mercer's recorded scream vibrated through the steel beneath their feet.

Lucian froze with one boot suspended over the warehouse threshold.

The scream came again, but this time Mara cried Sloane's name from somewhere behind them.

Every nerve in Sloane's body demanded she turn. She did not. River Road had brought them to an abandoned freight warehouse, not a miracle, and grief was exactly the kind of weapon that worked best when the target volunteered to bleed.

"That isn't live," Sloane said.

Lucian lowered his boot without putting weight on it. "You decided that quickly."

"Mara inhaled twice."

Another plea shuddered through the corrugated walls. "Sloane, please. It hurts. Please, don't let him—"

The words ended in a burst of static.

Sloane's nails bit into Lucian's jacket. The voice had Mara's pitch, Mara's clipped consonants, even the slight rasp that arrived when she was frightened. But the breath before please had repeated with identical timing.

An old fragment stitched to another old fragment.

Someone had cut Mara into pieces and made the building speak with her mouth.

Sloane released Lucian and crouched near the threshold. Dust lay across the concrete except for a narrow strip polished by recent friction. Above it, a nearly invisible wire ran into a drilled hole in the doorframe.

"Trip sensor?" Lucian asked.

"Too obvious."

"Sometimes obvious kills efficiently."

"Sometimes obvious makes an armed man watch the wire while he steps on something else."

She swept her gaze over the floor. No fresh footprints. No disturbed grit. A loading chain hung motionless six feet inside, its hook hovering over a square of bare concrete that looked almost inviting.

Sloane traced a small circle against the inside of her left wrist.

The sting from abused skin should have annoyed her. Instead, her attention snapped toward the right-hand wall, where waist-high dents marked the metal siding at evenly spaced intervals.

Her mind supplied angles before memory supplied reasons.

"Stay behind me."

Lucian's expression hardened. "No."

"That wasn't an emotional request."

"You think this place was designed for you."

"It was designed by someone who expects me to think like a firearms investigator." She pointed toward the dents. "Three shooting positions. One near the office platform, one behind the forklift cage, one above the loading bay. Overlapping lanes cover the central aisle."

His gaze followed her finger, measuring.

She hated that she recognized the exact moment he agreed with her. His shoulders settled. His attention narrowed. It was the posture of a man accepting an order he disliked.

"Left route has cover," he said.

"Left route has funnels. We go right."

"The right side is exposed."

"Only if the shooters are where we're meant to find them."

Mara screamed again.

The sound seemed to race along the wall, passing from the loading bay to the dark office suspended above the warehouse floor. Lucian turned toward it by instinct.

Sloane caught his sleeve. "The voice moved faster than a person could."

"Speaker array."

"Not an array."

She took one careful step inside, placing her boot against a rusted seam in the concrete. The floor gave no resistance. She shifted her weight, then gestured for Lucian to follow in her exact print.

He did.

That obedience unsettled her more than an argument would have.

They moved beside stacked sheets of corrugated steel, each panel tall enough to hide a shooter. Sloane paused at every gap and checked reflections in the scratched metal before crossing. The warped surfaces broke Lucian into distorted pieces: scarred eyebrow, dark shoulder, steady hands.

Mara's voice whispered, "You promised."

Sloane's stomach clenched.

That phrase was real. Mara had said it years ago over a sink full of chipped blue dishes, after Sloane had missed a birthday dinner for work. Or perhaps her damaged memory was simply desperate enough to manufacture a room around three stolen words.

"Do you recognize the recording?" Lucian asked quietly.

"Parts of it."

"From when?"

"If the missing months came labeled, this would be a much shorter night."

A metallic click sounded above them.

Sloane looked up.

A pallet loaded with empty drums dropped from the suspended rack.

Lucian hit her before the pallet did.

His body drove hers behind a low engine block, one arm locking around her shoulders as wood and steel crashed across the aisle. The impact punched dust into the air. A drum struck the engine block hard enough to ring it, then rolled past with a hollow roar.

Lucian covered her completely.

For one breath, Sloane knew only his weight, the heat of him through layers of clothing, and the rigid line of his arm protecting her head. He smelled faintly of gun solvent and the bitter citrus soap from the weapons cache.

Too familiar.

That was the danger. Not the trap. Not Mara's stolen voice.

The dangerous thing was the way Sloane's body recognized shelter in a man her mind still classified as a threat.

"Are you hit?" he asked.

"No."

His face was inches from hers. Dust had caught in the scar above his left eyebrow, whitening the thin ridge. His eyes searched hers with a tenderness so controlled it felt more frightening than panic.

"Sloane."

"Still no."

He began to lift himself.

Her hand remained against the center of his back.

One second too long.

Maybe two.

Beneath his jacket, her palm found a raised irregularity beside his spine. Not the shape of a holster or armor seam. Scar tissue.

Lucian went very still.

Sloane removed her hand. "Move."

He rolled away without comment.

Good. Comments would have required answers, and she currently had enough questions to stock a courtroom.

She rose into a crouch and studied the collapsed pallet. A braided cable trailed from its release latch to the loading chain near the entrance.

"First trigger was the wire," Lucian said.

"No. The wire was the safety."

His gaze shifted to her.

"The pallet couldn't fall while the door was closed," she continued. "Opening it pulled the safety pin. The actual release was somewhere along our route."

"You didn't touch anything."

"Then the release was timed or remote."

Mara sobbed from the wall to their right. The sound was louder there, but it carried no direction. It pressed through the steel itself, turning the whole warehouse into a throat.

Sloane followed the vibration.

At the nearest support column, a fresh bolt gleamed beneath a smear of old grease. She knelt and reached behind the steel flange, finding a puck-shaped device pressed tightly against it.

Coin-sized. Wired to a compact receiver. A vibration transducer.

She did not remove it.

Instead, she pressed two fingertips against the corrugated wall as Mara's voice pleaded again. The steel trembled under her skin, making each word seem to crawl away from her hand.

"One transducer," Sloane said. "The wall is the speaker. Whoever built the file wanted the cries to move whenever the listener moved."

Lucian examined the receiver without touching it. "Can you trace the transmission?"

"Not from this. It could be playing a stored loop."

As if insulted, the device clicked.

Mara's voice changed.

"River Road. Bay four. Come alone."

The sentence repeated exactly as it had in the message that lured them there.

Sloane stared at the tiny machine. "There. Different noise floor between River and Road. The words came from separate recordings."

"How many sources?"

"At least four. Probably more." She swallowed against the sourness in her throat. "Someone kept enough of her voice to build commands."

Lucian's jaw tightened.

"Don't do that," Sloane said.

"Do what?"

"Look guilty without contributing useful information."

His eyes met hers. "Guilt and usefulness aren't mutually exclusive."

"Charming. Put it on a pillow."

A sharp buzz came from beneath the transducer. Lucian reached for her.

Sloane was already moving.

She ripped the receiver wire free, killing the vibration an instant before a spark snapped at the exposed contact. Silence slammed down. No explosion followed.

"Decoy circuit," she said.

"Or you interrupted the signal."

"Optimism looks unnatural on you."

They continued deeper into the warehouse.

Bay four sat behind a row of empty storage cages. A portable work lamp illuminated the number painted on the floor, though no cable visibly fed it. Directly beneath the light rested a black impact-resistant case.

Too neat. Too centered. Too much like an answer.

Lucian stopped. "We leave it."

"No."

"The case is bait."

"Obviously."

"And that improves it?"

"It improves our expectations."

Sloane studied the floor around it. Dust had been swept into broad arcs, hiding recent disturbances. The cages on either side created a narrow corridor. High above, two old ventilation housings offered clean concealment for cameras or weapons.

She crouched and sighted along the concrete.

A faint ridge crossed the path four feet from the case. Not a wire. A rectangular plate had been set beneath a thin skin of dust-colored resin.

Lucian moved to step around her.

Sloane's fingers circled her left wrist.

Her other hand shot out and struck his thigh.

"Back."

His boot halted a fraction above the plate.

For a moment, neither breathed.

Then Lucian set his foot down behind him. "You couldn't see it from there."

She stared at the faint rectangle.

He was right. From her angle, the resin edge had been hidden by the shadow of his boot. Yet her hand had moved before she consciously understood the danger.

A sequence flickered through her muscles: circle, brace, interrupt, redirect.

No image followed. No recovered room. Just a sickening sense that her body had completed the opening note of a song her mind had chosen to erase.

"How did you know?" he asked.

"Excellent question."

"Sloane."

"Still excellent the second time."

She crawled closer without touching the plate. At its edge, a hair-thin pressure tube ran beneath the resin and disappeared toward the case. Pneumatic trigger. Weight compressed the plate, air moved through the line, and something activated.

Not necessarily an explosive. That uncertainty was part of the design.

She pulled a narrow probe from the compact tool roll in her coat. The tools had been waiting in the hidden cache with the list of seven names, selected by a version of Sloane who apparently enjoyed leaving gifts for her amnesiac future self.

Lucian watched her hands. "You know this mechanism."

"I know firearms discharge analysis. Pressure systems show up in improvised trigger assemblies."

"That isn't what I meant."

She slid the probe beneath the plate. "Then ask a factual question."

"Why did you stop me before you saw the line?"

Her wrist burned.

"I don't know."

The answer tasted like surrender, so she converted it into action.

The pressure tube joined a brass valve beneath the plate. Sloane wedged the probe against the valve stem, then used a clamp from the kit to hold it in the neutral position. If the plate depressed now, the trapped air would vent harmlessly through the open port.

She tested it with the probe.

A soft hiss escaped. Nothing else moved.

"Trigger disabled," she said.

Lucian glanced at the overhead housings. "Assuming there isn't a secondary."

"There is always a secondary."

She avoided the obvious path and edged along the cage wall. A narrow inspection mirror revealed a filament running from the case handle to a contact screwed into the floor.

"Tamper circuit."

Lucian crouched beside her, close without touching. "Can you break it?"

"I can disappoint it."

The filament maintained tension between two magnets. Lifting the case would separate them. Sloane slid a steel washer between the contacts, preserving the magnetic bridge, then cut the line on the case side.

Lucian exhaled through his nose. "You have done that before."

"So have plenty of criminals."

"You say that as though the categories don't overlap."

Sloane looked at him. "Seven innocent targets suggest they overlap just fine."

He accepted the hit without defending her from it. She almost resented him for that.

Almost.

Together, they backed the case out of the lit square using a hooked rod. Nothing fired. No pallet fell. No voice emerged from the walls.

Only when the case rested behind the engine block did Sloane examine it closely.

The shell bore deep impact scuffs and one crescent-shaped burn. Across the lid, silver paint marker formed block letters.

LUCIAN VEY OPENS THIS.

SLOANE MERCER DOES NOT.

Her handwriting.

She knew the hard downward cut of the L, the compressed spacing, the way the last letter of her surname tilted toward whatever came next. She traced a circle against her wrist as she read it again.

Lucian's gaze dropped to the motion.

"Don't," she said.

"I didn't say anything."

"You were about to."

"I was about to ask whether you trust your own instructions."

"No. But whoever set this trap expected both of us. The restrictions matter."

"That conclusion could get you killed."

"Opening it could get you killed."

His mouth curved without humor. "Concern?"

"Inventory management."

He rested one hand on the latch. "Step back."

Sloane did not.

"Sloane."

"If it contains a directional charge, distance at this range is decorative. Open it slowly."

He looked at her for a long second, then turned the rifle's focus ring one quarter rotation away from clarity. The gesture was precise, irritated, and strangely intimate, as if she had just watched him swallow an argument he had made many times before.

The first latch released.

Then the second.

Lucian Vey opens the case and finds a bloodstained shirt cut open along the spine, while a device beneath it activates in Sloane Mercer's recorded voice: "Make her look at your scars, Lucian Vey. If Sloane Mercer still cannot remember what she did to you, play the second file."
