# Chapter 7: Ancestral Record

"You're not supposed to have this," Elias van Doren mutters, clutching the maternity record with trembling hands.

The paper is brittle, edges jagged where it’s been torn from its parent file—an orphan sheet, the hospital logo ghosted half-off the header. He glances over his shoulder, lips taut with strain, as if expecting Sofia to pry it from his grasp or the marble walls to swallow him whole. The hush of the archive weighs heavy on them both, thick and reverberant.

Sofia steadies her tone, pressing warmth into every syllable. "Then it's fortunate you do, Elias. The system can only correct what’s been seen, not what’s been buried." She angles her body to block the narrow view from the frosted window. There’s a ripple of air—Pierre Leclerc outside, his outline shifting against the wavering glass.

Elias doesn't seem to hear her. His eyes jump from line to line on the record, pupils wide, lips moving without sound. "This... It’s not indexed. Tomasz said the master ledger stops at C003." He holds the page out just enough for Sofia to see. "But this is a fifth entry. C005. The ink’s faded here—see?"

There is a flash of her own name. Not the alias she’d assumed to protect the tribunal’s faith, not the version that threads so neatly through the official protocols, but her full birth entry: Bellini, Sofia, child of Petrescu, Nadia.

If Elias notices the involuntary clench of her jaw, he says nothing. He’s too busy tracing the handwritten annotation at the margin—a parenthesis, a date, and a single, slanted signature not her own.

Sofia breathes. She counts backwards from seven. The art is in moving without panic: real power means never letting them see you cornered.

"Where did you find it?" Her voice is silk. "Tomasz swore the archive was clear up to the last review."

Elias’s thumb circles the uneven edge. "Wedged behind the old witness registry. I almost missed it. Someone wanted it out of sight."

He looks up—a challenge, uncharacteristically direct. "You’re the last surviving witness of the summit, Sofia. Why does this matter—now?"

Sofia gives him a smile, so gentle it could cut glass. "Because the dead can’t speak for us." She lets the words hang, notes the flinch in his gaze. "You know how rumors blossom here. One unfiled record, and the tribunal will imagine a conspiracy."

"And isn’t there?" His words tumble out, raw, the stutter nearly breaking through. "Six dead. Altered statements. Now this."

A knock on the glass. Pierre Leclerc, features smeared by the distortion, beckons Elias with a clipped wave—impatient, demanding.

Sofia tucks her hair behind her ear, veiling the flicker of calculation. "Don’t let Pierre see. He’s looking for something to break us." She steps closer, so their bodies nearly touch, the maternity record a live wire between their hands.

Elias stares at her, defiance and fear colliding in his eyes. "Sofia... is it true?" He gestures at the page, voice trembling. "Nadia Petrescu—"

She interrupts, breath low and laced with practiced empathy. "My connection to Dr. Petrescu is academic. She trained half the staff who survived the summit. If this is—"

"Don’t." Elias’s grip tightens. "Don’t reframe me. I need—"

Her hand covers his, palm cool, pressing him gently back. "You need the tribunal to listen. If you shatter the narrative now, they'll fix nothing. They'll just—"

"Erase us." It’s almost a whisper. His knuckles blanch on the page.

In that moment, Sofia reads the map of his desperation. She could dissolve him with one word—recast him as a paranoid, another victim of stress. Instead, she leans in, the brush of her arm a calculated comfort.

"If you want the system to be accountable, you need allies. Not a scandal," she murmurs.

Footsteps thunder in the corridor. The archive’s hush fractures as Pierre Leclerc’s shape looms in the doorway, expression sharp as flint.

"I see you two have found a private study. Mind if I join?" Pierre’s gaze darts to the maternity record, then back to Elias. "Did Tomasz finally give you access to the B shelf?"

Elias hesitates, but Sofia steps in, serene. "We were consolidating witness entries. Procedure is everything, Pierre." Her hand, resting on the folder, does not move.

Pierre saunters closer, eyes missing nothing. "That’s a rare form you have, Mr. van Doren. May I?" He extends a hand.

Sofia’s breath does not catch. She glides into intervention, fingers brushing Elias’s as she slides a different file—one she prepped for just this moment—across the table. The label is perfectly typed: C005, Personnel Correction Log. Inside, blank forms and innocuous error codes. Nothing that could threaten her.

Pierre snatches it up, rifling through the empty pages. "Routine corrections. Still, the system registers only three corrections since the last roll. Why does the physical stack go to five?"

Sofia lets a dimple show at the corner of her mouth. Innocence at a premium. "Paper jams and misfeeds. Tomasz hates to waste stock. The system discards duplicates, but the tray never lies."

Pierre snorts, only half appeased. His focus bores into Elias. "And you, Mr. van Doren? Find anything worth sharing?"

Elias flushes. His mouth opens—and nothing comes. The real maternity record is shielded by Sofia’s hand. Her thumb traces the tear, a silent message: Let me handle this.

"We’re still sorting," Sofia says smoothly, glancing at Pierre. "If there’s a discrepancy, I’ll report it to Chief Adjudicator Rasmus myself."

Pierre lingers, then tucks the decoy file under his arm. "See that you do. The tribunal’s patience is finite, even for its golden witnesses."

He sweeps out, boots echoing, the false clue already working its way into the official mill.

Elias sags, every tendon in his neck taut with withheld outrage. "You made that up," he hisses, voice trembling. "That’s not a real correction log."

"The system rewards anticipation," Sofia says, unblinking. "If they’re chasing paper, they’re not hunting people. You should thank me."

He doesn’t. Instead, he stares at the maternity record, the reality of her parentage settling like a bruise under his skin.

"You lied to his face," he says, awe and accusation mingling.

"I protected you," Sofia corrects, quietly.

The silence stretches, broken only by the faint whir of the archive’s cooling system—an artificial breeze, too clean to be comforting.

Elias’s voice softens, the edges of suspicion blunting under the weight of shared secrecy. "Did you know Nadia Petrescu? Before?"

Sofia lets her smile turn wistful—the perfect shade of mournful. "I knew her reputation. She was devoted to clarity, above all else." Her heart thrums a sharp, private rhythm. Don’t give him room to ask for more.

He’s not satisfied. Of course he isn’t. "The pattern fits, Sofia. Every death, every correction. It started with her research group. You’re linked to all of them."

She doesn’t flinch. "Guilt by association is convenient, Elias. But it’s only ever rumor until the jury decides." She steps closer, eyes on his—invitation and challenge. "Are you going to be the one to convict me?"

He shakes his head, but his hands say otherwise—holding the record too tightly, the edges bending under his pressure.

From the hall, the tribunal’s bell chimes. It’s the summons for recalibration—a session meant to soothe public panic, another chance for Rasmus to peddle control.

Sofia’s mind races. If the maternity record leaves this room, her defense is finished. She has to redirect the narrative, now.

"Elias," she says, voice low and urgent, "you have to trust that what you saw at the summit means more than any paperwork. The tribunal needs an anchor—someone who wasn’t swept away."

He shakes his head again. "I don’t want to be an anchor. I want the truth."

Truth. Such a childish craving.

She watches him crumble, resolve softening as uncertainty seeps in. She could shatter him for good, right now, but something in his gaze—a plea for mercy, not submission—makes her hesitate.

She chooses, instead, a softer knife.

She glances around, ensuring they are alone, then takes the maternity record from his hands—not stealing, just guiding, as though she’s simply rearranging the folders for his convenience. Her fingers linger atop his, cool and firm, a precise pressure.

Her thumb brushes the uneven edge where the paper was torn—an accidental caress as plausible as any act of comfort. She holds his gaze just a heartbeat too long, daring him to stop her, daring him to trust.

She speaks quietly, so only the two of them can hear. "Let me keep this safe until after the session. If you trust me, we’ll fix this together."

His chest stutters with breath. For a moment, the world narrows to the heat of her palm against his, the friction of skin on paper. The archive, the tribunal, the deaths—it all contracts to this narrow corridor of almost-touch.

He releases the record.

There is no victory in her heart, only calculation. She tucks the file into her portfolio, the trail of her fingerprints nowhere near the signature belonging to Nadia Petrescu.

In the corridor, the tribunal is gathering—Rasmus’s voice already leaking through the stone, Tomasz’s stammer echoing, Kappel’s nervous laugh. Pierre passes by the window, the decoy C005 file in his hand, the trap set.

A new notification pings on the archive terminal—a silent line, nearly missed.

Correction Log Received: C005. Discrepancy noted. Investigation pending.

Sofia hides a smile. Let them chase shadows. The real danger is always just out of sight.

Elias moves to the doorway, pausing and glancing back, doubt hollowing his cheeks. "What are you hiding from me, Sofia?"

She smooths her features into the perfect mask of reassurance. "I’m trying to keep you safe."

He doesn’t answer, just disappears into the corridor, tension in every line of his body.

Sofia places her hand flat against the cold marble, heart rattling in her chest. One mistake—one crack in the mask—and everything unravels.

She turns to gather her files. Out of the corner of her eye, she catches Elias in the reflection, frozen at the far end of the hall, staring at her—the maternity record visible, peeking from the portfolio in her grip.

And she knows, as surely as the bell tolling for the next session: if she cannot redirect suspicion, if even one stray glance lands on the jagged edge of truth, it will all come apart.

She feels his eyes burning through her—waiting for her to turn, to trip, to reveal.

The tribunal is only steps away.

And she does not yet know what he will do.
