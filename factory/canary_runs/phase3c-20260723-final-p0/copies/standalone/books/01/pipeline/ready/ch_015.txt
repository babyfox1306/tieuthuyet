# Chapter 15: Dawn's Blind Interval

The figure stepped out of the pre-dawn shadows, a rifle in one hand, Mara Mercer struggling in the other. He wore Lucian Vey's mask, but Sloane Mercer knew the cold certainty in his eyes: Cassian Rook. The muzzle pressed beneath Mara's jaw before Sloane could lift her weapon.

"Put it down," Rook said.

Mara's hands were bound behind her. Blood darkened one sleeve, but she stood under her own power, her stare fixed on Sloane with an intensity that said more than any reassurance could have.

Lucian had vanished behind the wrecked service housing thirty seconds ago.

Thirty seconds was either enough time to plan a miracle or die attempting one. Sloane had never budgeted for miracles.

Her fingers tightened on the rifle.

Rook dragged Mara closer to the parapet. "The blind interval has started. No cameras. No recordings. No permanent evidence unless someone carries it away."

The ledger device rested in Sloane's inner pocket, warm against her ribs.

Of course he wanted privacy. Men like Rook only admired documentation when it belonged to them.

Sloane lowered the rifle by two inches. "If you wanted it on the ground, you should have specified."

His eyes narrowed behind the mask.

Same matte-black shell. Same severe lines. Same narrow opening around the eyes. On Lucian, it had become an extension of brutal control. On Rook, it looked like stolen skin.

"Lower it completely."

Sloane traced a small circle against the inside of her left wrist with her thumb.

The motion pulled at something below conscious thought.

Grip. Breath. Sightline.

Rook saw it. His posture changed by a fraction, satisfaction sharpening the angle of his head.

There. That was what he had come to retrieve.

Not merely the ledger.

Her.

"Left hand on the fore-end," he ordered. "Right hand at the grip."

Her body responded before her anger could stop it.

The rifle rose.

Mara went still.

Sloane's pulse struck hard once, but her hands settled into perfect alignment. Stock seated to shoulder. Cheek against comb. Finger straight along the trigger guard.

A position built through repetition until obedience could wear the costume of instinct.

Rook eased the muzzle away from Mara's throat and pointed it at Sloane instead. "Acquire the center line."

The rooftop fractured into measured distances.

Nine feet to Rook. Half that to Mara. Wind negligible behind the parapet. The damaged crane blocked the eastern approach. The western edge dropped to a maintenance ledge she had noticed earlier through the scope, narrow enough to break ankles and wide enough to postpone death.

Her sight found Mara's sternum.

No.

The refusal existed clearly in her mind.

Her muscles did not care.

"Circle," Rook said.

Her thumb moved against her wrist.

Once.

Pressure tightened behind her eyes. The rifle's weight became familiar in a way that made her stomach turn. Not because she remembered every order, every file, every name.

Because her hands did.

Mara lifted her bound fingers behind her back.

Tap. Tap.

Pause.

Sloane's gaze stayed through the optic, but she saw the movement at the edge of the sight picture.

Circle.

The true sequence.

Not Rook's command. Mara's rhythm.

Her rhythm.

Rook pulled Mara half a step sideways. "Acquire."

Sloane adjusted automatically.

The crosshairs tracked her sister.

A scrape sounded behind the damaged ventilation bank.

Rook's eyes flicked toward it, then back. "Do not break position."

Sloane's finger slid to the trigger.

Somewhere beneath the conditioning, fury began counting.

One breath.

Two.

On three, a black rifle case sailed over the service housing and struck the rooftop hard enough to burst open. Metal magazines scattered across the concrete, clattering in six directions.

Rook turned his weapon.

Lucian rose from the opposite side.

He wore no mask.

Blood marked his temple, and his pistol was held low, not aimed at Rook but at the hydraulic fluid shining beneath the jammed crane track.

His gaze found Sloane's.

"Now, sweetheart."

The endearment hit a place conditioning had not reached.

Sloane fired.

Not at Mara.

The round tore through the rifle sling across Rook's shoulder. The weapon dropped, caught briefly at his elbow, and Mara drove her heel down on his instep.

Lucian shot the crane release.

The jammed platform lurched sideways with a shriek of tortured metal. Rook shoved Mara to the ground and threw himself clear as the platform carved across the roof between them.

Sloane moved before it stopped.

She closed the distance, turned the rifle horizontally, and slammed the stock into Rook's forearm. His hand opened. The masked man struck her shoulder and caught the barrel before she could recover.

They twisted around the weapon.

"You always needed an interruption," Rook said through clenched teeth. "That is why you never finished cleanly."

Sloane drove her knee toward his ribs.

He blocked it and wrenched the rifle hard enough to pull her off balance.

"Funny. Lucian never complained."

Rook's mask snapped toward her.

A petty strike, but effective.

She released the rifle rather than let him control her through it. He staggered back with the sudden shift in force, and she punched the lower edge of his mask into his mouth.

The shell split his lip.

Rook answered with an elbow that caught her high in the chest. Pain flared. Sloane dropped, rolled beneath his next swing, and saw Mara crawling toward the fallen rifle.

Rook saw her too.

He kicked the weapon beyond her reach and caught the back of her jacket.

"Mara!"

Sloane lunged, but gunfire chewed through the parapet between them.

The assault perimeter had found an angle again.

Lucian seized Sloane around the waist and dragged her behind the thick concrete base of the service housing. A second burst struck where her head had been.

She hit his chest hard enough to knock the breath from both of them.

His back met the wall. Her body pressed flush to his, one thigh trapped between his, his arm locked around her hips to keep them below the line of fire.

For one reckless second, danger narrowed to heat.

Lucian's breathing moved against her. His bare hand spanned her waist beneath the torn edge of her jacket, skin against skin, holding her with a care that felt indecent beside the gunfire.

His face hovered inches from hers.

Sloane knew the shape of his mouth too well for a woman missing three months of her life. Worse, her body knew how his breath changed when she leaned closer.

His gaze dropped to her lips, then returned to her eyes.

"Can you move?" he asked.

"That depends. Is this tactical, or have rooftop assaults become your preferred seduction technique?"

His grip tightened once. "You are making jokes. Good."

"That was not a joke."

The corner of his mouth almost moved.

A round struck the other side of the concrete. Dust sifted over his shoulder.

Sloane flinched, and her thumb traced a circle on her left wrist.

The conditioning surged again.

Weapon ready. Sight aligned. Await instruction.

Her muscles locked against him.

Lucian saw it.

"Sloane."

Rook's voice cut across the rooftop. "Return to firing position."

Her right hand moved toward Lucian's pistol.

He caught her wrist, but he did not force it away. His eyes stayed on hers while her fingers hovered above the grip at his hip.

"Tell me what you need," he said.

Facts. She needed facts.

The wall at her back was concrete. Lucian's palm was warm. Rook was nine yards away. Mara remained alive. The pistol had twelve rounds if Lucian had not reloaded.

And Lucian was waiting for her choice instead of issuing one.

"Hold me tighter," she said.

His stare sharpened. "Yes?"

"Yes. Do it."

His arm closed around her.

The pressure fixed every boundary of her body in the present. Chest to chest. Hip to hip. His heartbeat steady against her sternum, refusing the rhythm Rook had trained into her.

Sloane dragged in a breath.

Rook commanded, "Circle and acquire."

She pressed her thumb to her wrist but did not complete the motion.

Instead, she turned the circle into the first sweep of the true sequence. Tap against Lucian's chest. Tap again. Pause.

His eyes widened.

She finished the circle over the scar beneath his ribs.

A fragment flashed behind her eyes.

The rooftop plans. A red mark beneath the western parapet. A maintenance ledge outside the surveillance arc.

And a notation in her own handwriting: Blind interval creates seven seconds of manual lockout at west anchor.

The contact had broken the loop.

Not erased it. Not cured it.

Broken it long enough for choice.

Sloane pulled Lucian's pistol free, turned within the cage of his arm, and fired twice at the bolts securing the west safety cable.

The anchor plate snapped loose and swung down over the parapet.

Lucian followed her sightline. "That ledge?"

"Unless you have developed wings without informing me."

"Later."

His mouth came close enough that the word warmed her cheek. For one dangerous beat, she thought he might kiss her.

He did not.

The restraint burned more than contact would have.

Sloane pushed away first. "Cover Mara."

Lucian leaned out and aimed.

Before firing, he touched the focus ring on the rifle beside him and turned it exactly one quarter rotation away from clarity. A choice he hated. An order he would still obey.

He shot the concrete beside Rook.

Rook recoiled, pulling Mara behind the crane support as fragments sprayed across his mask. Sloane sprinted low toward the west edge.

Three seconds into the manual lockout.

She grabbed the loose cable and wrapped it once around her forearm.

Four.

Lucian moved backward toward her, firing measured shots that forced Rook to stay behind cover without risking Mara.

Five.

Rook shoved Mara against the crane base and came out with his rifle raised.

Sunlight broke over the neighboring structure.

It struck both masks at once: Rook's on his face, Lucian's lying near the open rifle case. Identical black shells caught the rising sun and flashed like mirrors, blurring former lover and current enemy for one disorienting instant.

Sloane's wrist made another circle.

This one belonged to her.

She sighted the crane's damaged hydraulic line and fired.

Fluid erupted across the track. The mechanism bucked, swinging the suspended platform between Rook and Mara. He jumped back as steel crashed down, blocking his shot but leaving him close enough to seize Mara again.

Six seconds.

Lucian reached Sloane. "You first."

"No."

His jaw hardened. "This is not the time."

"Correct. So stop wasting it."

Seven.

The anchor lock released with a violent metallic crack.

Sloane wrapped one arm around Lucian's waist, shoved the ledger deeper into her pocket, and stepped onto the parapet.

Rook emerged above the twisted platform, one hand locked around Mara's bound arms.

"Sloane!"

Mara met her eyes.

Alive. Captive. Furious.

Not abandoned.

Sloane tapped her wrist twice, paused, and traced the circle.

A promise in the only language the conditioning could not corrupt.

Then she pulled Lucian over the edge with her.

They plummeted together as Rook's shout followed them down. The safety cable snapped taut, slamming them against the building before they dropped onto the narrow maintenance ledge below.

Lucian landed beneath her, one arm still locked around her body. Above them, Rook raged behind the parapet while Mara remained in his grasp.

Lucian's eyes met Sloane's, a silent promise burning between them.

We will save her. Together.
