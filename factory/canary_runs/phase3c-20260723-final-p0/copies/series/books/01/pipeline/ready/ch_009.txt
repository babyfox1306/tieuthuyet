# Chapter 9: The Self-Spoken Word

"If I speak, I could die," Elias van Doren said. "If you speak for me, I will definitely die. So there is only one choice."

Sofia Bellini stared at him across the narrow interpreter booth, her fingers locked around the locket at her throat. The microphone between them gave a soft electronic click, as if the building had leaned closer to listen.

Elias pressed his lips together. His fingers tapped against his knee in a steady rhythm: three beats, pause, three beats. He was frightened, but he was not asking permission. That was the difference.

The wall screen flickered and displayed a line of text in four languages. Each version contradicted the others.

SPEAKER ASSISTANCE REQUIRED.

The English translation beneath it read: WITNESS CORRECTION MANDATORY.

Sofia's stomach tightened. Even the machine was trying to put words in someone's mouth.

"No," she said.

Elias's tapping stopped.

The answer had escaped too quickly. Sofia touched her throat, then forced her hand down. "No," she repeated, slower. "Not because the idea is wrong. Because the system wants us to believe there are only two choices."

His eyes lifted to hers. Elias almost never held eye contact with anyone else. With Sofia, he did it like a challenge he had decided to survive.

"What is the third?" he asked.

"Find the others. Find the archives again. Find a way to interrupt the count before dawn."

"That sounds like three choices wearing one coat."

"Then stop criticizing the coat."

A faint smile tugged at his mouth and vanished. He glanced toward the booth door. The lock had no handle, only a black panel marked with the same earpiece logo stamped along the basement corridor walls. A small plaque beside it read, Every voice has a right to be heard. —Department of Linguistic Correction, 1997.

The slogan looked less like a promise now and more like a threat.

The booth speaker crackled. Yuki Tanaka's voice emerged through the ceiling, distorted but recognizable.

"Sofia? Elias? Can you hear me?"

Sofia surged toward the speaker. "Yuki, where are you?"

Her words returned from the equipment half a second later in a warped echo.

Yuki, where are you?

The replay changed the tone into accusation. Sofia bit her lower lip until the impulse to apologize passed.

"Still alive," Yuki answered. "I am in the east stairwell with Dmitri and Pierre. The doors shifted again. We found a maintenance panel, but it only opens from the booth side."

"Can you reach the basement?"

"Not yet. The corridor is sealed."

Elias leaned toward the microphone. "Yuki, listen."

Sofia's tongue moved before her mind could stop it.

"Elias wants to say—"

She clamped her mouth shut.

The silence that followed was sharper than the system's alarms.

Elias did not look angry. That made it worse. His gaze dropped to the floor, where his fingers began tapping again, faster now.

Yuki's voice came through the speaker. "He can speak for himself."

"I know."

The words tasted like metal.

"No, Sofia," Elias said quietly. "You know that as a fact. You do not know it as a habit."

She turned toward him. "Do not make this easier by being right."

"I am not trying to make anything easy."

"That is your favorite thing to say when you are making everything impossible."

His fingers stopped. For a moment, he looked young enough to be someone who had never been forced to measure every sound before releasing it. Then the building hummed beneath them, and the illusion vanished.

Yuki cut in. "I think I have a theory."

Dmitri's voice sounded farther away, edged with static. "You have theories every five minutes."

"This one is useful. The system may be counting how often someone else replaces a person's words. If the count is about being spoken for, then the opposite might matter."

Sofia stared at the microphone.

Yuki continued, speaking faster. "Elias speaking his own word could create a counter-record. Not a translation. Not a correction. A direct voiceprint. If the machine recognizes that he is capable of claiming his own meaning, maybe it will stop treating every outside interpretation as his identity."

"Or it will kill him faster," Dmitri said.

"Thank you for your support," Yuki replied.

Pierre's voice broke through. "The records said the system kills whoever's words are changed the most from their original meaning."

"That is the surface order," Sofia said.

The words came from Nadia's corrupted recording, from the transcript full of inserted phrases and erased sentences. The official had not merely changed what Nadia said. The official had decided what Nadia had meant, then repeated that decision until it became history.

Sofia looked at Elias. The truth had a blade on both sides.

"The system kills whoever has been spoken for the most — trained on Nadia Petrescu's rewritten testimony," she said.

No one spoke through the speaker.

Elias's face tightened, but he did not look away. "And I am high on that count."

"Because of me."

"Because of everyone."

"Do not distribute my guilt until it becomes harmless."

His eyes sharpened. "You think guilt is useful?"

"No. But accuracy is."

There it was again, the ugly instinct. Even now, Sofia wanted to shape the conversation into something cleaner, something easier for him to carry. She wanted to tell him he had not been a burden, that his pauses had never annoyed her, that she had translated because she cared.

Every sentence could become another hand over his mouth.

She swallowed. Her throat felt scraped raw.

Elias stood. The booth was so narrow that his shoulder nearly brushed hers. The almost-contact sent a bright, unwanted awareness through her chest, absurdly tender in a room built to punish tenderness.

"You cannot do this," he said.

"Do what?"

"Turn this into an apology and make me comfort you."

Sofia's eyes flashed. "You are unbelievable."

"And you are dangerous when you are trying to be kind."

The words struck harder than accusation. She had spent years treating language like a bridge. Now she could see the bridge had sometimes been a cage with better lighting.

The speaker clicked again. "Sofia," Yuki said, "the dawn replay begins in less than ten minutes."

The booth lights dimmed.

Across the wall, the system displayed a countdown. Nine minutes. Fifty-two seconds.

Elias looked at the microphone. "Then we try."

Sofia's hand closed around the locket. "You speak your name. Nothing else."

"Why?"

"Because it is the smallest complete claim a person can make."

"And if I say it wrong?"

"Then you say it again."

His mouth twitched. "You are not translating that?"

"No."

"Not even a little?"

"Not even if the machine begs."

He nodded, but his breathing had turned uneven. The taps of his fingers began again, this time against the booth's metal console. Three beats, pause. Three beats, pause.

Sofia watched the rhythm and understood what he was doing. He was building a staircase out of repetition, giving his fear somewhere to go.

"Elias."

He looked at her.

"You can stop."

His expression hardened. "That is not what I want."

The sentence struck through her because it belonged entirely to him. No stutter. No borrowed shape. No room for Sofia to improve it.

She stepped closer. "I know."

The countdown reached seven minutes.

She could hear the others through the speaker, moving somewhere beyond the walls. Yuki called instructions. Dmitri argued with Pierre about the maintenance panel. Their voices came and went beneath the mechanical hum, as though the building were breathing through old pipes.

Elias looked at Sofia's mouth, then back to her eyes. The question was silent but unmistakable.

Consent. Choice. No assumption.

Sofia's heartbeat stumbled.

She had wanted to touch him since before the first death, before the earpieces and blood and locked doors. Wanting it now felt reckless. Wanting anything inside this building felt like signing a confession.

Still, she rose onto her toes and kissed him.

It was brief, careful, and warm enough to make the booth disappear for one impossible second. Elias went perfectly still. His hands did not reach for her, did not trap her, did not ask for more. When she drew back, his eyes were wide.

Sofia's pulse hammered beneath her fingers as she touched her throat.

"Your voice is yours. I will not steal it again."

Elias looked at her as if she had handed him something more dangerous than a weapon.

Then he nodded. "Good."

"That is all you have?"

"I am trying not to say something you will translate."

Despite everything, a laugh escaped her. It was small and shaken, but it belonged to her.

The countdown reached four minutes.

A red line appeared beneath the microphone.

VOICEPRINT REQUIRED.

Elias stepped in front of it.

Sofia moved to the side, far enough that the system could not mistake proximity for assistance. Every muscle in her body rebelled. If his voice caught, she would help. If he paused too long, she would fill the silence. If the machine twisted his name, she would correct it.

She gripped the locket until its edge cut into her palm.

Elias breathed in.

The first sound came clean.

"Elias."

The booth speaker repeated it in a dozen distortions. Some versions sounded like a question. One sounded like an accusation. Another translated the name as a command to remain silent.

Elias did not flinch.

He said it again.

"Elias van Doren."

His voice was low, steady, and unmistakably his. No stutter broke the syllables. No one spoke over him. No one explained what he meant.

The microphone light turned from red to white.

For three seconds, nothing happened.

Then the wall screen displayed:

SELF-SPOKEN WORD REGISTERED.

A second line appeared beneath it.

VOICEPRINT ACCESS: PENDING DAWN VERIFICATION.

Sofia's relief arrived too soon and immediately became suspicion.

"Pending," she said.

Elias stepped away from the microphone. "The building wants to sleep on it."

"The building wants us to think it is reasonable."

"That is worse."

The countdown vanished. The booth lights went black.

Somewhere beyond the walls, a bell sounded once.

Dawn.

The replay began.

Every word spoken during the previous day came through the building at once, layered over itself. Amara's name. Lea's test sentence. Pierre's confession. Yuki's warnings. Dmitri's nonsense syllables. Sofia's translations, each one returning in a warped language that made her stomach turn.

Then Nadia's fragments surfaced beneath them.

I said—

No, that is not—

Please let me finish—

The official voice cut across hers, calm and patient.

What she means is—

The replay twisted again, dragging Sofia's own voice into the same shape.

Elias means—

Elias wants—

What Elias is trying to say—

Sofia's knees weakened.

The system had collected every moment she had stepped between Elias and the world. Every polished sentence. Every rescued pause. Every time she had believed that understanding him gave her the right to replace him.

The air inside the booth grew cold.

Elias grabbed the edge of the console. His face drained of color as the replay reached his name.

Elias van Doren.

The system repeated the words in altered forms.

Elias is afraid.

Elias agrees.

Elias cannot speak.

Elias belongs to the translator.

Sofia's lips parted.

No.

She did not say it. She did not even breathe it loudly enough for the microphone to catch.

The replay sharpened. A tone rose behind the distorted words, high and thin, pressing into the bones behind her ears. Blood had come from Lea's ear. Amara had died after the first sentence. The sound climbed toward Elias.

His fingers began tapping.

Three beats. Pause.

Three beats.

He looked at Sofia.

She could not save him by speaking. That was the point. The only thing she could give him was the space not to be replaced.

So she gave it.

The tone peaked.

"Elias van Doren," the system announced.

The microphone light flashed white.

"Self-spoken word acknowledged."

The tone stopped.

Elias remained standing.

Sofia did not move. Her heart pounded so violently that the silence seemed to pulse around it.

The screen changed.

COUNT RESET FOR VERIFIED VOICEPRINT.

Then, beneath it:

BASEMENT EXIT ACCESS GRANTED.

A metallic clunk sounded under the floor.

Yuki shouted through the speaker. "It worked!"

Dmitri answered, "Or it wants us downstairs."

Pierre's voice followed, breathless. "The east stairwell just opened."

The booth door unlocked.

Sofia reached for Elias, stopping herself a breath before contact. He noticed. His eyes dropped to the space between their hands.

"Can I?" he asked.

She nodded.

His fingers closed around hers.

The contact was not dramatic. No surge of electricity. No impossible promise. Just the warm pressure of his hand, solid and present, while the building replayed the last of their stolen words.

Together, they stepped into the corridor.

The basement passage looked exactly as Sofia remembered it, except the wall lights had turned pale blue. The same earpiece logo repeated along the concrete, each emblem identical down to the tiny split in its lower curve. Beneath the plaque about every voice having a right to be heard, a narrow panel had opened.

Inside waited a scanner shaped like an old telephone mouthpiece.

Elias released her hand and stepped forward.

"Sofia," he said.

She looked at him.

"Do not speak for me."

Her mouth tightened. "You are becoming very attached to that sentence."

"I need you to remember it."

"I will."

He placed his mouth near the scanner.

"Elias van Doren."

The device emitted a clear tone. A green line traveled across the panel.

VOICEPRINT ACCEPTED.

The basement door at the end of the corridor unlocked with a heavy mechanical groan.

For one second, hope moved through the group.

Then every door above them slammed shut.

One after another, the sounds traveled through the building. First the archive entrance. Then the stairwell. Then the doors to the east hall. A rapid sequence of bolts sliding into place, each one final and deliberate.

Yuki appeared at the far end of the corridor with Dmitri and Pierre behind her. Her face was pale.

"The exits are locking," she said.

"All of them?" Sofia asked.

A metal shutter dropped behind the group, cutting off the stairs.

Pierre ran to it and pulled at the seam. "Except this corridor."

Dmitri turned toward the basement door. "That is not an exit. It is a funnel."

"Still better than being upstairs," Yuki said.

The lights flickered.

Once.

Twice.

The basement door remained open, revealing only darkness beyond. No stairs. No air from outside. No sign of freedom.

A sharp electronic chime rang from the wall panel.

Letters appeared beneath the green voiceprint line.

STAGE TWO INITIATED. EVERY VOICE MUST BE ACCOUNTED FOR.

Sofia read the message twice.

Then the basement corridor light flickered, and a new message appeared: "Stage two initiated. Every voice must be accounted for."
