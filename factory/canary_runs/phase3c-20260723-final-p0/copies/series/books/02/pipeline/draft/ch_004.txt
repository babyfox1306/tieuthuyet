# Chapter 4: Beneath the Transcript

"That’s not what I wrote," Elias van Doren says, his voice barely above a whisper as the transcript is projected on the wall.

His words hang in the hush, flat and damning. The glow from the mural of black-and-white text flickers over their faces—a bureaucrat’s campfire, equal parts accusation and confession. Sofia Bellini stands silent beside him, eyes scanning the columns, lips held in faint amusement as if some private joke spools behind her teeth. There’s nothing funny here, of course, but humor is a better armor than panic.

Elias’s hands are shaking. Not enough to embarrass himself—only enough that Sofia, standing close, can see the feather-quick tremor in his thumb as he scrolls his own notes on a battered tablet. He double-checks a line: "The fire exit was jammed. I tried the stairs." What the wall displays is not his words at all.

"The fire exit was jammed. I instructed others not to attempt evacuation," reads the official transcript.

Tomasz Havel, tribunal technician, pretends to fidget with the projector controls, studiously not meeting anyone’s eye. The faint scent of ozone lingers in the air, curling from the transcript printer at the edge of the room—always strongest, Sofia has noticed, whenever a correction appears. That tang settles behind her teeth now, mingling with the scraped-metal taste of vigilance.

Chief Adjudicator Rasmus doesn’t bother to rise from behind his desk. He sets his elbows on the tabletop, fingers steepled, tone indulgent as he addresses the chamber. "We are all under pressure. Perhaps Mr. van Doren is simply misremembering—trauma clouds even the most faithful mind."

Sofia knows the script well: minimize, soothe, carry on. She is not here to create alarm.

Elias’s eyes dart to her, searching for affirmation, for rescue. She offers neither—only a tilt of her head that says, You can stand your ground, can’t you? Her gaze is the soft warning of a shepherd’s crook: steady, but not to be grasped.

"No," Elias says, stuttering on the consonant just a little. "I—I wrote it down in my own hand. I have—records. It’s—" He glances at Tomasz. "Mr. Havel, please. The printout from yesterday—may we see it?"

Tomasz’s eyes flicker, unwilling. "Of course. If you wish." He fetches a sheath of paper from a basket labeled CORRECTIONS, the edges still warm. He hands it to Elias without looking at him.

Elias pores over the lines, heartbeat drumming so loud Sofia can all but hear it. His lips move as he matches line to line, only to stop, lips pressed thin.

"This—" His voice cracks. "This is changed from yesterday’s. I never gave these orders. The record says I told people not to evacuate—"

Rasmus interrupts smoothly. "No one is blaming you, Mr. van Doren. Our process is rigorous. Perhaps your recollection is—"

"I am not mistaken," Elias blurts, then winces at the volume. His cheeks flush; the stutter returns, brutal in its timing. "S-s-sofia, you heard wh-what I—"

What a gift, that he lurches toward her for support. Sofia’s smile is immediate, reassuring. She never misses a cue. "He’s quite right, Chief Adjudicator. I was present. The confusion comes, perhaps, from the way we summarize under pressure. If I may—"

She steps forward, hands clasped almost prayerfully. Her voice is velvet, wrapping around every edge. "What Elias means is, he advised caution. And in a panic, people sometimes need to be told not to risk their lives for doors that won’t open. The way the transcript phrases it—it loses the context of his concern."

The room softens around her assertion, as rooms always do. Rasmus nods, a mask of patience. Tomasz allows himself a breath. The momentary threat—drama, accusation, chaos—is soothed, bottled, rendered manageable.

Sofia continues. "Perhaps the system condensed his words too much. I’m sure if we review the raw audio, we’ll find his intent is preserved, if not the precise phrasing."

Magda Lorenz—her mentor, her shadow—offers a brief, approving smile. That’s my girl, the smile intones. Magda reads the room like a battlefield; she knows the only blood tolerated here is metaphorical.

Sofia’s mind is already spinning. She can feel the language system stretching behind the scenes, words being pulled taut, then let go. She has learned to see the threads, the way a phrase spoken aloud can become truth once it is inscribed and stamped. It is power, she thinks—not pure, but potent. And she wields it like a scalpel, always with a gentle hand.

Rasmus waves a dismissive hand. "Let the record reflect: Mr. van Doren’s account was one of prudent caution in a crisis situation. The discrepancy is noted. We move forward."

But the record won’t reflect that—not the way anyone intends.

A pause, as the technician returns to his panel. The faint ozone grows sharper. A new line appears on the projected transcript. Sofia glances up sharply, catching the subtle shape of the revision: "Mr. van Doren advised all present to remain calm and await guidance." Neutral. Sanitized. Just enough to erase the stain of blame, while robbing the statement of all agency.

Elias’s jaw clenches. He knows—she can see it. He may not understand the mechanism, but he senses the theft.

Sofia wonders how long before the system begins rewriting even those who resist consolation.

"While we’re here," Rasmus says, "perhaps it’s best to address the regrettable loss of Ms. Schiller." His tone is funereal, but the cadence is brisk—already past mourning.

Magda shifts, her hands folded on her knees. The entire chamber adopts a posture of rehearsed sorrow.

Rasmus consults a memo, never looking at the faces around him. "The committee finds no evidence of external intervention. The cause is ruled as stress-induced cardiac event, possibly exacerbated by recent questioning. The system registered a correction to her transcript, but this has been reviewed and found to be immaterial to her health outcome."

Sofia nearly laughs. Immaterial. As if words and death could be separated, in this place.

Pierre Leclerc stands at the rear, notepad poised, always waiting for the gap between the official line and the story beneath it. His mouth twitches in a smirk. "So no deeper investigation, then? Three deaths in as many weeks, each following a correction, and we’re meant to trust in stress and… system errors?"

Rasmus’s patience thins. "We trust in process, monsieur. Unless you have direct evidence of tampering—"

Pierre’s eyes flick to Sofia, then flicker away. "Process," he echoes, the word bitten in half.

Sofia lifts her chin, voice as gentle as a lullaby. "The cost of panic is sometimes higher than the facts themselves. Ms. Schiller was never one to complain of stress, but perhaps the process asked too much. I hope the record will reflect our regret."

Every word is a balm, a blanket thrown over something broken. She is so very good at this—at making the system seem faultless, even when it gnaws its own children.

Rasmus nods, appeased. "Let that be noted. We will observe a moment of silence."

The chamber falls quiet. Even Pierre scribbles nothing.

Sofia lets her mind drift above the silence, skimming its surface. She wonders how many more silences must be observed before anyone dares count the bodies, instead of trusting the transcript.

When the moment breaks, Tomasz clears his throat. "For the record, all corrections proceed with authorization. There is no evidence of malicious edits."

Pierre’s pen snaps in his hand, so softly only Sofia notices.

Sofia resumes her role. She offers to clarify another witness’s testimony—a young man, panicked and contradictory in his account. Her intervention is subtle, just a gentle question: "Did you mean you saw the fire, or that you heard cries before the alarm?" The witness, grateful, nods, latching onto her prompt.

"Yes, I heard the crying before the alarm," he says, voice steadied by the certainty Sofia offers him.

The system pounces. The transcript marks: "Witness confirms hearing panic prior to alarm; sequence validated."

Conflict, averted; ambiguity, smoothed. Another line rewritten, and everyone relaxes a fraction.

Except Elias.

He stands back, watching the exchange, brow furrowed. When the witness leaves, his eyes meet Sofia’s.

"Do you remember, Sofia," he murmurs, voice trembling just a little, "what he actually said outside? He didn’t mention panic at all—just smoke, and that awful smell." He rubs his knuckles against his lips, searching her face. "It’s not him that’s changing, is it?"

She steps closer, close enough to see her reflection in his eyes—the worried line at his brow, the faith still unbroken. She keeps her voice low, a thread for only him. "He needed to be believed. Panic makes a better narrative than confusion. The record is safe for him, now. Isn’t that what matters?"

"But is it true?" The question is raw, urgent.

Sofia would laugh if it wouldn’t shatter him. Truth, here, is a matter of survival. She leans in, her words like silk through fingers. "What matters is that he lives to give another account. You trust me, don’t you?"

His breath is shallow. For a moment, he does not answer. Then, softly, "Of course I do."

"Good." She wants to touch his hand, but folds her arms instead, letting the moment linger in the space between their bodies.

Pierre approaches, irritation barely masked. "The facts are slithering, Miss Bellini. One day, even your words will wriggle out from under you."

She gifts him a smile, equal parts sympathy and warning. "I’m very careful with my words, monsieur."

The tribunal disperses, the day’s questions exhausted but not resolved. The scent of ozone sharpens as Sofia heads for the exit. As she passes the printer, Tomasz intercepts her with a folded sheet, his eyes averted.

"For you, Miss Bellini," he says quietly. "The record has been revised."

She unfolds the page. It is her own transcript, fresh from the machine. All but one line matches what she recalls from the hearing. Only one has changed—one that hadn’t been in question, one she had not touched.

She studies the revision, a chill crawling the length of her spine.

No one admits to making the change.

The chamber is empty but for the memory of voices and the faint, electric tang of ozone, clinging to the air as Sofia reads the line rewritten in her name—wondering, for the first time, whether words alone can keep her safe.
