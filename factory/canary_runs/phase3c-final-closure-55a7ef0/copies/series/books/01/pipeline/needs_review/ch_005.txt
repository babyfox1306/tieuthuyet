# Chapter 5: The Archivist Ghost

Pierre Leclerc held his press badge between two fingers. The plastic glinted under the dim ceiling light, a small betrayal made visible.

"My editor thinks I'm covering a linguistics fair," he said. "I'm supposed to expose this place. But I don't think I'm getting out to file the story."

The room went still. Eight faces stared at the badge.

Dmitri was the first to break the silence. "You came here under false pretenses?"

"Everyone came here under false pretenses." Pierre's voice cracked. "You're not here for academic exchange. You're here because the translation system needs fresh voices to feed on. I just knew the surface was a lie before I arrived. I didn't know it went this deep."

"How long?" Yuki asked, her voice hollow.

"Three months of background checks, forged credentials, bribes." His hand shook as he tucked the badge back into his pocket. "I thought I was chasing a story about corporate surveillance. A secret linguistics program run by a private foundation. I didn't sign up for a massacre."

He reached into his jacket again and pulled out a keycard. Matte black. Thicker than the standard magnetic strips they all carried.

"I found this in the coordinator's office during the welcome banquet. It was in a locked drawer behind a fake panel. I didn't take it then. I found it again the morning after Amara died. Someone—or something—had left it on my pillow."

"Left it?" Sofia's throat tightened. "You're saying the building gave you a key?"

"I'm saying I don't know who put it there. But it works. I tested it on the basement door before Lea's death."

"You tested it and didn't tell us?" Dmitri's voice rose.

"What was I supposed to do?" Pierre's face contorted. "I was supposed to be a journalist, not a leader. I thought if I found solid evidence, I could piece it together on my own."

Sofia took the keycard. It was unnaturally warm against her palm, as if it had been held recently.

"The archives are in the sub-basement," Pierre continued. "The building schematics had them below the heating system. If there's anything about how this place operates, it's down there."

"The sunset silence starts in under two hours," Yuki said. "We don't have long."

"Then we don't waste time." Sofia turned to the group. "Yuki, you're coming with me. Pierre, you stay here. If we don't return before the silence, you're the only one with enough deception instincts to keep the rest alive."

"She's right," Dmitri muttered. "You've been lying to us since we arrived. You're good at it."

Elias moved.

He didn't speak. He simply stepped forward, crossed the room, and stood beside her.

Sofia met his eyes. He didn't need words. She knew exactly what he was asking.

*Let me come with you.*

"I need you here," she said quietly. "If I fall, you're the only one the group trusts. You keep them together."

His jaw tightened. He didn't nod. But he stepped back, his hand brushing hers once, so quickly the others might not have seen.

She wanted to say something. To promise she'd return. But every second she wasted brought them closer to the silence window. She couldn't give him promises she wasn't sure she could keep.

The stairs to the lower level were narrow, the concrete worn smooth by decades of footsteps. The air grew colder as they descended, the mechanical hum from the main floor deepening into a rhythmic vibration that pulsed through the walls.

The corridor opened into a rectangular chamber lined with floor-to-ceiling shelves. Files, binders, and old hard drives filled the racks. The smell hit her like a wall: old glue, mold, the particular decay of paper left undisturbed for years.

And on the central table, completely alone, sat a single box.

The cardboard was crisp. The label was bright white, the ink sharp. It read: "N. PETRESCU, 1997."

Yuki touched the label. "This isn't old. This was placed here recently."

Sofia's skin prickled. Someone had prepared this. Wanted them to find it. She lifted the lid.

A disciplinary notice. A typed memo. A photograph of a young woman with dark hair and defiant eyes.

She read the memo aloud. "'Subject: Translation Error. Testimony subject to reinterpretation due to language overlap. File archived.'"

"Reinterpretation," Yuki breathed. "They changed her words. Made them fit their version of events. And then they buried her here."

Sofia pulled out her own notebook, flipping through the observations she'd made since the first death. Amara's final words had been mangled by the system. Lea's sentence had been perfectly translated, but Lea hadn't even delivered it herself—she'd asked someone else to confirm it first.

"They were translated," Sofia said slowly. "Amara's words were taken out of her mouth by the system. Lea's words were clarified by someone else. The system isn't killing the person whose words are twisted. It's killing the person whose words are *handled* by someone else."

Yuki's face went pale. "Sofia, who has been handling everyone's words?"

"I have."

The realization hit her like a physical blow. Every conversation she'd mediated. Every time she'd spoken for Elias. Every translation and clarification she'd provided. She had been feeding the system exactly what it needed.

"I've been doing what the system wants."

The compulsion burned in her throat even now. The urge to fill silence, to fix broken words, to speak for those who struggled. She thought she was protecting them. She was lining them up.

A siren ripped through the air. Once. Twice.

The sunset alarm.

They scrambled up the stairs, feet slipping on the damp concrete. Sofia clutched the Petrescu file against her chest, the edges of the folder digging into her ribs.

When they burst into the conference room, the others were frozen in place, watching the windows. The last sliver of sun dipped below the mountain ridge.

The silence fell.

Absolute. Inescapable. No breath. No shuffle. No whisper.

Elias was waiting for her.

His eyes searched her face. He saw the terror she couldn't voice. The confession she couldn't make.

He crossed to her. Took her hand. Pressed a folded piece of paper into her palm.

Then he stepped back, his fingers lingering against hers for a fraction of a second before releasing.

Sofia unfolded the note under the dim light.

The handwriting was uneven, each letter pressed hard into the paper.

*"I love you."*

The words blurred in front of her. Two words. The most absolute, terrifying, beautiful confession.

She couldn't say it back. She couldn't make a single sound. The silence had stolen her voice, her response, her chance to tell him that he was the only thing keeping her from shattering.

She looked up.

His eyes held hers. Dark. Steady. Afraid.

She pressed the note to her heart, the same way she'd pressed the first note he'd ever given her.

And she cried. Silently. Tears that caught the light.

The moment stretched. No one moved.

Then her hand brushed the Petrescu folder in her pocket. She pulled it out, her fingers trembling. The nearly empty file. The disciplinary memo.

She turned it over.

On the back, in handwriting that slanted hard and sharp, a single sentence was scrawled.

*"That is not what I said."*

Underlined twice. The pen had broken through the paper on the second line.

Sofia's breath caught in her throat.

She looked at Yuki. Yuki's eyes were wide, helpless. She looked at Pierre. He shook his head slowly.

She looked at Elias.

None of them could speak.

The building hummed beneath them, a low, patient vibration.

And Sofia held a message from a ghost—a warning, a scream, a plea—that she could not ask anyone to explain.

The system was listening.

And the sunset silence had locked her voice inside her throat.
