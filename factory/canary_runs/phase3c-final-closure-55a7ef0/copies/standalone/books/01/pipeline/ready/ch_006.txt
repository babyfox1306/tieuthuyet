# Chapter 6: The List of Seven

The paper crackled as Sloane Mercer unfolded it: seven names, seven dates, seven coordinates—each one a person she had been told was a threat, each one a lie Rook had fed her.

Her own handwriting filled the narrow margins.

Not notes. Corrections.

She flattened the page against the workbench in the weapons cache, then tore open the coat lining farther. Black thread snapped beneath her fingers. The hidden pocket had been stitched into the seam so neatly that she would never have noticed it without the recording.

Lucian stood on the other side of the bench. He did not reach for the list.

That restraint frightened her more than interest would have.

"How long did you know this was here?"

"I didn't."

"You knew about the coat."

"I recognized it."

"That wasn't the question."

His gaze moved over her face. "No, Sloane. I did not know there was a list in the lining."

Seven entries. Seven neat transactions disguised as threats.

The first name had a date from eleven weeks ago beside it and coordinates for an abandoned grain terminal. Under the printed intelligence summary, Sloane had written no weapon recovered.

The second had died at a highway rest stop. Her note read changed route after contact with Rook.

The third coordinate belonged to a storage yard. The fourth to a motel. The fifth to an industrial pier. The sixth to a private airfield.

The seventh line had no name.

Only a date, coordinates, and a small circle drawn beside the empty space.

Sloane's thumb pressed into the inside of her left wrist. She traced the same shape before she realized what she was doing.

A turn.

A pause.

The urge to complete another movement tightened through her hand, but she forced her fingers flat against the bench.

Lucian noticed. Of course he did.

"What?" she demanded.

"You stopped."

"Congratulations on your functioning eyesight."

"You usually don't."

Her throat tightened. "Usually?"

"When you start the circle, there are other motions."

"What motions?"

His silence gave her a familiar, infuriating answer: not yet.

Sloane looked down before she did something satisfying and stupid, like driving the heel of her hand into his mouth. The list offered safer violence.

Every intelligence summary followed the same structure. Rook had identified a threat, provided evidence of imminent harm, and recommended immediate removal. Every margin contained a discrepancy in Sloane's hand.

No weapons.

No hostile contact.

No encrypted traffic.

No secondary operative.

Seven targets reduced to seven marks in a rifle magazine.

"Tell me how they died," she said.

Lucian's expression closed.

"All of them."

"You have coordinates."

"I asked for method."

"Sloane."

"That tone might have worked when we were sleeping together. Right now, it makes me want to test how many teeth you actually need."

Something almost warm touched his eyes, then vanished. "The first was shot through glass from four hundred meters. The second at two hundred and eighty. The third was moving."

"And the others?"

"Clean distance shots."

"By you."

"Yes."

The word dropped without decoration.

Her stomach turned, but facts were useful. Facts did not care if she could live with them.

"Where was I?"

"Close enough to confirm."

"For all seven?"

"For the first six." His attention shifted to the blank final line. "The seventh changed."

Mara.

Sloane did not let herself say the name. Saying it would turn the page into a rooftop. Into blood. Into a sister whose death Sloane had purchased through a man who had once known how she liked to be touched.

She studied the printed text until the letters blurred.

"How questionable?"

Lucian did not pretend to misunderstand. "The targets."

"Yes."

"I saw inconsistencies."

"You saw innocent people."

"I saw files that didn't support the urgency attached to them."

"And then?"

"I followed your orders."

Her laugh came out thin and sharp. "How disciplined of you."

His jaw flexed once. "You had access I didn't. You told me the source had been verified."

"So you outsourced your conscience."

"No." He planted both hands on the bench. "I made the choice."

It would have been easier if he had defended himself. Easier if he had blamed contracts, conditioning, or her own forgotten authority. Instead, he stood there and owned the same ugliness she could feel opening inside her.

She tapped the first name. "Did this person threaten anyone?"

"I never saw proof."

"The second?"

"No."

"The third?"

"He ran."

"People run when someone points a rifle at them."

"Yes."

The answer had no resistance in it. That made her angrier.

Her fingertip moved down the list. "I ordered these."

"Yes."

"I reviewed the files."

"You challenged parts of them."

"And still gave the order."

"Yes."

Each answer stripped away another excuse she had not wanted but had apparently been saving.

Sloane had no memory of accepting those deaths. That did not make her innocent. It only made her guilty without the mercy of context.

Her left hand curled. Her thumb found her wrist again and began the small circle.

The cache vanished.

A white table snapped into focus beneath her palms. Not here. Somewhere cleaner, brighter, designed to make every shadow look guilty.

A file lay open.

The first target's photograph had been clipped to the corner. Sloane's hand—her remembered hand—dragged a transparent evidence sleeve across the page. Inside it sat a flattened bullet recovered from plaster.

Wrong rifling pattern.

Rook stood across from her, his body reduced by the memory to fragments: cuff, knuckles, the hard edge of a file he tried to close.

She stopped him with two fingers.

Then came the second file. A receipt. A security still. A timestamp that placed the target miles away from the alleged exchange.

The third held photographs of shipping containers. The serial numbers had been altered in black ink, but the pressure marks beneath preserved the originals.

Sloane circled them.

Once.

A quarter turn of her wrist.

Rook reached for the papers.

Her remembered voice cut through the room.

"They weren't assets."

His reply came blurred, swallowed by the rush in her ears.

She shoved all seven files across the table. Seven photographs spread between them.

"These people were witnesses."

The memory jolted.

Rook's hand came down on the files. Not panic. Control.

A sliver of violet light from a document scanner passed over the top sheet, and a watermark appeared beneath the printed intelligence summary.

A serial code.

CR-17.

Then darkness slammed back into place.

Sloane caught the edge of the workbench. Her knees did not fail, which was considerate of them.

Lucian rounded the bench. "What did you see?"

"UV light."

"What else?"

"Give me the lamp."

He opened a drawer beneath the ammunition case and removed a compact inspection light. Sloane snatched it from him and switched it on.

The paper looked ordinary for half a second.

Then violet light washed across the list, and a watermark rose from the fibers: CR-17, repeated in pale diagonal rows. Not printed over the document. Embedded in the paper itself.

Sloane stared at the code.

She had seen it in the recovered fragment, stamped on the edge of Rook's personal file. He had not merely approved the intelligence. His controlled paper stock had carried it.

His hands had been all over the list before hers ever touched it.

Lucian leaned closer without crowding her. "You recognize the code."

"Rook's file designation."

"You're sure?"

"No, I enjoy inventing administrative serials during neurological episodes."

His mouth tightened. "What did you remember?"

She angled the UV beam across the seven entries. Small marks appeared beside four coordinates, invisible in normal light: abbreviated container numbers matching the kind she had seen in the memory.

"I confronted him. There were files. Physical discrepancies." Her pulse hammered harder with every detail. "The intelligence had been manufactured. At least parts of it. These people weren't operational threats."

Lucian looked at the names again. "Then what were they?"

"Witnesses."

The word scraped her throat.

Not the whole answer. Not enough to explain what they had witnessed or why Sloane had still issued the orders. But enough to reshape every death.

Rook had aimed Lucian's rifle through her.

And she had let him.

"I knew," she said.

"You found out later."

"That changes the timeline, not the bodies."

"You confronted the source."

"And apparently failed to stop anything."

Her thumb pressed harder into her wrist. The circle began again, instinct trying to carry her somewhere conscious memory could not follow.

Lucian caught her hand before she finished.

Sloane looked at his fingers around hers. "Let go."

"You're bleeding."

A crescent from her thumbnail had opened the skin at her wrist. One bright bead rested inside the circle she had traced.

"I've survived paper cuts with less supervision."

"I remember."

The quiet endearment he did not say was somehow present anyway. It lived in the way his thumb avoided the broken skin and in the unbearable care with which he released her.

She hated that her body recognized his restraint.

"What happened after I confronted Rook?" she asked.

"I don't know."

"You expect me to believe that?"

"I expect you to decide based on evidence."

"That sounds suspiciously like something learned from me."

"It was."

The answer landed too close.

Sloane turned away, but the movement pulled the last loose thread from the coat lining. Something small fell onto the floor with a dry click.

Lucian bent, then paused.

"May I?"

The question should not have mattered. It did.

"Yes."

He picked up a thin plastic tab, no larger than the nail of her thumb. One side held dried adhesive. The other bore a handwritten sequence of three letters.

LGR.

"Ledger," Sloane said.

"Possibly."

"A marker for wherever I hid it?"

"Or bait for anyone searching the coat."

She took it from him. Nothing stirred. No helpful flash. No address unfolding in her skull. Just three letters and a maddening blank where certainty should have been.

"I don't know where it is."

Lucian went still.

She watched his face. "That concerns you."

"Rook believes you do."

"Good. Let him."

Understanding moved between them, dangerous and immediate.

Sloane placed the plastic tab beside the list. "We leak a location."

"To whom?"

"To the comm channel he used. We don't tell him we found the ledger. We let him intercept a message indicating we're retrieving it."

"He'll assume a trap."

"He assumes everything is a trap. Vanity will make him come anyway if he thinks the ledger exists."

"And if he sends someone else?"

"No one else knows enough." She met Lucian's eyes. "He has to confirm whether the threat is real."

Lucian studied her for a long second. "Location?"

"The grain terminal from the first entry."

"Too exposed."

"That's the point. We use the coordinate he manufactured, place the list where surveillance can catch it, and make him believe the ledger was hidden at the beginning of the chain."

"The terminal has three access points and elevated sightlines."

"You sound almost impressed."

"I'm deciding how many ways this gets you killed."

"Try counting the ways it gets him caught. It's more motivating."

She reached for the comm device, but her hand shook once.

One betrayal from her body. Small, fast, unacceptable.

Lucian saw it.

He stepped behind her slowly enough that she could have moved. His hands settled at her waist without pulling.

"Tell me to stop," he said.

She should have.

Instead, Sloane closed her eyes. "Don't."

His arms folded around her, firm but not confining. His chest met her back. His cheek rested against her hair, and the pressure of him held the scattered pieces in place without pretending they belonged together.

No absolution. No promise that the dead had deserved it after all.

Only shared weight.

"I ordered the killings," she said.

"Yes."

"I may have questioned the files, but I still authorized every shot."

"Yes."

"And you knew something was wrong."

His breath warmed her temple. "I knew the evidence was thin. I told myself you had more."

"You trusted me."

"I chose to."

There it was again. Choice, sharp enough to leave scars.

Sloane turned within his arms. He loosened them immediately, giving her space even as she stayed close.

"What were we?" she asked.

His gaze dropped to her mouth. "Complicated."

"That's not factual."

"It's the safest answer."

"I'm getting tired of safe answers."

"So am I."

She touched the scar above his eyebrow, testing another person's history through skin. Lucian did not move until her fingers slid to his jaw.

"May I kiss you?" he asked.

Consent should not have sounded like surrender.

"Yes."

His mouth met hers with controlled heat that lasted exactly one breath before control broke. Sloane caught his shirt, pulling him closer. The kiss deepened, edged with anger and familiarity her mind could not claim but her body answered.

A fragment flickered behind her eyes: Lucian's focus ring turning one quarter away from clarity. Her own thumb tracing a circle. A whispered command lost before she could hear it.

She broke the kiss.

Lucian's forehead rested against hers. "Memory?"

"Maybe."

"What?"

"Not enough."

It had changed something, though. Not forgiveness. Never that. The image had linked his ritual to her own, two procedural habits moving around an order she still could not retrieve.

She stepped away and picked up the comm.

"The false leak goes out in ten minutes," she said. "We make the grain terminal look like the ledger site, then watch who comes to erase it."

Lucian nodded. "We spring the trap together."

His phone buzzed.

The sound cut through the cache.

He pulled it out, and Sloane read the message from the unknown number over his arm.

"Mara is at the warehouse on River Road. Come alone, or she disappears."

Lucian and Sloane exchanged a look.

It was the trap.

But they had to spring it.
