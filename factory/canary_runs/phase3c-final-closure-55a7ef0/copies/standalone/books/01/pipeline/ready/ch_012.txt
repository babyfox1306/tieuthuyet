# Chapter 12: The Ledger's Pulse

The gunfire was a drumbeat against the steel, but in Sloane Mercer's mind, only Mara Mercer's frantic rhythm echoed, a silent cadence that suddenly clicked into place. Three taps, a pause, two taps, then the turn of her hand through the rifle scope. It was not a plea.

It was an instruction.

Sloane dropped behind the rooftop ventilation housing as another round punched through its upper seam. Metal fragments sprayed over her hair.

Lucian landed beside her, the rifle protected against his chest. "Tell me you saw a route out."

"I saw something better."

His stare sharpened. "That answer usually costs blood."

"Then try not to leak on the equipment."

She caught his wrist and tapped Mara's rhythm against his pulse. Three. Pause. Two. Then Sloane traced a small circle against the inside of her own left wrist.

Pressure gathered behind her eyes.

Her fingers remembered a trigger with no bullet beneath it. A stock braced to her shoulder. A focus ring turned away from clarity, then brought back one measured fraction at a time.

Mara's hand in the scope repeated the pattern.

Sloane understood.

"The rhythm is part of the firing sequence," she said. "Not the shot. The authentication."

Lucian glanced toward the roofline. Gunfire struck concrete beyond them, methodical enough to herd rather than kill. Rook was buying time or taking it away. With him, those were probably the same activity.

"How much of the sequence?" Lucian asked.

"The final part."

"You sound certain."

"My nerves are attempting to crawl out through my teeth. Certainty is the consolation prize."

She reached for the rifle. Lucian did not release it.

"Sloane."

"What?"

"Once you begin, he will know."

"He already knows. That is why someone keeps ventilating the roof."

"The surveillance feed cycles through a blind interval every seventy seconds. Four seconds with no visual coverage while the system transfers channels."

She looked toward the black camera dome above the access structure. Its indicator burned red, went dark, then returned.

Rook had built himself a blink.

"Can he reach us during it?"

"He can try."

"Then he can join the queue of men who underestimated me."

Lucian's hand tightened around the stock. Before shifting position, he turned the scope's focus ring exactly one quarter rotation away from clarity.

The hated order. The gesture she had noticed before without understanding why it made something inside her ache.

He obeyed anyway.

"Show me what your body remembers," he said quietly.

That sentence entered her like a key.

Sloane took the rifle and settled behind it. The scope still held Mara's image in the distant surveillance room, grainy and fractured by interference. Mara lifted her hand again.

Three taps against her palm.

Pause.

Two taps.

A circle drawn with one finger.

Then Mara closed her fist.

Sloane mirrored the first tap on the rifle's magazine housing. Nothing happened.

The second tap sent a faint vibration through the stock.

By the third, the scope display flickered green.

Lucian went still behind her. "Again."

She repeated it, but the pulse vanished.

"No." Her frustration came out sharp. "There is pressure involved. Position. Something physical."

A round snapped past the ventilation housing. Lucian pulled her lower, his chest hard against her back.

The contact detonated a memory fragment.

His mouth at her shoulder. Her hands wrapped around his over the rifle. His breath measuring time against the back of her neck.

Not instruction.

Intimacy disguised as training.

Sloane's pulse stumbled. "You taught me this position."

"No."

She turned her head enough to see him. "That was impressively fast."

"You taught me."

Another fragment struck. Sloane straddling his thighs on a concrete floor, the rifle balanced across them. Lucian's fingers spread over hers. Her mouth touching the corner of his as she turned the focus ring without looking.

A password written in pressure and want.

Her body knew him. That did not make him innocent, trustworthy, or entitled to a single inch of her.

It did make him useful.

"I need you behind me," she said. "Exactly where you were."

His gaze dropped to her mouth, then returned to her eyes. "Do you consent to me touching you?"

"Yes. Hands, mouth, whatever the sequence requires. I will tell you if that changes."

"And you stop the moment you want."

"The bullets may object to our excellent communication skills."

"They can wait."

His frightening tenderness was worse than the gunfire. It implied a history her mind could not cross-examine.

Sloane turned back to the rifle. Lucian fitted himself against her, one knee between hers to stabilize her position. His arm bracketed her ribs. One hand closed over her left hand on the stock while the other settled at her hip.

The fit was too exact.

Her breathing changed.

Lucian noticed. Of course he did.

"Still yes?" he asked against her ear.

"Still yes."

She tapped three times.

Pause.

Twice.

The stock vibrated again, a faint mechanical response under her palm.

"Circle," he murmured.

Sloane traced the inside of her wrist. The movement did not feel small anymore. It felt like the opening stroke of a signature.

A sequence unspooled through her muscles.

Rotate the wrist. Release the safety halfway. Press the stock plate with the heel of the palm. Turn the elevation dial seven clicks. Return the safety without engaging it.

But one movement remained blank.

Mara closed her fist in the scope.

Sloane's fingers locked.

Lucian's mouth brushed the place below her ear, so light it could have been accidental if either of them believed in accidents.

Heat ran through her, vivid and immediate.

A memory returned with it.

"Kiss me," she said.

He froze.

"That was not nostalgia, Vey. It was an instruction."

His hand flexed against her hip. "Where?"

The answer arrived from someplace beneath conscious thought. "My pulse."

Lucian's lips touched the inside of her left wrist, directly over the circle she had traced.

The sequence slammed into place.

Sloane gasped as her body arched against him. His arm tightened around her, keeping the rifle steady while sensation and memory collided. She saw their hands joined on the weapon in another room, another night. She remembered biting his lower lip when he tried to slow her down. She remembered him saying her name as if it belonged to both of them.

Then she remembered the rifle.

Her thumb swept the hidden catch beneath the cheek rest.

Lucian's breath caught. "There."

Sloane turned toward him and kissed him.

It was not gentle. Gentleness would have implied patience, and dawn was bleeding toward them one stolen minute at a time. Her hand caught the back of his neck as his mouth opened under hers.

He met her without taking control from her.

The rifle remained trapped between them, its stock pressed against her ribs. His hand moved from her hip to her lower back, holding her close enough that every breath became shared heat. Her body recognized the shape of his restraint and hated how much it trusted that part of him.

She broke the kiss only far enough to speak. "Do not stop."

His eyes were dark and stripped of their usual distance. "Tell me what you need."

"You. Here. Following my hands."

She faced the rifle again. Lucian pressed a kiss to her shoulder, then covered her fingers with his.

Together, they performed the sequence.

Three taps.

Pause.

Two taps.

Sloane circled her wrist, and Lucian guided the heel of her palm into the stock plate. She turned the elevation dial while his thumb held the hidden catch. Seven clicks landed in time with Mara's silent rhythm.

Sloane shifted the safety halfway.

The rifle emitted a low pulse.

"Focus," she said.

Lucian reached around her. Instead of correcting the focus ring, he stopped one fraction short.

Memory flashed.

Sloane beneath him on a narrow cot, laughing against his mouth while her fingers turned that same ring. Lucian holding perfectly still as she said, "Not clear. Never completely clear." That is where he cannot see.

Her forgotten self had made intimacy part of the cipher.

Not because romance was secure.

Because Rook would never believe she trusted anyone enough to build a key between two bodies.

Sloane closed her hand over Lucian's and completed the rotation.

A panel in the rifle's stock released with a quiet metallic click.

Neither of them moved.

A wafer-thin device slid from beneath the recoil pad, no larger than two stacked coins. Fine copper contacts unfolded against the stock's internal rail. There had been no cavity on any manufacturer schematic, no added weight obvious enough to expose it.

Sloane stared at it. "I built that."

"Yes."

"You knew?"

"No."

She believed his surprise. She disliked that on principle.

The device warmed beneath her palm. A green line passed across the scope display.

Then another.

The targeting image disappeared.

In its place, encrypted data began streaming through the scope, each line flashing with a faint green pulse like a digital heartbeat. Transaction numbers surfaced and vanished too quickly to follow. Dates. Transfer routes. Contract amendments.

The first condemned name appeared.

Beside it was Rook's authorization code.

Sloane stopped breathing.

A second name followed. The target classification had been changed forty-eight hours before execution. Witness became weapons broker. A source file checksum blinked red beside the alteration.

The third entry carried the same authorization code.

So did the fourth.

Seven deaths. Seven files touched by the same hand.

Lucian's body went rigid behind her.

"He altered them," Sloane said.

The words were barely sound. She did not need an explanation from him. The proof was moving beneath her eye one green pulse at a time.

She had remembered all seven names. Now the ledger showed what had been done to their files before she sent Lucian after them.

Her orders had still been hers.

The lies had not forced her finger onto a trigger. They had made murder look like duty, and she had accepted the transaction.

Guilt did not become innocence simply because another man had edited the invoice.

"The transfers," Lucian said. "Look at the receiving chain."

Sloane tracked the numbers. Each execution was followed by a shipment routed through a private weapons account. Rook's code appeared again, buried under layers of false designations.

The data accelerated.

"Can you capture it?" Lucian asked.

"The device is transmitting somewhere."

"Where?"

"If my erased self left decent documentation, perhaps straight into the sun."

Mara's rhythm continued inside Sloane's mind.

Three. Pause. Two. Circle.

The ledger was not fully open. Most fields remained encrypted, their characters breaking apart before she could read them. But the initial entries were enough to expose the shape of the manipulation.

Enough to make Rook desperate.

The camera indicator went dark.

Lucian's head snapped up. "Blind interval."

Four seconds.

Sloane tightened her grip on the rifle.

The roof access structure threw a long wedge of darkness across the concrete. Something moved inside it.

One second.

Lucian shifted to cover her, but Sloane drove her shoulder into his chest and forced him aside. Nobody was taking the rifle from her while it held the first honest record of the blood on her hands.

Two seconds.

A boot struck concrete.

Three.

The first lines of Cassian Rook's ledger scrolled across the scope as a dark figure lunged from the blind spot, aiming for the rifle.

The mask covering his face was identical to Lucian Vey's.

And as Cassian Rook's hand reached for the weapon she had just unlocked, Sloane realized the blind interval had not been designed to hide the ledger.

It had been designed to let him stand close enough to steal it.
