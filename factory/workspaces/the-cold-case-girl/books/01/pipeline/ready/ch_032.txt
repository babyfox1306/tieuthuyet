Victor led me to a steel table beneath the only working light.

"No Wren?" he asked.

"You burned that channel."

"I refined it."

"You blackmailed her with photographs of a child."

His expression did not change. "And she made a rational choice."

"Seventeen choices. She logged every one."

That reached him. Not fear, but recalculation.

I placed a paper folder on the table. It contained copies of the evidence against Vance and nothing about David.

"Your attorney signed a hold on C-010," I said.

"Gerald signs whatever is in front of him when he panics. He will correct it."

"The scheduled distribution was cancelled. Holt kept the earlier proffer sealed."

Victor smiled. "Detective Holt. You have no idea whose attention you are inviting."

"Evelyn's."

His eyes narrowed.

The name confirmed more than any answer. Evelyn had been a supplier he believed he controlled, not a partner whose independence he understood.

"She sold you a map," he said. "Did she tell you where it ends?"

"With her asking for another payment."

"At least you learn."

The conversation had lasted nine minutes. David needed more.

I moved around the table, keeping Victor's hands in view. The body camera under my coat recorded locally. There was no signal for anyone to intercept and no live audience he could threaten.

"Why keep me alive?" I asked.

"You mistake delay for mercy."

"You could have sent the Croft file to the police six weeks ago. Instead you quoted Wren's archive, staged deadlines, and arranged private meetings."

"I wanted you to understand what you are."

"You wanted me to say we're alike."

"We are."

"No. I kill after I build a case. You manufacture cases until people become useful."

The distinction did not make me good. It made his smile disappear.

"Your sister made you predictable," he said.

"Elise made you afraid."

His right hand moved below the edge of the table.

I stepped back. "Show me both hands."

He raised the left.

The gun came up in his right.

I turned as he fired. The bullet struck a rusted brace behind me and threw a steel fragment through my coat into the outside of my shoulder. Pain erased the room for a second.

The second shot struck the steel table.

I drove into him before he could reset his aim. We hit the concrete together. The gun skidded beneath a rusted press. He caught my injured arm and twisted until my vision sparked. I used the folding knife from my pocket once, across his thigh, and he released me.

I reached the gun first.

Victor stayed on one knee, one hand pressed to his leg. Blood ran between his fingers.

"Do it," he said.

I aimed at the center of his chest.

C-010 was held, not destroyed. C-011 was still inside a vault as far as I knew. Killing him in that moment could release both.

I lowered the muzzle without lowering my attention.

"You don't get to choose the time."

His laugh was thin with pain. "You think time belongs to you?"

"For the next ten minutes, it does."

I backed toward the door. When I was clear, I left his gun on the warehouse roof in a drainpipe and called emergency services from a public phone two blocks away. I reported a wounded man and gave the address.

Sasha waited in a car beyond the rail yard. She did not ask why I was bleeding until the doors were locked.

"Metal fragment," I said. "No bullet. We need urgent care before I lose more blood."

"David?"

I checked the blank phone.

Three messages.

The first contained photographs of the C-011 inventory: the recovered fuel-line fitting, marina footage, witness statement, Meridian ledger.

The second showed Lena's forged trusts beside Victor's handwritten instructions.

The third was a bank receipt.

C-011 contents destroyed by authorization of co-controller David Rhodes. Evidentiary copies relating to Meridian transferred to counsel. Croft material not retained.

David had kept the documents that cleared his daughter and implicated Victor. He had watched the bank destroy the rest.

"The physical file is gone," I said.

Sasha gripped the steering wheel. "Then we have C-010 under hold and Holt's sealed copy."

"And a living Victor who knows Vance turned."

"He doesn't know about David yet."

"He will."

My shoulder burned with every breath. Victor had failed to kill me because I had given him a moving target, not because he had chosen mercy. That correction mattered.

At an urgent-care clinic, a doctor irrigated the industrial-looking laceration, removed the fragment, and closed the wound. While I waited, I watched the body-camera recording. Victor's voice was clear. So was mine. He admitted nothing useful to a court, but his reaction to Evelyn's name told me where the last threat lived.

The trap had sprung on both of us.

He had wounded me.

I had stripped away the file he trusted most.

When he learned, he would stop performing control and start trying to recover it.

That was when he would become dangerous enough to make mistakes.
