Sasha verified Evelyn's folders through sources Evelyn had never named.

The Meridian trusts existed. Lena Rhodes's signatures matched forms held by her university. Victor's cardiology record came from a clinic he had visited for seven years. Vance's billing entries matched court filings and the private investigator's incorporation records.

Evelyn had given us truth.

That did not make her trustworthy.

Sasha and I took the first train to Seattle the next morning. She met David and Lena in a borrowed conference room near the vault. I waited in the adjoining office until David asked whether the person Victor called Nadia Cole was present.

When I entered, the trust documents were spread across the table. Lena sat rigid beside her father, reading the same forged signature for the fourth time.

David Rhodes looked nothing like Victor except around the eyes. He had the same habit of going still when anger arrived, but no pleasure in it.

"My brother said the accounts were dormant family trusts," he said. "He asked me to co-sign a vault agreement in case he died. Then, last year, he told me Lena's name was on transactions the government would misunderstand."

"He manufactured the misunderstanding," Sasha said. "These documents make her look complicit."

Lena turned a page. "Can we prove I didn't sign?"

"Yes," Sasha said. "The source forms have timestamps. One of these trust signatures was created while you were in a supervised clinical exam. We can build the defense."

"After the scandal destroys her career," David said.

No one contradicted him.

I placed the certified Meridian transfer to Patricia Dornan beside the trusts. "C-011 contains evidence against me. It also contains the ledger that proves Victor used Meridian to protect Julian Croft and to frame Lena."

"You want me to destroy it."

"I want you to inspect it first. Copy the documents that clear your daughter and prove Victor's corruption. Then authorize destruction of the evidence he kept only to control us."

"Evidence of a murder you committed."

"Yes."

The word sat between us without defense.

David looked at Lena. She did not look back. She was still studying the forged version of her name.

"If I do this," he said, "it is not for you."

"It shouldn't be."

"And if I find the file isn't what you claim?"

"You walk out and call the police."

That was the first risk I could not manage for him. It was also the reason he believed me.

David agreed to enter the vault the following evening with Marcus Chen as counsel. Sasha needed to remain in Portland for Vance and for me; Chen would verify what David retained and what the bank destroyed.

Before that could happen, C-010 had to be stopped.

We returned to Portland that night. The following afternoon, Sasha confronted Gerald Vance in his office at four. I watched through a one-way video call from the isolated laptop. She showed him the billing records, the investigator's authorization, and Helena Cruz's recantation.

"If C-010 leaves your custody," Sasha told him, "these records go to the state bar and the federal prosecutor before the first journalist opens your attachment. You can preserve Victor Rhodes's leverage or your license. You cannot preserve both."

Vance called it extortion.

Sasha called it notice of an impending complaint.

At 4:38, he signed a written hold on C-010, revoked the scheduled distribution, and copied Sasha on the cancellation notices. She called all four intended recipients from a recorded line. The three journalists confirmed they had received no package. Detective Marcus Holt disclosed that Vance had lodged one sealed envelope with his unit weeks earlier as an unsolicited attorney proffer. Holt would preserve it unopened pending a warrant.

"He won't destroy potential evidence on our promise," Sasha said afterward.

"Would you?"

"No."

Holt's sealed copy remained a risk, but C-010 alone contained allegations and fragments, not the physical Croft evidence. It could wound me. It could not complete the case without C-011.

At 4:51, Vance called Victor.

We knew because Victor called me ninety seconds later.

"You frightened my attorney," he said.

"You hired a frightened man."

"Mason Street warehouse. Tomorrow night. Come alone."

"Why?"

"Because you have mistaken movement for progress, and I would like to correct you."

The line went dead.

Sasha had heard it through the speaker. "You are not going."

"David enters the vault at the same time. Victor needs to be looking at me, not Seattle."

"He shot you through a photograph."

"He wanted me afraid. Tomorrow he will want me close enough to watch."

"That is not protection."

"No. It is a prediction."

I called David and changed nothing about his plan. He and Marcus Chen would enter the vault at nine. Lena would wait in the lobby with copies of every exculpatory timestamp. If David did not call by nine thirty, she would go to Holt.

Compartmentalization without isolation. Another rule learned too late.

That night, I prepared for the warehouse with no help from Wren and no live network. A paper map. A body camera recording to local storage. A tourniquet. One legal firearm registered to Sasha and carried by Sasha—outside, beyond the perimeter, because coming alone did not require being abandoned.

At 8:57 the next evening, David texted from the vault lobby:

Going in.

At 8:58, Victor opened the warehouse door.

"Right on time," he said.

Behind him, the building was dark.

He smiled as if he still owned every clock.
