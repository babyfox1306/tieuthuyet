Eighteen days after the warehouse, my shoulder had closed into a hard red line.

Victor's leg had healed enough for him to return to his studio. His first episode back was called "The Violence Behind the Voice." He never named me.

Vance's hold remained in force. C-011 was ash. Holt's copy of C-010 stayed sealed because he still had no warrant to open it.

We had removed Victor's weapons without removing Victor.

"The deadline is still on Vance's calendar," Sasha said. "A hold can be revoked. We cannot assume fear will keep him compliant forever."

Priya sat across from us in the safe house, her audit closure letter folded beside her. David joined through a secure call routed on Sasha's new, isolated laptop. No Wren infrastructure. No Evelyn.

I placed Victor's cardiology record on the table.

"He has a documented arrhythmia," I said. "He lives alone. If he dies at home—"

"Stop." Priya lifted one hand. "If you finish that sentence, we define the real problem first."

She opened the contingency memo I had drafted.

"Victor Rhodes has press photographs, medical imaging, dental records, fingerprints from his prosecutor credentials, and family who know his face. His identity will survive any examination. If this happens, David identifies his brother and I confirm the hospital imaging. The only question we could corrupt is cause of death."

Sasha looked at me. "One body. One identity. Every ordinary record intact."

"Yes," I said.

David's voice came through the speaker. "You are discussing my brother's murder as paperwork."

"We're discussing how he survives in every version except one," I said. "He rebuilds C-011. He forces Vance to lift the hold. He puts Lena's name into a second set of accounts."

"That doesn't make this easy."

"It shouldn't."

David looked down at something beyond the camera. When he raised his eyes again, anger had settled into decision.

"What do you need from me?"

Priya answered before I could.

"If he dies, you are the nearest willing relative. You identify the body—his actual body. You provide his cardiology history, decline a family-requested autopsy, and authorize private cremation at the first lawful time. You do not lie about who he is."

"What lie do I tell?"

"That you have no reason to suspect violence."

David absorbed that.

Priya turned to me. "A full toxicology screen could expose the cause. A second medical examiner could order an autopsy. Police could place a hold because of the warehouse assault. A delay at the crematory could give someone time to object. Any one of those events ends the plan."

"How long is the vulnerable window?"

"From discovery until cremation authorization clears. Hours if the case remains routine. Longer if it doesn't."

"And after cremation?"

"The identity remains true. The cardiology record remains true. Only a preserved sample or independent evidence can challenge my conclusion."

Sasha leaned forward. "Say the part none of us wants to hear."

Priya looked at me. "I would perform an external examination and certify sudden cardiac death consistent with his documented condition. That conclusion would be false. If I see trauma I can't explain, if emergency personnel flag the scene, or if anyone orders toxicology, I stop. I will not forge findings that contradict a body in front of me."

It was not obedience. It was a boundary.

"Agreed," I said.

The plan narrowed.

I would arrange a final meeting at Victor's house by offering the one thing he still wanted: the replacement network index and a recorded confession. He would control the address and believe he controlled the exchange.

Priya would give me a sealed injector containing an unnamed cardiac agent. She refused to discuss concentration on any line and would prepare only one dose. The compound could be found by targeted toxicology. It was not magic; it was a risk.

Sasha would verify that Vance's hold and recipient withdrawals remained active immediately before I entered.

David would wait nearby. If I confirmed Victor was dead and the scene remained quiet, David would enter through the front door, call emergency services, and say he had come after his brother missed a scheduled family meeting. The meeting would be real; Sasha had placed it on both calendars.

Priya would not arrive at the house. She would receive the case through ordinary rotation at the medical examiner's office. If another examiner drew it, we aborted the cover and accepted the consequences.

"Too many points of failure," Sasha said.

"Those are the real ones," Priya replied. "Removing them from the list would not remove them from the world."

David remained silent until we finished.

"Lena's documents are with three lawyers now," he said. "Victor can't bury them. If I do this, it is to stop him from building another cage around her."

"I understand."

"No," he said. "You understand your sister. Let me keep my reason."

I nodded.

When he left the call, only Sasha, Priya, and I remained.

"Wren is gone," Priya said. "Evelyn is outside. David is here for Lena. What remains is us choosing whether to do this."

Sasha looked at me. "Not for the network. Not for a theory of justice. Do you believe Victor will hurt someone again if he lives?"

I thought of Patricia Dornan, Helena Cruz, Elise, Wren, Lena. People converted into pressure points.

"Yes."

Priya closed the cardiology file. "Then I stay."

Sasha closed her laptop. "So do I."

Loyalty did not absolve the plan.

It only meant I would not carry its cost alone.
