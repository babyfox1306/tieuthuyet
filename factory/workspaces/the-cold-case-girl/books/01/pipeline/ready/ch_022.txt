"I know about the boat. I know about the fuel line. I know about Julian Croft."

I read Victor's text three times. He had not guessed a generic secret. He had named a method.

The burner screen lit the safe house, a storage unit converted into one bare room with no windows and one steel door. Isolation no longer meant concealment. It only meant I had nowhere else to look when fear arrived.

I called Wren.

"I need the custody path for the Croft material," I said. "Not a break-in. Not Victor's computer. Start with the case number in the photograph he sent."

"The marina report?"

"The report, the fitting, the parking record. Tell me where he could have acquired each one."

She was quiet for a beat. "That takes longer than ten minutes."

"Take the time."

I ended the call and wrote three headings on paper.

PUBLIC RECORD.

PHYSICAL EVIDENCE.

PRIVATE ARCHIVE.

The public record told anyone that Julian's yacht had burned at Marina del Rey about three years earlier and that his body had been recovered three days later. The recovery report contained the damaged fuel line, but the original investigator had treated it as fire damage.

The physical evidence told a harder story. I had loosened the fuel-line coupling in the engine compartment, left the yacht, and watched from the parking lot as leaked fuel vapor ignited. The recovered coupling carried the crescent from my wrench. A marina record preserved four digits from the card I used to park.

The private archive told the story only I could have written. It used the phrase "clean finish." It described intent.

Victor could build the first two columns with money and patient research. The third required a door inside my network.

Wren called back forty-three minutes later.

"A private investigations firm ordered the marina incident file six weeks ago," she said. "Same firm that paid for the signature queries at Priya's office. They requested the insurer's high-resolution recovery photographs three days later."

"Rhodes's firm?"

"Gerald Vance's firm paid the invoices. That links the research to Victor, but it doesn't tell us where he keeps the copies. His office advertises offline evidence storage. I cannot see inside it from here."

The limit made the answer more credible.

"What about the Croft report Sasha found in the Elise box?"

"Older than Vance's request. The evidence stamp belongs to a batch migration, and the two case numbers share the same final digits. The archive manifest shows a bad index, not an outside handler."

The page that had felt like Victor's first message had been clerical debris. He had found the same report later by paying for it.

"The fitting?"

"Transferred out of the Coast Guard evidence annex after the retention period expired. The release was signed to a licensed investigator working for Vance. Six weeks ago."

Victor had not solved an impossible mystery. He had paid people to preserve material everyone else was ready to discard.

"And my operational record?"

Silence gathered on the line.

"Wren?"

"No external request could produce that," she said. "If he has the language from your record, the breach is ours."

Ours. Not mine. A small word that spread responsibility without locating it.

"Freeze the archive logs," I said. "Read-only copies. No purge, no repair, no credential rotation until Sasha receives them."

"If someone is inside, waiting gives them time."

"Changing the system gives them cover."

She accepted that without argument. Wren usually argued when a technical decision was bad. Her obedience felt like another piece of data I did not yet know how to place.

I called Sasha next.

"The lawsuit is ready," she said. "Not filed. Once I file, the docket becomes another clock."

"Keep it ready. Vance's firm bought the Croft report, the recovery photographs, and the fitting six weeks ago."

"That explains Victor's evidence. It doesn't explain his access to your language."

"I know."

"Who can read the operational record?"

"I hold the root key. Wren administers the archive. You and Priya can read case summaries, not the maintenance layer."

Sasha let the answer sit between us.

"Do not accuse her yet," she said. "Access is opportunity, not proof."

"I'm not accusing anyone."

"You called me before you called Priya."

She knew me too well.

I called Priya. Her voice was tired but steadier than it had been that morning.

"The first onsite sweep ended," she said. "No findings yet. No formal closure either."

"And Holt?"

"He asked again about the contractor that queried my signature samples. Marcus Chen made him separate those questions from RH03. Holt agreed."

"Did he ask about Julian Croft?"

"No. Why would a Portland detective ask me about a California boating fire?"

Because Victor wanted me to believe every moving part belonged to him.

"He wouldn't," I said. "I was testing a theory."

"Did it survive?"

"No."

That answer should have relieved me. Instead, it exposed the shape of the trick. Holt had his own investigation. RH03 had its own schedule. Victor had learned about both and presented coincidence as reach.

A new message appeared before I ended the call.

"Holt asks patient questions. Auditors ask dull ones. Which kind frightens Priya more?"

Victor knew Holt had visited. He did not claim to have sent him. He did not need to. The message made another man's investigation feel like Victor's hand.

"Priya, who knew Holt's name?"

"My supervisor. Marcus Chen. Sasha. You. Wren pulled his badge record."

"No one else?"

"Not from me."

I ended the call and photographed Victor's message with an unconnected camera.

The second message arrived a minute later.

"Sasha protects the law. Priya protects her daughter. Wren protects her access. What do you protect, Nadia?"

He had named all three women and the fear attached to each. Public research might reveal Sasha's profession and Priya's family. It could not explain the sequence or Wren's role in the archive.

I did not answer.

Instead, I called Sasha from the fixed line and read both messages aloud.

"Now we have proof of what?" she asked.

"That he knows the network exists."

"And?"

"That someone gave him the map."

"Still not a name."

"No."

That was the discipline Victor kept trying to take from me: the distance between a pattern and a conclusion.

I sent one instruction to each member through separate channels.

"Preserve your records. Do not erase anything. Do not discuss this message with the others."

Sasha replied first.

"Understood."

Priya replied next.

"Done."

Wren took eleven minutes.

"Already preserving."

Already.

I placed the responses beside the Croft custody map. Victor's evidence had a visible acquisition path. His private knowledge did not.

The fracture was no longer a feeling. It was a question with a finite set of answers.

I turned off the burner and sat in the dark.

The hunter had not become supernatural.

He had found a door.
