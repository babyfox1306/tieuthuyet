Victor had been dead for less than a day, and Evelyn already occupied the space he left.

Sasha examined the silver drive on the isolated laptop. The creation time matched Evelyn's warning and predated my final call to Victor. He had prepared the note and Croft copy before he knew exactly how the night would end.

"So he didn't place it during the fight," she said.

"He expected one of us to survive and wanted the survivor to find it."

"And the forty-eight hours?"

"A contact window. Not another bomb."

We wrote the distinction at the top of the new case ledger. No countdown to publication. No automatic upload. Evelyn held a human decision and had asked Victor for time to price it.

That made her more flexible than a machine and less predictable.

Priya called after her shift.

"Was it worth it?" she asked.

I could have answered with Victor's victims. I could have said he would never threaten another child or buy another witness. Those facts were true and still avoided the question.

"I don't regret stopping him," I said. "I regret making your license and David's daughter part of the weapon."

"That isn't an answer either."

"Then no. Nothing that required this much from other people can be called worth it."

She was quiet.

"Would you do it again?" she asked.

I looked at the silver drive. "If the same people were in danger and I knew no other way, yes."

"That is a terrible answer."

"It's an honest one."

"For once."

Priya told me she was taking leave. Not disappearing. Not waiting for permission. She would retain counsel and document every legitimate decision in Victor's case without volunteering the crime behind it.

"The network is over for me," she said.

"I understand."

"Do you?"

"I'm learning."

When the call ended, Sasha and I prepared the season finale of The Unheard. We did not claim Victor had personally strangled Elise; the evidence could not prove that. We presented what it did prove: his affair with Elise, the intimidation of Helena Cruz, payments around the suspension of the investigation, the Meridian accounts Elise discovered, and the same financial network used to buy Patricia Dornan's false alibi.

The records cleared Lena and implicated Victor without turning inference into fact.

I recorded the opening once.

"This is Nadia Cole, and this is the season finale of The Unheard.

"Victor Rhodes is dead. His death does not close the Elise Marchetti case. It removes the man who spent years bending that case away from the truth.

"The documents published with this episode show that Rhodes paid, threatened, and manipulated people around two murdered women. They show why Elise was dangerous to him. They do not answer every question about the hands that killed her.

"Those unanswered questions still matter."

I stopped the recording.

The old version of me would have called the case closed because the target was dead. The evidence did not permit that ending.

Sasha listened from the doorway. "Keep it."

We scheduled publication after legal review and made three offline copies of the source documents. The Croft material stayed out.

At 9:16 PM, a message arrived on the blank phone.

An image loaded first: the brass fitting from Julian's yacht, the crescent from my wrench visible beneath the evidence tag.

Then Evelyn called.

"Victor told you I would contact you," she said.

"He said you asked for time to set a price."

"He liked making ordinary negotiations sound prophetic."

"You copied C-011 before David destroyed it."

"Victor paid me to assemble and preserve it. He never bought exclusivity."

"Do you intend to release it?"

"Not today."

"That isn't an answer."

"It is the only answer you get without terms."

I signaled Sasha to start the local recorder. Evelyn would assume every call was recorded. The act was for preservation, not surprise.

"You said our old favor ended at the diner," I said.

"It did."

"Then this is business."

"Yes."

"Name it."

"A detective is dismantling the logistics market Victor used. He is close enough to make my clients nervous and principled enough not to be purchased."

Marcus Holt.

Evelyn had seeded his name before Victor died. She had not invented an enemy overnight.

"What did he do?" I asked.

"That is what I want you to determine."

"You already have investigators."

"I have collectors. You build cases people believe."

"And if the case clears him?"

A small pause.

"We will discuss that result when you have it."

"No. We discuss the rule now."

Evelyn's voice cooled. "You are not holding the leverage."

"Then release the Croft file and lose the only person you think can solve your problem."

Silence stretched across the line.

For the first time, I understood the offer beneath the threat. Evelyn did not merely want violence. She wanted my process—the public credibility Victor had tried to break.

"I will send the Holt file," she said. "Then you can state your rule."

The call ended.

Sasha stopped the recorder.

"She thinks the file makes the rule negotiable," she said.

"Then she still doesn't understand what she bought."

The Holt dossier arrived a minute later.

I did not open it online.

I moved it to the isolated machine and began a new case ledger.
