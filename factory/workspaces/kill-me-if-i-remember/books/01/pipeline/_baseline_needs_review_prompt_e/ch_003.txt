# Chapter 3: The Shot Was Built to Lie

"On three, shoot the pipe behind me," Sloane Mercer tells Lucian Vey, aiming a stolen sidearm at his ribs. "If you turn toward Mara Mercer, I shoot you first."

Lucian does not look offended. That would have been easier.

He only studies her face for half a second, then the weapon pressed against him, then the rusted pipe crossing the far side of the rooftop. His calm is almost insulting.

Almost.

"One," Sloane says.

The distant scrape of metal above the rooftop edge cuts through the air.

Not wind. Not settling steel.

A position change.

Her fingers tighten around the pistol.

"Two."

Lucian turns the rifle's focus ring exactly one quarter rotation away from clarity.

The movement is small enough that most people would miss it. Sloane notices because her brain has apparently decided to keep useless details while deleting entire months of her life.

"Three."

The rifle cracks.

The bullet hits the pipe behind her, exactly where she ordered.

The impact sends a sharp metallic snap across the roof. A fragment tears loose and drops near the far ledge.

Lucian moves at the same instant.

Not toward her.

Not toward Mara.

He steps into the narrow shadow between two rooftop structures, following the path Sloane indicated without asking why.

A second shot follows.

It misses.

Because there is nowhere to aim.

Sloane watches the angle, the echo, the delay between the shot and impact. Her mind strips the chaos into measurements.

Three possible firing positions. One eliminated by the first ricochet. One blocked by the ventilation housing. The remaining position has a clear line to the original point where she found Mara.

She hates how easy it is.

She hates how familiar it feels.

"Left roof access," she says.

Lucian does not question her.

"Six meters. Stay low. They cannot see through the vent housing."

His eyes flick toward her.

"You know that."

Sloane keeps the pistol against him.

"I know angles."

"You know more than angles."

"Careful. Compliments make you sound human."

A faint shift touches his expression. Not a smile. Something close enough to be dangerous.

Another impact punches into the rooftop surface.

The shooter is adjusting.

Sloane calculates the new position before the second echo finishes.

"Down."

Lucian drops immediately.

The bullet passes above them.

She moves after him, using the cover he created. Her body follows instructions her memory cannot locate. Her mind argues with every step.

This was not training.

This was not instinct.

This was something worse.

Something learned.

The rooftop has become a puzzle built by someone who knows exactly how she thinks.

And the most disturbing possibility is that person might be her.

A mechanical click sounds from the rooftop access structure.

Sloane turns.

The lock panel beside the door flashes once.

Then dies.

"That's new," Lucian says.

"No. It's old."

She hears herself say it before she knows why.

Her gaze drops to the panel. A tiny scratch pattern runs around the edge of the casing. Not damage. Not random.

A marker.

Her left wrist begins moving.

A small circle.

The same motion she makes when fear tries to take control.

Except this time the circle does not stop.

Her thumb traces the shape once, twice, then presses a hidden seam beneath the panel.

A piece of the metal housing shifts.

Sloane freezes.

She stares at her own hand.

"What did you just do?"

The question comes from Lucian.

The worse question comes from inside her own head.

How did you just do that?

She pulls her hand away.

"I have no idea."

That answer sounds ridiculous.

Lucian does not laugh.

That bothers her more.

The access point opens with a soft mechanical release.

Then every light on the rooftop shuts off.

Not a power failure.

A choice.

Somebody is removing the scene.

Sloane turns back toward Mara.

"No."

Lucian catches her arm before she crosses the open space.

The contact is brief.

Controlled.

Not forceful.

But there is something in the way he stops her that feels less like following an order and more like trying to keep her alive.

She looks down at his hand.

He releases her immediately.

"The roof is being cleaned," he says.

"By who?"

A pause.

Too small for most people.

Not small enough for her.

"You know I cannot answer that."

"Convenient."

"Accurate."

Sloane almost hates that she cannot argue with him.

Almost.

She pulls free and moves anyway.

Mara's jacket lies where the shooting happened.

The evidence is disappearing.

The contract in her pocket. The casing. The blood.

Everything.

She reaches the edge of the scene and drops beside the clothing.

Not Mara.

The clothing.

A distinction she forces herself to maintain because facts matter more than panic.

Her fingers search the torn fabric.

The wound area does not behave like a normal impact.

The damage is wrong.

The blood pattern is wrong.

Her hand finds a thin piece of material caught inside the fabric.

A membrane.

Not skin.

Not a medical dressing.

Something manufactured.

She lifts it carefully.

The inside edge has tiny square heat-seal marks spaced at exact intervals.

Four millimeters.

A laboratory evidence pouch sealer.

Not a commercial effects packet.

Her pulse changes.

The evidence is not what it appears to be.

That does not answer what happened.

It only proves someone wanted the answer buried.

A metallic clatter sounds behind her.

Lucian is at the rooftop entrance, watching the opposite buildings.

"Two minutes."

"Until what?"

"Until there is nothing left to examine."

She looks back at the scene.

She wants more time.

She wants the impossible thing investigators always want.

A clean answer.

Instead, she gets a rooftop designed to lie.

Sloane forces herself to move.

"C001."

Lucian looks at her.

"The casing from the first shot."

She pulls the fragment from her pocket, holding it beneath the weak emergency light.

"Frangible."

"Yes."

"You knew."

"I knew the ammunition requirements."

The words land harder than the gunfire.

Sloane slowly turns.

"Say that again."

Lucian remains still.

"You provided the operational instructions. Ammunition requirements. Timing."

Her grip tightens.

"I provided them."

"Yes."

"To you."

"Yes."

The rooftop suddenly feels smaller.

Because there are only two explanations.

Either Lucian is lying.

Or she did this.

She looks at the damaged contract still protected inside the evidence sleeve.

Her handwriting.

Her instructions.

Her death scheduled at sunrise.

Her stomach twists with cold anger.

"That is impossible."

"Is it?"

She hates the question because it does not sound cruel.

It sounds like he has asked it before.

"Someone copied my handwriting."

Lucian says nothing.

That silence is worse than agreement.

She steps closer.

"Tell me I am wrong."

He looks at her.

No.

He does not tell her.

The answer is there.

Not complete.

Not enough.

But enough to make her furious.

Sloane backs away.

"I would never arrange something like this."

Lucian's expression changes.

Only slightly.

But she catches it.

A reaction.

"You are very certain."

"Yes."

"You are also missing information."

"That is a charming way to describe a hole in my memory."

Another sound interrupts them.

A small mechanical buzz.

Then another.

Tiny devices moving through the rooftop vents.

Not searching.

Destroying.

The damaged contract sleeve begins to melt at the edge.

Sloane moves instantly.

She grabs the contract.

Lucian reaches for the evidence fragment.

Together, they pull back from the center of the roof as the cleanup mechanism spreads.

The scene disappears piece by piece.

Not erased by accident.

Erased with precision.

"C002," Sloane says.

Lucian looks over.

"The membrane."

She holds it up.

"Blood-pack evidence."

He studies it.

"You are saying the shooting was staged?"

"No."

The answer comes immediately.

Too immediately.

She hates that too.

"I am saying the evidence was manufactured."

A distinction.

A necessary one.

Because the truth is still behind a locked door.

They reach the access corridor.

A second lock blocks the way.

Sloane stares at it.

Her hand moves before she decides.

Circle.

Press.

Turn.

A sequence.

Mechanical.

Exact.

The lock releases.

She pulls her hand back like the door has bitten her.

Lucian watches her.

"How many things do you know without remembering?"

"Do not make that sound philosophical."

"It is not."

"Good."

"It is a problem."

She looks at him.

The honesty catches her off guard.

Not because he admits danger.

Because he admits concern.

The pistol remains in her hand.

Still pressed near his ribs as they move through the narrow corridor.

He notices.

He says nothing.

Most people would have reacted.

Most people would have disarmed her.

Lucian lets her keep the weapon there.

"Why?"

"Why what?"

"Why let me hold a gun against you?"

His eyes stay forward.

"You trusted me once."

The words strike somewhere she cannot reach.

A locked room inside her own mind.

She hates that.

She hates that his voice makes the missing pieces feel close.

Not safe.

Close.

They emerge into another section of the building, carrying only what survived.

The damaged contract.

The frangible fragment.

The torn blood-pack membrane.

Enough evidence to prove the roof was built to lie.

Not enough evidence to explain who built it.

Sloane keeps walking.

Her mind runs through every possibility.

Someone used her handwriting.

Someone used her knowledge.

Someone knew her methods.

And somehow, some part of her helped.

That is the piece she cannot ignore.

Lucian stays beside her.

Not ahead.

Not behind.

Beside.

She notices details because details are what keep people alive.

The way he never reaches for the weapon.

The way he never asks her to trust him.

The way he moves like he already knows exactly how far she will go before stopping.

A former version of herself knew him.

That thought arrives uninvited.

She rejects it.

Not because it is impossible.

Because it is dangerous.

They stop at a sealed maintenance room.

Sloane looks at the door.

"I did not know this existed."

Lucian says nothing.

Her hand rises toward the lock.

Again, before thought.

Again, before choice.

A circle.

A press.

A turn.

The door opens.

Inside sits a small concealed evidence case.

No label.

No visible handle.

Only one marking etched into the surface.

A forensic code.

Her forensic code.

Sloane stares at it.

The room suddenly feels colder.

"That belongs to me."

Lucian looks at the case.

"No."

She turns.

"What?"

His voice lowers.

"It was made for you."

Her fingers move toward the latch.

She does not tell them to move.

They move anyway.

The case clicks open beneath Sloane Mercer's conditioned fingers, and a recorder activates with Sloane Mercer's own voice saying, "If you do not remember Lucian Vey, do not let him tell you who he is."
