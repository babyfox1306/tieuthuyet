# Chapter 13: The Trap She Built

"The archive accepts the rhythm," Sloane Mercer said, watching the decryption bar advance across the last locked section. "And it's showing me something Rook didn't want me to see."

Lucian Vey stood at the service room door, rifle angled toward the corridor. His focus stayed on the hall, but his voice carried. "How long?"

"Thirty seconds."

The bar moved in segments, each one drawing a deeper line through the black zone that had resisted every other method. She had pressed her thumb against the screen and traced the hand rhythm from memory, the same five-beat sequence Mara had shown through the scope. The archive had recognized it instantly.

Sloane's wrist rotated once, the circle tightening as the first decrypted frame appeared.

It was not a text file.

A image resolved: seven documents laid side by side, each with a timestamp and an authorization code. Her stomach turned cold as she recognized the pattern. The toner voids on the margins of each file matched exactly—same printer, same drum defect, same master header. Rook had not drafted seven separate orders. He had altered one original and duplicated it six times.

C006 paid off.

"He changed them all from a single template," she said. "Every falsified target authorization. One source document."

Lucian stepped closer, gaze still split between the screen and the hallway. "Timestamps?"

"Altered forty-eight hours before each shooting." She scrolled through the metadata. "Same window. Same editing signature. He planned the sequence as a batch operation."

The decryption bar reached seventy percent.

A new layer activated. The screen emitted a low-frequency click, too quiet for the corridor to carry, but Sloane caught it through the device's speaker. The reverse-phase signal she had heard once before, during her first handler assessment, when she had been tested for auditory sensitivity.

C007 resolved.

Hidden inside the archive's architecture was a broadcast override. Not a leak—a deliberate channel. Her past self had built a secondary transmission path that bypassed the archive's normal output protocols. It could push the entire ledger to every connected node in Rook's network within seconds.

"If we had only accessed the fragment," Sloane murmured, "we would have seen the data. But we wouldn't have been able to transmit it."

"The override," Lucian said. It was not a question.

She nodded. "My past self designed it. She knew Rook would guard the method of release. So she buried it in a signal only the archive device could generate."

Eighty-five percent.

The bar became a straight line. The final layer unlocked.

A schematic appeared, overlaid with a location marker and a single instruction.

SLOANE — SERVICE ROOM 3. VENTILATION GRILLE. UNIT 4B.

She looked up. "There's something here."

Lucian crossed the room in three strides. He found the grille marked on the schematic, pried it open with the flat of his blade, and reached inside. His fingers emerged holding a sealed envelope.

Evidence-stock paper. The same weight as the contract.

Sloane took it carefully. Her thumb brushed the seal, and she felt two small perforations at exact spacing. She held it closer. Twin holes, punched precisely where her writing-grip calluses would align. A personalization no forger could replicate.

C009 paid off.

"This is mine," she said. "I prepared this."

She broke the seal. Inside was a small data chip and a folded note in her own handwriting: "Play me first. Then burn everything."

Sloane slotted the chip into the archive device. The screen flickered, then displayed a recording interface. Her own face appeared—her past self, seated in this same service room, the same ventilation grille behind her. The lighting was identical. The time stamp on the recording read three months ago.

"Hello, me," the recording began. "If you're watching this, the decryption worked. Rook is trying to restore the obedient identity he built in you. He will do it at sunrise with a frequency burst targeted at the conditioning architecture in your nervous system."

Sloane's throat tightened.

"I planned for that," the recording continued. "I staged Mara's death to give you cover. I hid the complete transaction ledger inside your own conditioned reflexes—your hand knows the rhythm, your wrist remembers the sequence. I erased my memory so Rook could not extract the truth. And I designed a broadcast that will expose his entire network."

The image paused. Then her past self leaned closer.

"The broadcast must originate from the rooftop where the shot was staged. Only Mara can trigger it with the hand rhythm. The location you are in now is the relay point. I placed the archive here because it is the only room in this building with a direct hardline to the network hub."

MR006 delivered. MR007 delivered.

Sloane felt Lucian's presence beside her, silent and still.

"One more thing," the recording said. "You loved him. I could not take that from you, even if you do not remember why."

The screen went dark.

Sloane stared at the black rectangle. The words settled into her chest like stones.

"You loved him."

She repeated them aloud, the sound flat and foreign.

Lucian's voice came low and rough. "You did."

She looked at him. The weight of his confession from earlier pressed against the air between them. She had no memory of that love, no sensation to anchor it. But her wrist began its circle, and she realized it was the same motion she used before she fired.

"You could have told me earlier," she said.

"You were not ready."

"And now?"

His jaw tightened. "You are still not ready. But you are closer."

The moment held. His hand moved, stopped, hovered an inch from her arm. She could feel the heat of his skin without contact, and it burned more than the touch itself.

A crash echoed from the ground floor.

Lucian turned instantly, rifle rising. Footsteps, multiple, heavy and disciplined. Voices distorted by radios.

"Rook's breach team," he said.

Sloane pocketed the archive device and scanned the room. One door. No windows. Metal shelving units bolted to the wall. The service hatch they had entered through was already sealed.

"We need to barricade," she said.

Lucian was already moving, shoving a steel cabinet toward the door. Sloane grabbed the second unit, her muscles screaming as they dragged it across the concrete. The door had no internal lock—only a push bar. Once the cabinets were in place, it would hold for minutes, not hours.

The footsteps grew louder.

Sloane checked the archive device. The complete ledger was loaded, the broadcast override armed. It displayed a single status line: AWAITING TRIGGER SEQUENCE.

"We have to wait for Mara's rhythm," she said.

Lucian finished bracing the last cabinet. He pulled the optic to his eye, training it on the door.

"They will try to breach through the walls," he said. "The structure is sheet metal."

"Then we need to finish this before they do."

Sloane crouched behind the cabinets, the device in her palm. The screen glowed the only light in the room.

The footsteps stopped outside.

Silence.

Then the overhead lights flickered.

A second later, the room went dark.

The archive's display remained, casting her reflection back at her. She looked at Lucian. In the pale blue glow, his face was carved in shadow and determination.

Then the recording's final words replayed in her memory:

"If you are hearing this, Rook is already trying to reach sunrise. Mara is the only one who can complete the relay. And I am sorry you will not remember the reason."

The lights did not return.

Sloane's wrist traced the circle, ready. The siege had begun.
