# Chapter 15: The Only Choice That Remains

Eleven. Twelve.

The ventilation grille gave way with a metallic screech that was swallowed by the heavy thrum of the servers below. Sloane Mercer counted the final beat in her head, the rhythm syncing with the thudding of her own heart. She perched on the edge of the service shaft, the cold metal biting into her knees, and looked down into the loading bay. That was all she needed. Twelve seconds of perfect timing to turn a suicide run into a calculated strike.

Her eyes locked onto the two figures in the center of the chaos. Lucian Vey was on his knees, his hands bound behind his back, blood dripping from a cut above his brow but his posture unbroken. Beside him, Mara was strapped to a relay terminal, the wires running from the machine to her sister's temples looking like IV lines for a machine that ate memories.

Rook stood over them, his back to the rafters, adjusting a dial on a portable frequency emitter.

Sloane tightened her grip on the archive device she had scavenged from the lab. Its weight was comforting, solid and real in a night that had felt like a fever dream. Below her, the power cable for the main broadcast terminal snaked across the floor. Even from this height, she could see the strip of medical tape wrapped around the rubber casing, the black permanent marker standing out against the white adhesive. Her own handwriting stared back at her.

IF YOU ARE READING THIS, YOU ALREADY KNOW WHAT TO DO.

A sharp, cynical laugh tried to claw its way up her throat. Trust her past self to be as cryptic in death as she was in life. But the instruction was clear. She knew what to do. She had to burn Rook's kingdom down to save the people inside it.

She checked her weapon. The modified suppressor was screwed tight. One shot. She had to sever the magnetic lock on Mara’s restraint without damaging the relay wire running through the cuff. If she nicked the wire, the connection severed, and the rhythm—Mara's pulse, the key to the broadcast—would be lost.

She slid over the edge, dropping soundlessly to a stack of crates, then to the floor. The shadows embraced her.

Ten feet away, Rook spoke, his voice smooth and condescending, amplified by the acoustics of the bay.

"It is fascinating, Lucian," Rook said, turning slightly. "You believe love is a variable that disrupts the equation. But love is just a chemical dependency. I can overwrite that. I have been overwriting it in Sloane for years. Sunrise is just the formality."

Lucian didn't reply. He just stared at the floor, his breathing controlled. But Sloane saw the tension in his shoulders, the way his muscles coiled. He was waiting.

"Initiate sequence," Rook commanded the technician at the console.

Sloane moved.

She didn't stand to fire. She rolled from behind the crates, coming up to one knee, the rifle stock pressed tight against her shoulder. The world narrowed to the scope. The magnetic lock on Mara’s wrist glowed red.

The wrist circle tic flared on her left hand, a burning itch that demanded she trace the pattern. She ignored it, forcing her focus into the reticle. She exhaled.

The shot cracked, sound shattering into a brief, sharp hiss.

The magnetic lock shattered, sparks showering Mara’s arm. The wire held. Mara gasped, her eyes flying open, but she didn't pull away. She knew the drill. She knew the wire was her lifeline.

"Security!" Rook roared, spinning around.

The distraction was total. The lights in the bay flickered as Sloane jammed the archive device into the auxiliary port she had spotted from the shaft.

"Uploading," she muttered to no one, her fingers flying across the holographic keypad.

The massive exterior screens that lined the loading bay walls, usually displaying Rook's weapon schematics or sterile efficiency metrics, flared to life. But they didn't show schematics. They showed the ledger. The decrypted, raw, unfiltered truth of his private network. Target names, bribes, falsified death certificates.

The guards froze. They stared at the screens, at the evidence of their crimes flashing in high-definition blood-red text. Chaos erupted. Shouts of confusion rose, orders conflicting, the discipline of Rook’s private army fracturing under the weight of public exposure.

"Kill her!" Rook screamed, abandoning the emitter. He drew a sidearm, aiming wildly at the rafters.

Sloane didn't run. She stood her ground in the center of the room, the archive device in one hand, her rifle in the other. She was the eye of the storm.

"Lucian," she called out. Her voice didn't waver.

Lucian moved. He wasn't bound by incompetence, only by plastic ties. He surged upward, the snap of the ties loud as he broke them with a burst of adrenaline-fueled strength. A guard lunged for him, but Lucian used the man's momentum, flipping him over his shoulder and slamming him into the console.

He didn't come for Sloane. He went for the transmitter.

Rook saw it. "Stop him!"

But Lucian was a blur of grim purpose. He reached the main console where Rook had been standing. His hand hovered over the frequency dial.

Sloane’s heart hammered against her ribs. She knew what Rook had been doing. The frequency emitter. It wasn't just a torture device. It was a trigger. For her.

A high-pitched whine pierced the air, audible only to her, it seemed, vibrating in her bones. The itch on her left wrist exploded into agony. Her hand spasmed. The circle tic wasn't just a habit anymore; it was a command. Her fingers began to twitch, tracing the circle against her will, the first motion of the firing sequence she had been conditioned to perform.

Her mind screamed, No.

Her body whispered, Kill target. Target is Lucian.

Her rifle barrel swung an inch to the left, toward Lucian’s back. Her finger tightened on the trigger.

"Not—today—" she gritted out, fighting her own muscles. It was like swimming upstream in a tsunami.

Lucian slammed his hand onto the console. He didn't shut it down. He keyed in a sequence. The surgical-film pleats from the lab report flashed through her mind—the delay instruction. C005.

He entered the twelve-second delay.

The whine in her ear stuttered. The invisible vice gripping her wrist loosened just enough. Her finger stopped short of the breakpoint. She gasped, air rushing back into her lungs, and forced the rifle barrel down toward the concrete floor.

Lucian turned to look at her. The chaos of the room—the shouting guards, the flashing ledger on the screens—seemed to vanish. There was only blood on his face, and a terrifying tenderness in his eyes.

He looked at the rifle she had almost pointed at him, then up at her eyes. He didn't speak. There were no words for this.

But she remembered. She remembered the rooftop. She remembered the things he had tried to say, the things she had been too afraid to hear.

Sloane Mercer, who never begged, who never softened, felt her throat tighten. She looked at him, really looked at him, and knew she wasn't doing this for the truth. She wasn't doing this for the ledger. She was doing this so this man could look at her tomorrow.

She didn't say the words. She just mouthed them, her lips forming the shape of the promise she had broken and now remade.

"You will."

You will live. You will walk away. You will see the sunrise.

Lucian’s eyes widened slightly. A ghost of a smile touched his lips, so fleeting it might have been a trick of the strobing lights. He nodded once. A shared verdict.

Then, Sloane turned her back on him. She turned to her sister.

Mara was still strapped to the chair, her eyes wide, watching the standoff. Her left hand was free, the wire dangling from the terminal. She looked terrified, but she was alive.

"Mara," Sloane said, her voice cutting through the noise. "The rhythm."

She didn't know why it worked. She only knew it was the key. The broadcast key. The way to get the data out of this room and into the world where Rook couldn't touch it.

"Do it, Mara. Now."

Mara lifted her hand. Her fingers trembled, but she began to tap. Against her thigh. A slow, steady beat. One, two, three-pause.

The archive device in Sloane's pocket chirped. It was receiving the signal. The progress bar on her wrist display lit up.

UPLOADING... 5%

Rook pushed through a ring of confused guards. He had regained his composure, his face a mask of cold fury. He saw Mara. He saw the tapping. He understood what was happening.

"Shoot the hand!" Rook ordered.

Sloane stepped in front of the terminal. She placed herself directly between Rook and her sister.

Her own wrist was still burning, the conditioned reflex fighting the delay Lucian had programmed. Every second was a war. Her left hand kept trying to rise, to complete the circle. She clenched it into a fist, knuckles white.

"Try it," Sloane snarled, raising her rifle. "You want to stop the broadcast? You have to go through me."

Rook didn't hesitate. He raised his weapon, aiming not at Sloane, but past her.

"No!" Sloane shouted, realizing too late his target.

Lucian was still at the main console. Rook wasn't aiming at the girl with the rhythm. He was aiming at the man who had bought Sloane twelve seconds of freedom.

Sloane pivoted, her body moving on instinct. She had to intercept. She had to fire.

But as she turned, the frequency emitter surged again. The delay Lucian had inserted was fighting a losing battle against the overpowering command signal. The pulse hit her like a physical blow to the chest.

The rhythm from Mara's hand—tap, tap, tap—echoed in her mind, but it began to warp. It sounded like a gunshot. It sounded like an order.

Her left hand flew open. The circle tic began, fast and frantic. Not a circle anymore.
