# Chapter 8: Seven Files That Should Match

Lucian Vey slammed the hydraulic pressure door on the armed pursuer's rifle as the barrel fired, the shot tearing into the metal frame instead of their bodies. "Decrypt the archive before this door takes my hand."

The door screamed against the force trying to reopen it.

Sloane Mercer dropped beside the stolen archive device, fingers already moving across the cracked interface. Behind her, Lucian held the hydraulic lock manually, his shoulder pressed against the reinforced panel while the mechanism fought to reset.

A person with better survival instincts would have questioned why she was crouched over a stolen data core while bullets were trying to make a new ventilation system through the door.

Sloane had apparently decided survival instincts were optional.

"How long?" Lucian asked.

"Depends."

"On what?"

"Whether Rook built this archive to protect information or punish anyone who touches it."

The device flashed.

ACCESS DENIED.

Sloane narrowed her eyes.

"Comforting."

Another impact struck the other side of the door.

Lucian did not move away.

The tendons in his arm tightened as he forced the hydraulic lever lower. His expression stayed controlled, but she noticed the slight change in his breathing.

Not fear.

Calculation.

He was measuring how long he could hold the door before the machine won.

Sloane hated that she noticed details like that.

It made people complicated.

Evidence was easier.

Evidence did not look at her like it knew things she had forgotten.

"Try the sequence again," Lucian said.

"You sound confident."

"I watched you open it before."

"You watched me accidentally activate a buried conditioning response."

"You opened it."

"That is a generous interpretation."

"It is the accurate one."

The answer irritated her because it was reasonable.

She entered the sequence again.

The archive rejected the first attempt.

Then the second.

Her jaw tightened.

The device contained partial copies from Cassian Rook's handler system, fragments recovered from the hidden archive beneath the loading bay. It was damaged, encrypted, and designed by someone who believed secrets should be locked behind layers of suspicion.

Rook.

Everything about him suggested control.

Control of files.

Control of people.

Control of memories.

The third attempt opened a limited access window.

Sloane froze.

Lucian noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"That was not nothing."

She ignored him and pulled the archive closer.

The first folder displayed a series of restricted records.

HANDLER AUTHORITY REVIEW.

TARGET FILE CONFLICTS.

CONDITIONING PROTOCOLS.

Her stomach tightened.

Not because the words surprised her.

Because some part of her recognized the structure.

The arrangement.

The way the information was categorized.

She had seen systems like this before.

Maybe not this one.

Maybe not under this name.

But the logic was familiar.

Someone had built a machine for making decisions and hiding responsibility inside layers of paperwork.

Sloane scrolled.

Seven target files appeared.

Each contained conflicting authorization entries.

Two different approval chains.

Two different explanations for the same assignments.

Two years of records.

Two years of someone changing outcomes without changing the official trail.

"Rook altered files," she said.

Lucian glanced back briefly.

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

"How?"

"Because whoever did this understood the system."

She enlarged one record.

"The changes were not random. They were placed inside existing approvals. Someone wanted the files to look untouched unless you knew where to search."

The door shook again.

Lucian adjusted his stance.

The pressure alarm above them began flashing.

Sloane looked at him.

"Your hand."

"It is fine."

"It is currently being crushed by a hydraulic mechanism."

"It has been worse."

"That is not reassuring."

"It was not intended to be."

She almost laughed.

Almost.

Instead, she returned to the archive.

The conditioning folder opened halfway.

Most of it was corrupted.

Session records.

Behavioral triggers.

Response mapping.

Subject designation.

Her name appeared.

SLOANE MERCER.

The air inside the narrow channel seemed to become heavier.

She stared at the screen.

A file about herself.

Written by someone else.

Stored like she was a piece of equipment.

"How much did you know?" she asked.

Lucian did not answer immediately.

That pause was worse than a lie.

Sloane turned.

"Lucian."

"I knew Rook used conditioning procedures."

The admission landed harder than she expected.

"You knew."

"Yes."

"And you said nothing."

"I did not know how much remained."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the one I have."

The door groaned.

Lucian's focus stayed on the pressure mechanism, but his voice lowered.

"I was afraid of what finding the answer would do."

Sloane studied him.

Lucian Vey did not sound afraid often.

He sounded like someone admitting a failure he had carried too long.

That bothered her more than anger would have.

"You were afraid."

"Yes."

"Of me?"

His eyes moved to hers.

For one second, the chaos around them disappeared.

Then he looked away.

"Of what was done to you."

Sloane looked back at the archive.

She did not know what to do with that answer.

So she did what she always did.

She looked for proof.

The conditioning protocol contained instructions for reinforcement patterns and compliance responses. Most of the technical details were missing, but enough remained to show intent.

The system was not designed to erase a person.

It was designed to create a version of a person that could follow commands under specific conditions.

A manufactured obedience layer.

Sloane felt cold anger settle behind her ribs.

"Someone spent years building this."

Lucian's voice was quiet.

"Yes."

"And you knew."

"I knew parts."

"Convenient."

"No."

The answer came too quickly.

Sloane looked at him.

Lucian's expression did not change.

"I should have found more."

That was not an excuse.

That was worse.

Because he meant it.

She returned to the archive before the conversation could become something she was not prepared to examine.

The files contained another section.

DISPUTED AUTHORIZATION RECORDS.

Seven sheets appeared as scanned images.

Sloane opened them one by one.

The approvals matched.

The signatures matched.

The dates matched.

Too perfectly.

Her eyes narrowed.

"That is wrong."

Lucian looked over.

"What?"

"These should not match."

She enlarged the scans.

The authorization sheets appeared identical in every meaningful way.

Same spacing.

Same formatting.

Same approval marks.

But the imperfections were different.

Not enough for anyone casually reviewing the files.

Enough for her.

A small crescent-shaped void appeared near the edge of each page.

No toner.

No ink.

A missing line in the print.

She zoomed closer.

The mark was no wider than an eyelash.

It repeated across all seven sheets.

Different locations.

Same shape.

"Look."

Lucian leaned closer.

"What am I seeing?"

"A machine defect."

"That seems ordinary."

"It would be, if it appeared once."

She moved between the files.

"The imaging process leaves patterns. Damage. Dust. Alignment errors."

"And this?"

"This appeared seven times."

Her finger hovered above the screen.

"Same defect. Different locations."

She frowned.

"All seven records may have come from one altered master."

"Meaning?"

"Meaning someone created a source version, then reproduced it."

"Who?"

She stared at the empty metadata field.

"Unknown."

"Why?"

"Also unknown."

She hated those words.

Unknown.

Unknown.

Unknown.

Every answer in her life currently arrived wearing a locked door.

She copied the files.

Seven disputed authorization sheets.

Partial conditioning protocol.

Everything accessible.

Then she found the waveform record.

The session data was incomplete, but one file remained readable.

A calibration sequence.

She opened it.

A visual waveform appeared.

Seven regular pulses.

Then another.

Sloane leaned closer.

"That is strange."

Lucian looked at the display.

"What?"

"The pattern."

"Explain."

She shook her head.

"Not yet."

The first seven clicks followed the documented calibration sequence.

The eighth was different.

The waveform moved in reverse phase.

A mirrored pattern.

Not matching the others.

Not mentioned in the protocol.

"Was this supposed to be there?" Lucian asked.

"No."

"How do you know?"

"Because someone who documents everything does not accidentally forget an eighth signal."

The archive suddenly flickered.

The screen distorted.

Sloane looked up.

"That was not me."

The device attempted to reconnect.

A warning appeared.

REMOTE ACCESS DETECTED.

Lucian released the pressure lever for half a second, then forced it back down as the door shifted.

"We need to move."

"I need another thirty seconds."

"You have ten."

"That is a terrible estimate."

"It is the only one available."

She grabbed the copied files.

The device transferred the remaining accessible data.

Complete enough.

Not complete enough.

The worst possible category.

Sloane secured the archive.

The door behind Lucian bent inward.

He turned the rifle focus ring exactly one quarter rotation away from clarity before shifting his position.

She noticed again.

The ritual.

The restraint.

The fact that he always did it before following an order he hated.

She did not know why that mattered.

She only knew it did.

The pressure door finally gave.

Not open.

Just enough.

Enough for a weapon barrel to push through.

Lucian moved.

Sloane moved with him.

They crossed into the next freight channel as the door collapsed behind them.

The passage was narrow and filled with old equipment rails. Their footsteps echoed through the industrial corridor while the stolen device continued copying the final fragments.

Sloane checked the transfer.

"Seven files saved."

"Good."

"The waveform saved."

"Good."

"The conditioning protocol saved."

Lucian looked at her.

"Good."

She hated how much relief appeared in his voice.

Not because of the files.

Because she was still standing.

"Do not look at me like that."

"Like what?"

"Like you are waiting for me to disappear."

His expression changed slightly.

Only slightly.

But she saw it.

"I am not."

"You are."

A silence passed between them.

Then the archive device made a sharp sound.

The transfer stopped.

The screen went black.

Sloane's eyes narrowed.

"Rook."

Lucian looked toward the device.

"Can he access it?"

"Apparently."

The screen returned.

Every saved file was still there.

But the folders began changing.

One by one.

Deleting.

Not physically.

Rewriting.

A corruption pattern.

Someone was reaching through the system and destroying the evidence before they could read more.

Sloane grabbed the device.

"No."

She copied the final directory manually.

The process slowed.

The freight channel lights flickered.

Then the device froze.

For one second, there was nothing.

No movement.

No warning.

No sound.

Then the speaker activated.

Cassian Rook's recorded voice filled the corridor.

Calm.

Precise.

Familiar.

"Circle, acquire, correct."

Sloane's body moved before her mind understood why.

Her left hand reached toward Lucian.

Not toward his arm.

Not toward his shoulder.

Toward the weapon at his side.

Her fingers closed around Lucian Vey's sidearm before Sloane Mercer consciously decided to move.
