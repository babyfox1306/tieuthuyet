# Chapter 15: The Decision

The safehouse was cold, but the silence between them was colder. Sloane Mercer pressed her thumb into the palm of her left hand and felt the answer rise through her bones.

Her body knew things her mind couldn't touch.

The circle on her wrist itched. Not a nervous tic anymore. A compulsion. Her thumb traced the inside of her left wrist once, twice, a third time, and then her feet moved without her consent. She crossed the room, past the chipped table where Lucian sat with his rifle across his knees, and stopped at the wall panel beside the dead fireplace.

"Sloane."

She didn't answer. Her hand pressed against the wood, found the seam her fingertips already knew existed. She pulled. The panel swung open on silent hinges, revealing a shallow compartment lined with gray felt.

The same felt as her jacket pocket.

She reached inside. Her fingers closed around a flat plastic card. Standard access key. No markings. But the weight of it was familiar in a way that made her stomach drop.

She didn't remember hiding this.

But her hands had remembered for her.

"What did you find?"

Lucian's voice behind her. Close. She turned and held up the card. "The way in."

He studied it without touching it. His jaw was tight, the muscles working beneath the stubble. The safehouse had one small window, and the dark outside pressed against it like a held breath.

"Rook knows you have a card," he said. "He knows what it accesses."

"He knows I had something. But he doesn't know I don't remember where I put it." She slipped the key card into her jacket pocket. "My past self was thorough. She didn't trust anyone. Not even me."

"She trusted you enough to build you a trail."

"She built me a maze." Sloane leaned against the wall, suddenly exhausted. The adrenaline from the rooftop, from the church, from the warehouse—it was all crashing into the same hollow space behind her ribs. "She buried everything so deep that only my spinal column could find it."

Lucian set the rifle aside and stood. The room felt smaller with him on his feet. He crossed to her, close enough that she could smell the cordite and cold air still clinging to his coat.

"Sloane."

The way he said her name. Like it cost him something.

"What happens when we reach that terminal?" he asked.

"We get Mara back."

"And if Rook has already restored the obedient identity?"

She looked at him. The binding condition sat between them like a physical weight. She could feel the shape of it in the air. Lucian must kill Sloane at sunrise only if Rook restores the obedient identity.

He was asking her if she was willing to die.

Or asking her for permission to kill her.

She didn't know which one hurt more.

"Did you ever find the woman I used to be?" she asked instead of answering.

"I found glimpses." His voice was rough. "In the way you hold a pen. In the way you laugh when something is so terrible it stops being funny and starts being absurd. In the way you don't beg."

"That's not the same."

"No. But every time I see a piece of her in you, I understand why I fell in love with her."

The words hit her like a blade between the ribs. She didn't look away. Couldn't.

"Don't," she said. "Don't make this into something it isn't. I'm not her. I'm the version she made so she could survive. I don't remember loving you. I don't remember you loving me. I just have a body that flinches when you get too close, and a wrist that burns every time I think about the contract I wrote."

He reached up. Slow. Giving her every chance to pull away.

His palm cupped her cheek.

The warmth of his hand was a shock against her cold skin. Her breath caught. She should step back. She should focus on the mission. Mara was out there, held by a man who had spent two years feeding her false targets.

But his thumb traced the line of her cheekbone, and the world stopped tilting.

She leaned into his touch.

Just for a moment. Just long enough to remember what it felt like to be someone who deserved tenderness.

"I trust her," Lucian said quietly. "The woman who built this. She knew what she was doing. She knew the cost. And she still chose you to finish it."

"I don't know if I can."

"That's why she erased you. If you knew the plan, Rook would have ripped it out of your head. You have to find it in the dark."

Sloane closed her eyes. His thumb traced her lower lip once, featherlight, and then he let his hand fall.

The cold rushed back in.

She pulled the key card from her pocket and looked at it. The plastic was warm from her body heat. Married to her. Planted by a woman who had trusted her own erased self to finish a war.

"This card accesses the terminal's central server," she said. "Full transaction records. Every shipment, every payoff, every name Rook tried to bury. I stored the complete ledger there."

"How do you know if you don't remember?"

"Because she—I—left notes." Sloane tapped her temple. "Not in my memory. In my muscle memory. The wrist circle isn't just a tic. It's the firing sequence. It's the confirmation code. It's the key to the whole system."

"Rook will expect you to try to retrieve it."

"He's counting on it. He wants me to lead him straight to the data so he can destroy it."

"Then why go?"

"Because Mara is there." Sloane's voice hardened. "And because I didn't go through all of this—erasing my own mind, staging my sister's death, writing a contract that ends with a bullet in my chest—just to let him win."

Lucian picked up the rifle. Checked the load. "What's the plan?"

"We walk in. We trade the key card for Mara."

"He won't let us walk out."

"I know. But it buys me time to access the server before he can scrub it."

"Time you don't have. The sun—"

"I know what time it is." She met his eyes. "I know what happens if Rook manages to trigger the obedient identity. I wrote that condition, Lucian. I knew what I was asking you to do."

"And you still want to walk into his trap?"

"Yes." She crossed to the door and grabbed her coat. "Because I'm done running from the woman I used to be. She trusted me to finish this. I'm going to honor that."

He held her gaze for a long moment. Something shifted in his expression. Resignation. Respect. She couldn't tell which.

"Then let's go."

The car was a nondescript sedan parked in the alley behind the safehouse. Lucian drove. Sloane sat in the passenger seat with the key card balanced on her knee, her fingers tracing the circle against her wrist.

The streets blurred past. Dark warehouses. Chain-link fences. The industrial district stretched out toward the waterfront, where the shipping terminal rose against the pre-dawn sky. She tried to remember this place. The way the air would smell. Salt water. Diesel. Rust.

But her memory was a wall.

She had been here. She had stored the ledger here. She had built her last stand here.

And she couldn't remember a single second of it.

Her thumb pressed harder into her wrist. The circle burned. It was a voice speaking in a language only her nerves understood, telling her she was exactly where she was supposed to be.

Floodlights cut through the dark, stark white against the deep blue of the approaching morning.

Sloane checked her weapon.

The shipping terminal appeared on the horizon, floodlights cutting through the pre-dawn dark.

Sloane Mercer checked her weapon.

"We get Mara. Then we burn the whole thing down."
