# Chapter 6: The Second Shadow

"There's more," Mara whispered, pulling a bundle of yellowed letters from the open drawer. Her fingers trembled as she unfolded the first page, the ink bleeding into the thin paper like old bruises.

Jude moved closer, his shoulder brushing hers. The warmth of him was a shock against the cold that leaked through the old walls. "What does it say?"

She scanned the cramped cursive. The date was 1989. Four years before she was born.

*My dearest Ilse,*

*The house is everything you promised. The silence here is a blessing. I can think. I can breathe. I look out from the attic window and I see our future. Please, don't keep me waiting. Don't make me wait in this beautiful cage alone.*

*Ever yours,*
*Lachlan*

Mara's throat tightened. "He was here. Before I was born. He knew the house."

She pulled out another letter. And another. Each one more obsessed than the last. Lachlan writing to Ilse about the garden, the basement, the view from the attic. The way he described the house—it wasn't love. It was hunger.

"The lower rooms smell of earth and secrets," she read aloud. "I feel like a king in his tomb."

She looked up at Jude. "My father called himself a king. In a tomb."

"Doesn't sound stable."

"No shit." Her laugh was hollow, brittle. She pulled out her phone, ignoring the no-signal icon that flickered in the corner of the screen.

"Don't do it if you're not ready for the answer," Jude said quietly.

"I've spent my whole life not being ready. It didn't help."

She pressed call.

One ring. Two. Three.

Voicemail.

Her mother's voice, stiff and distant. *"You've reached Lydia. Leave a message."*

The beep cut through the silence like a knife.

"Mom. It's me." Mara's voice cracked. "I know we don't talk. I know you hate this place. But I'm here. At Ilse's house. I found letters. Lachlan's letters. Is he my father? Please. I need you to tell me the truth. I need you to pick up the phone. I'm scared, Mom. I'm really scared."

The line went dead.

She stared at the screen. The silence in the room was crushing. The old radiators knocked once, twice, like a heartbeat that didn't belong to the house.

"He wrote to her for years," Mara said, her voice flat. "She never told me. She sent me here to clean out a dead woman's secrets, and she never told me my father was in love with her sister."

Jude's hand found hers. His fingers were warm, steady. "We can leave. Right now."

"And go where? Pretend I didn't see any of this?"

Before he could answer, the room went cold.

Not a draft. A sudden, absolute drop in temperature that hit her like a wall of ice. Her breath fogged in the dim light.

The lamp on the nightstand flickered. Once. Twice.

Mara's blood turned to liquid nitrogen.

In the corner, between the wardrobe and the window, the darkness thickened. It took shape. Not the small form of the girl in blue. This was taller. Broader. Male.

It stood perfectly still, absorbing the light around it, a void in the shape of a man.

"Jude," she whispered, her voice barely a breath.

He was already on his feet, standing between her and the shape. But what good was his body against a shadow?

The shape shifted. An arm rose. A finger pointed.

At her.

The terror was a physical blow. Her mind screamed for logic, for an explanation, but there was nothing rational left. There was only this—the cold, the dark, and the thing that watched her.

Then Jude's hand was at her waist, spinning her around. His mouth crashed onto hers. No gentleness. No careful kindness. A raw, claiming force that stole her breath.

The kiss tasted like fear and adrenaline. She didn't pull away. She clawed at his shoulders, pulling him closer, needing the solid heat of him to burn away the phantom.

"Don't look at it," he growled against her mouth. "Look at me."

His hands found the hem of her shirt. He yanked it over her head, buttons scattering across the floor. The cold air bit her skin, but his mouth was hot on her neck, her collarbone, her chest.

She tore at his belt, her fingers clumsy and frantic. The metal clinked. The leather gave way. His jeans pooled at his ankles, and she didn't wait. She wrapped her hand around him, feeling him hard and ready against her palm.

He groaned, a sound torn from somewhere deep. "Mara—"

"Now." She didn't recognize her own voice. "I need you now."

He lifted her. Her back hit the wall, the old plaster cold against her flushed skin. She wrapped her legs around his waist, gasping as the head of him pressed against her, slick and blunt and urgent.

Their eyes locked. His were dark, pupils blown wide, the mask of calm completely shattered.

He drove into her in one brutal, perfect thrust.

The air left her lungs. She was full, stretched around his width, anchored to this moment by the exquisite pressure of it. He didn't pause. He didn't wait for her to adjust. He moved. Hard. Fast. A frantic rhythm born of pure adrenaline and the desperate need to feel alive.

His breath was a hot rasp against her ear. "You feel so good. So fucking tight."

She couldn't speak. She could only feel. The scrape of the wall on her back. The raw slap of skin on skin. The wet heat of him driving into her again and again and again.

"Look at me," he demanded.

She forced her eyes open. His face was clenched, a muscle jumping in his jaw. He was undone. Raw. Hungry.

"I'm here," he said, the words punched out of him with a deep thrust. "I'm real."

The coil in her belly snapped. She climaxed with a ragged cry, her whole body clenching around him, her nails raking down his spine. He followed her over the edge, groaning her name as he buried himself deep and spilled into her.

They stayed there, bound together, breathing harshly into the dark.

The cold was gone. The shadow had retreated. Only the sound of their hearts remained.

Jude lowered her slowly, her legs barely holding her. He cupped her face in both hands, tilting her chin up. His forehead pressed against hers.

"I'm here," he whispered again. "Whatever this house throws at you. Whatever ghosts you have. I'm here."

She believed him. For a moment, she let herself believe him.

Then she felt it.

A presence. Small. Cold.

She turned her head.

The girl in blue stood in the doorway.

She was holding Wick. The one-eyed bear dangled from her small hand, its glass eye catching the faint light from the hallway.

The girl didn't speak. She just looked at Mara with her wide, empty eyes. Then she turned and walked down the hall.

Mara dressed in a hurry, pulling on her torn shirt, yanking up her jeans. Jude followed without question.

"She wants to show me something."

They followed the girl through the dark hallway, past the locked basement door, through the kitchen. The back door was ajar, swinging gently in the cold wind.

The girl led them into the garden. The grass was dead and wet under their bare feet. The air smelled of rot and damp earth. The moon hung low and pale, casting long shadows across the overgrown lawn.

The girl stopped at the old oak tree. She pointed up at the branches.

Then she was gone.

"What is she trying to tell us?" Jude asked.

Mara looked up. Something dark shifted behind the trunk.

The shadow stepped out from behind the oak, and Mara saw it had no face—just a dark silhouette with a hand raised, pointing back at the house.
