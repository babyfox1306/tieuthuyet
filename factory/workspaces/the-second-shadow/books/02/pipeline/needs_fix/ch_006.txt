# Chapter 6: The Confession

Mara found Asher in the library, holding Julian's letter like it was a loaded weapon. "Tell me the truth," she said. "Did you come here to destroy my family?"

The words hung between them, sharp as broken glass. Asher's jaw tightened, and he set the letter down with the careful precision of a man defusing a bomb. The brass acorn on Ilse's keychain caught the lamplight as Mara's hand instinctively went to her scarred palm.

"No." His voice was steady, but something flickered behind his eyes. "I came here to find out what happened to my uncle. That's all I wanted."

"Your uncle." Mara stepped closer, the floorboards groaning beneath her boots. "The man in the letters. The man who was supposed to meet Ilse that summer."

Asher ran his fingers along his jawline, a gesture she was beginning to recognize. "Julian Croft. My mother's younger brother. He was an artist, a photographer. He went to document the Vale estate for an architecture magazine, and he never came home."

"And you think my family had something to do with that."

"I don't know what I think anymore." He looked at her, and for the first time, she saw the exhaustion beneath his calm surface. "For twenty-six years, my family believed he'd abandoned us. No body, no explanation—just a note saying he'd be back in a week, and then silence. My grandmother died waiting for him to call."

Mara's throat tightened. She remembered the photograph in Ilse's album—the man with the camera, smiling at something just outside the frame. "Ilse never mentioned him. Not once."

"Would you?" Asher's voice cracked slightly. "If someone you loved disappeared without a trace, would you talk about it?"

The radiator in the corner knocked twice, a sound like someone rapping on a door. Mara flinched.

"I should have told you sooner," Asher continued. "When Gerald mentioned your aunt's name at the funeral home, I recognized it. I'd been searching for Julian for years—hiring private investigators, tracking down anyone who might have known him. Ilse Vale was the last person he photographed."

"And you thought I'd lead you to answers."

"I thought you deserved the truth." He took a step toward her, then stopped. "But I didn't know how to tell you without sounding like I was using you. Which I was, at first. Until I met you."

The radiator knocked again, three times this time. The sound seemed to come from inside the walls.

"Tell me everything," Mara said. "From the beginning."

Asher pulled out a chair at the library table, its legs scraping against the wood floor. Mara sat across from him, the letter between them like a boundary line.

"Julian was thirty-two when he disappeared," Asher began. "He'd been struggling with depression—my mother thought the trip might help him. The Vale estate was famous in architectural circles; the Gothic Revival detailing, the original ironwork. He was supposed to spend a week photographing the house, then move on to the next assignment."

"But he never moved on."

"No. He stayed for three weeks, according to his journal. My mother found it in his apartment after he vanished." Asher reached into his jacket and pulled out a worn leather notebook, its pages yellowed with age. "He wrote about Ilse. About how she was different from anyone he'd ever met. About how he wanted to stay."

Mara took the journal, her fingers brushing against Asher's. The touch sent a jolt through her, electric and unexpected.

"He fell in love with her," she said, flipping through the pages. The handwriting was elegant, the ink fading to brown. "That's what the letter says too."

"Read the last entry," Asher said quietly.

She found it near the back of the journal, dated August 3rd—the week Mara had come to stay with Ilse.

*I saw something tonight. I shouldn't have been in the attic, but I heard the crying and followed it. There's a child up there—the little girl who arrived yesterday. She was holding a bear, rocking it, whispering to it like it could hear. But it wasn't the child that frightened me. It was the woman who came after her.*

*Lydia Vale doesn't know I saw her. She doesn't know I watched her take the bear from the child's hands and tell the girl to forget. "It never happened," she said. "You never saw anything. If you remember, something terrible will happen to you."*

*The little girl just nodded. She didn't cry. She didn't fight. She looked at her mother like she was already dead.*

*I have to leave tomorrow. I have to tell someone. But Ilse made me promise I wouldn't. She's protecting her sister—or maybe she's protecting the girl. I don't know anymore. All I know is that something is very wrong in this house.*

Mara's hands trembled. The letter blurred before her eyes.

"She was eight," she whispered. "I was eight years old."

"Your mother told you to forget something." Asher's voice was gentle, almost fragile. "Do you know what it was?"

"No." The word came out broken. "I don't remember anything from that summer. Nothing. Ilse used to say I was 'too sensitive,' that I'd blocked it out. But I never believed her."

The radiator knocked again, louder this time. The lights flickered.

"It's getting worse," Mara said, looking around the room. "Every day I'm here, the sounds get louder. The shadows get longer. The girl in blue keeps appearing."

"The girl in blue?"

"She looks like me. When I was small." Mara's voice dropped to a whisper. "She wants me to find something. A bear. A one-eyed bear named Wick."

Asher's expression shifted—recognition, then concern. "Julian mentioned a bear in his journal. A doll the little girl was holding."

"Wick," Mara repeated. "I had him when I was a child. I don't remember where he came from, but I know he was important. And I know my mother took him from me."

"Where is he now?"

"I don't know." Mara looked toward the ceiling, toward the attic they'd discovered the day before. "But Ilse knew. She kept journals—detailed notes about everything. She must have written down where the bear is."

The lights flickered again, and a cold draft swept through the library. The temperature dropped so fast Mara could see her breath.

"She's here," she said, not daring to look at the corner of the room. "The girl in blue. She's standing by the window."

Asher turned, but there was nothing there. Only the moonlight streaming through the cracked pane, casting the room in a pale blue glow.

"She doesn't show herself to everyone," Mara said. "Only to me."

"Why you?"

"Because she is me." The words came out before she could stop them. "She's the part of me I lost. The part that remembers."

Asher reached across the table and took her hand. His fingers were warm against her cold skin. "Then we find the bear together. We find out what happened that summer."

"You don't have to do this."

"I know." He squeezed her hand, and something in her chest loosened. "But I want to. I've spent twenty-six years chasing ghosts. I don't want to chase them alone anymore."

Mara looked at him, at the sincerity in his eyes, and she felt the walls she'd built begin to crumble. She'd spent so long refusing to rely on anyone, believing that forming attachments only led to loss. But Asher was here, holding her hand, offering to walk into the darkness with her.

"I'm scared," she admitted.

"Good." He almost smiled. "That means you're paying attention."

The radiator knocked three times, and a sound came from above—soft at first, then more insistent. Footsteps. Small, light footsteps, pacing back and forth across the attic floor.

Mara pulled her hand away and stood, her legs unsteady. "Ilse's hidden box. I found it in the attic wall yesterday. I didn't open it—I was too afraid."

"Afraid of what?"

"Of what I might find." She headed for the stairs, Asher following close behind. "But I can't run anymore. I need to know."

The attic was dark, the only light coming from the bare bulb that hung from the ceiling. The box was where she'd left it, hidden behind a loose board in the wall. She pulled it out, her fingers trembling as she lifted the lid.

Inside was a single key, tarnished with age, and a note.

*Mara—*

*If you're reading this, I'm gone. I'm sorry I never told you the truth. I thought I was protecting you. I thought if I kept you away from this house, you'd be safe.*

*But the truth has a way of finding us, no matter how far we run.*

*The bear is in the root cellar beneath the summer kitchen. I hid it there the night you came to stay with me. I wanted to burn it. I wanted to bury it so deep no one would ever find it. But I couldn't. The bear carries too much weight.*

*You don't remember what happened that summer because your mother made you forget. She made you split yourself in two—the part that saw, and the part that forgot. The bear holds the part you lost.*

*If you're reading this, it means you're ready to remember. But be careful, my darling. Some truths are heavy enough to break you.*

*Find the bear. Find yourself.*

*I love you always,*
*Ilse*

Mara's vision blurred with tears. She read the note again, and then a third time, the words burning into her memory.

"She knew," she said, her voice breaking. "She knew what happened, and she kept it from me to protect me."

Asher was silent beside her, his hand resting on her shoulder.

"My mother swore me to secrecy when I was eight years old." Mara's voice dropped to a whisper. "She told me if I remembered, something terrible would happen. And I believed her. I believed her so much I forgot everything."

She turned to face him, and the tears spilled over her cheeks. "But I'm not afraid anymore. I've been hiding my entire life, and I'm done."

Asher pulled her into his arms, and she collapsed against him, her body shaking with sobs. He held her tight, his chin resting on her head, his hands firm against her back.

"You're the bravest person I've ever met," he said into her hair. "And whatever we find—we find it together."

Mara clung to him, the warmth of his body seeping through her skin. For a moment, the world fell away. There was no ghost, no mystery, no past waiting to destroy her. There was only this—two broken people holding each other in the dark.

When she finally pulled back, her face was wet, but her eyes were clear.

"The bear," she said, wiping her cheeks with the back of her hand. "We have to find the bear before my mother does."

Asher's jaw tightened. "Your mother is coming here?"

"She's always been here. She never really left." Mara slipped the key into her pocket. "She's the one who made me forget. She's the one who's been haunting this house."

The radiator knocked three times.

From the attic above, a child's footsteps began to pace.
