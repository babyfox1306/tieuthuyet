# Chapter 4: The Neighbor's Warning

Gerald's fist hit the doorframe before he stepped into the hall, his boots tracking mud across the clean floor.

The sound cracked through the silence like a gunshot, making the dust motes dancing in the afternoon light seem to freeze mid-air. I jumped, my heart hammering a frantic rhythm against my ribs, and my left hand instinctively curled into a fist, rubbing the jagged scar tissue that ran along my palm. It was a reflex, a useless shield I hadn't been able to put down since I was eight years old.

Standing in the doorway, Gerald Vance filled the frame with a bulk that had nothing to do with muscle and everything to do with aggressive posture. He smelled of wet earth, motor oil, and something faintly metallic, like old pennies. His gaze darted from me to Asher, lingering on the space between us with an accusation that hung heavy and unsaid.

"You're making a lot of noise for a dead-end street," Gerald said. His voice was gravel grinding on pavement.

I straightened my spine, forcing my shoulders down. I wasn't that scared little girl anymore, no matter how much the house tried to convince me otherwise. "Mr. Vance. Was there something you needed? Or do you just enjoy kicking in doorframes?"

He stepped fully into the hallway, ignoring my question. His eyes, small and bead-like, swept over the exposed floorboards, the half-opened boxes, and finally landed on the attic stairs. "I heard you up there. Thumping around."

"We're cleaning," I said, keeping my voice flat. "My aunt left a mess. We're just sorting through it."

"Some things aren't meant to be sorted, Mara." He used my name like a warning label. He took another step forward, the mud on his soles making a wet, sucking sound against the hardwood. I glanced at his hands. They were stained black with engine grease, ground deep into the cuticles and creases of his knuckles. But it was his left hand that caught my attention—specifically, the pale strip of skin around his ring finger where a wedding band had sat for years, now absent. The tan line was stark, a ghost of a life he'd recently shed or lost.

"It's my house, Gerald. What I sort through is my business." I crossed my arms, hating the defensive note in my tone. Hating that I had to defend my right to exist in my own family home to the man who used to borrow sugar from Ilse.

Asher moved. He didn't say a word, just shifted his weight, placing himself slightly between Gerald and me. It wasn't a aggressive stance, but it was deliberate. A barrier. He ran his fingers along his jawline, his eyes calm, assessing, locked on Gerald.

"Is there a problem, neighbor?" Asher asked. His tone was polite, but it had that dry, sharp edge to it, like a knife wrapped in velvet.

Gerald turned his glare on Asher, looking him up and down with blatant disdain. "You the new handyman? Or just another stray she picked up?"

"Consultant," Asher corrected smoothly. "And Mr. Vance, tracking mud into a historical home isn't the best way to make friends."

Gerald ignored the jab. He leaned in, the smell of grease intensifying. "You think you're smart, don't you? Coming in here, asking questions. But let me tell you something, kid. This house doesn't like questions. It eats people who ask too many."

A cold draft swept down the hallway, despite the summer warmth outside. It whispered past my ankles, carrying the scent of stale air and something like rotting wood. I suppressed a shudder. "Are you threatening us?"

"I'm warning you." Gerald pointed a grease-stained finger at me. "Ilse knew when to stop. She knew which doors to keep locked. You keep pulling on those loose threads, and you're going to unravel the whole damn sweater. And guess who gets cut when the strings snap?"

He stepped back, his boots leaving dark, wet prints on the floorboards I had spent all morning scrubbing. The sight of it sparked a flare of irrational anger. I cleaned this. I was trying to make it right.

"Leave," I said, my voice dropping an octave. "Get off my property."

Gerald held my gaze for a long second, his eyes twitching. For a moment, I thought he was going to say more. He looked like a man holding onto a secret that was physically choking him. But then he grunted, spat on the floor—a glob of saliva that landed dangerously close to my boot—and turned on his heel.

"Don't say I didn't warn you," he muttered, stomping back down the porch steps. The screen door slammed shut behind him, the finality of it echoing through the empty house.

I let out a breath I didn't realize I'd been holding. My knees felt watery, a traitorous weakness that made me grip the doorframe to steady myself. I looked down at the mud prints. "Great. Just great."

"He's charming," Asher said dryly, turning to face me. The tension in his shoulders had evaporated the moment Gerald left, his calm demeanor sliding back into place as if it had never been disturbed.

"A real delight." I rubbed my scarred hand again, the skin feeling tight and sensitive. "He knows something, Asher. He's terrified of this house."

"Or terrified of what's in it," Asher agreed. He looked toward the pantry, then back at me. "Are you okay?"

"I'm fine." The automatic response was out before I could think about it. Denial was my native language. "I just hate people walking in here like they own the place."

"He's angry," Asher observed. "And defensive. Did you notice his hands?"

"The grease?" I asked, though my mind flashed to the pale strip on his finger.

"The grease, yes. But the way he kept them clenched. Like he was hiding something." Asher stepped closer. The air between us felt charged, heavy with the adrenaline of the confrontation. "He's not just a grumpy neighbor, Mara. He's a guard dog."

"Guarding what?" I challenged. "Dust and bad memories?"

"Something worse." Asher reached out. I flinched automatically, pulling back, but his hand didn't stop. It settled gently on my shoulder. His palm was warm, firm, and incredibly grounding.

I froze. Years of conditioning screamed at me to pull away, to retreat behind the walls of logic and solitude where no one could hurt me. I didn't do touch. Touch implied trust, and trust implied loss. But as I stood there, looking at the mud on the floor and feeling the phantom cold of the house pressing in, I found myself leaning into him. Just a fraction. An inch.

It was terrifying. And it was the only thing keeping me from screaming.

"You're shaking," Asher said softly. He didn't remove his hand. He didn't try to pull me into a hug or make it about him. He just anchored me. "Breathe, Mara. He's gone. He's just a man with dirty boots and a bad attitude."

I took a shuddering breath. "He hates us being here."

"Good," Asher said, a grim satisfaction in his voice. "If we're making him nervous, we're doing something right."

He squeezed my shoulder once, a silent reassurance, and then let his hand drop. The loss of contact was immediate and jarring, leaving a cold spot on my shoulder where his warmth had been. I wrapped my arms around myself, trying to preserve the heat.

"We need to keep moving," I said, forcing the words out. "I'm not letting him scare me off."

"I know." Asher looked toward the kitchen. "Actually, while you were... handling the welcome committee... I found something in the pantry."

My interest piqued, despite the lingering anxiety. "In the pantry? I looked in there. It's just canned peaches from 1998."

"Not the shelves." He walked toward the kitchen, and I followed, grateful for the distraction. "The back panel. It's loose. There's a false wall behind the spice rack."

We entered the kitchen. The air here was still, heavy with the scent of old herbs and dried dust. Asher went to the pantry, a small, narrow space that always felt claustrophobic. He reached behind a row of rusted tin cans and pushed. A section of the paneling groaned and popped inward, revealing a dark, hollow space.

"Show off," I muttered, but I was impressed.

He reached in and pulled out a small, leather-bound book. It was battered, the cover scuffed and faded to a dull brown. The edges of the pages were yellowed, brittle with age.

"This was hidden deep," Asher said, blowing a layer of dust off the cover. "It wasn't just put there; it was secured."

I took the book from him. The leather was cool to the touch. I opened the front cover. There was no name, just a date written in elegant, looping cursive: *June 14th*.

"Ilse's journal?" I asked, though I already knew. I recognized the handwriting from the recipe cards I'd found in the kitchen drawer.

"Looks like," Asher said. He leaned against the doorframe, watching me. "Let's see what she thought was worth hiding from the world."

I turned the first page. The paper crackled, sounding dry and loud in the quiet kitchen.

*The heat is unbearable this summer. The house is stifling, but it is nowhere near as hot as the tempers flaring under this roof.*

I frowned. "She's writing about the house."

"Keep reading," Asher urged.

I turned the page.

*Lydia is walking on glass. I can see it in the way she moves, the way she flinches when the floorboards creak. She thinks I don't know, but a mother always knows. Or in this case, an aunt knows too much.*

I paused, my throat tightening. My mother. Lydia. She was walking on glass? My memories of her were fragmented—sharp flashes of red lipstick, the smell of gin, and the sound of slamming doors. But I never remembered her being afraid. She was the one who inspired fear.

"What is it?" Asher asked, his voice low.

"She's talking about my mom. Something... someone was making her nervous." I read on, my eyes scanning the frantic scrawl of the next entry.

*He came by again today. Julian. The name leaves a bad taste in my mouth, like copper and ash. He acts like he owns the place, leaning against the doorframe with that arrogant smirk. But I see the darkness in his eyes. It's not just arrogance; it's violence waiting for a spark. He grabbed Lydia's arm today, hard enough to bruise. She covered it with a sweater, but I saw the mark. She claims he's passionate, that he loves her. I say he's a bomb ticking down to zero.*

"Julian," I whispered. The name felt foreign, yet it scratched at the back of my mind, like a song I couldn't quite remember. "Who is Julian?"

Asher went very still. The air in the pantry suddenly felt thinner. "Julian?"

"Yes. The man Mom was seeing? Apparently, he was aggressive." I looked up at Asher. "Does that name mean anything to you?"

Asher’s expression was carefully blank, a mask of polite curiosity that didn't quite reach his eyes. He stared at the horizon outside the kitchen window, his jawline working as he ground his teeth. "No," he said finally. The word was too quick. Too sharp. "It's a common name. Someone from her past?"

"I guess." I looked back at the book, a prickle of suspicion crawling up my spine. Why did he react like that? But the mystery of the journal pulled me back in. I had to know.

I turned the page, desperate for more context. Who was this man, and why did Ilse hide this diary?

*Julian is demanding more. He wants access to the money, the deed, everything. He says he's the man of the house now, but this isn't his house. It never will be. I found him in the attic yesterday, prowling around like a wolf. He claims he was looking for a spare key, but I know he was looking for leverage. I told him to get out, but he just laughed. He said Lydia loves him, that she'd do anything for him. I'm afraid he's right. I'm afraid of what she'll do to keep him. I'm afraid of what he'll do if she tries to leave.*

The timeline clicked into place. This was the summer I was eight. The summer I couldn't remember. The summer everything broke.

"He was dangerous," I said, my voice trembling slightly. "Ilse was trying to protect her. Protect us."

"It seems that way," Asher said quietly. He had moved closer, reading over my shoulder. His presence was solid, a wall of warmth against the chill of the diary's words.

"Violent," I murmured, tracing the faded ink. "Possessive. If he was hurting my mother, and she felt trapped..." I stopped, the logic spinning out into dark territories I wasn't ready to explore. "Could he be the reason we left?"

"Or the reason you stayed," Asher countered gently. "Mara, look at the next page. There's a photograph tucked in there."

I hadn't noticed the corner of the glossy paper sticking out from the binding. I carefully pulled it free. The photo was black and white, slightly grainy, but the image was clear.

It showed a man standing on the front porch of this very house. He was young, maybe in his late twenties, with dark, tousled hair that fell over his forehead. He was handsome in a way that felt familiar—too familiar. He had a sharp jawline and eyes that seemed to look right through the camera lens, intense and burning.

My breath hitched. I knew that face.

I reached into my back pocket and pulled out the photograph I had found in my mother's secret album the day before. The one of the laughing couple on the beach. I held them side by side.

It was the same man. The tousled hair, the smirk, the intensity. In the beach photo, he was holding my mother close, looking like they owned the world. In this photo, standing alone on the porch, he looked like he was casing it.

"You recognize him," Asher stated. It wasn't a question.

"It's him," I said, my stomach turning over. "From the album. This is the man my mother was with. This is Julian."

I looked down at the journal page beneath where the photo had been hidden. There was a caption written in Ilse's handwriting underneath an empty slot, as if she had intended to label the photo but changed her mind. It was a date.

*July 12th.*

I froze. The blood drained from my face so fast I felt dizzy.

"What is it?" Asher asked, his hand moving to the small of my back, steadying me. "Mara?"

"July 12th," I whispered. "That date."

I remembered the date from the police report I'd requested years ago, the one that had been redacted and useless. The date the neighbor had reported a loud disturbance from the house. The date my mother packed us up in the middle of the night and drove away, never to return.

"This is the day," I said, my voice cracking. "This is the day of the fall."

I stared at the photograph of the man with the tousled hair, the man with the violent temper and the possessive love, and felt the house groan around us. I looked at the date inked in Ilse's terrified hand. The bear in the attic, the missing memories, the guilt that had plagued me for twenty-one years—it all circled back to this face. To Julian.

And as I stood there, Asher's hand warm against my back, the silence of the house broke. From the floor above, directly over our heads, came the heavy, dragging sound of a trunk being moved across the attic floorboards.

The attic where we had just been. The attic that was supposed to be empty.
