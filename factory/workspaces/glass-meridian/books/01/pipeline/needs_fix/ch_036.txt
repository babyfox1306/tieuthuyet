# Chapter 36: The Cost of Confrontation

The air crackles with tension as the mercenaries close in, their boots echoing against the café’s tiled floor. Lin Wei’s hand finds Adrian’s arm, her fingers digging into his sleeve as she calculates their odds—seven armed men, two exits, zero weapons.

“Adrian.” Thorne’s voice carries the weight of a man who has already won. “Did you really think I wouldn’t recognize my own work?”

Adrian’s jaw clenches, and she feels the tremor run through his body—rage or fear, she can’t tell. “You taught me everything I know.”

“And you used it against me.” Thorne shakes his head slowly, almost pitying. “The recursive hash loop. The tracer algorithm. Elegant, efficient, entirely predictable. I built the foundation you’re standing on, son. Did you think I wouldn’t see my own scaffolding when you put it on display?”

Lin Wei’s mind races, cataloging escape routes, potential weapons, anything. The café’s kitchen has a back door—she spotted it when they entered. But the mercenaries have already positioned themselves to block both exits.

“Mr. Thorne,” she interrupts, forcing her voice steady. “You’ve made your point. What do you want?”

Thorne’s gaze shifts to her, and she feels the full weight of his attention—assessing, calculating, dismissive. “The journalist. I’ve read your work, Miss Lin. Impressive, for someone operating without the full picture.”

“Then enlighten me.”

A soft laugh. “No. You’ve already been given more than you deserve. My son’s sentimentality has compromised this operation enough.”

Adrian steps forward, positioning himself between her and Thorne. “She stays with me.”

“That’s not your decision to make.”

“It is.” Adrian’s voice drops, taking on a cold edge she hasn’t heard before. “You’ve already taken everything from me—my mother, my childhood, my goddamn identity. You don’t get to take her too.”

Lin Wei’s breath catches. She’s never heard him speak about his mother. The vulnerability in his voice is raw, unguarded.

Thorne’s expression flickers—something almost like pain, quickly suppressed. “Your mother made her choices, Adrian. I gave her every opportunity to—”

“Don’t.” The word is a blade. “Don’t you dare speak about her.”

The mercenaries shift, waiting for orders. The café owner has disappeared into the back, smartly choosing survival over heroism. Through the front windows, Lin Wei can see people on the street—oblivious, going about their lives while hers hangs in the balance.

She focuses on the details. The ozone scent from the decrypted files lingers in her memory, a phantom smell that seems to cling to everything they’ve touched. The data chip from Vance’s desk is still tucked in her jacket pocket, a fragile thread of hope.

“Here’s what’s going to happen.” Thorne straightens his jacket, the gesture so casual it’s almost insulting. “You’re going to return to Aurelian Group. You’re going to release Elias Vance. And you’re going to destroy every piece of evidence you’ve collected.”

“And if we refuse?” Lin Wei asks.

“Then I’ll have you both killed.” He says it like he’s ordering coffee. “But I’d prefer not to. Adrian is still my son, despite his misguided loyalties. And you, Miss Lin—you have potential. The Glass Meridian could use someone with your determination.”

The offer hangs in the air, obscene and tempting in equal measure.

“Join us.” Thorne spreads his hands. “Learn the truth about Project Phoenix. Save your mother.”

Lin Wei feels the ground shift beneath her feet. “What do you know about my mother?”

“Everything.” The word is a door opening into darkness. “I know she was brilliant. I know she was compromised. I know the illness that’s killing her isn’t natural—it’s the consequence of her own research.”

Her lungs seize. The air won’t come.

Adrian’s hand finds hers, squeezing hard. “Don’t listen to him. He lies to manipulate.”

“Do I?” Thorne’s eyes gleam. “Miss Lin, ask yourself: why did your mother’s symptoms begin exactly three years after she left Project Phoenix? Why does her medical file contain gaps that align perfectly with the Glass Meridian’s known research cycles? Why does she show signs of cellular regeneration that no natural illness produces?”

Lin Wei’s knees weaken. Every word confirms suspicions she’s buried for months—the inconsistencies in her mother’s records, the strange patterns in her blood work that Professor Chen couldn’t explain, the way her mother sometimes speaks in half-phrases about things that never happened.

“You’re lying,” she whispers, but even she doesn’t believe it.

“I never lie, Miss Lin. I simply choose which truths to reveal.”

Adrian pulls her closer, his arm around her waist steadying her. “We’re leaving.”

“You’re not.”

The mercenaries move in unison, blocking the path to the door. Adrian’s hand slides to his pocket, and Lin Wei knows he’s reaching for the weapon he always carries.

“Don’t.” Thorne’s voice sharpens. “You’re outnumbered, outgunned, and outmaneuvered. Accept defeat gracefully.”

“I don’t accept anything from you.”

“Then you’ll die.” Thorne checks his watch. “You have thirty seconds to decide.”

Lin Wei’s mind spins through options, discarding each one. Fight—impossible. Flight—blocked. Negotiation—Thorne holds all the cards. The data chip burns against her thigh, a useless trophy.

But she’s a journalist. And journalists know that information is never truly dead.

“Adrian,” she whispers, her lips brushing his ear. “The chip. I need time.”

He understands immediately. “Father. One hour. Let me explain why I did this.”

“I don’t need explanations.”

“You need something.” Adrian’s voice is desperate now, playing a role she’s never seen him assume. “You need me to understand that I lost. That you won. Give me that, and I’ll cooperate.”

Thorne studies him, and Lin Wei holds her breath. The calculation behind those eyes is terrifying—a mind that sees human emotion as a variable to be optimized.

“One hour,” Thorne says finally. “In my private suite at the hotel. No weapons. No devices. And if I sense any deception, the deal is off.”

Adrian nods, and Lin Wei watches him shed his jacket, handing it to the nearest mercenary. She follows suit, her phone, her recorder, everything going into a plastic bag.

The only thing she keeps is the data chip, tucked into the seam of her bra where they won’t think to search.

As they’re led out of the café, the morning sun feels obscene—too bright, too cheerful for a day that’s already cost them everything. The hotel’s glass facade gleams, pristine and indifferent to the war being fought in its shadow.

“You played well,” Thorne says as they walk, his tone almost admiring. “The broadcast was a masterstroke. If I hadn’t anticipated it, you might have actually damaged me.”

“But you did anticipate it.”

“I always anticipate, Adrian. It’s the first lesson I taught you.”

They enter the hotel through a service entrance, navigating corridors that smell of bleach and stale air. The private suite on the top floor is opulent, all cream marble and gold fixtures, a monument to wealth that makes Lin Wei’s skin crawl.

Thorne gestures to a sofa. “Sit.”

They obey, and the mercenaries take positions at the doors and windows. Thorne settles into an armchair across from them, crossing his legs with the ease of a man hosting a business meeting rather than interrogating his own son.

“Now. Explain.”

Adrian takes a breath, and Lin Wei watches him construct the narrative—the half-truths, the careful omissions, the performance of a man who has learned from the best.

“I’ve been investigating the Glass Meridian for three years,” he begins. “Ever since I found my mother’s journals.”

Thorne’s expression doesn’t change, but Lin Wei catches the micro-tension in his jaw.

“She documented everything. The experiments, the cover-ups, the people who died. And she documented you—how you sold her research to the Meridian in exchange for control of Aurelian Group.”

“Your mother was a sentimental woman.”

“My mother was a survivor who trusted the wrong person.” Adrian’s voice cracks, and Lin Wei feels the grief behind it. “I wanted to expose you. To destroy everything you built. To make you pay for what you did to her.”

“And Lin Wei?”

“She was a tool. A journalist with access to medical records I couldn’t obtain. I used her.”

The words hit like a slap, even though she knows they’re part of the performance. She forces herself to react—hurt, betrayal, the sting of being reduced to an instrument.

“Adrian?” Her voice trembles, and she’s proud of how convincing it sounds. “You said you needed my help.”

“I needed your access.” He won’t meet her eyes. “Nothing more.”

Thorne laughs, soft and satisfied. “You always were pragmatic. It’s the only quality of yours I respect.”

“Then you understand why I did it.”

“I understand that you failed.” Thorne stands, walking to the window. “And failure has consequences, Adrian. You’ve compromised Elias. You’ve revealed your capabilities. You’ve demonstrated that you cannot be trusted.”

“What are you going to do?”

Thorne turns, and his smile is the coldest thing Lin Wei has ever seen. “I’m going to give you what you wanted. The truth.”

He presses something on his phone, and a screen descends from the ceiling. The image that appears makes Lin Wei’s blood freeze.

It’s her mother.

Lying in a hospital bed, tubes trailing from her arms, machines beeping in the background. But the room isn’t the one Lin Wei knows—the wallpaper is wrong, the equipment is different, the lighting too harsh.

“This is where your mother is being treated, Miss Lin. The real treatment.” Thorne gestures at the screen. “Professor Chen has been reporting to us for months. Every symptom, every response, every scan—sent directly to the Glass Meridian’s medical division.”

“You’ve been monitoring her.”

“We’ve been treating her. The medication you thought was managing her illness? It’s been stabilizing her Phoenix markers. Without it, she would have died six months ago.”

Lin Wei’s hands are shaking. “You expect me to be grateful?”

“I expect you to understand the stakes.” Thorne’s voice softens, taking on a paternal tone that makes her stomach turn. “Your mother is alive because of us. She will continue to be alive as long as you cooperate. The moment you stop being useful—”

“You’ll kill her.”

“I’ll stop treating her. The distinction is yours to make.”

Adrian stands, his fists clenched. “This ends now. I won’t let you—”

“You won’t let me what, Adrian?” Thorne’s voice cracks like a whip. “You’ve already lost. The evidence is destroyed. Your ally is compromised. The person you care about most is under my control. What leverage do you possibly possess?”

The silence that follows is the sound of a door slamming shut.

Lin Wei’s mind races through the implications. Her mother’s medical records, the treatment schedule, Professor Chen’s reassuring presence—all of it a carefully constructed cage. She’s been dancing on strings she didn’t know existed.

The data chip presses against her skin, a reminder that she still has one card to play. But even as she thinks it, she knows the truth: Thorne has already accounted for every variable except one.

He doesn’t know about the chip.

“One hour,” Adrian says, his voice hollow. “You promised one hour.”

“I lied.” Thorne waves his hand. “Take them to the basement. I’ll decide what to do with them later.”

The mercenaries move, grabbing their arms, hauling them toward the door. Lin Wei locks eyes with Adrian, and she sees what she needs—a flicker of recognition, a plan forming behind his mask of defeat.

He’s not done fighting.

Neither is she.

As they’re marched through the corridor, she memorizes the layout, the guard positions, the exits. The data chip is still safe. The fight is still alive.

But the cost of this game is becoming clear.

Her mother’s life.

Adrian’s freedom.

Her own safety.

And as the elevator doors close, swallowing them into the hotel’s depths, she realizes the most devastating truth of all: they only made the surface bleed.

The real wound is still hidden.
