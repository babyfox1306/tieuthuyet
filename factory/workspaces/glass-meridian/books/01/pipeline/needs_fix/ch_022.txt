# Chapter 22: The Price of Truth

The sterile hum of servers filled the windowless room, a constant white noise that masked the rapid cadence of Lin Wei's breathing. Her fingers flew across the keyboard, a blur of motion against the dim glow of four monitors, each screen a cascade of code and network pathways. Beside her, Adrian Vale worked another terminal with the same fluid precision, his jaw set tight, his eyes never leaving the data stream.

"The decryption algorithm is running," Lin Wei said, her voice barely above a whisper. "The authentication handshake will take another forty seconds."

Adrian grunted in acknowledgment, his gaze flicking to a security feed he'd pulled up on a smaller display. "We have a window. The night shift rotation creates a seven-minute gap in active monitoring. We're using three of them now."

She didn't respond. Every second mattered. Her focus narrowed to the progress bar crawling across the screen, each percentage point a step closer to the data nexus that held the Glass Meridian's operational records. If she could just reach the root directory structure, she could map their digital footprint, trace their influence. Thirty-five percent. Forty.

"You're rushing," Adrian said, his voice low, almost a warning.

"I know what I'm doing."

"Rushing leaves traces."

"Staying in here leaves bodies," she shot back, not breaking focus. Her mother was stable for now, but the clock on Project Phoenix's secrets wasn't forgiving.

The progress bar hit sixty-two percent. Then seventy. She could almost feel the data on the other side, the truth crystallizing into something tangible.

Adrian shifted beside her, his presence a constant awareness against the edges of her perception. She caught the faint scent of something expensive in the air—a perfume. It didn't belong in a server room. Before she could place it, he leaned closer, his hand brushing her wrist.

"Careful. You're about to trigger an anomaly detector."

She pulled back, recalibrating. He was right. The interface had highlighted a suspicious pattern in the query volume. "I see it. I'll reroute through a proxy chain."

"Too slow. Use this backdoor in the authentication protocol." He typed a sequence into his own terminal, sending a snippet of code to hers. "It's an old vulnerability. Marcus designed the network architecture—he never closed the gap."

Lin Wei studied the script. Elegant. Exploitable. "You knew about this?"

"Part of the inheritance I didn't ask for."

She deployed the backdoor. The progress bar jumped to eighty-eight percent. Ninety-one. The data nexus was within reach, a digital gateway shimmering with promise.

Then the console flashed red.

**WARNING: CRITICAL SYSTEM OVERLOAD**

A high-pitched whine sliced through the hum. The monitors flickered, the brightness surging to a painful white. Lin Wei's retinas burned as the glare intensified, her hands frozen over the keyboard.

"Shit!" She tried to pull back, but the light was blinding, disorienting.

She felt hands grasp her shoulders, shoving her away from the console. Adrian's body pressed against hers, his arms forming a shield between her and the blinding screens. She stumbled backward with him, her back hitting the edge of a server rack, his chest solid against her.

The whine died. The glare subsided to a dull glow.

Lin Wei blinked, trying to clear the spots from her vision. When she focused, Adrian was inches from her, his face tight with concern, his breath warm against her cheek. The air between them crackled, electric and heavy. She could see the micro-expressions crossing his face: relief, anger, something deeper she couldn't name.

"You okay?" His voice was rough, strained.

"I'm fine." But her hands trembled. The near-critical overload had been close to frying the room's power grid—and her along with it.

He didn't release her immediately. His eyes searched hers, and she felt a pull, a magnetic need to close the distance, to lose herself in his gravity.

Before she could act on it, a new sound shattered the moment.

Alarms.

A deep, pulsating siren drilled through the facility, followed by the thud of automatic locks engaging. The security feed on Adrian's display flickered to life, showing figures in tactical gear moving through the corridor.

"Someone tripped the silent alarm," Adrian said, his voice hardening. He released her, stepping back with visible reluctance.

"I didn't. The overload didn't trigger it. Someone knew we were here."

He didn't argue. He was already scanning the room. "We have sixty seconds before they seal the floor."

Lin Wei spun back to her terminal. The decryption had reached ninety-seven percent before aborting. She slammed the cancel command, then initiated a memory wipe. The drives needed to be scrubbed.

"Leave it," Adrian ordered. "We don't have time."

"I'm not giving them the access logs."

"Then pull the physical connection!"

She yanked the network cable, severing the link just as the progress bar hit ninety-nine percent. A fragment of data burned onto the screen before it went dark—a file directory path and a single name: **C006**.

"What's that?" Adrian asked.

"I don't know. But it was being staged for extraction." She copied the path into her mental map. Another clue. Possibly a trap.

The alarms grew louder. The door was reinforced, but not invulnerable.

Adrian grabbed her wrist, pulling her toward a maintenance hatch at the back of the room. "This leads to the ventilation shafts. Follow me, and don't stop."

They crawled through the narrow tunnel, dust and grime coating their clothes. The vents ended at a service elevator, dark and offline. Adrian forced the doors open with a crowbar from his kit, revealing a steep ladder shaft.

"Down," he said. "Parking level two. There's a car prepped."

Lin Wei descended, adrenaline fueling her movements. At the bottom, they emerged into a concrete garage, cold and empty. A black sedan waited in the shadows.

But as she turned toward the vehicle, she caught it again—the faint scent of perfume, lingering in the air. Not from the server room. Here. Recent.

She stopped. "Adrian."

He was already at the driver's door. "What?"

"Someone was here. Before we arrived. That perfume—I noticed it in the server room too."

His face went pale, controlled anger flashing in his eyes. "You're sure?"

"I'm a journalist. I notice details." And she remembered that scent from somewhere. A charity gala. A woman in a blue dress. Sophia Petrova.

Adrian's hand tightened on the door handle. "Then we have a problem."

Alarms echoed from the garage entrance. Lights flashed.

He shoved a USB drive into her palm. "Take this. It's the fragment we managed to preserve. If I'm detained, destroy it. Don't trust anyone except yourself."

"We're both leaving," she said, her tone sharp.

"We're both leaving." He opened the door for her, and they slid into the car. The engine roared to life, and he tore through the exit just as security sealed the gates.

As the city lights blurred past, Lin Wei stared at the USB drive. The clue. The betrayal. The betrayal. Someone had sold them out. Someone who wore expensive perfume and had access to the facility. Someone who knew the plan.

The realization settled into her bones like ice water. They hadn't just been discovered. They'd been delivered.

And the scent still clung to her memory, a whisper of betrayal she couldn't yet name.
