# Chapter 7: The Weight of Memory

The water didn't stop at the landing, Julian hissed, pulling Elena back by the waist as a dark, oily tide began to lap at the top of the attic stairs. 

Elena stumbled, her back colliding with the solid wall of Julian's chest. The impact knocked the air from her lungs, but the sensation was distant, muffled by the roaring in her ears. She stared at the staircase, where the shadows should have been. Instead, a viscous, black liquid was pouring upward, defying gravity, swallowing the steps with a silent, ravenous hunger. 

It wasn't just water. 

"Julian," Elena whispered, her voice cracking. "The floor." 

The attic floorboards, usually dry and brittle with age, were slick. The liquid crept across them, seeking them out with the deliberation of a predator. It looked like tar, but it smelled wrong. There was no scent of rot or decay, only that sharp, electric tang of ozone and the heavy, suffocating musk of deep earth. The wood groaned, a low, mournful sound that vibrated in the soles of Elena's boots. 

Julian's grip tightened, his arm a steel band across her stomach. He wasn't looking at the water; he was looking at the beams, the windows, the corners of the room where the light bent. "Don't move," he commanded, his voice stripped of its usual calm, reduced to a raw edge. "The gradient is shifting. The house is destabilizing." 

"It's not the house," Elena said, rubbing her thumb frantically against her own index finger. The friction was meant to ground her, but the skin felt numb. "It's coming from the trunk." 

The old steamer trunk they had pried open only minutes ago sat in the center of the room, half-submerged. The diary they had found, the pages filled with a child's frantic scrawls about a boy named Tomas, was now dissolving. The ink ran off the paper like black blood, swirling into the rising tide. 

Elena tried to pull away, needing to save the book, needing to stop the destruction of the only proof she had that she wasn't insane. "I have to—"

"No." Julian yanked her back harder, stepping between her and the encroaching dark. "Elena, look at it. It's not wet. It's not liquid." 

She looked. 

He was right. The substance lapped against the legs of the trunk, but the wood did not darken. It did not swell. The water in the attic left no wetness on the wood, only the lingering smell of ozone and deep earth. It was a hallucination made solid, a psychic hemorrhage flooding the room. 

The water rose to their ankles, then their shins. Elena felt the pressure, the crushing weight of deep water, but when she looked down, her jeans were dry. 

"It's a memory," Elena realized, the taste of copper flooding her mouth. "It's not happening now. It's happening then." 

The room tilted. The angled ceiling of the attic stretched upward, vanishing into a murky, green haze. The walls of the house dissolved, replaced by the reeking embrace of weeping willows and the cold, endless press of lake water. 

A sound broke through the silence—not the creak of the house, but a giggle. A wet, bubbling sound that churned Elena's stomach. 

In the center of the room, where the black water reflected the dim light of the single bulb, the surface began to churn. 

From the reflection, a shape rose. 

Elena stopped breathing. 

It was a girl, small and pale, wearing a dress the color of a bruised sky. She emerged from the oily flood as if stepping up from the bottom of a pool, but her feet never touched the floor. She remained suspended within the liquid mirror, her hair floating around her face like seaweed. 

It was Elena's face. Not the woman she was now, with the lines of exhaustion and the guarded eyes, but the child she had been. The girl in blue. 

The girl's eyes, wide and unblinking, locked onto Elena. 

"You left him," the girl whispered. The voice did not come from a throat but vibrated directly in Elena's bones, a child's whisper overlaid with the static of dead radio waves. "You hid." 

Elena's knees buckled. The memory slammed into her with the force of a physical blow. 

She was no longer in the attic. She was eight years old. The air was thick with summer heat and the drone of cicadas. The lake was a flat, grey sheet of glass beneath the pier. 

"You're it, Elena," a boy's voice said. Tomas. He was grinning, his teeth too white in the sun. "Count to a hundred. No peeking." 

"Go," she had said, her own voice small and shrill. "Go hide." 

She squeezed her eyes shut, pressing her hands over her ears. One, two, three... 

The attic faded. The smell of ozone was replaced by the scent of algae and mud. She was standing on the muddy bank, her toes sinking into the silt. 

"It's just a game," she whispered to herself, her thumb rubbing against her palm hard enough to bleed. "It's just a game." 

"Four, five, six..." 

The water in the attic began to rise rapidly, covering her waist, her chest. It was cold, so cold it burned. In the distance, beneath the surface of the black water, she saw him. Tomas. He was thrashing, his hand reaching up, breaking the surface, grasping at nothing. 

His eyes. 

God, his eyes. They were wide, panicked, rolling back in his head as the water filled his lungs. He wasn't playing anymore. 

The girl in blue floated closer, her translucent hands reaching out. She looked sad. She looked terrified. 

"You didn't count," the girl accused, her voice cracking. "You stopped counting." 

"I didn't mean to," Elena choked out, tears streaming down her face, mixing with the phantom water. "I forgot. I just forgot." 

The guilt was a physical weight, heavier than the ocean. It dragged her down. She needed to go to him. She needed to finish the count. She needed to pull him up. 

Elena took a step forward. The water surged, welcoming her, ready to claim the piece of herself that had been left behind. 

"I have to go," she gasped, lunging toward the center of the room where the boy was drowning. 

She didn't get far. 

A vice gripped her shoulders, spinning her around. The attic snapped back into focus with violent clarity—the beams, the dust, the bulb swinging overhead. Julian was there, his face pale, his eyes wild with a fear that had nothing to do with ghosts. 

He held her arms, his fingers digging in, grounding her in the present. 

"Elena, stop!" he shouted, his voice cutting through the water rushing in her ears. "It's not real. Look at the floor!" 

"He's drowning!" Elena screamed, fighting him. She clawed at his hands, desperate to reach the water, desperate to reach the boy. "I have to save him! I have to count!" 

"There is no one here!" Julian shook her, a rough, jarring motion that snapped her head back. "You are not eight years old. You are here. With me." 

The girl in blue screamed, a sound like tearing metal, and the water churned into a vortex. The vision of Tomas dissolved into bubbles, leaving only the dark, empty tide and the accusing eyes of her reflection. 

Elena slumped, the fight draining out of her as the adrenaline crashed. She was hyperventilating, her lungs burning for air that didn't taste like mud. She stared at Julian's shirt, soaking in the reality of him—the rough weave of the fabric, the warmth radiating from his skin, the steady, terrified rhythm of his heart she could feel where her hands pressed against his chest. 

Julian let go of her shoulders, his hands trembling slightly. He reached up, hesitating for a fraction of a second, before he framed her face with his palms. His skin was calloused, rough against her cheeks, but his touch was incredibly gentle. 

He forced her to look up, to meet his gaze. His eyes were storm-grey, searching hers, stripping away the layers of denial she had built for twenty years. 

"Listen to me," Julian said, his voice raw, stripped of all academic distance. The low rumble of it vibrated in her chest, chasing away the cold. "Look at me. You are here. You are real. Don't you dare leave me in this house alone." 

The words hit her harder than the memory. Not because he was commanding her, but because of the terror beneath the command. He wasn't just a caretaker of this ruin; he was a man standing on the edge of an abyss, terrified of watching her fall in. 

Elena stared at him, the blue water still swirling around their legs, but the heat of his hands anchored her. She focused on the small scar above his eyebrow, the way his breath hitched, the dilation of his pupils. 

"I'm here," she breathed, the truth of it finally settling in. "I'm here." 

Julian held her gaze for a long moment, his thumbs tracing the line of her jaw, a possessive, protective gesture that sent a shiver through her that had nothing to do with the cold. He exhaled sharply, a ragged sound, and stepped back, breaking the contact but staying close enough to catch her if she fell. 

The room seemed to shudder. 

The black tide, which had risen to Elena's ribs, suddenly stilled. The girl in blue flickered, her form blurring like a watercolor painting left in the rain. She looked at Elena one last time, her expression shifting from accusation to pity. 

Then, she dissolved. 

As if a plug had been pulled, the water began to recede. It rushed backward, defying gravity once
