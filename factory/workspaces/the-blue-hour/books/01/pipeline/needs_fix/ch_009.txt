# Chapter 9: The Surface Breaks

"I have to go in," Elena said, her voice steady for the first time since she arrived, her hand resting on the wet wood of the dock.

Julian's face tightened. He stepped closer, boots hitting the damp planks with a hollow sound. "You don't know what's down there. The current is unpredictable. The bottom drops out in the middle."

"I know what's down there." She finally looked at him. His eyes were dark, desperate, fixed on her like she was standing on a ledge. "I left her there. I need to bring her back."

He reached for her arm. His grip was firm, but his hand trembled. "There has to be another way."

"There isn't."

She pulled free. The air was cold for late morning, but the water looked black and still, like a mirror turned upside down. She kicked off her shoes. The wood of the dock was rough and cold under her bare feet.

She didn't test the temperature. She stepped forward and let the lake swallow her whole.

The cold hit her like a wall. It stole the air from her lungs, pushed against her chest, wrapped around her bones. She didn't fight it. She let herself sink.

The light above her faded. The world turned green, then silver, then black. The water pressed against her eardrums. Her lungs began to burn.

She kept sinking.

The pressure changed. The cold vanished, replaced by something thick and warm, like stepping from an air-conditioned car into summer heat. The darkness around her shifted.

She was standing on dry ground.

The clearing was the same as it had been twenty years ago. Sunlight dappled through the leaves. Cicadas buzzed in the long grass. The air smelled of pine and dust and the sweet rot of fallen fruit.

A boy stood at the edge of the lake. He was nine years old, dark hair plastered to his forehead, a gap-toothed grin splitting his face. Tomas.

"Your turn, Lena," he called. "You have to hold your breath the longest. That's the rule."

Adult Elena stood a few feet away, watching. She remembered this. She remembered the dare, the way his voice had grated on her nerves, the way she wanted to win just once.

The eight-year-old version of herself stepped forward. She was small and skinny, her hair in two braids, her sundress too big for her frame. She was smiling.

"I can hold it longer than you," young Elena said.

"No you can't."

"Can too."

The game began.

Young Elena hid in the reeds. Tomas waded into the water. He turned and grinned at her before plunging under.

She held her breath.

The seconds passed. The water smoothed.

Adult Elena remembered the anger that had flickered through her young chest. He was showing off. He was trying to scare her.

She waited.

The water stayed still.

Adult Elena watched herself crouch deeper into the reeds. Watched the smile fade from her face. Watched the fear creep in.

She remembered now. She remembered the exact moment she chose to stay silent.

Tomas had been bragging all summer. He could swim farther, dive deeper, hold his breath longer. She was tired of losing. When he went under, she wanted him to fail. She didn't want him to drown. She just wanted him to come up sputtering, red-faced, admitting she had won.

But he didn't come up.

The water smoothed like glass.

The toy boat floated on its side.

And eight-year-old Elena didn't scream. She didn't call for help. She ran. She ran back to the house, hid under her bed, and told herself she hadn't seen anything.

She told herself so many times she believed it.

The memory froze.

The girl in blue stepped out of the reeds.

She was Elena at eight, but wrong. Her dress was wet and heavy. Her skin was pale, almost translucent. Her eyes were dark with a guilt she had carried alone for two decades.

"You left me here," the girl said. Her voice was thin, hollow, like wind through a cracked window. "You made me watch him sink. You made me carry this alone."

"I was a child," Elena said. Her voice broke on the words. "I was scared."

"You let him die."

"I made a mistake." Tears were hot on her cold face. "A terrible, horrible mistake. I was eight years old. I didn't understand what I was doing."

"Neither did he."

The words cut deep. Elena didn't look away. She stepped forward, her bare feet sinking into the damp earth of the memory.

"I have carried you for twenty years," she said. "I have carried the silence. The nightmares. The way I can't look at my own reflection without feeling like a stranger. I didn't know why. I didn't remember." She reached out and took the girl's hand.

The girl's fingers were cold and brittle, like dried paper.

Elena held tight.

She didn't feel cold water in her lungs. She felt the dry, warm air of the summer day she couldn't remember. The sun on her skin. The grass under her feet. The weight of the secret she had buried alive.

"You are not the monster," Elena said. "You are the part of me that survived. You are the girl who was too small to face what she did, so she hid. I don't blame you. I forgive you. I need you. I need all of you. Please, come back to me."

The girl in blue stared at her. Her eyes were wet, but she didn't blink.

"I don't know how," she whispered.

"Just let go. Let me hold it now."

The girl's hand tightened. Then she stepped forward and pressed her face into Elena's chest.

She dissolved.

It wasn't cold. It wasn't pain. It was warmth. A flood of warmth and grief and guilt so vast it stopped Elena's heart. She saw Tomas's face, heard his laugh, felt the exact moment the game turned wrong. She felt the panic of being eight years old and realizing she had done something she could never undo. She felt the walls she had built in her own mind, brick by brick, until the memory was buried so deep she didn't even know it was there.

She felt it all.

And she didn't shatter.

The water around her churned. The memory dissolved into dark, cold lake water. She was sinking again, her lungs screaming for air.

A hand caught her wrist.

Julian.

His face was pale, his jaw tight. He wrapped an arm around her waist and kicked hard. They broke the surface together.

She coughed, choked, spat lake water. The air was sharp and cold and wonderful.

Julian dragged her onto the dock. He didn't stop until they were on solid ground, her body pressed against his, his arms locked around her like he was afraid she would dissolve in his grasp.

"Don't you ever do that again," he said. His voice was ragged, barely controlled. "Do you hear me? Never again."

She couldn't speak. She was shaking too hard.

He pulled her to her feet and carried her to the shore. He sat down on the damp grass and pulled her onto his lap, wrapping his coat around her. It was heavy and smelled of wood smoke and coffee. His arms circled her, holding her tight against his chest.

"You are safe," he murmured into her wet hair. "You are forgiven. You are loved. Do you hear me, Elena? You are loved."

She sobbed.

She cried for Tomas, for the girl in blue, for the years she had spent running from herself. She cried until she had nothing left. Julian held her through all of it, rocking her slowly, his cheek pressed against the top of her head.

"You are safe," he repeated. "You are forgiven. You are loved."

She clung to his shirt and let herself believe it.

The sun climbed higher. The wind dried the water on her skin. Her shivers slowed, then stopped.

Julian didn't let her go until she was warm.

They walked back to the house in silence. The door was unlocked. The air inside was still and quiet.

The house was just a house.

Elena felt hollow, but clean. The weight was gone. The silence no longer pressed against her ears. It simply was.

Julian built a fire while she changed into dry clothes. He made coffee without asking how she took it. He handed her the mug and sat across from her, watching her like he was still afraid she might disappear.

"We should go," he said gently. "The road is passable now. There's nothing left for you here."

She looked around the parlor. The furniture. The dust. The piano with the broken key. The ghost of the girl she had been.

He was right. There was nothing left.

She nodded.

She went to pack her things.

Pip was sitting on the desk where she had left it. The teddy bear looked harmless now. Just a toy. An old, worn toy with button eyes and a crooked smile.

She picked it up to put it in her bag.

Her fingers brushed the stitching on its ear. There was a hard lump inside.

She frowned. She had searched this bear a dozen times. There had never been a lump before.

She reached for Julian's pocketknife on the desk. Carefully, she unpicked the stitches.

A small brass key fell into her palm.

She stared at it. The key was old, tarnished, the head shaped like a curled leaf. It looked like it belonged to a diary or a lockbox.

She reached into the ear again. Her fingers found folded paper.

She pulled out a photograph.

It was old, the colors faded to sepia. Two women stood in front of the lake house. The younger one was her mother. Diane. She was maybe seventeen, smiling wide, her arm slung around the other woman's shoulders.

The other woman was labeled in faded ink on the bottom of the photo. *Cora. Spring '85.*

Elena turned the photograph over.

Her mother's handwriting was unmistakable. Shaky, looping, the letters pressed hard into the paper.

*It wasn't your fault, Lena. It was mine. I passed it on.*

The blood drained from her face.

The key was cold in her palm. The photograph was warm from being pressed against her skin.

Her mother knew.

Diane knew what happened at the lake. Diane had been here. Diane had hidden the evidence in the bear's ear, knowing Elena would find it. Knowing Elena would have to go through the nightmare to uncover it.

Elena had thought this was Cora's game. Cora's test.

It was her mother's.

The key. The photo. The guilt.

It wasn't hers alone. It had been given to her.

Julian appeared in the doorway. "Elena? I've got the car running. Are you ready?"

She looked up at him. Her eyes were dry, but something hard had settled in her chest.

"Not yet," she said. "I have to find her."

The photograph trembled in her hand.

*I passed it on.*

Elena had forgiven herself.

But she wasn't finished yet.
