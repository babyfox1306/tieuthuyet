# Chapter 6: High Water Mark

Run, Julian barked, the word cracking like a whip as the floorboards beneath their feet began to weep.

Elena didn't argue. She didn't ask for a logical explanation or a building code citation. The air in the attic had shifted the moment she read the last entry in the diary, the ink drying on a sentence about holding breath, and now the house was excreting water from its very pores. It wasn't a leak from the roof. It wasn't a burst pipe. The wide, aging pine planks were secreting a clear, viscous fluid that pooled instantly around the toes of her boots, smelling intensely of deep lake weeds and rotting kelp.

She scrambled for the attic stairs, the diary clutched against her chest like a shield. The liquid was already ankle-deep, sucking at her movements with a greedy slurping sound. It was freezing. A biting, unnatural cold that seeped through the leather of her boots and numbed her toes instantly.

Julian was beside her in a stride, his hand clamping around her upper arm with a grip that bordered on bruising. He pulled her forward, his momentum nearly knocking her off balance. The water was rising with terrifying speed. It didn't splash or flow like normal water; it welled up from the gaps between the boards with a silent, pulsating pressure, a living tide being released from the belly of the house.

The attic, normally a dusty, dormant space, was transforming into a tank. The smell grew worse, choking and thick—the scent of silt, old mud, and things that had been underwater for too long.

Move, Julian gritted out, his voice stripped of its usual stoic calm. He wasn't looking at the water. He was looking at the far wall, where the antique mirror leaned against a stack of trunks. The glass was trembling, vibrating with a frequency that made Elena's teeth ache.

I am moving, Elena snapped back, though her voice wavered. She tried to jerk her arm free, but he held fast, dragging her around a heavy oak writing desk. The water is coming from the floor, Julian. There is no plumbing up here. This isn't a leak.

I know, he bit off. He didn't elaborate. He didn't have to. They could both see the impossible physics at play. The water was clear, not murky like the lake outside, but cold enough to burn.

They reached the top of the narrow staircase that led down to the second-floor landing. But the descent was blocked. The water hadn't just risen in the attic; it had flooded the stairs below, filling the narrow throat of the staircase completely. The surface was perfectly still, a sheet of glass reflecting the bare bulbs of the attic fixtures.

It's deep, Julian said, stopping so abruptly that Elena crashed into his back. He was solid muscle, unyielding as a stone wall. He scanned the water, then the room, his jaw working rhythmically.

Elena peered around his shoulder. The attic was now an island of floating debris. The trunks were bobbing. The smaller chairs were circling in slow, lazy arcs. It was hypnotic and wrong. The furniture moved with a sinister intent, bumping into one another like cattle in a squeeze chute, or perhaps sharks circling prey.

We have to jump, Elena said, her heart hammering against her ribs so hard she felt nauseous. It's just water. We swim down.

No, Julian said sharply. You don't know what's in it. He looked at the ceiling, then the window. The window was small, barred by rusted iron bars Cora had installed years ago in a fit of paranoia. No exit there.

The water lapped at their knees now. Within seconds it was at their thighs. The cold was agonizing, stealing the strength from Elena's legs. She shivered violently, her teeth chattering.

We can't stay here, Elena insisted. She looked at the floating furniture. A heavy mahogany wardrobe was drifting closer, its doors swinging open and closed with the gentle current, looking like a hungry mouth. Julian, the furniture.

He saw it too. The wardrobe was picking up speed, caught in a current he couldn't see. It was heading straight for them, a massive wooden battering ram.

Grab my hand, he ordered.

He didn't wait for her compliance. He reached down, threading his fingers through hers, his grip hot and urgent against her freezing skin. He pulled her toward the center of the room, trying to navigate the gap between the floating sharks.

Suddenly, the floorboards beneath Elena gave a sickening lurch. A geyser of water erupted directly beneath her, knocking her feet out from under her. She cried out, pitching backward into the icy blackness.

Julian moved faster than she thought possible. He didn't try to pull her up by the arm; he hooked his other arm around her waist, hauling her bodily out of the water's grasp. For a moment, she was entirely airborne, suspended by his strength, her chest pressed flush against his.

He didn't set her down gently. The water was waist-high now, surging and chaotic. He lifted her over a particularly deep patch where the floor had rotted away entirely, exposing the dark, cavernous gap of the story below. His hands were brands on her skin, burning through the damp fabric of her shirt. He held her not like a fragile doll, but like something vital that he refused to lose.

I've got you, he growled, the rumble of his chest vibrating against her own.

She instinctively wrapped her legs around his waist, clinging to him as he waded through the freezing sludge. It was an undignified, desperate position, stripped of all pretense. Her face was buried in the crook of his neck, smelling the rainwater on his coat and the sharp, clean scent of his sweat. It was the only real thing in a room that had gone mad.

The wardrobe crashed into the spot where they had been standing a second ago, the wood splintering with a sound like a gunshot. The impact sent a shockwave through the water, rocking them.

Julian staggered but kept his footing. He carried her to the far side of the room, near the chimney breast, where the floor seemed higher, more solid. The water was lower here, only at their shins.

He set her down, but he didn't let go. His hands slid to her shoulders, grounding her. His eyes were wild, scanning the room, but when they landed on her, they sharpened with intense focus.

Are you hurt? he demanded.

Elena shook her head, unable to speak. She was gasping for air, her lungs burning from the cold shock. She stared past him, watching the destruction of the attic. Books were dissolving in the water, pages turning to pulp. Fabrics were staining. The water was rising still, relentless and silent.

We need to get to the roof, Julian said, his voice tight. The house is... it's taking the attic. We go up.

He pointed to the small scuttle hatch in the ceiling near the chimney. It was their only chance.

Go, Elena said, shoving him toward the wall. Boost me.

Julian stepped onto the solid brick of the hearth. He laced his fingers together, creating a stirrup. Put your foot here.

Elena didn't hesitate. She placed her boot in his hands and grabbed the edge of the hatch frame. She pushed up with her legs, her muscles screaming, and he heaved, lifting her with tremendous force. She scrambled through the opening, tumbling onto the slanted roof of the upper turret.

She immediately turned back, reaching down through the hole. Julian, hand.

He grabbed her forearm. His hand was slipping, wet and cold. She bit her lip, ignoring the strain on her shoulder, and pulled with everything she had. He jumped, finding purchase on the brick, and hauled himself up through the hatch.

They collapsed onto the roof, the wind tearing at them, the rain immediately soaking them to the bone. But it was just rain. Normal, cold rain. Not the silent, predatory fluid filling the house below.

Elena rolled onto her back, gasping, staring up at the gray, churning sky. For a moment, she thought it was over. They had escaped the attic.

Then she felt the vibration beneath her.

The roof tiles were humming.

Julian scrambled to his knees, looking back down at the scuttle hatch they had just climbed through. It wasn't a dark hole anymore.

Water was pouring out of it. Not leaking. Pouring. A geyser that shot three feet into the air before cascading down the roof tiles.

It's not stopping, Julian said, his voice hollow with disbelief. It followed us.

He grabbed Elena's hand again, pulling her to her feet. We have to get to the main gable. The stone. The wood won't hold.

They scrambled up the slick slate tiles, fighting the gale-force wind. The water from the hatch was chasing them, a river flowing uphill, defying gravity. It moved with purpose, slithering over the shingles like a snake.

They reached the peak of the roof. Below them, the lake was a churning gray beast, waves crashing against the pilings. The house groaned, a deep, resonant sound of timber giving way.

We can't stay here, Julian shouted over the wind. The window in my room. Second floor, north side. It overlooks the courtyard. If we can drop to the patio...

Drop? Elena yelled back. We're three stories up!

The stone wall! There's a trellis. It might hold. It's better than the roof dissolving under us.

As if to prove his point, a section of the gutter near the chimney tore away with a metallic screech, crashing into the yard below. The water rising around their boots was eroding the mortar.

They moved to the lee side of the house, dropping down onto a narrow stone ledge that ran beneath the second-floor windows. The wind was less violent here, buffered by the house itself.

Julian moved first, testing his weight on the stones. He reached the window he had spoken of. It was locked, of course. He didn't bother with politeness. He smashed the pane with his elbow, knocking out the shards with a wrapped fist.

In, he commanded, clearing the remaining glass from the frame.

Elena shimmied along the ledge, her back pressed against the cold stone. The water from the roof was cascading over the eaves above them, creating a curtain they had to push through.

She reached the window. Julian's hands were there, gripping her waist, pulling her inside. She tumbled onto the floor of his bedroom. It was dark, the curtains drawn, smelling faintly of cedar and him.

She scrambled up, turning to help him, but he was already inside, slamming the sash shut and locking it. He leaned his forehead against the glass, his chest heaving.

For a moment, the only sound was the rain lashing the glass and their ragged breathing.

Safe? Elena whispered, the word tasting like ash.

Julian turned slowly. He looked at the floorboards of his room.

We need to go, he said quietly.

Before Elena could ask why, she saw it. The floorboards weren't weeping yet. They were bowing. The wood was swelling, expanding as if soaking up water from the inside out.

The sound started again. The low, mournful hum of the house.

Downstairs, Julian said, grabbing her hand again. We try the front door. If the attic is gone, maybe the ground floor is still...

He didn't finish the sentence. They both knew it was a lie. The house wasn't flooding from the top down. It was filling from the bottom up. The attic was just the first place the water breached because it was the lowest point of the house's consciousness, the place where the memories were stored.

They ran out into the hallway. The second floor looked normal, terrifyingly so. The Persian runner on the floor was dry. The oil paintings on the walls were straight.

Run, Julian said again, sensing the trap before it sprang.

They reached the grand staircase. The foyer below was a disaster. The water was already there, a dark, churning pool filling the entrance hall. The front door was a rectangle of wood half-submerged in the black liquid.

But the stairs...

The water on the stairs wasn't coming from the hall. It was bubbling up from the steps themselves.

Julian skidded to a halt at the top of the flight. The water was rising rapidly, climbing the stairs like a frantic animal, rushing upward to meet them. It was higher here than it had been in the attic. The pressure was immense.

The banister groaned, popping its nails as the wood swelled.

We're trapped, Elena said, the realization hitting her with the force of a physical blow. There is no down.

Julian looked left, toward the guest wing, then right, toward the master suite. The water was surging up the main staircase in a torrent, blocking the path to the foyer.

The east wing, he said. The balcony. Maybe we can signal...

They turned and ran toward the east wing. But as they passed the landing, a massive beam from the ceiling above cracked. Plaster rained down, dust choking the air.

A large chunk of ornate molding, heavy as a log, broke free and plummeted straight toward Elena.

She saw it out of the corner of her eye—a shadow detaching itself from the ceiling. She froze, her feet refusing to move in the rising water that was now sloshing over the landing's threshold.

Julian didn't freeze. He threw himself at her, his shoulder slamming into her midsection. The impact knocked the wind out of her as he drove them both backward, pinning her against the wall just as the heavy molding crashed into the spot where she had been standing. It smashed into the floorboards, splintering the wood and sending a shockwave through the water that nearly knocked them off their feet.

Elena found herself pressed hard against the wallpaper, Julian's body forming a barricade between her and the falling debris. He had one hand braced against the wall by her head, the other arm wrapped tightly around her waist, holding her up as her legs shook.
