# Chapter 8: The Dock at Dusk

"We can't stay here," Elena said, her voice hollow as she stared at the diary page, "the house is just a shell. The answer is out there."

The words hung in the damp air of the parlor. Julian stood by the window, his silhouette cutting the last of the gray twilight. Water still dripped from the ceiling joists, a steady percussion against the warped floorboards. The flood in the attic had receded, leaving behind the smell of silt and rot and something older than the wood itself.

Elena traced her finger over the final entry. The handwriting was shaky, almost illegible, as if Cora had written it in the dark.

*Meet me where the water holds the sky. The game ends where it began.*

She looked up at Julian. "The dock. It has to be the dock."

He didn't argue. The flood had stripped him of rational excuses. He let out a long breath and sank into the chair across from her, elbows on his knees, hands clasped loose. His watch chain caught the candlelight, a dull gleam against his chest.

"There's something I should have told you the first night," he said.

Elena closed the diary. "I'm listening."

"Your aunt didn't just hire me to fix the shutters." Julian rubbed the back of his neck, a rare crack in his composure. "She asked me to watch the house. To watch for you. She said a March girl always comes back to the water eventually."

"That doesn't explain why you knew about the girl in the mirror."

He was quiet for a long moment. When he spoke, his voice was low. "I stayed here for a month after Cora died. Before the estate was settled. I thought it was just a job—check the pipes, keep the roof from caving in."

"And?"

"And on the third night, I saw her." Julian met her eyes. "A little girl in a blue dress standing behind me in the bathroom mirror. When I turned around, no one was there."

Elena's chest tightened. "You saw her too."

"I didn't sleep in the house after that. I stayed in the boathouse. Told myself it was exhaustion. Told myself the story of Tomas was just small-town gossip." He shook his head. "But last night, in the attic, I saw her again. She was standing behind you. Like she was waiting for you to turn around."

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"Because the moment I said it out loud, it became real." Julian's jaw tightened. "And if it was real, then the game was real. And if the game was real, then you—"

"I killed him," Elena said flatly.

"You didn't kill him, Elena. You were eight years old. You played a game. He didn't come up."

"Running is killing when no one else is looking."

The words came out bitter, sharp. She had held them in for twenty years. It felt good to let them bleed.

Julian leaned forward. "The previous tenant—the man who rented this place before Cora bought it—he kept a journal. I found it in the boathouse. He saw the girl too. He wrote about the water in the hallway, the humming floorboards. He left after three weeks. Told the real estate agent the house was cursed."

"The house isn't cursed," Elena said. "I cursed it. I put my shame in the walls."

"Then it's time to take it back."

She looked at him. For the first time since she'd arrived, his eyes held no evasions. No careful distance. Just the hard, patient weight of someone who had chosen to believe her.

Elena stood. She folded the diary page carefully and tucked it into her pocket. Her legs felt steady. The fear was still there, coiled in her stomach, but it had been joined by something else. A cold, clear certainty.

She walked to the foyer. The wetness on the door handle had dried, leaving behind the faint, brackish smell of the lake. She found a white candle on the mantel that the flood had spared, untouched, and placed it in the center of the floor.

"What are you doing?" Julian asked from behind her.

"Breaking the cycle." She struck a match and lit the wick. The flame was small, trembling, but it threw amber light into the shadows that had clung to the corners of the hallway. "The house doesn't own me. It kept the memory, but the memory is mine. I'm taking it back."

She turned to face him. "I'm coming back here. I'm not abandoning the house. But I'm not hiding in it either."

Julian studied her for a long moment. There was no softness in his expression. Only the hard line of his jaw and the flicker of something close to respect.

"Then let's go," he said.

They stepped out into the Blue Hour.

The air smelled of damp earth and pine needles. The lake was visible between the trees, a sheet of black glass catching the last traces of daylight. The path to the dock was rough, worn down by years of rain and neglect. Every shadow seemed to breathe.

Elena walked ahead, her bare feet sure against the worn earth. She had stopped wearing shoes inside the house days ago. The ground felt solid. Real.

"Elena."

Julian's voice stopped her halfway down the path.

She turned.

He closed the distance slowly. His hand found hers, fingers threading through hers with a gentleness that didn't match the callouses on his palms. He stopped her completely, turning her to face him.

"Don't make a promise you can't keep," he said.

"I'm not promising anything. I'm choosing."

His other hand came up, cupping her face. His thumb grazed her cheekbone, a touch that was almost reverent. His eyes searched hers, as if he was trying to memorize the shape of her. The angle of her jaw. The shadows under her eyes.

He pressed his forehead to hers. "Then come back."

She felt his lips against her brow. A kiss that was soft and lingering, heavy with everything he couldn't bring himself to say out loud. An unspoken prayer. A plea.

Elena closed her eyes for a single breath. The warmth of his mouth stayed on her skin.

When she pulled back, her voice was steady. "I will."

They walked the rest of the path together.

The dock stretched out into the lake, gray wood slick with moisture. The mooring rope hung frayed and empty. No boat. No wind. The water was perfectly still, reflecting the deepening violet of the sky.

Elena stepped onto the wooden slats.

The sun dipped below the horizon.

The world held its breath.

Beneath the dock, a soft blue light began to pulse. It thrummed in time with the beat of her heart, steady and patient, as if it had been waiting for her whole life to arrive at this moment.

Elena walked to the edge. The blue light was coming from the water itself, a glow that wrapped around the wooden pilings like bioluminescence. It was warm. It was familiar.

She sat down slowly, gripping the edge of the dock for balance. She slipped off her shoes and let her bare feet dangle over the side.

Her toes broke the surface of the lake.

The water was not cold.

It was warm. A summer breeze touched her skin, carrying the ghost of laughter and the scent of pine needles baked in July sun. The blue light wrapped around her ankle like a greeting.

Elena looked down into the water.

A girl stared back.

Not a reflection. A girl in a blue dress, standing just beneath the surface. Her dark hair floated around her face like silk, but her eyes were wide and clear and achingly young.

Elena's breath caught.

The girl smiled.

It was not a cruel smile. It was the smile of an eight-year-old who had been waiting a very long time for someone to remember her.

"I know who you are," Elena whispered.

The blue light pulsed beneath the slats.

The water hummed.

And the girl in the lake lifted her hand, reaching for the surface.
