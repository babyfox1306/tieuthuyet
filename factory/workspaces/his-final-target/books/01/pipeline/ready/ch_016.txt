# Chapter 16: The Reckoning

The maintenance ledge still shuddered from their landing when the ledger device in Sloane Mercer's pocket pulsed hard — Rook was moving Mara. The signal painted a narrow blue line toward the data relay station nineteen floors below.

Sloane pressed her back against the cold concrete and watched the line split into a grid pattern across the cracked screen. Rook had her on a forced march. East stairwell. Level fourteen. Fifteen. Sixteen.

Lucian moved beside her, a shadow in the dim light filtering through the external grille. "The relay station is a dead end for him. No exit, no roof access from that floor."

"Then he's planning to use her as a bargaining chip. A bullet. A shield." Sloane kept her eyes on the screen. "He doesn't know I have the mirror key yet. He still thinks I'm blind."

"Then we move while he's confident."

They dropped through the maintenance hatch. The metal ladder groaned under their weight, the rungs slick with condensation. Nineteen floors of controlled descent. Sloane's shoulders burned by the tenth landing, but she didn't slow. Every second Rook had alone with Mara was a second too long.

The screen pulsed again. Level eighteen. The blue line stopped.

He was there.

Sloane hit the maintenance door at level nineteen with her shoulder, the lock snapping under the impact. The corridor beyond was dark, lined with server cabinets that hummed with the building's central nervous system. The relay station was at the end.

Lucian caught her arm. "Security grid."

She looked. Infrared beams lined the hall, crossing at ankle, waist, and neck height. A model she recognized without knowing why. Her hands moved before her conscious mind caught up, tracing the control panel mounted beside the doorframe.

"Third panel from the left. Red auxiliary. Don't touch the black wire."

"Are you remembering?" Lucian asked.

"No. My hands do."

She touched the circle on her wrist. The skin was raw, permanently creased from the restraint cuff. A brand Rook had given her. She used the gesture to steady herself, then cut the first wire.

The alarm system went silent.

The infrared beams vanished.

They moved.

The relay station door was reinforced steel, but the locking mechanism was a standard magnetic seal. Sloane pulled the blade from her ankle holster and wedged it into the seam, leveraging the frame until the magnet released with a sharp crack.

The door swung open.

Mara was on the other side. Bound to a steel chair, her wrists raw from the zip ties. Bruises bloomed across her jaw and down her collarbone. But her eyes were the same — sharp, furious, alive.

"Took you long enough."

Sloane dropped to her knees. The blade was out, sawing through the plastic. "I had to wait for the dramatic entrance."

"You always did love a stage."

The cuffs snapped. Sloane pulled her sister into her arms. The embrace was brief, hard, electric with guilt and relief. "Are you hit?"

"Just the face. Rook has a mean right hook." Mara touched her swollen jaw and winced. "He asked where the mirror key was. I told him it was buried in a cemetery he'd never find."

"He didn't believe you."

"No. Hence the face." Mara looked past Sloane to Lucian standing in the doorway. "You brought him."

"He brought himself."

Mara's eyes narrowed. "The sunrise clause."

"Still in effect," Lucian said quietly. "But I'm still here."

Sloane pulled her sister to her feet. The marks on Mara's wrists were raw, identical to the pressure circle on Sloane's own arm. Permanent. They wore the same brand now.

"The mirror key," Sloane said.

Mara reached into her collar and pulled out a folded piece of paper, worn and soft from being pressed against her skin. "He searched me twice. Never thought to check under the collar. Rook's blind spot is his belief that conditioning is stronger than choice."

Sloane took the paper. It was covered in Mara's handwriting, a sequence of numbers and codes that matched the gaps in the ledger's encryption. The final piece of the mirror key.

She crossed to the relay station's mainframe and plugged the ledger device into the port. The cracked screen lit up. Mara's codes unlocked the final encrypted segment.

The complete transaction ledger bloomed across the screen.

Every contract. Every payment. Every recording. Cassian Rook's entire weapons network, laid out in black and white. The seven target files Lucian had been ordered to execute. The witnesses Rook had eliminated. The clean-up crews. The false intelligence reports.

Sloane's hands trembled over the keyboard. This was the evidence she had buried in her own conditioned memory. The truth she had erased to survive Rook's interrogation.

Mara stood beside her. "Once it goes out, there's no taking it back."

"That's the point."

Sloane selected the transmission protocols. Multiple agencies. Multiple news outlets. FBI. Department of Justice. The major networks. She programmed the send command for simultaneous release.

Her finger hovered over the key.

"I sold seven people to this man," Sloane said. "I ordered their deaths. I built the system that made it clean."

"You built the system that caught him," Mara said. "You staged my death. You erased your memory. You made yourself the trap so that he couldn't escape. Those seven deaths are on his hands, Sloane. You were his weapon. But you chose to break the chain."

Sloane pressed send.

The screen displayed a progress bar. 10%. 25%. 75%.

Outside, the first sirens started. Blue and red light bled through the high windows, slicing through the dust. The authorities were surrounding the building. They had minutes before the cordon was complete.

100%. Transmission complete.

The evidence was out. Rook's network was exposed. The information war was won.

But Rook was still inside.

Lucian appeared in the doorway. "He abandoned the control room. He's heading back up the east stairwell. Going to the rooftop."

Sloane nodded. It made sense. The rooftop was where the blind interval had started. Where he had worn Lucian's face. Where he had tried to break her with her own conditioning.

He crossed the room and took her hand.

His palm was warm, rough with calluses from years of holding a rifle. His thumb pressed against her knuckles, grounding her.

"I will stay with you, Sloane. No matter what."

She looked down at their joined hands. The permanent circle on her wrist was stark against her skin. His thumb traced the edge of the scar tissue.

"That doesn't frighten you?" she asked. "Knowing what I am? What I ordered? The blood on my hands?"

"It frightens me more than dying," he said quietly. "But I'm still here. I'm not leaving."

Sloane closed her eyes for half a second. She allowed herself to feel the warmth of his hand, the solid weight of his presence. It felt like a lifeline. It felt like a trap. She didn't care which anymore.

She squeezed his hand once, hard. Then let go.

"Then let's finish this."

Sloane pressed the comm into Mara's palm. "Stay behind the blast door. Don't open it for anyone but us. The authorities can secure you. Just stay alive."

Mara grabbed her wrist. "Sloane. Listen to me. He will use the conditioning. The circle. The voice. He built you to obey. He spent years turning you into a weapon he could point at anyone."

Sloane met her sister's eyes. "Then I'll have to be faster than my own memory."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the only one I have."

Lucian held the service elevator door open. The steel box waited, a narrow rectangle of light against the darkness of the corridor.

Sloane stepped inside.

He followed.

The doors slid closed.

The elevator began to rise.

Cassian Rook's voice echoed through the building's PA system, his voice distorted by the speakers on every floor. "The sunrise clause is still in effect, Vey. Let's see whose loyalty is stronger — the contract's, or the woman who made you break it."
