# Chapter 10: The Scope's Hidden Truth

The cold wind whipped Sloane Mercer's hair as she stepped onto the rooftop, the rifle a dark silhouette against the pre-dawn sky, waiting like a judge for her final verdict. It stood exactly where the monitor in the memory-erasure room had shown it. Mounted, aligned, and aimed at the building across the street.

Which meant someone had reset the scene for her.

Sloane stopped two steps beyond the access door. Her left fingertip traced a small circle against the inside of her wrist.

Pressure gathered behind her eyes, but no memory came.

Lucian moved past her, silent and alert. He checked the parapet, the neighboring rooflines, then the shadows beneath the ventilation structures. His hand stayed near the pistol under his jacket.

"No movement," he said.

"That means no movement you can see."

"It means exactly what it sounds like."

"Then try sounding less confident."

He glanced at her. "You used to find confidence attractive."

She looked at the rifle. "Apparently, past Sloane had terrible judgment."

The weapon sat on a portable bench anchored by four magnetic feet. Its bipod had been removed. A cable ran from the trigger assembly into a narrow housing bolted beneath the table, too clean to have survived the night's earlier violence.

The rifle had been placed after Mara's body disappeared.

Or before Sloane and Lucian returned, by someone who knew they would.

She approached without touching it. A thin layer of moisture silvered the scope and barrel, except for a dry oval on the stock where a hand had recently rested.

"Fresh contact," she said. "Someone handled it within the last ten minutes."

Lucian crouched beside the mounting bracket. "No residue on the bolts."

"Recently installed."

"Or recently cleaned."

"That would be an absurdly specific housekeeping emergency."

His mouth almost moved. Not quite a smile, but close enough to irritate her.

Sloane circled the table. The rifle's muzzle pointed toward a dark office window on the opposite building. The angle was wrong for Mara's earlier position and wrong for any ordinary shot from this roof.

It aimed at glass.

"Why mount a rifle facing an empty office?" she asked.

Lucian rose. "Maybe it isn't empty."

"Then lend me your supernatural eyesight."

"No supernatural claims. Only excellent eyesight."

"Modesty remains among your lesser talents."

She bent toward the scope, careful not to disturb the cheek rest. The lens showed the office window enlarged inside the reticle. Nothing moved behind it. No silhouette, no heat shimmer, no curtain disturbed by a hidden body.

Only darkness and a faint shape near the bottom edge.

Sloane adjusted her angle.

The shape vanished.

"Did you touch the focus?" Lucian asked.

"No."

"Don't."

She looked at him. "Useful instruction usually comes with a reason."

"The ring may be indexed to a trigger point."

There it was again: the selective caution of a man who knew which traps had teeth but refused to explain how often they had bitten him.

Sloane examined the focus ring. A tiny notch had been cut into its underside. Lucian saw her find it, and something tightened around his eyes.

Before reaching for the rifle, he turned the ring exactly one quarter rotation away from clarity.

The office window blurred.

His jaw flexed as though the movement had cost him.

"You do that before obeying an order you hate," Sloane said.

He released the scope. "You remember?"

"No. I observe. It is less sentimental and generally more reliable."

Her finger circled her wrist again. This time a pulse jumped beneath her skin, followed by a flash of sensation: cold metal against her shoulder, her thumb near a safety lever, breath held at the bottom of an exhale.

Then nothing.

Sloane gripped the table until the dizziness passed.

Lucian took one step toward her.

"Don't," she said.

He stopped immediately.

That should not have mattered. It did.

Sloane leaned toward the scope again. The quarter turn had altered the reflection on the lens. A geometric mark hovered beneath the reticle, so faint it could have been a scratch: two vertical cuts crossed by a hooked diagonal.

She knew that mark.

Not from conscious memory. From the instant recoil inside her, sharp enough to make her teeth ache.

"Rook," she whispered.

Lucian came to her side without touching her. "What do you see?"

"His insignia."

"Where?"

"In the lens. Not through it. On it."

Sloane shifted left. The mark changed shape. A dark curve appeared beside it, then a pale sliver that resembled the edge of a cheek reflected from somewhere behind the scope.

She turned fast.

The roof remained empty.

When she looked back, a single drop of condensation had gathered at the top of the objective lens. It slid slowly across the reflection, pulling the pale curve downward. For one impossible second, the distortion made it look like a tear falling from Rook's eye.

A cheap trick of light.

Her body reacted as though he were standing behind her.

She traced another circle against her wrist, harder now. A sequence stirred beneath thought: circle, brace, settle, breathe.

The next movement refused to surface.

"His face is embedded in the reflection," she said. "Or an image of it. The insignia is aligned with the reticle."

Lucian studied the lens from several angles. "A projection film."

"Too thin to see directly."

"Placed to be visible only at the correct focus."

"Which you moved away from."

His gaze met hers.

Sloane straightened slowly. "You knew something was there."

"I knew the setting was deliberate."

"That was not the question."

"No."

"Charming. Try again with an answer."

Lucian looked toward the paling horizon. A narrow line of gray had opened beneath the clouds.

The sight of it changed him.

Not dramatically. Lucian did nothing dramatically when stillness could be more frightening. But his shoulders went rigid, and his attention fixed on the first hint of dawn as if it had placed a blade against Sloane's throat.

Her sarcasm died before it reached her mouth.

"What happens at sunrise?"

He said nothing.

Sloane stepped between him and the horizon. "What happens?"

His eyes lowered to her face. She saw conflict there, stripped of his usual control, and knew the answer would hurt before he gave it.

"Lucian."

"I loved you," he said.

The confession landed with brutal softness.

Sloane stared at him. "Past tense?"

"No."

One word, quietly spoken. No defense attached.

Her chest tightened. That was the dangerous part of him, not the rifle, not the scars, not the efficiency with which he measured every exit. It was the tenderness he offered as if he still knew the shape of a woman she could not access.

"That does not answer the question."

"It explains why answering it is difficult."

"Difficulty is not an exemption."

"I know."

"Then stop deciding what truth can be survived."

His expression flinched. Barely, but she caught it.

The sky behind her grew lighter.

Lucian lifted both hands slowly, giving her time to refuse. "May I touch you?"

Every alarm in her body advised distance. Every fractured instinct wanted the exact opposite.

"For what purpose?"

"To keep you looking at me when you hear this."

Not comfort. An anchor.

Sloane hated that she needed one.

"Yes," she said. "Only my face."

His palms settled against her cheeks, warm despite the cold. He held her gently, with none of the ownership her forgotten decisions had tried to impose on both of them.

Her hands remained at her sides.

"The recording you recovered was edited," he said. "There was a final clause."

The air seemed to sharpen.

"Full wording," Sloane demanded.

His thumbs stayed motionless beside her jaw. "If you failed to recover the memories required to identify and expose Cassian Rook before sunrise, the contract would enter terminal enforcement."

The rifle waited behind him.

Sloane heard its silence.

"What does terminal enforcement mean?"

Lucian's gaze did not leave hers. "I am required to execute you."

Her mind rejected the sentence, then accepted every word at once.

The rooftop tilted.

Lucian steadied her face but did not pull her closer. He let her decide whether to stand or fall, and somehow that mercy made the horror worse.

"At sunrise," she said.

"Yes."

"Today."

"Yes."

"And you accepted this?"

"With full knowledge."

A broken laugh escaped her. "Of course you did. Apparently, former Sloane could turn emotional devastation into contract language."

"You believed restoration might make you dangerous."

"So she appointed her lover as executioner."

"You appointed the one person you believed would honor your choice."

"That is not romantic."

"No."

"It is monstrous."

"Yes."

Her knees struck the concrete.

Lucian went down with her, hands leaving her face the instant she folded. He remained close enough to catch her only if she asked, which was considerate of him and deeply inconvenient.

Sloane pressed both hands against the rooftop. Grit bit into her palms. Facts. Concrete. Rifle. Dawn. Contract.

Her left finger drew a circle against her wrist.

Circle, brace, settle, breathe.

A flash tore through her: Lucian framed in a scope, not as a target but as a witness. Her own hand turning the focus ring. A red indicator blinking beneath the trigger guard.

The memory vanished before she could seize it.

She sucked in air.

"Sloane."

"Do not say my name like that."

"Like what?"

"Like it belongs to someone you are already grieving."

His face hardened. "You are not dead."

"Your contract seems optimistic."

He leaned closer, stopping outside the boundary she had set. "There is still time."

"How much?"

His silence answered.

She pushed herself upright. Emotional collapse could have five minutes. After that, it became self-indulgence.

"Then we use the rifle."

Lucian rose with her. "The mounting system may be wired."

"Everything is wired. So am I, apparently."

She positioned herself behind the stock. The cheek rest met her jaw with disturbing familiarity. Her right hand settled around the grip without hesitation.

Her left finger circled the inside of her wrist.

Circle.

Her hand moved to the fore-end.

Brace.

She aligned her shoulder.

Settle.

Her breath emptied.

Breathe.

The reticle steadied over the opposite window. Rook's insignia appeared faintly along its lower edge. The condensation drop had reached the bottom of the lens, stretching the reflected face into something narrow and amused.

Nothing unlocked.

No recording played. No compartment opened. No memory arrived.

Sloane lifted her head. "Again."

She repeated the sequence.

Circle. Grip. Shoulder. Exhale.

Nothing.

She altered the pressure of her thumb. Adjusted the stock. Touched the focus ring and turned it back to the marked notch.

Lucian caught her wrist before the rotation completed.

She froze.

His grip was firm, his face strained. "That could arm the mount."

"Release me."

He did.

She completed the turn.

A click sounded beneath the table.

Both of them went still.

Static burst from somewhere overhead.

"Closer," Cassian Rook's voice said.

Sloane searched the rooftop structures. The sound came from a speaker hidden inside the ventilation frame, inaccessible without dismantling the metal housing.

Rook continued, "The hands remember long after the conscience gets cleaned."

Her fingertip circled her wrist again before she could stop it.

Lucian noticed.

Sloane removed her hand from the rifle. "Watching from a safe distance?"

A low chuckle crackled through the speaker. "Distance is the privilege of preparation."

"Then prepare for disappointment."

"You already did. You prepared every part of this, including him."

Lucian's attention tracked the speaker grille, calculating angles that offered nothing to shoot.

Rook's voice softened. "Restoration is such an ugly word for what comes next. You are not recovering, Sloane. You are returning."

The rifle's trigger assembly emitted a brief electronic chirp.

Sloane looked down.

A narrow strip beneath the housing had illuminated. Four empty positions glowed in a line, waiting for inputs she did not understand.

She placed her hand on the grip and tried the sequence a third time.

Circle. Brace. Settle. Breathe.

The lights remained empty.

Rook laughed again. "You always hated failing under observation."

"Then this must be thrilling for you."

"Not as thrilling as sunrise."

Lucian stepped between her and the rifle.

Rook's voice sharpened with satisfaction. "Will he tell you how precise the clause is? Will he let you watch the light reach the eastern wall before he raises the weapon? Or will tenderness make him late?"

Sloane's stomach turned.

Lucian did not deny it.

"Contracts are useful things," Rook said. "They reveal which promise matters when every choice becomes unforgivable."

The speaker hissed. The gray line at the horizon brightened, touching the farthest rooftop.

Sloane looked at Lucian. His face held no surprise, only the terrible restraint of a man who had been counting down in silence.

Rook's voice faded into static.

Sloane's eyes widened as a terror greater than her own death closed around her.

"You knew," she said. "All this time, you knew you might have to kill me."
