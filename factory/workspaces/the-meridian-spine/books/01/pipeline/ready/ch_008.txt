# Chapter 8: The Broken Seal

"Take your hand off the coupon," Iris Kane said as Petra Lund reached across the cutting bench.

Lund's fingers stopped an inch from the polished steel fixture. She straightened slowly, her expression flickering through indignation before settling into something blank and professional. "I was simply positioning it for the saw."

"You were touching the evidence." Iris kept her voice flat, her eyes on Lund's hands as they retreated to her sides. "Step away from the bench."

The storage bay stood open behind them. Its steel door had been swung wide to expose the replacement Spine coupon, a perfect counterpart to the fractured piece Iris had recovered from the certification line. The clerk had broken the tamper strip at eight that morning. The official transfer was clean on paper.

"Iris." Nadia Okafor's voice came from the observation window. She stood in the anteroom with a tablet pressed against her chest, her reflection ghosting across the glass. "We're on a timetable. The public briefing is in thirty-six hours. Stellan Marsh expects the test result before we go live."

"The briefing can wait for clean procedure." Iris pulled a pair of nitrile gloves from her coat pocket and worked them over her fingers. The latex snapped tight against her wrists. "Who sealed this bay?"

"Standard protocol," Lund said. "I sealed it myself after the replacement coupon was logged in last night. The night shift supervisor witnessed."

"Alone?"

"With supervision."

Iris moved past Lund to the open bay. The interior was climate-controlled, the air dry and frigid. The replacement coupon sat in a fitted cradle, identical in dimension to the fractured piece, its surface unblemished. No drag marks. No visible compression points. A perfect substitute.

She leaned closer to the door frame. The tamper strip was a laminated plastic band, two centimeters wide, fixed across the seam between the door and the frame. It bore the Directorate's embossed logo and a twelve-digit serial number. The clerk had cut it cleanly at the seam, which meant it had been intact at the time of today's opening.

But the seal itself was wrong.

Iris pulled a magnifying loupe from her case and pressed it to her eye. The tamper strip had two rows of pinprick registration holes, designed to align with matching perforations in the frame. If the strip was applied correctly, the holes overlapped perfectly, creating a continuous row of apertures.

These did not overlap.

The offset was smaller than a fingernail clipping but too regular to be accidental. The factory-punched holes on the strip sat a fraction of a millimeter higher than the frame holes. Every single one. As if the strip had been applied by hand, without the alignment tool, by someone who could not see the discrepancy at normal scale.

"Who installed this strip?" Iris asked.

"I did," Lund said. "As I told you."

"And the strip came from the Directorate's supply?"

"Yes."

Iris straightened. She pulled a marking pen from her pocket and drew a small circle on the frame next to the misaligned holes. "This bay was opened and resealed."

"Impossible," Lund said. "The strip was intact when we opened it today."

"The strip was intact. The seal beneath it was not." Iris touched the circle. "There's an older adhesive residue under the current strip — a crescent shape, roughly two centimeters long. The residue is yellowed, brittle. Someone applied the current strip over an older seal residue without cleaning the surface first. They were in a hurry."

She stared at the crescent for a moment, its yellowed edge curling slightly away from the metal. The residue was old enough to have developed micro-cracks along its surface. This was not a touch-up or a clumsy application. This was a deliberate disassembly and reassembly of a sealed evidence environment. The question was not if someone had accessed the bay, but what they had done while the seal was broken.

Nadia had stepped into the room. "You're saying the bay was opened before your authorized access today?"

"I'm saying the evidence environment was compromised." Iris pulled an evidence kit from her bag. She set out a series of sterile sleeves, each labeled with a marker. "I'm preserving the current strip for analysis. The frame seal. The latch backing. Every component."

"Iris." Nadia's voice carried a warning edge. "The clock is running. If you spend the morning cataloguing adhesive residue instead of cutting the coupon, we miss the briefing window. You don't have the luxury of a full-scale forensic dig."

"I have the luxury of a clean chain of custody, or I have nothing." Iris didn't look up. She slid a thin blade beneath the tamper strip and lifted it from the frame. The adhesive released with a faint tearing sound. She placed the strip in the first sleeve, pressing it flat with her palm. "If I cut the coupon without verifying the integrity of the storage environment, the entire test result can be challenged. You wanted a reproducible result. This is how you get one."

Nadia's jaw tightened. "You have six hours. I want hourly updates."

"You'll get them."

Iris worked the frame screws free with a small hex driver. The latch plate came away from the frame with a clean pop. She placed it in a second sleeve. Then she turned to the exposed metal surface where the seal compound had been pressed against the factory paint.

A faint crescent of dried adhesive remained. It was brittle, slightly yellowed, with a texture distinct from the fresh strip she had just removed. Iris scraped a sample into a third sleeve, using a sterile spatula to lift the fragments.

"The seal backing," she said. "I'm taking a contact swab from the rubber compound."

"That's excessive," Lund said.

"That's standard." Iris pressed a sterile cotton tip against the interior seal surface, rotating it slowly. The fibers came away clean, but when she held the swab under the penlight, she caught a glint of something embedded in the tip.

She brought the loupe closer.

A blue grain, angular and faceted, sat trapped in the cotton fibers. It was small enough to be invisible to the naked eye, but under magnification its structure was unmistakable. It was a precision-ground industrial abrasive, consistent with the compound she had seen in the micrographs of the Kessler override jig. The fabrication floor at the Directorate used a different grade — white, commercial aluminum oxide. Blue abrasive was specialty stock. Someone had brought it into this room on their hands or their tools.

"Nadia, I need a portable microscope. The high-resolution unit from the main lab."

"Iris —"

"Now."

Nadia made a call. Three minutes later, a clerk arrived with the scope. Iris mounted it on the frame, adjusted the focus, and centered the image on the swab tip.

The blue grain was clearly visible against the white cotton. It sat at the boundary between the fresh adhesive residue and the older crescent beneath it, pressed into the compound as if someone had touched the seal surface while the adhesive was still wet.

She zoomed in further. The grain was not alone. A thin trail of smaller fragments led from the seal edge toward the latch mechanism, forming a faint constellation of microscopic blue flecks, too small to see without the scope, impossible to clean without a solvent.

"The bay was opened," Iris said quietly. "The original seal was removed. Someone accessed the interior. Then they resealed it with a new strip, but they didn't clean the surface. This grain — this specialty abrasive — was on their glove or their tool. It transferred to the adhesive when they pressed the second strip down."

She looked at Nadia.

"This grain came from the fabrication floor."

Nadia's face went still. "You can't know that."

"I can match the compound. I can trace the supplier. And I can prove the bay was breached." Iris held up the swab. "This grain is trapped between two layers of a seal that was supposed to prove this bay had never been touched."

She placed the swab in a fourth sleeve.

"I'm preserving the latch, the current seal strip, the adhesive sample, the blue grain, and the full door frame swath." She capped the marker and initialed each label. "The coupon stays in the bay until I complete a full environmental inspection of the fabrication floor."

"You can't shut down the test," Lund said.

"I'm not shutting down the test. I'm expanding the sample." Iris turned back to the microscope.

She adjusted the focus, centering the image on the boundary between the fresh adhesive and the older residue beneath it. There, sealed perfectly between the two layers, a cluster of blue grains caught the crosslight.

Physical proof that the supposedly untouched bay had been opened on the fabrication floor and closed again.

Iris lowered the loupe.

"It's here," she said. "The evidence is sealed between the layers. The fabrication floor left it behind."

Nadia's silence was the loudest thing in the room. She was supposed to be Iris's partner, the woman who had pushed for the original investigation. But when Iris had found the blue grain, Nadia's face had not registered surprise or concern. It registered calculation.

"I want a full inventory of every abrasive compound used in the Directorate's fabrication bay for the last six months," Iris said. "And I want the names of everyone who had access to this storage unit during the night shift."

Lund started to speak. Iris cut her off.

"I'm not asking. The seal is broken. And the fabrication floor left the proof."

She closed the microscope case and snapped the latches shut.

Six hours.

She was going to use every one.
