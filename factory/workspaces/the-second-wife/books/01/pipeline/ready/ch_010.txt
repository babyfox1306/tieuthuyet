# Chapter 10: The Inheritance

Lydia smiled as she placed the will on the desk. “Sign it, Marcus. You know it’s the only way.”

The pen trembled in his hand. I watched from the corner, the locket cold against my chest. I had one move left.

The study smelled like old paper and fear. Marcus sat behind his desk, the document spread before him like a death warrant. His fingers hovered over the signature line, shaking.

“You’re signing over everything,” he said, his voice hollow. “The estate. The accounts. The company shares.”

“Everything I deserve,” Lydia replied. She stood beside him, one hand resting on his shoulder, her fingers pressing just hard enough to remind him who was in control. “After everything I’ve endured, Marcus. After Clara. After all of it.”

I stayed in the shadows near the bookshelf, the locket pressed against my chest. The memory card was still inside, warm from the footage. I could feel it like a second heartbeat.

Marcus looked up at me, his eyes desperate. “Anna. Tell her. Tell her what you found.”

“She knows what I found,” I said quietly.

Lydia’s smile didn’t waver. She reached into her pocket and pulled out a small USB drive. “I have something to show you, Marcus. Something that will explain everything.”

She walked to the laptop on the side table and inserted the drive. The screen flickered, and then the footage appeared — the same footage I’d watched in the east wing study. But this version was different. The camera angle was wider, the quality sharper. It showed me entering Marcus’s study, pulling back the floorboard, and holding up the bloodstained shirt.

“Your housekeeper,” Lydia said, her voice smooth as silk, “has been planting evidence against you. I caught her on camera three weeks ago, and I’ve been waiting for the right moment to expose her.”

Marcus stared at the screen, his face pale. “That’s not what happened. That’s not — ”

“I have the original footage,” Lydia interrupted. “I have backups. I have everything I need to prove that Anna is the one who killed Clara and tried to frame you for it.”

I stepped forward, the locket warm in my hand. “Show him the rest of the footage, Lydia. Show him what happens before I enter the room.”

Lydia’s smile faltered. “What are you talking about?”

“The full footage,” I said. “The part where you plant the shirt under the floorboard. The part where you smile at the camera. The part where you check your hair before leaving.”

“There is no such footage.”

“There is.” I opened the locket and pulled out the memory card. “I found it in the teddy bear camera you set up in the east wing. The one you thought I wouldn’t notice.”

The room went still. Marcus’s pen clattered to the desk. Lydia’s face drained of color.

“You’re lying,” she said, but her voice cracked.

“I’m not.” I held up the memory card. “This card has the full footage. You, planting the shirt. You, setting up the frame. You, smiling like you’d just won the lottery.”

Lydia’s hand moved toward her pocket. I saw the shift in her posture, the way her body tensed for action.

“Don’t,” I said.

She pulled out a gun.

It was small, silver, the kind of weapon that fit in a woman’s palm. She pointed it at me, her hand steady. “Give me the card.”

“Or what? You’ll shoot me? In front of your husband? In front of the cameras you installed everywhere?”

“I’ll shoot you and say you attacked me. Marcus will back me up. Won’t you, Marcus?”

Marcus didn’t answer. He was staring at the gun, his hands shaking.

“He won’t back you up,” I said. “He knows the truth. He knows what you did to Clara.”

“Clara was a mistake.” Lydia’s voice was cold now, flat. “She found the journal. She was going to tell Marcus. I didn’t mean to kill her, but she wouldn’t stop screaming.”

“So you pushed her down the stairs.”

“I pushed her. She fell. It was over in seconds.”

Marcus stood up, his chair scraping against the floor. “You killed her. You killed Clara and you let me think — ”

“I let you think you were in control,” Lydia snapped. “I let you blackmail me because it was easier than dealing with you directly. But I’m done playing games.”

She turned the gun toward Marcus.

I moved without thinking.

I lunged forward, my hand reaching for the gun. Lydia saw me coming and fired. The shot was loud, deafening in the small room. Marcus gasped and stumbled backward, his hand clutching his chest.

He’d been hit.

Lydia stared at the gun in her hand, her face frozen in shock. “I didn’t mean — ”

I didn’t wait for her to finish. I grabbed her wrist and twisted, forcing the gun down. She screamed and pulled away, stumbling backward toward the stairs.

The stairs.

I saw it happen in slow motion. Her heel caught on the edge of the rug. Her arms flailed. She tipped backward, her eyes wide with surprise.

She fell.

The sound of her body hitting the stairs was wet and final. She tumbled down, her limbs flopping like a broken doll, until she landed at the bottom in a crumpled heap.

I stood at the top of the stairs, breathing hard, the gun still in my hand.

Marcus was on the floor, blood spreading across his chest. He was alive, but barely.

“Anna,” he whispered. “Anna, come here.”

I knelt beside him, the gun forgotten in my hand.

“There’s a safe,” he said, his voice barely audible. “In the wall behind the painting in my study. The combination is 04-19-87.”

“Why are you telling me this?”

“Because the documents inside will destroy you.” He coughed, blood bubbling on his lips. “I had you investigated. After my wife told me to hire you, I had you investigated. I know everything. Your real name. Your record. The things you did before prison.”

I felt the world tilt beneath me.

“The safe has copies of everything,” he continued. “Police reports. Testimonies. Photographs. If anyone finds that safe, you’ll go back to prison. And this time, you won’t come out.”

“Why would you keep that?”

“Insurance.” He smiled weakly. “I thought I might need leverage someday. I didn’t think it would be like this.”

He closed his eyes. His chest stopped moving.

I stayed there for a long moment, kneeling beside his body, the gun still in my hand. The house was silent. Lydia was dead at the bottom of the stairs. Marcus was dead on the floor.

I was the only one left.

I stood up and walked to the study. The painting behind the desk — a landscape of rolling hills — was slightly crooked. I pushed it aside and found the safe, a small metal box embedded in the wall.

04-19-87.

The lock clicked open.

Inside were documents. Photographs. A thick envelope with my real name on it. I pulled it out and opened it, my hands shaking.

Marcus was right. Everything was there. My arrest records. My testimony from the trial. Photographs of me leaving the courthouse. Notes from the detective who worked my case. A summary of the crimes I’d committed before prison, the ones that never made it to court.

If this went to the police, I was finished.

I looked at the memory card still in my hand. The footage of Lydia planting the shirt. The evidence that could clear me.

I looked at the safe. The documents that could destroy me.

I made a choice.

I took the documents from the safe and placed them in the fireplace. I struck a match and watched them burn, the flames catching the edges, curling the paper into ash.

Then I took the memory card and snapped it in half. I dropped the pieces into the fire.

No evidence. No proof. No trail.

I was the only one who knew the truth. And I intended to keep it that way.

I walked through the house one last time, my footsteps echoing in the empty halls. I didn’t look at the bodies. I didn’t stop to pack my things. I just walked.

The front door was unlocked. I stepped out into the cold night air, the locket empty in my pocket.

The sirens were distant, growing closer. Someone must have heard the gunshot. Someone had called the police.

I walked down the driveway, my pace steady, my breath fogging in the cold air. I reached the gate and stopped.

My phone buzzed.

I pulled it out. A text from an unknown number:

“I know what you did. I have the safe.”

I stared at the screen, my blood turning to ice.

I looked back at the estate. The lights were on. The bodies were inside. The fire was still burning.

Someone else knew.

Someone had been watching.

My phone buzzed again. Another text from the same number:

“You’re not done, Anna. Not yet.”

I closed my eyes and took a deep breath.

Then I started walking.
