An Elder's Revolution - The Art of Sect Politics

by Candis

Honored by countless victories grasped and foes vanquished, Sect Elder Liu Wei is one of the most prominent figures of the Lunar Peaks Sect.

Known for his strict treatment of those below him and the harsh punishments he hands out to those who cross him, his name invokes both fear and awe to the sect's countless members, disciples and subordinates.

As a breakthrough leads him to ascend to the same level of cultivation as the sect's patriarch, he is well-positioned to further extend his power within the sect. Only, what should have been his greatest moment of triumph is instead one doubt and uncertainty: For the first time in his well over four-hundred year long journey of cultivaton, he has hit a complete roadblock, denying him any further steps on the way to immortality.

Without a family, any close friends or a particularly deep loyalty towards his sect, this leaves Liu Wei without a purpose to dedicate his efforts towards, even as the flame of ambition keeps burning in his chest. He now has no choice but to deeply contemplate what he wishes to do with the vast power he has spent his unfathomably long life accumulating.

As his contemplations lead him to rediscover a long discarded sense of compassion, a new goal beginns to form: bring as much justice and fairness into this ancient system of opression as his abilities afford him to.

Liu Wei shall walk on a path of defiance.

Quick overview of what you can expect from this story:

This is a story about Liu Wei, but it will be told from many perspectives. Giving the perspectives of several important side-characters room to breathe is my way of dealing with a main-character who is several centuries old and thus rather knowledgeable about the world and difficult to surprise. All of these characters have their own little stories and sub-plots that will be explored as the main story moves on.

As the title says, this novel will focus on sect politics. This includes detailed explorations of schemes, power-dynamics and the like. Action will be rather sparse, especially in the beginning. Most conflicts will need time to build up before ultimately erupting into violence. They will though, and when they do I hope the action will feel gratifying and consequential.

This story features power-progression in the form of cultivation. This includes the MC who will eventually regain his ability to cultivate, if you're concerned about that. This progression is featured prominently as a motivation for many characters, but will not be the focus of the story itself. The MC is already at a very high level of cultivation and will thus take a long time to progress further.

The world-building is one of the things I love most about writing, as such you can expect it to be detailed and extensive. The supernatural elements and cultivation system(s) are built from the ground up with their own internal logic in a way that I hope makes sense. I try to stay rather grounded with them - we won't be encountering anyone throwing cities or splitting mountains for a long time (if ever).

Questions of morality and ethics are central to the plot of this novel and will thus be explored in adequate detail in the form of both internal reflection and debates. Of course this isn't a philosophic essay, so these sections will mostly remain relatively short.

The tone of this story is usually rather serious, but there are occasionaly slight satirical elements as it seeks to subvert expectations regarding common Xianxia tropes.

Updates three times a week, Wed/Fri/Sun.

Enjoy!

Overall

Great novel with a refreshing take on Xianxia.

Reviewed at: Chapter 44: The Festival of the Seven Heroes
by trucha17 8/26/2025, 11:27 AM

Let me say that if you’re looking for a novel full of combat scene, this isn’t for you. 
There is some amount of action but it isn’t the main focus of the novel. 
It’s more encompassing and touch multiple facet of the life of a cultivator and his outlook on the world at large. 
The world building is a strong point but I would say it lacks a bit in term of small details not so important themself, but helpful to provide context and a full picture. A better sense of scale of the geography and things like that. 
Also, while the stages of cultivation themselves were explained and are slightly off the regular power system, we lack some fundamentals bits that would haven been assumed in a more cliché progression. Things like the ratio of people capable of becoming cultivator and if it’s hereditary, what constitute talent, simply comprehension or a bodily component? Normally there would be things like spiritual root and rate of absorption of Qi that is essential but here Qi doesn’t seem to be a bottleneck. Is there things like physique?

Furthermore, I especially like that it isn’t from the perspective of an oppressed kid on a quest of vengeance to right the wrong of the world.
It’s more nuanced and actually show character development and a realistic implementation. In most ending with a reformed society often seen in xianxia, the philosophical outlook doesn’t truly change. It’s still the same system with the strong imposing their view on the weak. End of discussion. It’s just that in those the strongest one is the Mc with some level of moral compass. Remove him and society will return to what it used to be. 
Here while it surely takes power in its various form to accomplish his goal, it’s a bottom up approach instead trying to instil some morals and ethics at the individual level and spreading it organically to bring reform to society. I find this approach way more fulfilling than the Mc bringing his fist down and saying: "now you’ll behave like good people".

The side character actually serve a purpose and each has their own journey instead of just making the Mc look good. 

It often involves planning, which is very rare to see in online novel with the people actually using their brain (most of them). 

Finally, I like the choice of including various POV as the purpose of the story isn’t the life of MC himself, but the impact he has on the world. It also helps to bring dynamic interactions in a living world instead of everything gravitating around the Mc sequentially.
Author should take note to not fall into the trap of always wanting to show various POV and ending up slowing down the pace too much. 

It is also very helpful to keep the suspension of disbelief because if I take for example the lack of countermove of the various families to his plan after the first council meeting, it would deeply frustrate me seeing it only from Liu Wei eye.
Even if the plot would eventually be exposed in the future for whatever reason and understanding the behavior retroactively, the enjoyment of reading in chapter by chapter wouldn’t make it a pleasant experience always questioning the stupidity of the supposed political old monsters. Having these glimpses help to provide the right context for certains choices that while we are not privy to the whole picture until the plot unfold, we know there is definetely something going on and it’s not just bad writing.

Very good start, fell off a bit but high potential

Reviewed at: Chapter 90: Announcement in the Sunset
by Voroni 3/17/2025, 5:58 PM

I was about to post this as a comment on chapter 90 but figured it would work as a review too.

Thanks for the chapters, just caught up. I like the story but have to admit I liked it more in the beginning. I am not sure why I feel like this but if I were to guess it's because of how the story is structured coupled with what the story has been focusing on. I found the tournament arc to be a lot less interesting than what happened before that, and I enjoy tournament arcs. I think this could be due to three things, or a combination of all three.

First off, because of all of the POV switching, the story is structured to revolve around the sect and not really about a small group of people. A tournament arc following a person or a group of people that we've gotten to know deeply for multiple chapters is fun and exciting, but this would not give a lot of time for other POV's without breaking the flow of the story. As mentioned this story focuses more on the sect as a whole compared to other stories in the same setting, as a side effect the reader won't have as much of an emotional investment to the characters participating in the tournament.

Secondly, if there was a main character it would be elder Wei. I like seeing the perspective of all the other characters and their growth, but elder Wei is the main driving force of change and is a fun character to follow. I like the POV switching but I think that it can drag out sometimes, it's a lot more fun if the actions of the side characters more closely tie together with the elder.

And lastly, it might just be the case of nothing happening a lot of the time. Like everything between the city arc and the tournament arc, I don't remember anything that happened in those chapters and I just read all of it this last week. But in my mind I feel like this was a considerable amount of chapters.

Some other critique I have is that I find the action sections to be less interesting than the politics. This might also be due to the above three points, but I enjoy reading about the elders yapping. And I think that sometimes there's a disconnect between the behavior and language of the elders compared to their position. For example, in a recent chapter an elder made a offensive remark to the sect patriarch and you wrote "What???" to represent the inner thoughts of the elders present. This is not language I would expect 300 year old chinese martial arts geezers to use, even when done in their head. After the written internal outburst you continued with "Shocked silence filled the room", just skipping the internal outburst would have made this scene a lot better. It doesn't happen all the time, but there is a lot of similar instances that happen here and there throughout the story. For example, I understand that elder Wei is trying to treat everyone equally, but that doesn't mean that he should speak the same way the younger members of the sect do. He's an old man and I think he should speak formally like one.

Closing thoughts, I like elder Wei, I like reading about elder Wei, I like reading about other characters experiences as a result of something elder Wei did, I like reading about what happens to the sect due to elder Wei's actions, and I like the reverse too. Like how elder Wei learnt from the mortals academy or got an epiphany due to his disciples accomplishments. I like the different POV's, I just think they need more direction and more "meat". There is potential for a good feedback loop here. I want more of that. 

I really wish you could post reviews without a score because I like the story and don't want to reduce the score. But I would be lying if I gave it a higher one, At the start of the story I would have given it something like 4.3, meaning it's very enjoyable and gripping but not a masterpiece. Where we currently are I rate it at something like 3.7, not gripping me but still enjoyable.
