What happens when a young master from a declining cultivator clan decides to… be a decent human being?

Zhao Ren was not a typical young master. No arrogance, no entourage, no random face‑slapping, at least not any more. He preferred to read books over throwing his weight around. Raised to turn around his family's fortunes, he struggles to live up to their lofty ideals. But man plans and the heavens laugh. Instead he found his own way to the Dao. 

A cultivator who likes a sustainable approach to growing herbs and leaves behind inheritances instead of looting everything not nailed down? Who even spares a thought for those who come after?

Who does that?

Why would you refine a precious pill and not swallow it immediately? Why would you craft spirit weapons with your hard earned, expensive ingredients without ever intending to use or sell them? Why even bother planting a tree that won’t bear fruit until long after you’re dead?

This guy must be crazy! So how does he keep getting stronger?!

What to expect:

-Mass release initially then 1 chapter a day M-W-F. 

- ~2k words per chapter (Slightly longer first chapter)

-Slightly more farming, crafting and slice-of-life than most xianxias

-MC doesn't discover his own path until Ch 10 so be patient

-Gradually accumulating power growth.

-No Harem

Fine Story to Pass the Time

Reviewed at: Chapter 24 – The Board is Set
by Headmaster Ham 3/25/2026, 2:25 AM

As others have said, I'm torn on my rating. There are numbers of things that could be better, but I still enjoy reading.

The writing is straightforward, as are the MC's actions and intentions. 

He quickly realizes that his dao is one of shared fortune through positive karmic connections, which is a nice change of pace. While it comes off as a little parasitic, he does seem to view himself as a good person trying to do good deeds.

However, the MC progresses seemingly very quickly, keeping up with the main "talents" despite not having the best "roots" and heavily diversifying his actions (despite consistently saying it's important to specialize). He quickly picks up (and excels at) a wide variety of challenging endeavors with little guidance and no training from before entering the sect. Honestly, he seems ill-suited to the life he takes on, even if it's pleasant to read about. As another review mentioned, the depiction of forging felt particularly lacking. I would have much rather he specialized in formations or learned a bit about each craft and worked with his friends to cover/assist with the other main aspects of cultivation.

The author also has a tendency to skip over details, like saying that a bunch of people sought out the MC, but only a few are actually named (or depicted at all), or the whole process of learning alchemy (which is said to be extremely difficult and expensive to learn).

There are events that are foreshadowed, but then never come to pass (see spoiler).

Spoiler

There's mention of a side character (the stereotypical talented young master) working hard with the intention of facing him in the first tournament, but it never happens.

 

And yet despite that, I find myself continuing to read. It's easy to digest without getting heavily invested, and the characters are likeable enough. I'd like to see events expanded on in future revisions, but it's still a nice way to pass the time.
