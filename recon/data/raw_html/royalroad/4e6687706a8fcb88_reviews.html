Raika used to be a Cultivator. She lived in a sect, fought in tournaments, and cultivated to spite the heavens, as a cultivator should. While she was never the strongest, fastest or wisest, she held it in her heart to never, ever give up, and to stand for her ideals.

And then an Imperial Cultivator destroyed her soul organs and left her crippled.


In a world with a sun made of flaming serpents, reality-defying entities running amok, and a timeless, all-knowing Emperor ruling all through the power of his Blades and the constant advances and experiments of his Divisions, Raika is less than worthless, unable to see and feel the blood of reality- but when it's surrender or madness, she chooses madness. Embarking on a new path, Raika will find strange secrets, hidden paths to transformation, and abandon humanity, sanity, and the rule of the Empire to become something far stranger and far more powerful than she ever thought possible.


In a world replete with enslaved Daemons used as WMDs, reality-bending powers, eldritch-punk technology, and strange pathways of cultivation and magic, she'll have to find allies, confront gods and monster, and defy the Heavens, her own body, and common sense to find a way to become something new. Facing a millenia-old Empire rife with corruption and unknown plans, impossible horrors from the edges of the world, Daemons, Witches, and her own broken soul, she won't stop until she's become truly free- but what will it cost, and can her insanity match the madness of the reality itself?


What to expect:
Slowburn power progression
Expansive worldbuilding and complex story
Weak to Extremely strong protagonist with a unique powerset
Complex villains and characters
A raging bisexual of a protagonist
Mutants, madness and mayhem, oh my!

This story is designed to add lovecraftian cosmic horror and weirdness to a Xianxia setting. If you like some biohorror, self-modification, well written violence and geopolitics under the eyes of ancient, alien beings, this is the one for you, darlings!

______________________________________ OLD REVIEW:

TLDR: It's exactly what it says in the blurb. Very good. Give it a go, but skip the prologue (chapter 1).

(You only need to know that the world, the monsters and the empire are all very lovecraftian. And there were outer gods who left ages ago.)

____________

Overall: Somewhat eldritch xianxia, if a bit less lovecraftian than I expected going in. From the prologue, I'd thought she would start her progress by stealing a spleen from a spawn of Chtulu and shoving it in place of her dantian. 

Instead, her cultivation is much closer to the proper one. Basically the same thing, but coming at it from a different angle.

I expect that to change radically (and get more tentacly) the higher she goes, though

____________

Style: Usually I don't care about this, but this time it's my biggest gripe. There are a lot of lovecraftian things happening and the author tries to convey how eerie and uncomprehensible they are, with good old metaphors and ramblings.

It's usually cool, but they (the author) often get carried away and it becomes purple prose. 

For example, chapter 56 is all about a breakthrough and I somehow figured out where it was going in the first few sentences. Unfortunately, that made it a slog to read through all the ramblings just to get to the obvious conclusion.

Basically, sometimes the mystical mumbo jumbo is too overdone and you realize that you're just reading cool-sounding nonsense.

It's the reason I advise you to skip the prologue.

____________

Grammar: Good, nothing more to say

____________

Story: As usual with most xianxia, there isn't a much of story. It's mostly our protagonist stumbling around and trying to scrap by and get stronger in a world that hates her.

There is a quest for vengeance but it's so clearly out of her grasp for now, that it doesn't really qualify as plot.

Still enjoyable.

One thing I'm annoyed about is the empire. Having what is apparently the whole of humanity under one man and one banner seems awfully restrictive, even if the world is millions of square kilometers

It just sounds... small.

____________

Character: This suffers from xianxia solitude: there are many antagonists right in your face, but no allies whatsoever.

And if there are, then they will take a very different course, and we won't see them for hundreds of chapters, except the occasional POV switch.

One thing I didn't like is chapter... Can't say the number to avoid spoilers. Let's just say that the author preferred more conflict and angst to some positive relationships

Personally, I think that this story had conflict and hardships in spades already. What it needs are friends.

And after that chapter, that just won't happen for a LONG time

Woah just woah

Reviewed at: Chapter 75 - My Beloved Monster And Me
by Hidden famous RR author 4/1/2024, 6:49 PM

Drawn by the allure of eldritch cultivation, I was immediately captured. Right from chapter 1 to where I have read the author doesn't just deliver but Serves. 

Style:
Beautiful and poetic. I didn't know I got so used to the average writing on RR until I read this. The author has something which I'd like to call special here. Where others try to draw you in with horrific scenes blood and gore, Silv3ertonge tries something else that fits almost perfectly. Take this bit from chapter one for example:

"The scars and glories of conquests and tragedies may be remembered for eons, twisting and changing as they crawl through time on the backs of those most affected."

It is almost magical to me. The style isn't for anyone since it doesn't allow for easy reading (not that it is a bad thing). I will also admit that the author sometimes takes it a single step too far, bordering close to the confusing and somewhat unreadable, hence the 4.5 rating. 

Grammar:
There are a few mistakes here and there but they seem to get fixed when a reader gives a suggestion. Editing is hard. As long as anyone can understand what is being said, I don't have anything against it. 

Story:
Once again wow. It is set in a mostly normal xiaxia world (I've only read up to chapter 70 as of writing this), but the character makes it so interesting. Reika doesn't follow the basic cultivation system and normally that always results in something edgy or an MC that has a lot of undeserved power. Yet, here it is the opposite. While outside of the standard cultivation system most people use, Silv3ertongue manages to fit into the world particularly well since they set up the stage in prior chapters. 

Character:
Reika is an interesting character. Through the chapters, we see her change, learn, suffer for her mistakes and grow from them. Some people hate it when an MC makes a kind of dumb decision but I am all for it. It makes her human, which is kind of funny if you consider that this is eldritch cultivation.

After reading chapter 2, I was a bit scared that this was yet another MC with endless willpower, able to barge through anything if they persisted just long enough. And damn, author, you proved me wrong. 

The other characters outside of the MC are also truly alive. They are interesting on their own and I can't get enough of them. 

In conclusion 
I don't have much bad to say, only that Silv3ertongue sometimes gets a bit too poetic. I will also admit that this is one of those stories that is not for everyone. It does exceptionally at what it promises. Yet it has a target audience. My personal advice is that the author does not try to shift that audience. What you have works, don't change it if anyone tells you to. You are writing the story, not us. 

Kudos!

Pacing and Homogeneity

Reviewed at: Chapter 13 - Don’t Threaten Me With a Good Time
by GreatWyrmGold 5/23/2025, 8:34 PM

I want to start by saying a couple nice things. The prose is good, the protagonist is strongly characterized, and the first chapters get you invested in her struggles.

The problem is that for at least the next dozen chapters, her struggles are kinda monotonous. Endure the hardships of being a disabled beggar, do weird things to maybe start cultivating again, do weird cultivation while enduring those hardships. It all blurs together.

There's not even much variation in the kinds of hardship she endures; hunger and illness and cold and so forth all feel the same. She gets into a fight once, but then it's over and we're back to the same old rhythm, just with a student.

I want to see how her story goes, but not enough to push through this monotony. Especially since I have the sneaking suspicion that when she finally improves her circumstances, she'll keep hitting the same emotional notes of a slightly different kind of struggle for another dozen chapters.

The indomitable human spirit

Reviewed at: Chapter 56 - It's Alive! Aliiiiiive, I Tell You!
by Anta 1/27/2024, 1:18 AM

Great xianxia with an original setting. Its main draws are battle and progression, but also dao mysticism in eldritch style. You can really feel the influences of kill six billion demons, so if you like that just start reading, otherwise keep reading reviews.

It does not have an idealist view of people, but it is far from the bestial worldview of classic xianxia. This is why the dystopia tag fits even though the world is culturally close to classic xianxia.

Style: The prose feels eloquent, flowing through complex sentences. A strict english teacher might subtract a bit for run-on sentences, but I like it. My only small gripe is that the limited 3rd person viewpoint sometimes uses 1st person pronouns (I, our, etc) in idioms and common phrases. This is not grammatically incorrect as the storyteller always refers to itself, not the character it's following, it's just a little jarring for me.

Story: The story has been delightfully circuitous until now. I won't spoil anything but the twists of fate are entertaining.

Grammar: The grammar is great, there are barely any mistakes. There were some early mistakes with the pronouns of a specific character, but the author seems to have gotten a handle on it.

Character: The characters are interesting and seem rather '3D' so far, especially Raika and a later quasi-antagonist.

All in all I'd definitely recommend this story.
