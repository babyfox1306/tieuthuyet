Liu Feng wanted to be a cultivator who defied the heavens, surpassed all limits, and had jade-skinned beauties clamoring around him. He wanted to fight anyone who got in his way, and shatter the arrogance of those young masters!

Unfortunately for him, he died, and now I'm stuck here.

Jade-like beauties? No thanks, I have an awesome magical energy to study.

Young masters? Life and death battles? No, I would rather read some books on cultivation, and master some techniques that clearly don't obey the laws of physics.

There is a junior who has trash talent but suddenly started advancing by leaps and bounds? And he seems to be favored by the heavens themselves? Yeah, that has nothing to do with me.

Great read

Reviewed at: Chapter 155 - A Family
by Eduardo Gallindo 5/8/2025, 12:24 AM

This is a fantastic read for Xianxia fans tired of repetitive tropes.

Instead of following yet another power-hungry cultivator, the protagonist enters the world of cultivation as an outsider, more of a curious scholar than a typical powerhungry murderhobo MC.

Their fascination with martial techniques and cultivation lore feels fresh, like a nerd geeking out over magic systems while trying to avoid deadly schemes, ancient monsters, and jade beauties with ulterior motives.

The MC has no fateful encounters, no heaven luck, no heavenly talents, no ring grandpa, he just gets by with his wits and the knowledge of his previous reads, both from his previous life and from the current one.

Just another xinxia novel, nothing new.

Reviewed at: Chapter 90 - Plans & Schemes
by DangerMittens 6/25/2024, 10:23 PM

Good parts:

  • The main character (MC) tries to understand how the world works, which is uncommon in other xinxia novels.
  • The author put effort into making the world interesting, and it shows.
  • The fights are great and fun, better than in most xinxia novels.

Bad parts:

  • Apart from the MC and a few characters, every other cultivator is depicted as stupid, trash, or incompetent. It feels like the MC is the only character capable of making smart decisions.
  • The MC is good at everything, and nearly every character who interacts with him has a favorable opinion. His family is well off, he has no rivals, and all problems are solved by people close to him. Of the four potential rivals, two lose their cultivation, one becomes a friend, and the other is too scared to be near the MC. There is no real stake in the story.
  • Every other chapter has the MC making a xinxia reference. Occasional references are fine, but too many break immersion. The author has not provided an alternative to classic xinxia tropes.
  • There is zero character building. The characters in the series don't seem real.

Summary:

This feels like just another xinxia novel where the MC is aware that it's just another xinxia. To make it worse, the author removed the good parts of xinxia while retaining the bad stuff.

A solid tropey anti-trope story with a good start!

Reviewed at: Chapter 44 - Killer Instincts
by Consequence6 3/18/2024, 3:30 PM

Style: Good. Nothing too fancy! It could use a few more descriptions of the locations and characters, but beyond that, I'm enjoying the writing style. I enjoy the occasional cut to another POV to see how characters are reacting to the MC, it adds an interesting, almost slice-of-life element to it.

Grammar: A misplaced comma or two, and one or two weird word choices. Other than that, my only real complaint is the description of the translucent, mucus-like white goo he shot globs of onto his teammates (it's a shield).

Story: Good! I'm enjoying "MC is aware of and trying hard to avoid typical xianxia tropes but keeps getting caught up in it." It seems like it's going to be a fairly slow burn, as he's still the same overall stage that he started at, but there have been hints that he can punch decently over his power-rating because he's actually training, rather than just being a drug addict. Speeding it up a bit would be convenient, or if he learned anything new from his studies beyond a few techniques. It seems like he's spending a lot of time reading, but has only really come up with questions, not answers.

Character: The main character is good. He's a nice, kind guy, which is always refreshing in a cultivation story. He's a bit bland, though this is by design. The side-characters are all fairly over-the-top xianxia character tropes, which is also by design. I'm not a fan of Shan Sha, as their aggressively insulting banter seemed to come out of nowhere and seems a bit out of character. Other than that, a solid and varied cast.

Well done, and I'm excited to keep reading!

Your standard cliche cultivation (in a bad way)

Reviewed at: Chapter 234 - That Girl is So Dangerous~
by Frimlin 10/26/2025, 1:09 PM

The story starts well, with the idea of a mc who's not really into all this 'training to become the apex'. Instead, he likes to read and learn, and it's where it go downhill pretty fast.

First, he, somehow, even dedicated a lot of his time to theoretical learning and broad education managed to cultivate and train really fast, faster than anyone else besides top prodigies. Like everyone else in the world did it wrong before he arrived.

His personality quickly changed from reasonable modern person to your average murderhobo mc mentality. So, 'might makes right', 'I should cultivate to the top to feel safe', 'I should murder/cripple everyone who crossed me so they don't retaliate', you get the idea.

Writing started to annoy me at some point, mc overly paranoid and overthink everything, to the point that you will start read pages of his worries and mind games inside his head without anything actually happened.

Honestly, if you are into more grim/edgy versions of xianxia, with sect's of running psychopaths who exterminate eachother's 9 generations, crippling people for the smallest grudge then you will probably like this dog-eat-dog world, it was interesting to see how some side characters, who will be a proper mc's in they own story clashed and interacted. But protagonist made this unbearable.

A Welcome Change

Reviewed at: Chapter 110 - Brutal Battles
by GenPowerEveryNight 6/20/2024, 10:58 PM

This novel has a very funny and realistic approach to the cultivation genre. The MC is very rational and acts with common sense in comparison to most, if not all the rest of the characters he ends up meeting and talking too. Simple things like always being truthful, trying to find the truth to a situation before he acts, and always having a backup plan for any type of situation he might encounter.

His strength isn't overwhelming, and his cultivation is above average. The real charm of this story is when he interacts with the other classic cultivation-archetype characters, whether it be the chubby friend whos name he didn't know for almost 30 chapters, or the berserker act-first think later type person who takes him under her wing simply because he doesn't lie, and seems pretty useful.

The story is well written and well paced. I found the plot to be funny and easily followable for all of the chapters. The cultivation isn't a main part of the story, as the MC naturally grows the more he trains and practices on his techniques. Nothing is super overpowered, but he steadily progresses throughout the novel. Again, my favorite parts were definitely the funnier moments of when he says weird things that are really just common sense, but not normally found in the genre.

Definitely read if you want a good story + cultivation + funny moments. I found the novel a very entertaining read and binged it in about three hours.

A nice fresh story to binge

Reviewed at: Chapter 143 - The Third Wheel
by Chrones_ 8/6/2024, 3:23 PM

The story so far has a solid fresh take on the xianxia genre. It's not special or genre defining, but it did get me to read the entire 1k+ pages that are written right now in two days.

The positives - The prose/grammar is solid, the "smart" characters aren't dumb, the banter is enjoyable and characters goals stay consistent. Action scenes are written well enough.

Everything else like the cultivation, the skill system and worldbuilding is simple and doesn't detract from the story - much better than having 3 chapters of infodumps in my personal opinion.

The negatives -

1.Conflicts don't drag on, but their resolutions aren't always satisfying, it feels like the MC gets bailed out by friends/connections constantly, rather than given challenges he could resolve with his own skill/smarts, which can be pretty unsatisfying. Especially when his first fight in my opinion was the best one.

2. MCs "lack" of cultivation talent mini rant with lots of spoilers ahead

Spoiler

We're told that someone with C grade talent can reach outer elder level(foundation establishment) in a decade or two if they just cultivate diligently, WITH NO OUTSIDE HELP?? Which is weird considering we've seen that even QI gathering cultivators are bigshots in the outside towns/cities. But if a C grade talent is still just an outer disciple there should be a myriad of foundation establishment elders by now? Where are they?

My problem with this is either the MC is cultivating much much faster than he should with C grade talent and no external help for no explained reason, other than qi-sense(This is what's happening) OR everyone else with C grade talent in the outer sect is a lazy bum who can't even cultivate as a full time job for 20 years(Since C grade roots get tired they don't even need to cultivate 24/7)?!

There's a third option where everyone is just stuck at 9 stars of their current stage and can't break through, which is where the story might go once the characters get older, which is why I don't detract my rating.

 

Conclusion, If you like less ridiculous progression fantasy with some fresh takes and fresh characters and you're looking to kill a few days reading - this is the book.
