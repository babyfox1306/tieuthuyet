Money and Qi are the keys to power in the Republic of Ardus. Ren has neither.

He had a good life—until debt collectors tore him from his loved ones and threw him out onto the streets.

Life in the dark alleys of Katarn is merciless to the weak, and Ren craves the power to change his fate—like the immortal cultivators of legend and the martial heroes who parade through the streets to fanfare.

Yet he has no teacher, nor master—fated to rot away like the pile of refuse he calls home. Until an encounter with a dying immortal gives him a chance to change everything.

But life is rarely kind, and before he can step onto the path of immortality his spirit is tainted by the venom of a Mythical Beast.

Will the song in his soul and a lost cultivation technique be enough to survive this cruel world?

[Part 1 of the Broken Path series]

What to Expect:

-A Cultivation Tale Written for Fans of Epic Fantasy and Berserk (Golden Age)-

-Lovingly Crafted & Efficient Prose-

-An Intelligent Yet Flawed Main Character-

-An Animal Companion-

-An Unfolding Story That Will Eventually Span the Globe-

-Weak to Strong Progression Fantasy- Grounded in emotion and personal motivation. Progression is a consistent theme, and this story will take the time to ensure it feels meaningful in a broader sense. While Ren will receive a few advantages at several points, the goal is progression that feels earned.

-Gritty, Well Thought Out Combat- Trying to fight against a spear with your sword? Better find a way to compensate for the gap in reach. Take an arrow to the face? You're taking a dirt nap. Fireball? I think you can see where I'm going. Complete with tactical fighting, archery, martial arts, monster battles, and xianxia-inspired magic system.

-A Non-Typical Xianxia Setting- An original world that takes inspiration from the blending of cultures facilitated by the Silk Road at its height. The world is in a constant state of flux and over the course of the story we will see it transform as power changes hands and new technologies shift the balance of civilization. Heavily flavored with middle-eastern influence and dollops of classic fantasy, this is not another clone of ancient china, though eventually we will see some of the classic tropes such as young masters, honor duels, and cultivator sects. Some of these will be deconstructed, some will be played with because they are fun and/or awesome. Don't worry, this is not a harem story.

-Adult Themes and Honest Exploration of Character and the Human Experience- This is a story that will explore the light and the dark. Beauty and horror. While I aim to avoid being needlessly gratuitous, this story does not shy away from the horrors of war and painful psychological challenges that come with a life filled with tribulation. It will also linger in moments of connection, beauty, and peace. This is not Grimdark, though it can be dark at times.

Author’s Note:

I drew inspiration for the Broken Path series from countless sources, but the most obvious are: Berserk, The Name of the Wind, Cradle, Dragon Heart, Dawn of Awakening, Beware of Chicken, (1,001)Arabian Nights, a broad smattering of Myths and FolkTales, and my own time spent studying and practicing martial arts and mystical traditions from across the globe (looking at you Hafiz).

I love Cultivation/Xianxia stories, especially when they manage to stay grounded in the plights of mortals and strong character development. This is my own personal spin set in a world of my own design inspired by the concept and mythos of the silk road as a thread that has drawn together stories from many places. I hope you enjoy reading about Ren’s journey as much as I enjoy writing it.

A sadist fantasy?

Reviewed at: Chapter 38: The Way Forward
by noob_sword 7/28/2023, 5:07 AM

While the story is very well written, I think only a sadist could enjoy the MC constantly being pummelled into the ground. I don't understand his insane drive to keep up with all the torture that the author keeps throwing at him. To save his family from indentured servitude? His family lives far better than him. To keep providing medical treatment for his father? Why doesn't his father leave to save the rest of his family? For his dream to be a hero? What in this world the author has painted deserves to be saved?

It's a torture to read the MC constantly being beaten down while the author keeps piling on pressure to save his family and to continue to defend against the shadow inheritance when it would be so much easier to just give up.

I would be apalled if this becomes a bleeding heart hero story where the MC continues to hold on to the good in his heart while eating abuse from the entire world and is then required to save his country or some other nonsense. A civilization that survives only because of the extraordinary sacrifice of a few individuals deserves to burn.

From the latest chapters, it is looking like the story will go in the direction of making the MC a bleeding heart hero.

A Magical Xianxia Experience

Reviewed at: Chapter: 60 Blood
by ButterBoy9000 10/24/2023, 8:40 AM

Sometimes I read things, and I'm not even sure why I like them. This isn't sarcasm or being snide on my part, but I find myself writing this review on the tail end of devouring the first 25 chapters of this story, and I can't pin down the reason I like it so much.

Perhaps it was the writing / prose, which simply fell away from the page, leaving me engrossed. The writing here is blatantly professional quality, without any typos, awkwardness, wasted space, or meandering. The character dialogue is crisp, and clean. I couldn't tell you if the prose leaned more poetic or direct, because I was too busy inhaling chapters to notice - I personally consider this a very good sign.

The characters help matters, all feeling three dimensional with their own naratives and motivations. We technically even have different POVs, but I don't find myself caring, as they're all well done and not overly drawn out. The characters we have interacted with all feel real, and raw, adding colour to the story. Our main character has demonstrated through his actions thoroughly who he is over the chapters, and clearly is his own person, and not a stand in for wish fulfilment or other things.

The tone and style of the book is perhaps what lends itself to some of its charm. We have clear elements of Xianxia, and the worldbuliding remains intriguing and well crafted so far, though perhaps on the lite side still. We're very much still peeling the onion. Tonally, the story is what I'd classify as leaning gritty, but optimistic, many harsh realities are presented, but thus far I would definitely classify the story as an optimistic and hopefull one.

I can't give 5 stars for "story", as we're just not that far into it yet. Perhaps I'll update later once we have more chapters and something more substantive.

Overall, the story so far has been raw, emotional, and outright magical for me so far, and no, I don't have a clue why. Maybe I'm just in a mood.

Regardless, I can't recommend the story enough for people who are fans of Xianxia, or just good fantasy in general.

Update as of finishing book 1:

Much of the above still holds true, the prose is emotional and the writing remains tight. Do not come into this story expecting power fantasy.

Instead you get a story of violence, music, soul, self sabotage, and humanity.

I have issues with some of the characters, in the sense that many of them have a tendency to lean into an archetype or a role, instead of being fully fleshed out, with their own motivations and goals. This isn't taken to a drastic degree of course, but it's there enough to be somewhat irritating. For example (major book 1 spoliers here): 

Spoiler

Ren makes some decisions that lead to his squadron basically getting slaughtered. Now admittedly, you could argue that this was inevitable, however in the context its generally pretty clear that it's his fault. When he tells this to the commander, the commander, instead of disciplining him, or displaying signs of anger, accepts it with relatively calm resignation, and only minorly chastises him.

At no point does the commander deviate from the understanding, competent leader / father figure archetype. Even when he's been captured by the enemy and tortured, he never displays any anger towards Ren, because he seemingly has sympathy for him.

There are other examples of this, which I won't get into.

Ren's character growth is hard to watch as well, as you basically have to watch him get emotionally bludgeoned until he sort of gets the message. That said, the pay off is pretty satisfying.

I love it!!

Reviewed at: Chapter 49: Lieutenant Kareem
by erishya puru 9/16/2023, 9:29 AM

The first webnovels I read were all translated cultivation pieces. I loved them then, but have since grown tired of the genre for various reasons. This story feels like an answer to those reasons. The plot is superb, the characters are all so life like, I can relate and connect with them even though I don’t share many experiences with them. Anyway let’s look at the elements that make the story.

style: I give this a full 5 for style. The author makes expert use of poetic prose to bring the reader into the world and even though it’s written in third person the author knows when and how to zoom in so that you get a first person like immersive experience in the moments that really matter.

story/plot: This is one adventure you won’t soon forget. There is love and loss, a grinding struggle to get stronger and improve, not just in power, but also a person. Ren has many challenges to overcome both internal and external. I think what really makes the plot for me though is that every single moment, even the little ones, the slow ones, and the painful ones, all of them feel relevant and like a necessary step for Ren’s journey. At the same time the story doesn’t feel formulaic or like it follows a set track that is overly predictable. Bonus stars are warranted here.

Grammer: As with any story, there are minor typos here and there. Many have been caught and corrected, but some still exist they will not impact your experience.

Character: this is where Broken Path truly shines. Ren has become one of my favorite MC’s and I love that all the other characters are so well developed as well, even the minor characters feel more alive and real than I was expecting.

overall this is a real gem!
