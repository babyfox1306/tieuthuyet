She will find a way to win, no matter how many deaths it takes. 

For ten years, Huan Yue has lived a quiet life, serving tea and hiding from the world of god-like cultivators who shattered her family for a petty slight. All she wants is to remain invisible. But on the morning she turns twenty, a mysterious cultivator hunts her down and murders her without mercy.

And then she awakens in her bed, at the start of the very same day, trapped in a waking nightmare.

Hunted by a team of relentless and seemingly invincible assassins, Yue is caught in a brutal time loop. But in this curse, Yue discovers a weapon.

If she cannot escape as a mortal, she will become something more. With each death, the terror fades, replaced by a burning rage. Forced to walk the path of the very people she despises, she will use the endless cycle to forge her spirit and sharpen her skills. Her hunters believe she is an insignificant pest to be exterminated. They don't realize they've given their victim the one thing she needs to claim her revenge: time.

This one…. This one made me say it out loud: AMAZING! I am not a good critic, I can only say I was captured and am hanging on. Waiting patiently, but eagerly. 

This is such a novel take for me, and… with substance! You know a Xianxia with substance ? And with time loop on top, I’ve never read this combination. Mainly because I got tired of the same plots in Xianxia and have not read them in more then 2 years. But this one !! This one knows how to make it fleshed out, captivating and engaging ! 

PS. For the author

Putting any kind of art for a public to see is nerve wrecking. It’s like getting nude and walking on the street, knowing every person will look at you. 
The problem is… since putting art online the interactors, aka the anonymous readers, have a chance to judge and be able to attack you with it. 

Do know that the ones liking or even loving the novel do not interact online.  20-30% do, and those are the voices that really want to be heard somewhere in their lives. 

 In my case, since I don’t have people to share my love of these novels with, I post comments sometimes. Or when inconsistencies take me out of the flow of reading and then I write my thoughts about it. 

Another example, there are a kind of people that puts their own pain and suffering out. And hope to stir any kind of feelings from others. And the artist in this case is so in the naked, like in you could cut your body open and the hit goes directly on what’s underneath. So it hurts.

Know that the brain is made to focus on the bad. You can’t just ignore it, no matter how many people tell you to.

So it’s nice you could take the anxiety out of the equation giving yourself time. But I am actually scared of this turning to be now a big mountain on your way. 

It does help to know you have fans, people that actually appreciate the author behind the story. Just know you got one in me. 

I believe in you

Very enjoyable story

Reviewed at: 19: Body Refinement (8)
by Galefury 5/5/2026, 10:49 PM

I read a lot of timeloop stories, and it's always interesting to see what different authors create with the timeloop trope. In this story I like the sense of urgency from the start, and the idea that even if death isn't permanent, living still matters to the MC. She's not some kind of detached mastermind who plans everything out, but she does have goals and things that are genuinely important to her, and she's willing to die for these things repeatedly. She usually doesn't get what she wants, but she keeps struggling to live and improve her situation and (hopefully eventually) the situation of people around her. And she keeps struggling to spite her enemies. Most other characters aren't really fleshed out, but that's fine IMO.

There's not really a whole lot of "story" so far, it's mostly  personal survival and progression (of character and power). We slowly learn about the MC and the world together with her, by watching her struggle.

It's currently on hiatus, but I'm really hoping the author will pick it up again! I'm stoked to see how this story will go on.

Fantastic!

Reviewed at: 19: Body Refinement (8)
by AlcatrazIsland5 2/24/2026, 10:58 AM

This is one of the most beautiful stories I've read on here or anywhere. The flow, the characterization, the background, and world development are top notch examples of how amazing the xianxia genre can be. And I haven't seen a story written this well in some time. The author is an artist with their grammer and prose and stand toe to toe with many of the big xianxia stories.

It's not overblown slop or underdeveloped nonsense. This story has real substance and character. The mc is sympathetic and very well written, the side characters are qell developed and bring a nice flavor and feel like real humans and not some dumb caricatures. The villains of the story are written like the arrogant cultivators that they are! There's no mustache twirling nonsense, just pure narcissistic belief in their own power. Just like a good xianxia villain should be written. The time-loop is fantastically written and doesn't degrade the tension in the least. Yue tries her best in every loop. Even knowing that her actions don't truly matter as long as the loop exists they still matter to her. She holds onto every memory and scrap of insight and personal development. She makes every moment matter no matter how impermanent they may be.

A truly beautiful story and one that I can't wait to continue reading and experiencing.

Please use less soaring affect

Reviewed at: 6: Crimson Moon (6)
by AdvisorVisor 2/26/2026, 6:17 AM

It was a day of boredom, and of general disdain. But maybe, just maybe, as I clicked on this story, everything was about to change. 

The tight, focused loop situation, where the protagonist had no option, but to struggle for life itself in a net built for her, was a gripping situation that made me know I was in for a good time.

But I started to notice, a wrong note in the symphony, again, again, again, and a growing sense that I didn't quite enjoy what I was reading. 

The hook lines, the endless anticipation moments. Scene conclusions galore. The merciless assault of existential connections. I have no perfect name for what exactly I describe, and ChatGPT can't find one for me, so all I can do is give you examples. Here's 4 in direct proximity in *one chapter*. 

She had made a pact with herself. It was a small thing, perhaps. Petty, even. But pettiness was all she had left, and she guarded it fiercely.

She would leave them with something, some small mark of her defiance, even if that mark was merely tea stains on pristine robes or the echo of her laughter in their ears.

At least then, if death finally claimed her for good, she could face the darkness knowing she'd been her to the very end.

And when the day's lessons were complete, find one of the four cultivators and make their life just a little bit worse before they ended hers.

It was not a sophisticated philosophy. But it was hers.

Here's some others.

A single grain of rice in an endless paddy. And somehow, impossibly, that feeling of smallness was not crushing but liberating. She was too small to matter. Too insignificant to hunt. Out here, she could simply... disappear.

But for this moment, in this meadow, with the sun warm on her face and the wind in her hair and the whole world spread out before her like a gift… For this moment, she didn’t have to worry about them.

She would have known these hills. Would have walked these meadows a thousand times, until every stone and stream and stand of trees was as familiar as her own hands.

But the anger was a distant thing, muted by the overwhelming presence of everything around her. She would have time to be angry later. Right now, she just wanted to look.

Her heart was hammering against her ribs, but she forced herself to look. To observe. That was why she was here.

Not every moment needs a grand interpretation. Not every bite of my bagel is a revelation that will be spoken of for generations. Just letting the story and the flow of events breathe on their own is something enough. Maybe even let me put some meaning into the journey, as I accompany you, in this eternal, yet far too short moment.

refreshing and fun

Reviewed at: 19: Body Refinement (8)
by Pliny the medium 6/2/2026, 2:12 PM

I really enjoyed this story, and I hope to see more chapters soon.

The story is well-written and I have not noticed grammatical errors. It is easy to read and well suited to the story. I like the author's style.

The plot is engaging and delivers on the promise of a time loop / xianxia fusion. The main character does not retain her advancement through the reset of the loops, which is not what I had expected, but which is poses an interesting challenge to read. I look forward to seeing how this develops in future chapters.

I like the main character, and I feel we get a lot of characterisation through the chapters. It feels plausible. Her backstory is delivered relatively quickly, and informs a lot of her actions. It is somewhat gruesome, but I don't think it is overdone.

In the first chapters, I was worried we would stay stuck to the same village/cast and villains. While the original quartet of enemies remains the main character's main obstacle, we quickly get to see some of the larger world and what is happening there-in. I particularly like another cultivator who has been introduced in the last few chapters.

I can't wait to read more.
