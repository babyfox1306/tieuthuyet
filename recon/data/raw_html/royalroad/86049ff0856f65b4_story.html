Elder Hu Xin, the Crimson Saint of the Pathless Night Sect, is missing. Not physically, his body is still there, walking about the sect like he owns the place. But behind his eyes, a stranger is panicking, because his transmigration experience came with no instructions, or any of Elder Hu's centuries of memories. He doesn't know how to work his own cultivation, let alone nurture someone else's.

They say the best way to learn something is to teach it. Unfortunately, that's only true if the penalty for failing the test isn't death. Fellow elders, disciples, dogs of the orthodoxy, everyone has expectations about who Hu Xin is. And Hu Xin doesn't have any idea of what those expectations are, let alone how to satisfy them. And so, shielded by audacity and armed with bullshit, this newly venerable and grossly unqualified demon is making this shit up as he goes.








This story is a multi-perspective Xiania story written by a westerner, with somewhat flatter/more abstract power scaling than a lot of the genre. Expect more intrigue and less violence, cultivation realms being less of absolute barriers than in many Xianxia, and fighting to be less about bigger numbers and more about people leveraging the unique advantages specific to their cultivation.

Currently updates once a week on Sunday evenings.

Yeah it's pretty good

Reviewed at: Chapter 18 - Xianyang
by Broken Blade 11/23/2023, 1:18 AM

Style: pretty hard to judge, but there hasn't been any sudden shifts or breaks in the story. It all flows pretty naturally 

Story: pretty good, the only issue (probably a temporary one) is that there aren't any extreme stakes or real goals. Just a small time limit or perhaps an upcoming tournament might make things a bit more dire (just a suggestion) but otherwise it sets out to do as intended: a person practicing the Dao of bullshit 

Grammar: nothing noticeable enough to ruin my enjoyment

Characters: currently the strongest aspect of the story. Our MC is well characterized as being intelligent but cautious and many of his mistakes in understanding is logically led to so we know why he thinks a certain way. My only concern is that he is often very calm even under stressful situations, which isn't bad but makes him seem weirdly competent even when he should be struggling. As for the side characters, weirdly enough they're all distinct except for the MC's disciple who's just....sort of there? She just feels a bit flat: generic kid wanting revenge. It's not too surprising since we mostly see from the MC's perspective and he hardly interacts with her, but giving her a few more things other than revenge might be good

Well written Xianxia

Reviewed at: Chapter 10 - Everything In This World
by Zonzog 11/8/2023, 6:37 AM

It is a very nicely written Xianxia. Has very decent depth of characters. This reminds me very much about "Forge of Destiny", but with different point of view. There are no system crutches, and while it is still very early, we already have a well shaped world-building that feels believable and organic.

Just like in Forge of Destiny, we are learning about cultivation the same way as main character and his disciple do it. In a way it is a novel with two main characters - one is the transmigrated elder, who flies by the seat of his pants, and his disciple, who struggles to succeed in a sect, despite not being the most talented or having any cheats aside form actually getting the elder to teach her.

Side characters have well shaped personalities and are memorable enough to not be a faceless crowd, but actual characters with their own lives and agendas.

All things considered, we have another top tier xianxia on our hands.

It gets better

by Blorgh the muppet 12/4/2023, 2:02 PM

It gets better later on,

which is not to say its bad early on. these early chapter stand head and shoulders over most other stories but joy oh joy it gets even better as the story proceeds, the author hitting his stride if you will.

as for story points: i feel by now were all familiar with these concepts of reincarnation and starting off as an old monster in unfamiliar waters, this story seperates itself from common dreg through not paying lip service. Hes in a demonic sect, weakness is not tolerated. period. he cant fuck up his act. 

There is no new disciple that quickly gets accepted, no obvious mistakes that can be brushed away as a cultivators quirk. He starts as an outsider in his sect, this doesnt give him the luxury of acting out, infact it puts more attention on him as a unique case. And our fresh protag needs to navigate still waters knowing fully well these waters run deep.(mind you our protag does both, but it has consequences and they come quickly)

also i keep making water references cause the story flows well. and cultivation here seems a much more spiritually and "self reflectory" than most other novels of this genre. Cultivation is a fascination, a mystery not a system to game.

the protag is likeable and sharp and the story is already setting itself up for success. it seems to me its already setting up the general gist of the story 50 chapters in advance and even this early you can slowly see it take it shape. not because the author has a plot in mind but because all outside characters and the protag himself make stupid but so so humane choices. There is never a charicature of life but just flawed people making flawed choices with flawed information. honestly i keep thinking im done with the review but then i come back to edit cause something else needs to be mentioned. so to summarize, this seems to be gold, and hard to summarize. gotta read it for yourself to form opinions about. 

i like it a lot, slow updates tho understandable if you wanna wait till there are more chapters but i recommend keeping it as a bookmark and checking it out whenever youre comfortable.
