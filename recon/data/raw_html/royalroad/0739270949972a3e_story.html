One upon a time, Shen Bu destroyed everything his father had ever worked for. He burned Shen Yu's new home to ashes, consumed his mother's cultivation, and slew the unborn daughter within her.

In another time, Shen Yu's daughter was born fourteen years before that event... but Shen Bu's nature did not change.

For three hundred years Shen Yu has been searching for something, anything, to save his daughter's life. Crippled by her Elder Brother and stuck in a hidden realm, where time is slowed down to preserve her life.

No progress has been made. For three hundred years, Shen Yu has failed to find an answer. One day, feeling the weight of his quest, he returns to the city of his birth to try and drown his sorrows.... and meets a boy that finally gives him hope.

=================================

Waking up in the body of an eight year old kid dying of a plague in a Xianxia setting was not my idea of a good time. Where was my cheat?! Where was my system, damn it?

My only lucky break was meeting that middle aged old man, whose teaching me how to cultivate. I'm not getting any pills, or some super powerful style or anything, so hes probably nobody important.

He does like talking about medical stuff, though. I'm no doctor, but giving him ideas on stuff like organ cloning is probably fine.

Amazing, in [some] ways better than og.

Reviewed at: Chapter 11: Heracles
by Fallen Saint 2/14/2026, 7:23 AM

Overall: Great story, I wish there was more, but so far, it does the idea justice. It is faster pace than the og, but it has its own vibe to it and doesn't feel rushed. Though this story has its own share of the tragedies in the og, overall, it's like a ray of sun shine so far comparatively seeing how contented Shen Yu is. It makes more use of modern knowledge to further the world, which I always love in stories, especially since it doesn't seem like the mc is a forced genius. 

The style is impeckable, as always (see what it did there? Peck?... I'll shut up now) There is a reason why BOC is the champion of Royal Road, and this is just more proof. It has a slightly different angle to the writing, which comes more from the idea than a difference in writing. 

The story has immense potential,  especially after having some foreknowledge from having read the og. You can see how some things will play out, and can anticipate some of the future events, but instead of making it feel boring and unoriginal, it makes you become more excited as you wait in anticipation of an event that you know is coming. And, of course, it has Bi De, so 6 stars/5 stars.

Grammar is fine, though there are some spelling mistakes. However, there has been no editing, and it it doesn't detract from the story, especially because it would only require a quick go-through to make it perfect because there are no grammar issues, only spelling and formatting errors. Noticeable, but well within the realm of tolerance.

The characters are great, with the ease of depth that comes with having written hundreds of original characters throughout the author's writing career. The BOC series always makes you forget that the characters are just words in a book... and sad that they are, too. Each character makes you feel like they are real people, especially the main character and main supporting cast. The attachment grows as you continue to read.

great story

Reviewed at: BOC AU: Elder But Younger Sister 9: Seeds and Eggs
by shoco, scholar of the nameless 10/31/2025, 9:57 PM

A truly great story — it shares some similarities with the author’s other works but features a terrific twist that takes the plot in a completely new direction!

The writing is excellent, and the characters are deep and well-developed.

The only issue I have is the somewhat rushed and slightly awkward relationship between Jin and Quinji. It also feels like her personality didn’t change much after everything she went through — we see some effects, but not nearly enough considering the trauma she experienced.

In addition, it would be great to have a clearer problem or antagonist to work toward resolving. One of the things I love about this author’s stories is their wholesomeness, but at times they can feel a bit aimless without a well-structured plot.

All in all, these are just minor flaws — the story is truly amazing and absolutely worth a read!

They Just Don't Stop

Reviewed at: Chapter 11: Heracles
by Saval 3/17/2026, 7:32 PM

   At this point, I wouldn't complain if Casual Farmer took breaks from the main story every time they had a unique BoC idea, just to write them down for us to read, cause getting to re-meet their adorable world and see Jin's adaptations to it each time just feels so fun. I don't know which is my favorite so far, probably the Soaring Isles one, but I'll have to wait and see.

   The original BoC is great, but was definitely designed around the side characters, who I miss in these spin-offs, but Jin having his time to shine without being as strong as he is in the original books is great, especially since it shows how his interactions with the world not only changes the world, but also completely changes him. The comparable willingness to take up a blade, to go as far as he did when his people were threatened? It makes this story only take second place to the Soaring Isles sect one because that story's had more time to grow, and has had some wonderfully wholesome interactions that endeared it to me.
