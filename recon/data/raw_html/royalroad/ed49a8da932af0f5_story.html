The saying goes that when a man is born the Fates weave his destiny and swaddle him in it. Then one day the man dies, and the swaddle becomes a shroud. Heaven moves on.

It is audacity to question the Fates. Olympus is Olympus. The land of men is the land of men. To transgress that, to cross the line of divinity and scale Olympus Mons? To defy the Fates and cast off their threads?

That is hubris. It’s a mark that every philosopher bears plainly on their soul.

Amazingly well-constructed world and characters

Reviewed at: 1.52 [Jason]
by ProdigalHobo 12/24/2021, 8:14 PM

This is a story that will stick with you throughout your day and occupy your mind at night. This is the first review I have ever written, because this is the first time I have found one of the greats of Royal Road before it's become entrenched on 'popular this week'

The main draw of this story is the style - Ya Boy's writing style is refined, well-considered and stunninging unique. I cannot express how much I have enjoyed reading Ya Boy's works so far becase I do not possess his same semantic flair.

The grammar is of very high quality. I have noticed a handful of minor errors across dozens of chapters. The quality is very high.

The story itself is still, I assume, in its infancy. Every answer births three more questions and our understanding of the setting expands with every release. Ya Boy has, in my opinion, done a fine job of mixing worldbuilding with narrative progression in a way that ensures that the worldbuilding explains the progression, and the progression naturally establishes further worldbuilding. I have very high expectations for the future of the novel.

The  characters themselves are incredibly well-done. The characters are layered, evolving and reasonably believable. Each character has a defined set of beliefs and values that arise naturally from their established history, and interactions between each character are very well done. The personalities of Virtuous Sons are one of the most incredible parts. I am very impressed.

Overall, this is a very high quality piece of work with what appears to be a very bright future ahead. I look forward to seeing where Ya Boy takes this. I highly encourage any reader unsure of whether this is worth the read to give the first few chapters a try - the story speaks for itself immediately, and the care and consideration that has gone into crafting a world with depth is immediately obvious.

Read the story. If you don't, you'll feel bad when it's parked in 'best rated' six months from now.

Probably the best Xianxia I've ever read.

Reviewed at: 1.16
by HereByChoice 3/1/2022, 10:47 AM

Striker has given life to this slowly dying genre. In a time were pill refining and beast taming has become overwhelmingly commonplace Virtuous Sons shines evermore brilliantly. 

While other Xianxias rely on tried and trite tropes to evoke humor and do their best to try and subvert your expections, (Which usually only goes so far as a few gimmicks introduced early on and forgotten) Virtuous Sons takes the familiar eastern setting and asks "but why tho?" and turns it on its head. 

I don't think a review will truly do the story justice so I'll just provide a summary of why I really enjoyed it.

1. References to philosophical theory appropriate to the time. Holy crud it's amazing. Seeing Plato's theory of the soul be included as a feature of cultivation is one of the coolest things I've ever seen done in a story.

2. The attitude of our main character. He's everything an arrogant young master should be. Someone who was born better than everyone around him and acts like it. I love it. He's been confined to this one area his entire life and been told he is greater than his clansmen who are greater than the ordinary people of the world. Lio is so starved for entertainment and conflict that he will purposely allow for slights against his person just to experience as new perspectives and emotions. If you like Gilgamesh from Fate you'll enjoy the story. 

3. The Bromance. It's what we are all here for really. The interaction between the two duetrogonists is what really sets this story apart from others. Just so well written.

I'm not the best writer but I hope that my love for this story came across.

Fascinating. A breath of fresh air

Reviewed at: 1.101
by Shaka 4/23/2022, 1:28 AM

A fascinating and well written story that seamlessly integrates xianxia with Greek and Roman mythology and history, breathing fresh air into the stale standards of the genre. The clean grammar and the flowing narration help too.

With richly written main characters and less deeply written - but still interesting - secondary ones, this story enthralls the reader with its world full of mysteries (pun intended) and its pace, that varies depending on what the author mainly wants to focus on: sometimes it's character development, other times it's adventure and discovery.

The dynamics between the two protagonists really carries the story, with their depth, well-defined traits and clear objectives.

The world is one we know - sort of - but clearly not ours, given the historical differences, so despite the familiarity we are still left wondering: "What other differences are there?", "What of this city, and of that country?", "What of said hero?", "What happened to the gods?", and so on; anticipation in our minds.

Being a long-time enthusiast of Greek and Roman epics, history and philosophy, the way the author has adapted the xianxia genre to a Mediterranean setting is extremely well done and fascinating, though perhaps some concepts of Roman and Greek philosophy could be a bit obscure or difficult to interiorize at first, and some things - such as the raven part - are not too clear.

All in all, kudos to the author for writing such a great story with engrossing characters and interesting ethical concepts to think about.
