The sterile hum of Aurelian Group's servers fills the secure room as Lin Wei and Adrian's fingers fly across keyboards, the digital gateway to the Glass Meridian shimmering within their grasp. The monitor displays a cascading waterfall of code, each line peeling back another layer of encryption, revealing the organization's carefully hidden infrastructure.

"Authentication bypass is holding," Lin Wei mutters, her eyes fixed on the scrolling data. "But we've got a window of maybe four minutes before the system does a hash check."

Adrian doesn't look up. "Three minutes fifty seconds. I built that protocol, remember? It double-checks every ninety seconds during off-peak hours."

"You built it when you were seventeen."

"And my seventeen-year-old self was paranoid enough to leave a backdoor." He taps a sequence of commands, and the waterfall slows to a crawl. "There. I've locked the hash chain into a recursive loop. We've got longer."

Lin Wei fights the urge to ask how much longer. Trust has never come easily to her, and this man—this heir to an empire built on shadows—demands a level of faith she isn't ready to give. But the data cascade is real, tangible evidence of the Glass Meridian's reach. She can't deny the results.

"Look," she says, pointing to a cluster of flagged records. "These transaction logs. They're routing through a shell bank in Singapore, but the originating IP keeps bouncing through Aurelian's internal network."

Adrian leans closer, and she catches the faint scent of his cologne—something woodsy, expensive. "That's the vulnerability. They think they're hidden behind Aurelian's legitimate traffic, but the encryption handshake leaves a signature every time."

"Can we trace it?"

"Already doing it." His voice is a low murmur, intense. "I'm injecting a tracer algorithm that mirrors their handshake pattern. If it works, we'll have a direct line into their master routing table."

Lin Wei's heart pounds. This is it. The thread that unravels the entire conspiracy. She watches the progress bar on her monitor crawl toward completion—87%, 88%, 89%—

The room shudders.

A low groan echoes from the server racks, followed by a sharp crack. Smoke curls from the ventilation panel above them.

"What was that?" Lin Wei is on her feet, pen falling to the floor.

Adrian's jaw tightens. "Overload. Someone triggered a purge protocol remotely." He's already moving, grabbing her arm. "Get away from the console."

The monitor sparks. The progress bar freezes at 94%.

"No—" Lin Wei reaches for the keyboard, but Adrian yanks her back with more force than she expected. She stumbles into him, and he wraps his arms around her, turning his body to shield hers as the server rack directly beside them erupts in a shower of sparks and molten plastic.

The heat washes over them, acrid and suffocating. Lin Wei's face is pressed against his chest, her senses overwhelmed by the crackle of burning circuitry and the steady thrum of his heartbeat beneath her cheek. For three seconds—maybe four—the world contracts to just this: the solid weight of his body braced over hers, the tension in his muscles as he absorbs the impact of the blast.

Then it's over.

Adrian releases her immediately, stepping back with a controlled exhale. "You okay?"

She nods, breathless from something that has nothing to do with the smoke. "Fine. Barely."

But there's no time to process the moment. Alarms erupt from every speaker in the room—a piercing shriek that drills into her skull. Red warning lights strobe across the ceiling.

"Security breach," Adrian says, his voice flat. "They know we're here."

Lin Wei grabs her bag, shoving the portable drive into her pocket. "We need the tracer data. If we leave it running—"

"We can't stay. They're locking down the building." He's already at the door, pressing an earpiece to his ear. "I'm getting comm interference. They've activated a full spectrum jammer."

Lin Wei hesitates, her gaze darting between the computer terminal and the door. The data is almost complete. 94% traced. But the alarms are shrieking, and she can hear footsteps pounding in the corridor outside.

"We have to go," Adrian says, not a suggestion anymore.

"I know." She makes a decision. She pulls a small data chip from her bag—the one she'd prepared as a fail-safe—and presses it into the back of a loose vent cover near the server rack. It's not much, but it's a thread she can pull later. A digital ghost in the machine.

Then she runs.

The hallway is chaos. Smoke pours from ceiling vents, mixing with the strobe lights into a disorienting fog. Lin Wei follows Adrian's silhouette, trusting his knowledge of the building's layout. They turn left, then right, down a stairwell that should lead to a service exit.

"Wait." Adrian stops abruptly, holding up a hand. "There's a security detail two floors down. They're sweeping upward."

"Trapped?"

"Not yet." He scans the corridor with those assessing eyes. "There's an old maintenance shaft near the server room. Leads to the parking garage. It'll be tight, but it's our only option."

Lin Wei hates the idea of squeezing through dark, narrow spaces. But she hates the idea of being captured by the Glass Meridian more.

"Lead the way."

They double back, retracing their steps through the haze. The maintenance shaft is hidden behind a false panel, barely wide enough for her shoulders. Adrian goes first, offering her a hand as she follows. The metal ladder groans under their weight, and every sound echoes in the confined space.

It takes five minutes to descend three floors. Lin Wei's arms burn, but she doesn't stop. When they finally emerge into the parking garage, the air is cooler, less acrid. Adrian leads her to a nondescript sedan, and they slide into the seats.

He starts the engine without speaking, pulling out of the garage with controlled urgency. They don't see any pursuit. Not yet.

As the city lights blur past, Lin Wei finally lets out the breath she's been holding. Her hands are shaking. "That was too close. They knew exactly when to trigger the overload. The timing—it was too precise."

Adrian's grip on the steering wheel tightens. "Someone on the inside sold us out. Someone who knew our access point and our timeline."

She turns to look at him, the full weight of the realization settling over her. "Someone we trusted."

His jaw clenches, but he doesn't argue.

They drive in silence for a long moment, the only sounds the hum of tires on asphalt and the echo of that alarm in her memory. Lin Wei replays every interaction of the past forty-eight hours, every word exchanged. Betrayal leaves a cold, familiar taste in her mouth.

Just before they turn onto the highway, she catches it—the faint scent of a distinctive, expensive perfume lingering in the air of the sedan's cabin. It's out of place. Her perfume. She never wears that brand.

Her blood runs cold.

Someone else was in this car recently. Someone who knew they would need a quick escape.

She meets Adrian's eyes in the rearview mirror. He smells it too. The question hangs between them, unspoken: Who else knows their plans?

The night stretches ahead, full of shadows and answers that slip further away with every mile.
