The server room hummed around them, a sterile cocoon of blinking lights and chilled air. Lin Wei stood behind Adrian, watching his reflection in the darkened monitor as he navigated through layers of encrypted data. His jaw was set, his movements precise, but she caught the almost imperceptible tremor in his hand when he paused over a certain file.

"What is it?"

He didn't answer immediately. Instead, he pulled up a secondary window and began running a decryption key she recognized from their earlier work on the Aurelian network. This time, his rhythm was different—hesitant, almost reluctant.

"Adrian."

"I need to be sure before I say it." His voice was flat, but there was an edge to it. Fragile.

She stepped closer, close enough to see the screen. It displayed a series of transaction logs, each stamped with a routing code that terminated at a private account. The account name was obscured by layers of shell companies, but the digital signature at the bottom told a different story.

A stylized 'T' embedded in the encryption pattern. It was subtle, easy to miss if you weren't looking for it. But now that she saw it, she couldn't unsee it.

"Your father."

Adrian's silence was confirmation enough.

Lin Wei's mind raced, piecing together the implications. They had suspected Marcus Thorne's involvement, known he was connected to the Glass Meridian through Vance's confession. But this was something else entirely. This was definitive proof that Thorne wasn't just a facilitator—he was the hand that moved the pieces.

"How deep does it go?"

Adrian pulled up another file, this one dated six months ago. It was a memo, encrypted with the same signature, detailing the acquisition of a medical research firm in Zurich. The memo included instructions to suppress certain trial results and redirect funding to a subsidiary that, according to the ledger, didn't officially exist.

"The architect," Lin Wei murmured. "Not just the door. The entire building."

Adrian's hand stopped trembling. He clenched it into a fist, then slowly relaxed his fingers. "I built the encryption protocol he used for these messages. I was seventeen. He asked me to create something unbreakable for his personal communications, and I was proud of it."

"Because you didn't know what he was using it for."

"No." He turned to face her, and she saw something raw in his eyes. "But that's the problem, isn't it? I should have known. I built his weapon."

Lin Wei held his gaze. "You were a kid trying to impress your father. That's not the same as being complicit."

"It makes me a tool. One he's been using for eighteen years."

She wanted to say something more, but she knew it wouldn't matter. He wasn't looking for absolution. He needed her to understand.

She reached out and placed her hand over his, a light touch that carried more weight than any words could. His fingers were cold, but they curled around hers, a silent acknowledgment.

"Then we use it against him."

He blinked. "What?"

"The encryption protocol. You built it, so you know its weaknesses. That's our way in."

He studied her for a long moment, searching for something. Whatever he found made his shoulders drop a fraction of an inch. "It's risky. If he detects any tampering, he'll know exactly who did it."

"Then we make sure he doesn't detect it until it's too late."

She released his hand and turned back to the monitor. "What's the next transaction? The one that hasn't been processed yet?"

Adrian pulled up a schedule. "There's a wire transfer scheduled to go through tomorrow morning. From a Geneva account to a research facility in the Mediterranean corridor. The facility was flagged in the chip you found."

"The same one Vance mentioned."

"Exactly. It's part of their expansion plans."

Lin Wei's pulse quickened. "If we intercept the transfer and embed a tracing agent, we can map the entire network. Find every node connected to his operations."

"Assuming he doesn't have countermeasures in place." Adrian's expression was wary, but there was a flicker of something else. Respect, maybe. "He taught me everything I know about digital security. He'll have layered defenses."

"Then we need a way to bypass them without triggering an alert."

Adrian was quiet. Then he opened a new window, pulled up a string of code that looked like part of the encryption protocol she'd seen earlier. "There's a backdoor in the initialization sequence. A bug I left intentionally, so I could access his files if I ever needed to. He never caught it because I embedded it in the pseudorandom number generator, making it look like a rounding error."

"Can you still use it?"

"It's dormant. I'd need to reactivate it remotely, which means sending a handshake signal through the same channel he uses. The window is narrow—if he's monitoring traffic at that moment, he'll see the anomaly."

Lin Wei considered. "Then we need a distraction, something that occupies his attention long enough for you to send the signal."

"What kind of distraction?"

She thought about Vance's data chip, the information they'd already gathered. "What if we send him a report that looks like it came from one of his own operatives? Something that demands immediate attention. A security breach in another branch."

"That would require insider knowledge of their internal protocols."

"We have his encryption patterns. We have the routing codes. We can fake a communication from one of the shell companies."

Adrian was already typing, pulling up a template. "It might work. But if he scrutinizes the metadata, he'll see the mismatch."

"Then we make sure the metadata is perfect." Lin Wei pulled up the file they'd obtained from Sophia, the one that contained details about C009 and the Interpol operative. "We use his own channels to deliver the message. Route it through the same nodes he uses."

For the first time, Adrian's expression shifted. The tension didn't leave his face, but it softened into something resembling grim determination. "You're willing to burn everything we've gathered on a single gambit."

"I'm willing to burn it if it means we finally have a chance to expose him."

He held her gaze for a long moment. Then he nodded.

The next two hours were a blur of code, timestamps, and simulated traffic patterns. Adrian worked methodically, constructing the fake communication while Lin Wei cross-referenced the routing logs to ensure the metadata matched. She felt the weight of every second, the knowledge that one mistake would tip Thorne off and end their window permanently.

When they were ready, Adrian paused.

"Once I send this, there's no going back. We'll have committed to a direct confrontation."

Lin Wei didn't hesitate. "We've been fighting from the shadows since the beginning. It's time to light a match."

He typed the final command.

The screen went dark for a moment, then displayed a single line of text: Handshake established. Backdoor activated.

Adrian exhaled. "We're in."

They scrolled through the decrypted data, a flood of files and communications that painted a picture Lin Wei had only glimpsed before. Transaction logs, research reports, personnel files, and at the center of it all, the same digital signature.

The architect.

But it was the last file that made her blood run cold. A directive, signed with Thorne's signature, addressed to a facility in Eastern Europe.

Operation Palingenesis—Initiate Phase Two. Target: Neural mapping on viable subjects. Begin acquisition of genetic material from Project Phoenix descendants.

"The Mediterranean corridor," Lin Wei said. "It was a decoy. The real plan is further east."

Adrian's jaw tightened. "He's not expanding. He's consolidating. This facility is where the Meridian's research is being centralized. If Phase Two goes through, they'll have everything they need to start human trials."

Lin Wei's thoughts flew to her mother. The empty hospital room. Thorne's leverage.

"Irrefutable proof of his involvement," she said. "And a timeline to stop him."

"In theory." Adrian met her eyes, and she saw the conflict there. "But if we expose him now, he'll know it came from us. He'll accelerate the timeline and move my mother deeper into his network."

"Your mother?"

He looked away. "She's still alive. I've been tracking her location through the same encrypted channels. If I move against Thorne publicly, he'll make sure I never see her again."

Lin Wei processed this. The choice crystallized: expose Thorne and risk losing his mother, or continue fighting from the shadows, hoping to find another angle.

"What do you want to do?"

Adrian was silent. The hum of the server room seemed deafening.

Then he turned back to the screen.

"We find my mother first. Then we burn him to the ground."

Lin Wei nodded. She understood. Some sacrifices were too heavy to ask.

But she also knew the clock was ticking. Phase Two was already in motion.

And Marcus Thorne was still waiting for them to make the next move.
