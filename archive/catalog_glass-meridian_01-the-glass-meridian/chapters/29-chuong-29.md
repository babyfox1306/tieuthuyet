The ticking clock wasn’t just metaphorical. It beat in Lin Wei’s head like a war drum, her pulse syncing with its relentless march. Across the table, Adrian Vale’s posture was rigid, his eyes narrowed at the screen between them. The room was dim, save for the cold glow of LED monitors, their fragmented intel spilling across the surfaces like shards of glass. 

“Cross-reference it again,” Lin Wei said, her voice sharper than intended. The tremor in her hand as she gripped the edge of the table betrayed the immense strain she was under. She willed it to stop, curling her fingers into a fist.

Adrian’s gaze flicked to her briefly before returning to the data. “I already did. Twice,” he replied, his voice low and controlled. Yet, there was something beneath it—a tensile edge that hinted at the weight pressing on him as well. “If the Meridian’s target is this sequence, then we’re looking at a high-level activation. Something they’ve spent years preparing for.”

She leaned in, scanning the strings of numbers and words on the screen. The suspicious activity within Aurelian Group’s network—the encrypted messages, the purged records—it all pointed to one chilling conclusion. The Glass Meridian wasn’t just moving resources; it was moving people. And the name “Ascension Program” kept surfacing in the data trails like a ghost.

“They’re gathering participants,” she murmured, her analytical mind racing. “But why this specific genetic marker? It’s like they’re—”

“Selecting,” Adrian finished, his tone grim. “This isn’t random. It never was. They’re curating something.”

Lin Wei’s stomach churned. Her journalist instincts told her to dig deeper, to pull apart every thread until the full picture emerged. But the human side of her recoiled at what that picture might be. “We need more than speculation,” she said tightly. “We need their immediate target. What’s the next step in this... program?”

Adrian’s jaw clenched—a tic she’d come to recognize as his sign of restrained frustration. “The sequencing file,” he said, gesturing to the corrupted data. “If we could decrypt the missing fragments...”

“Assuming the Meridian hasn’t completely erased it,” she shot back, exhaling sharply. “It’s like chasing shadows.”

Adrian didn’t respond immediately. Instead, he reached over, his fingers gliding across the keyboard in measured movements. Lin Wei watched as another layer of encryption peeled away, revealing a location buried within the metadata: Hanfield Research Facility—Site A.

“Hanfield,” Adrian said. “It’s one of the ‘clean’ subsidiaries on Aurelian’s books. Publicly, it’s a biotech lab specializing in pharmacogenomics.”

“But that’s the glossed-over version,” Lin Wei interjected. “What’s beneath it?”

Adrian met her eyes, his gaze like dark steel. “If it’s connected to the Meridian, it’s where they test people who fit their criteria.”

She felt the blood drain from her face. “Test? So they’re experimenting on... candidates?”

“Or refining them,” he replied quietly. “We might be looking at the final stage before... whatever ‘Ascension’ entails.”

Lin Wei recoiled slightly, her mind churning with horrific possibilities. But the fear calcified into determination. “Then we don’t have time to wait around. We need to intercept before they move forward.”

“And walk into a Glass Meridian stronghold without backup or a secure extraction?” Adrian countered, his voice dipping into incredulity. “Do you even hear yourself?”

“Do you have a better idea?” she challenged, stepping closer until they were nearly face-to-face. The proximity forced them into an unyielding standoff, the tension electric. She added, “Because if we wait, they’re going to disappear again. And next time, we might not get this close.”

Adrian’s eyes searched hers, some internal conflict playing out that she couldn’t quite read. Finally, he exhaled a controlled breath. “If we do this, we do it my way. Minimal exposure.”

Lin Wei stiffened but nodded reluctantly. “Fine. Your way. For now.”

He arched a brow at her, the faintest flicker of amusement breaking through his otherwise stoic expression. “As much as I appreciate your boundless trust.”

She rolled her eyes, but the smallest smirk tugged at her lips despite the grim situation. “Don’t flatter yourself.”

Adrian turned back to the screen, his focus sharpening. “Hanfield has a critical window of vulnerability in their shift rotations, about two hours from now. That’s our in.” He adjusted the schematic display, highlighting areas marked as high-security zones. “The mainframe housing the sequencing data will be there—if it hasn’t already been transferred.”

“And if we’re caught?” Lin Wei asked, crossing her arms.

“We won’t be,” Adrian replied, though the slight hesitation in his tone didn’t inspire confidence.

The plan unfolded swiftly, the urgency leaving no room for second-guessing. Lin Wei continued to trace the digital footprints, isolating suspicious activity that corroborated Adrian’s insider intel. Together, they pieced together an infiltration strategy that felt as fragile as glass. One wrong move, and it would shatter.

At one point, Lin Wei’s hand hovered over a file that refused to open. Her fingers trembled slightly, and she curled them into a fist again to still the motion. Adrian noticed—it was impossible for him not to, with the way his sharp gaze seemed to catch everything. But he said nothing. Instead, his hand came to rest lightly on her shoulder, an unspoken reassurance that startled her more than she cared to admit.

“Focus,” he said softly, his voice threading between command and comfort.

She swallowed hard, nodding. “I am.”

The car ride to Hanfield was tense, the hum of the engine underscored by the weight of what they were about to do. Lin Wei found herself gazing out the window, the city lights streaking past in a blur. Her mind raced through potential scenarios, contingencies, and escape routes.

“Second thoughts?” Adrian’s question broke through her thoughts. He was watching her, his expression unreadable.

“About exposing myself to a shadow organization with access to god knows what? Constantly,” she replied dryly. “You?”

His lips curved into a ghost of a smile. “It’s not my first dance with the Meridian,” he said, though there was a distant note in his voice that she couldn’t place. “But this time... I care about the outcome.”

She blinked at him, startled. The weight of his words lingered in the space between them, heavier than the ticking clock had ever been.

When they arrived, Hanfield looked exactly like what it was designed to be: innocuous. The facility loomed in sterile whites and grays, its modern architecture blending seamlessly with the industrial park around it. Lin Wei’s pulse quickened. It was too quiet.

They slipped past the perimeter, using the gaps Adrian had identified in the cameras. Inside, the air buzzed faintly with the hum of machinery and the occasional click of footsteps echoing through the corridors. Lin Wei’s every nerve was on edge.

Near the central lab, Lin Wei and Adrian paused beside a door marked Authorized Personnel Only. Adrian keyed in the stolen credentials, and the lock released with a muted click. Lin Wei gave him a sharp look. “You’ve been busy.”

“I try,” he said, pushing the door open.

Inside, rows of servers blinked softly in the dimly lit room. Lin Wei set to work immediately, her fingers flying across the console. Adrian stood behind her, his presence a solid, grounding force.

“It’s here,” she whispered, her chest tightening as she accessed the sequencing data. The fragmented files filled the screen, and the pieces began to align. Hanfield wasn’t just a facility—it was a launchpad. The Meridian’s immediate goal was to trigger a controlled activation of their “Ascension” candidates, using tailored genetic engineering to push the subjects to a new physiological threshold.

Her breath caught. “This... this is worse than I thought.”

Adrian’s brow furrowed. “What do you mean?”

“They’re not just selecting people,” she said, her voice low and urgent. “They’re rewriting them.”

Before Adrian could respond, the unmistakable sound of heavy boots echoed down the corridor. Lin Wei froze, her blood running cold.

Adrian grabbed her wrist, pulling her back and into the shadows of the room. His fingers tightened around hers, a silent but deliberate gesture. Their eyes met in the dim light, and Lin Wei felt the intensity of his gaze like a physical thing. For a moment, the world narrowed to just them—their breathing, their pounding hearts, their unspoken resolve.

“We stick to the plan,” he said softly, his voice like steel wrapped in velvet. His hand didn’t let go of hers.

She nodded, her pulse roaring in her ears as they prepared to move. But as they edged toward the exit, the data on the screen shifted.

An alert flashed: “Initiation Sequence—20 Minutes Remaining.”

Lin Wei’s heart leapt into her throat. “Adrian,” she hissed. “They’re starting it. Now.”

His expression darkened, his free hand flexing at his side. “Then we stop it.”

“Or we die trying,” she said under her breath.

The sound of footsteps grew louder, closer—an ominous drumbeat heralding their confrontation with an enemy that always seemed one step ahead. Lin Wei tightened her grip on Adrian’s hand, a rare moment of vulnerability cutting through her mounting fear. 

“Ready?” he asked, his voice low.

She looked up at him, her determination burning like fire. “Always.”

As the footsteps stopped outside the door, they braced themselves. Whatever waited for them beyond it would determine not only their fates but the timeline for a chilling new era of human manipulation.

The door began to open, flooding the room with cold, white light.
