The sterile hum of servers enveloped them like a living thing, vibrating through the concrete floor and into Lin Wei's bones. Her fingers flew across the keyboard with practiced precision, each keystroke a calculated strike against the darkness she was only beginning to understand. Beside her, Adrian worked in parallel, his face illuminated by the cold glow of three monitors, his jaw set in that familiar rigid line she had come to read as barely contained tension.

"This legacy system access point is a ghost," she said, not taking her eyes off the cascading lines of code. "Whoever configured it knew what they were doing. It exists in the gaps between authenticated networks."

"That's because it was designed for deniability." Adrian's voice was low, stripped of its usual commanding edge. "My father had it built for off-the-books data transfers. Only a handful of people ever knew it existed."

Lin Wei's fingers paused for a fraction of a second. "And you're one of them."

"I'm the one who helped design the encryption protocols." He glanced at her, something unreadable flickering in his eyes. "When I was seventeen. Before I understood what he was using it for."

The admission hung heavy between them, but there was no time to unpack it. The digital landscape before them was a maze of firewalls and honeypots, each layer designed to trap the unwary. Lin Wei traced a path through the architecture, her mind mapping connections like neural pathways.

"Here," she murmured, highlighting a cluster of data nodes. "This nexus point channels traffic from three different departments—research, medical archives, and strategic development. If the Glass Meridian has a digital footprint inside Aurelian, it would route through here."

Adrian leaned closer, his shoulder brushing hers. The contact was brief, but she felt the warmth of it linger. "That's what I was hoping you'd find. The data cascade protocol masks anomalies by distributing them across legitimate traffic. But the pattern recognition required to spot it—"

"Is exactly why you need me," she finished, a trace of sardonic pride in her voice.

He didn't deny it.

Together, they worked the vulnerability. Lin Wei felt the familiar rush of discovery as the system's defenses peeled back, revealing a hidden partition that hummed with encrypted data. The Glass Meridian's operations were here—fragmented, obscured, but present. She could taste the truth on the edge of her consciousness.

"I'm in," she breathed. "But the decryption key is complex. It'll take at least three minutes to brute-force."

"We don't have three minutes," Adrian said, his eyes scanning a secondary monitor that displayed network traffic patterns. "Someone's running a diagnostic sweep. Standard maintenance, but if it intersects with our access point—"

"Then we get creative." Lin Wei rerouted the decryption process through a series of proxy servers, masking the activity as routine data synchronization. The progress bar crept forward: 12 percent. 18 percent.

Adrian's hand moved to his earpiece, listening. "We're clear for now. But whoever set up this sweep has elevated clearance. It's not random."

Lin Wei's stomach tightened. "Someone knows we're here."

"Not yet. But they will if we don't accelerate."

The decryption hit 43 percent. Lin Wei pushed harder, her fingers dancing across the keyboard. The monitor flickered, and a warning icon appeared.

SYSTEM OVERLOAD DETECTED. OPTIMAL PERFORMANCE COMPROMISED.

"The brute-force algorithm is generating too much heat," she said, her voice tight. "If I pull back, we lose progress. If I push forward, the system might crash and trigger a full lockdown."

Adrian stood abruptly, moving behind her. His hand landed on her shoulder, firm and grounding. "Don't stop. I'll handle the fallout."

Before she could argue, the console erupted in blinding white light. The overload hit critical mass, the screen blazing with raw luminescence that seared her vision. Lin Wei threw up an arm instinctively, but before she could reel back, Adrian's body was there—solid, shielding, his arms wrapping around her and pulling her against his chest as he turned his back to the flare.

The world narrowed to the beat of his heart against her ear, the heat of his breath on her hair, the impossible gentleness of his hands cradling her head. The glare lasted only seconds, but the electric charge of his proximity lingered, crackling in the space between them.

"You're reckless," he murmured, his voice a low vibration through his ribs.

"And you're predictable." She pulled back just enough to meet his eyes. "Always playing the hero."

A ghost of a smile tugged at his lips. "Someone has to keep you alive long enough to finish this."

The moment shattered as an alarm screamed through the server room. Red lights strobed across the walls, and Adrian's earpiece erupted with frantic chatter.

"Company's coming," he said, already moving. "They know. We've been compromised."

Lin Wei's mind raced. "The decryption was masked—how could they—"

"Someone tipped them off." Adrian's voice was ice. "This was too precise. They were waiting for us to trigger the overload."

They scrambled. Lin Wei grabbed a portable drive, initiating a desperate data grab. The main objective was lost—the decryption was only at 67 percent, and the system was locking down. But as she disconnected, a single file caught her attention, its name jumping out from the cascading directory:

PHOENIX_OVERRIDE_PROTOCOL — ASSIGNMENT: C006

It sat in a folder labeled "Decoy Operations," accessible only because the lockdown had momentarily exposed hidden directories. It could be a trap. It could be nothing. But Lin Wei's gut screamed that it was connected.

She copied it.

"Now!" Adrian seized her arm, dragging her toward the emergency exit. Behind them, the server room doors burst open, the heavy footfalls of security personnel echoing off the metal walls.

They plunged into a maintenance corridor, Adrian leading with the confidence of someone who had memorized every escape route years ago. Lin Wei's lungs burned as they sprinted through the dark, the alarm sirens fading behind thick concrete walls.

They emerged into a parking garage, sliding into a nondescript sedan. Adrian had the engine running before Lin Wei had closed her door. They sped into the night, leaving Aurelian Group's headquarters shrinking in the rearview mirror.

Silence stretched between them, heavy and accusing.

Lin Wei's hand trembled as she inserted the drive into her laptop. The file was there—a single document, encrypted with a cipher she didn't recognize. A note was attached in plaintext:

The path to Phoenix is not built on ruins but on sacrifice. Follow the scent of betrayal to its source. C006 will lead you to the heart of the flame.

She read it aloud, her voice hollow.

Adrian's grip on the steering wheel tightened until his knuckles went white. "We were set up. Someone we trusted—personally trusted—sold the timeline to the Glass Meridian."

Lin Wei closed her eyes, and the memory surfaced unbidden: the faint, unmistakable scent of expensive perfume lingering in the sterile server room. Jasmine and sandalwood, the kind that clung to silk and whispered of boardroom power. She had dismissed it as Adrian's cologne at first, but he wore something earthier. This was different. This was a signature.

She opened her eyes, meeting Adrian's gaze in the reflection of the windshield.

"They knew exactly when we'd be there. They left a calling card."

The words hung in the air between them, a chilling verdict.

Someone close to them—someone they had let into the fragile circle of trust—had already been swallowed by the Glass Meridian.

And now, the price of truth had just been tallied.
