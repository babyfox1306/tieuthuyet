Evelyn's dossier accused Marcus Holt of theft, coercion, and protecting violent informants.

Its evidence proved none of those things.

The theft was a court-authorized seizure from a contractor who sold medical-record access. The coercion claim came from a broker Holt had arrested after the man threatened a witness. The violent informant had been removed from an operation six months before Holt inherited it.

The rest of the file explained the real offense.

For eighteen months, Holt had traced a private market that sold credentials, surveillance, transport, and clean identities to wealthy clients. His warrants touched the contractor who queried Priya's office. His financial map reached two of Evelyn's shell companies. The transmission record for his still-sealed copy of C-010 placed Victor's attorney at the edge of the same network without revealing the package's contents.

Holt was not dangerous to Evelyn because he was corrupt.

He was dangerous because he was close.

I checked the first five claims through public court dockets, archived news reports, and records Sasha obtained independently. Four collapsed. The fifth—a complaint that Holt had concealed evidence—remained unresolved, but the complainant worked for one of Evelyn's companies.

"This isn't a target file," Sasha said. "It's a list of reasons she wants him gone."

"She knows."

"Then why hand it to you?"

"Because she thinks the Croft evidence makes my standard irrelevant."

I looked at Holt's photograph. Dark hair beginning to gray, badge clipped at his belt, expression impatient with whoever stood behind the camera. He was the detective who had questioned Priya. The pressure point Rhodes exploited but did not create.

"What are you going to tell her?" Sasha asked.

"That I accept the case."

"Nadia."

"A case. Not a kill."

Accepting gave me access to Evelyn's claims, her collection methods, and the pieces of her network she exposed every time she tried to direct me. Refusing immediately would give her nothing to preserve except the Croft file.

"And if Holt is innocent?" Sasha asked.

"I warn him before Evelyn can replace me. Then I give him a route into her market that doesn't expose Priya or David."

"That may not be possible."

"Then I find out before I promise it."

The season finale finished legal review at noon.

I entered the recording booth and turned on the red light.

"This is Nadia Cole, and you're listening to The Unheard.

"Elise Marchetti was twenty-eight years old when she was killed. She worked in a Portland bookstore. She was a daughter, a friend, and a woman whose life mattered beyond the file number assigned to her death.

"The documents released today show how Victor Rhodes corrupted the investigation around her. They show payments, intimidation, buried evidence, and a financial network that connected Elise's discovery to another failed murder case.

"They do not prove who put hands around Elise's throat.

"So this season ends without pretending every question is closed. Victor Rhodes cannot threaten another witness. The people who helped him still can.

"Justice is not a clean finish. It is the work left after the story stops being satisfying."

I let the silence hold for one beat.

"We are still listening."

I stopped the recording and published the episode.

Within minutes, listeners opened the source archive. Reporters called Vance. Federal investigators acknowledged the Meridian filing that cleared Lena. Elise's family released a statement asking the state to reopen the homicide.

The public story moved without my secret inside it.

For now.

My blank phone rang.

"You read the Holt file," Evelyn said.

"I started verifying it."

"You have one week."

"You don't set a kill date before I establish a crime."

"The Croft file establishes yours."

"Release it, and you lose my work. Hold it, and you accept my process."

"You are bargaining with your life."

"So are you. Holt has two shell companies. If I can find that in an afternoon, he may already know your name."

Evelyn went quiet.

"What do you need?" she asked.

"Raw collection. No summaries. Warrants, source interviews, payment records, and the identity of the complainant who accused him of hiding evidence."

"And when you confirm what he is?"

"I decide what the proof supports."

"That was not the agreement."

"It is the only agreement available."

The line remained open long enough for me to hear her revise her estimate of me.

"You will receive the raw file," she said. "Do not mistake that for trust."

"I won't."

She disconnected.

A transfer notification appeared on the isolated intake address. I left it unopened until Sasha could witness the import.

On the wall beside the microphone were four photographs I never displayed publicly.

Julian Croft.

Marcus Webb.

Daniel Ortega.

Victor Rhodes.

Four men. Four cases. Four times I had told myself the ending justified the method.

Evelyn believed she had acquired that method.

What she had actually given me was a way into her network and a reason to test whether Marcus Holt deserved protection from it.

I opened a paper notebook untouched by Wren's systems and wrote:

MARCUS HOLT — INVESTIGATION ONLY.

Beneath it, I wrote the rule that had survived grief, betrayal, and every attempt to turn me into a weapon.

No proof, no kill.
